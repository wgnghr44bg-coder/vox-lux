On a Saturday afternoon in July 2018, a quiet grocery store in Los Angeles became the scene of a shootout, a hostage crisis, and a death that would take eight years to reach a final verdict. This is the story of the Trader Joe's standoff in Silver Lake, told with the body camera footage released by the Los Angeles Police Department.

[BEELD: kaart Los Angeles, South LA naar Silver Lake]

It started across the city, in South Los Angeles. Twenty-eight-year-old Gene Evin Atkins was staying at his grandmother's house. According to his family, he had recently lost his job, and there had been arguments at home, including about his girlfriend living there.

At around one thirty in the afternoon on July twenty-first, Atkins shot his grandmother, who was in her seventies, several times. He also shot his girlfriend. Then he forced the wounded girlfriend into his grandmother's Toyota and drove away.

[BEELD: helikopter / achtervolging]

Police soon found the car. A helicopter followed it north, toward Hollywood, while patrol cars stayed close behind. During the chase, Atkins fired shots at the officers following him.

[BEELD: bodycam – auto crasht tegen paal voor de winkel]

In Silver Lake, on Hyperion Avenue, the Toyota crashed into a power pole right in front of a Trader Joe's. Atkins jumped out and ran toward the store entrance, still firing at the officers behind him.

[BEELD: bodycam – officieren schieten, vertraagd / ingezoomd, zonder stem]

Two officers fired back. In a few seconds, they fired eight shots in his direction. One bullet hit Atkins in the arm. Another struck someone inside the store.

Her name was Melyda Corado. Her friends called her Mely. She was twenty-seven years old and an assistant manager at the store. She died at the scene.

[BEELD: bodycam – buiten de winkel, officieren in dekking]

Inside, Atkins now had dozens of people with him: shoppers and employees, somewhere between forty and fifty. Some managed to escape. Others were trapped inside.

For about three hours, police negotiators talked with Atkins, while SWAT officers surrounded the building.

[BEELD: overgave]

At around six thirty in the evening, Atkins agreed to give up. He put handcuffs on himself, and walked out of the store together with several hostages. Officers took him into custody. No one else inside was killed.

[BEELD: kaart/tekst – na afloop]

A few days later, the Los Angeles police chief said that the bullet that killed Melyda Corado had come from an officer's gun, not from Atkins. In December 2020, the district attorney decided that the two officers had acted lawfully, and they were not charged. In 2024, the city of Los Angeles agreed to pay her family nine and a half million dollars.

Atkins himself was charged with murder, because prosecutors said his actions had caused her death. In June 2026, a jury convicted him of forty charges, but could not agree on the murder charge. At a second trial, in August 2026, he was found guilty of second-degree murder. In October 2026, he was sentenced to more than two hundred years to life in prison.

[BEELD: rustig slotbeeld van de winkel]

The standoff lasted only a few hours. But the questions it raised, about police shooting in crowded places, and about who is responsible when a bystander dies, are still being asked today.
