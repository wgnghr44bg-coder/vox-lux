# Titel (voorstel): Black Friday Will Break a Record. That's Not Good News
# Opbouw: antwoord vooraan (record, maar vooral door hogere prijzen en de rijkste huishoudens), dan cijfers, prijzen, huishouden, rijk en arm, lenen, deals, wat nu.
@scene open-record
This holiday season, Americans are expected to spend more than one trillion dollars in stores.
That has never happened before.
And on Black Friday alone, shoppers are expected to spend almost thirteen billion dollars online.
@scene open-maar
It sounds like a booming economy.
But look a little closer, and the picture changes.
@scene open-antwoord
About half of that growth is not people buying more.
It is people paying more for the same things.
The average family still plans to spend less than it did two years ago.
And a big share of the spending comes from the richest households.
@scene open-vraag
So who is really behind this record?
And what does it say about the economy you live in?
Let's start with the numbers.
@scene h1-nrf
On October fifteenth, the National Retail Federation released its forecast.
It expects holiday sales in November and December to grow four point two percent.
That would bring the total to one point zero one trillion dollars.
Last year, sales grew three point one percent, to nine hundred seventy-two billion.
This year's growth would also beat the average of the last twenty years.
@scene h1-adobe
Online, the numbers look even bigger.
Adobe, which tracks spending on thousands of retail websites, expects two hundred seventy-five billion dollars in online sales this season.
On Black Friday, it expects twelve point nine billion dollars.
That is nine percent more than last year.
@scene h1-week
And the Monday after, Cyber Monday, could reach fifteen point one billion dollars.
No single day of online shopping has ever crossed fifteen billion.
Even Thanksgiving Day itself is expected to bring in almost seven billion dollars online.
From Thanksgiving to Cyber Monday, Americans are expected to spend forty-seven and a half billion dollars online in just five days.
@scene h1-banen
Stores are getting ready.
Retailers plan to hire between three hundred twenty-five thousand and three hundred seventy-five thousand seasonal workers.
But even the retail federation calls them cautious.
It points to higher gas prices, persistent inflation, higher interest rates and consumer debt.
@scene h2-prijzen
And that is the first catch.
These forecasts are not adjusted for inflation.
They measure dollars, not things.
According to the Associated Press, forecasters expect higher prices to make up about half of the growth.
The Mastercard Economics Institute said the same about its own forecast.
@scene h2-voorbeeld
Think of it like this.
If a toy cost twenty dollars last year and twenty-one dollars now, sales go up.
But nobody bought an extra toy.
@scene h2-benzine
One price hit harder than any other.
After the war with Iran began, gas prices jumped.
Before the war, a gallon cost less than three dollars.
In the middle of October, it was four dollars and thirty-three cents.
In October, gasoline was thirty-seven percent more expensive than a year earlier.
@scene h2-nu
Gas has started to come down again.
Just before Thanksgiving week, the national average was about three dollars and seventy cents.
But that is still almost sixty cents more than a year ago.
And every dollar spent at the pump is a dollar that does not go to gifts.
@scene h2-verwachting
Shoppers know it.
In a survey by Deloitte, seventy-nine percent of Americans said they expect higher prices on holiday items this year.
Two years ago, it was sixty-nine percent.
The latest yearly figure from the government shows prices up three point seven percent.
@scene h3-huishouden
Now let's look at a single family.
Deloitte asked four thousand Americans how much they plan to spend this holiday season.
The answer was one thousand six hundred fifty-four dollars per household.
That is four percent more than last year.
@scene h3-terug
But here is what the headlines leave out.
Last year, that number had dropped sharply.
In 2024, the average household planned to spend one thousand seven hundred seventy-eight dollars.
So even after this year's increase, families plan to spend about one hundred twenty-four dollars less than two years ago.
And that is before you count higher prices.
@scene h3-spullen
How they spend is changing too.
Spending on gifts and other goods is expected to rise ten percent.
But spending on holiday activities, like travel, events and hosting, is expected to fall four percent.
People are cutting back on going out, to keep buying presents.
@scene h3-jagers
And they are hunting for deals harder than ever.
Thirty-eight percent of shoppers say they use at least five ways to save money.
They wait for sales, switch brands when prices rise, and cut other spending to pay for the holidays.
Many also started early.
By the end of October, shoppers had already spent twenty-eight percent of their holiday budget.
That is the highest share in six years.
@scene h4-kloof
So if the average family is careful, where does the record come from?
The answer is the top.
In the Deloitte survey, households earning more than two hundred thousand dollars a year plan to spend about three thousand six hundred dollars.
Households earning less than fifty thousand plan to spend just six hundred ninety-three.
The richer households plan to spend more than five times as much.
@scene h4-k
Economists call this a K-shaped economy.
One group goes up, the other goes down.
People with stocks and homes have seen their wealth grow.
People who live from paycheck to paycheck feel every price increase.
The average hides both groups.
@scene h4-zandi
Mark Zandi, the chief economist at Moody's Analytics, has tried to measure this.
By his estimate, the top ten percent of earners now make up about forty-five percent of all consumer spending.
And the top twenty percent, households earning more than one hundred seventy-five thousand dollars a year, make up almost sixty percent.
Other economists say his method is too high, and their numbers are lower.
But they agree on the direction.
More and more of the spending depends on the people at the top.
@scene h4-risico
That makes the economy fragile in a new way.
If the stock market falls, the richest households may spend less.
And because they spend so much, the whole economy would feel it.
@scene h4-gevoel
You can also see the split in how people feel.
In November, the University of Michigan's index of consumer sentiment was fifty-one point five.
That is one of the lowest readings in its history.
The researchers said sentiment is still nine percent below February, before the war with Iran began.
So Americans are expected to spend a record amount, while feeling gloomier than they have in a long time.
@scene h5-lenen
And some are paying for it with money they do not have yet.
Adobe expects Americans to spend twenty-one point three billion dollars this season using buy now, pay later.
These services split a purchase into smaller payments, often four, without interest.
On Black Friday alone, Adobe expects more than eight hundred million dollars of these payments.
@scene h5-te-laat
The problem shows up later.
In a LendingTree survey from March, forty-seven percent of buy now, pay later users said they had paid late at least once in the past year.
Two years earlier, it was thirty-four percent.
And twenty-nine percent said they used it to pay for groceries.
@scene h5-waarom
When people split a grocery bill into four payments, that is not a sign of a strong consumer.
It is a sign of a stretched one.
And those payments do not disappear after the holidays.
They keep coming in January and February.
@scene h6-deals
So are the Black Friday deals at least real?
According to Adobe, discounts this year are about the same as last year.
The biggest are on electronics, up to thirty percent off, and on toys, up to twenty-nine percent.
TVs, computers and clothing can reach about twenty-three percent.
@scene h6-wanneer
Timing matters.
Adobe expects the best prices on TVs, toys, clothing, appliances and furniture on Black Friday itself.
For laptops and other electronics, the best day is usually Cyber Monday.
And earlier in November, discounts were much smaller, around fourteen percent.
@scene h6-basis
There is one more trend that tells you a lot.
People are using Black Friday to stock up on everyday things.
Compared to September, Adobe expects online sales of basic clothing to more than triple during Cyber Week.
Personal care products could rise one hundred fifty percent, and baby products more than double.
When people wait for a sale to buy diapers and soap, money is tight.
@scene h7-volgende
So what should you watch?
First, the next reading of consumer sentiment from the University of Michigan, on December fourth.
Second, the real sales numbers from Adobe after the weekend, compared to the forecast.
Third, gas prices, because they decide how much money is left for everything else.
And fourth, late payments on buy now, pay later in the new year, when the bills arrive.
@scene slot
This Black Friday will probably break a record.
But a record in dollars is not the same as a record in confidence.
The real story is not how much Americans spend.
It is who is doing the spending, and how they are paying for it.
