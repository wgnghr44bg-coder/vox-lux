# Titel (voorstel): Costco Sells a $1.50 Hot Dog. Here's How It Made $9 Billion
# Opbouw: antwoord vooraan (lidmaatschap = het grootste deel van de winst), dan hotdog/kip, het model, de leden, waarom het werkt, risico's.
@scene open-hotdog
At Costco, a hot dog and a soda cost one dollar and fifty cents.
That was the price in 1985.
It is still the price today.
A whole rotisserie chicken costs four dollars and ninety-nine cents.
On some of these things, Costco probably makes almost nothing, or even loses money.
@scene open-winst
And yet, in its last fiscal year, Costco made a profit of about nine point two billion dollars.
Its sales were almost three hundred billion dollars.
@scene open-antwoord
So how does a store that keeps its prices this low make that much money?
The short answer is: Costco does not really make its money selling you things.
It makes its money selling you a card.
Last year, membership fees brought in about five point nine billion dollars.
That is equal to almost two thirds of the company's entire profit.
Let's see how the model works, why people keep paying, and what could go wrong.
@scene h1-sinegal
To understand Costco, you have to start with the man who built it.
Jim Sinegal co-founded Costco in 1983, with the businessman Jeffrey Brotman.
The first warehouse opened in Seattle.
Sinegal had one obsession: keeping prices low, no matter what.
@scene h1-dreiging
The hot dog became the symbol of that idea.
Years later, when the price of everything was rising, Costco's next chief executive, Craig Jelinek, went to Sinegal and asked about raising the price of the hot dog.
According to Jelinek, Sinegal answered, quote, "If you raise the price of the hot dog, I will kill you."
The price stayed at one dollar fifty.
@scene h2-model
Here is how the model works.
To shop at Costco, you need a membership.
In the United States, the basic card costs sixty-five dollars a year.
The Executive card costs one hundred thirty dollars, and gives you some money back on what you spend.
@scene h2-marge
Costco then sells products with a very small markup.
For a long time, the company had a rule: no more than about fourteen or fifteen percent above its own cost.
A normal supermarket often charges much more than that.
So on the products themselves, Costco earns very little.
It is designed that way.
@scene h2-truc
The low prices are what make the membership worth it.
And the membership fees are almost pure profit.
There are no shelves to fill for a membership card.
No trucks, no warehouses, no spoiled food.
Every new member, and every renewal, goes almost straight to the bottom line.
@scene h3-leden
And people keep paying.
At the end of its last fiscal year, Costco had eighty-four point one million paid members.
Counting family members with an extra card, there were more than one hundred fifty million cardholders.
@scene h3-trouw
The most impressive number is the renewal rate.
In the United States and Canada, ninety-two point three percent of members renewed.
Worldwide, almost ninety percent.
Very few businesses keep nine out of ten customers every single year.
@scene h3-executive
And more than half of the paid members, forty-two point three million, now have the more expensive Executive card.
@scene h3-verhoging
Because the members are so loyal, Costco can raise the fee every few years.
In September 2024, it raised the basic fee from sixty to sixty-five dollars, and the Executive fee from one hundred twenty to one hundred thirty.
Most members simply kept paying.
@scene h4-weinig
There is another trick, and you can see it in every warehouse.
Costco sells far fewer different products than a normal supermarket.
Usually only around four thousand items, compared to tens of thousands in a big grocery store.
That gives Costco enormous buying power for each product.
When it orders, it orders a lot, and suppliers give it better prices.
@scene h4-kirkland
Then there is Costco's own brand, Kirkland Signature.
From batteries to olive oil, Kirkland products are often made by well known manufacturers, and sold for less.
Kirkland is one of the biggest consumer brands in America, and it gives Costco even more control over its prices.
@scene h4-schat
Costco also plays a game with your mind.
Next to the basics, there is always a small part of the store that keeps changing.
A kayak one week, a designer handbag the next, and then it is gone.
Inside the company, this is often called the treasure hunt.
It gives you a reason to come back often, and to buy something you did not plan to buy.
@scene h4-goud
Sometimes the treasure is unusual.
In 2023, Costco started selling one ounce gold bars to its members.
They often sold out within hours.
For a while, analysts estimated that Costco was selling gold worth hundreds of millions of dollars every month.
It is a strange product for a warehouse store.
But it brings people in, and it makes headlines.
@scene h4-benzine
The same idea works at the gas station.
Many Costco warehouses have their own gas pumps, with fuel that is usually cheaper than nearby stations.
Only members can fill up there.
For many families, the money they save on gas alone pays for the membership.
And while they are there, they go inside.
@scene h4-kip
And the four dollar ninety-nine chicken?
It is placed at the back of the store, so you walk past everything else to get it.
Costco even built its own chicken plant in Nebraska, to keep that price where it is.
The chicken is not there to make money.
It is there to bring you in.
@scene h4-personeel
There is one more part of the model that surprises many people.
Costco is known for paying its workers more than most retailers.
Its founders believed that well paid workers stay longer, work better, and steal less.
Costco says that many of its employees stay for years, which saves money on hiring and training.
In a business with very small margins, that loyalty matters.
@scene h4-retour
It is the same with customers.
Costco is famous for its generous return policy.
On most products, if you are not happy, you can bring it back.
That costs money.
But it also builds trust, and trust is exactly what makes people renew the card.
@scene h5-groei
Costco keeps growing, carefully.
It opens a few dozen new warehouses each year, in America and in countries like Japan, Korea, Australia, and parts of Europe.
Every new warehouse brings in thousands of new members.
And every new member starts paying the fee.
@scene h5-gevaar
So what could go wrong?
The first risk is that growth from the last fee increase is fading.
In the last quarter, membership fee income grew about seven percent.
A year earlier, it grew about fourteen percent.
That is because the effect of the 2024 price increase has now almost fully worked through.
@scene h5-concurrent
The second risk is competition.
Walmart's Sam's Club runs the same kind of warehouse model, and online shopping keeps getting faster.
And the third risk is the price of the stock itself.
Investors already pay a lot for every dollar Costco earns, because they expect the model to keep working.
@scene h6-waarom
But so far, the model has been remarkably steady.
Costco does not need to make big profits on what it sells.
It needs you to believe that the card is worth it.
And nine out of ten members, year after year, say that it is.
None of this is financial advice.
@scene slot
The hot dog still costs one dollar fifty.
It probably always will, because it is not really for sale.
It is a promise.
And as long as people believe that promise, they will keep paying for the card.
