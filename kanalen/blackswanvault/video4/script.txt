# Titel: The Nasdaq Just Fell 10%. Here's What Investors Are Afraid Of
# 20 nov middag: antwoord vooraan (4 angsten direct na de opening, Azie/goud/VIX daarna). Oude volgorde: script-voor-volgorde.txt

@scene open-donderdag
On Thursday, November nineteenth, the biggest stock market listing in history finally happened.
Anthropic's shares closed seventeen percent above their offering price.
It was supposed to be a celebration for the whole AI industry.
Instead, the rest of the market fell apart.
@scene open-cijfers
The S&P 500 dropped two percent.
The Nasdaq lost more than two percent.
And the Dow Jones closed below fifty thousand for the first time since June.
@scene open-vraag
Since its peak in early October, the Nasdaq has now fallen more than thirteen percent.
On Wall Street, a fall of ten percent has a name.
A correction.
And a fall of twenty percent has a scarier one.
A bear market.
So what are investors suddenly so afraid of?
There is not one answer.
There are four.
@scene open-vier
Doubts about AI.
Inflation coming back.
Interest rates that may rise.
And chips going to China.
@scene h2-ai
The first fear is the biggest one.
For three years, artificial intelligence has carried the stock market.
Now investors are starting to doubt it.
@scene h2-bigtech
On Thursday alone, Amazon fell almost four percent.
Alphabet and Meta both lost more than three.
@scene h2-nvidia
No company shows this better than Nvidia.
In just six trading days, its stock fell sixteen percent.
That wiped out roughly six hundred seventy billion dollars in value.
Since its peak on October sixth, Nvidia has lost almost forty percent.
Its co-founder and chief executive, Jensen Huang, stepped down last week.
We told that story in an earlier video.
But the selling did not stop with him.
@scene h2-amd
On Thursday, it was AMD's turn.
A short seller called Hunterbrook published a report that claimed AMD technology kept reaching a blacklisted Chinese company.
A short seller is an investor who makes money when a stock falls.
So a report like this is never neutral.
The report also claimed that AMD bills more than twenty percent of its revenue to China and Hong Kong.
AMD said the claims misrepresent its business.
Its stock still fell almost eight percent.
@scene h2-groei
There is also a question about growth.
According to numbers cited by CNBC, the combined revenue growth of OpenAI and Anthropic slowed from seventy-five percent in the second quarter to forty-eight percent in the third.
At the same time, their combined losses grew to about eight point four billion dollars.
That matters, because the whole AI boom rests on one bet.
That these companies will one day earn back the enormous amounts being spent on chips and data centers.
@scene h2-coreweave
And the companies that build AI data centers with borrowed money are feeling it too.
CoreWeave, one of the biggest, fell more than six percent on Thursday.
@scene h2-fed-rapport
Even the Federal Reserve is worried.
In its latest financial stability report, eighty-seven percent of the market experts it surveyed named AI as a major risk.
In the spring, it was fifty percent.
The same report found that hedge funds are borrowing more to invest than at any time since 2013.
@scene h3-inflatie
The second fear is an old one.
Inflation is coming back.
@scene h3-cpi
In October, consumer prices in the United States rose zero point four percent in a single month.
Economists had expected zero point three.
Without food and energy, prices still rose zero point three percent.
Also more than expected.
@scene h3-ppi
Prices that companies pay rose even faster.
Wholesale prices are what businesses pay before goods reach the shelves.
When they rise, shop prices often follow.
Over the past year, wholesale prices are up six point five percent.
That is the highest since November 2022.
For goods alone, the yearly increase was more than ten percent.
@scene h3-wereld
And it is not just America.
In Britain, record diesel prices pushed inflation to three point six percent, its highest in a year.
The Bank of England expects inflation in Britain to reach about five percent by the middle of next year.
@scene h4-rente
That leads to the third fear.
Interest rates may go up, not down.
@scene h4-musalem
On November seventeenth, Alberto Musalem, the president of the St. Louis Federal Reserve, said the economy will likely require several interest rate increases before the spring of 2027.
He said up to four may be needed.
Right now, the Fed's key rate is between three point seven five and four percent.
In October, it kept it unchanged.
@scene h4-markt
Traders now fully expect the Fed to raise rates by a quarter point at its meeting on December eighth and ninth.
In Britain, the Bank of England already did it.
On November fifth, it raised its rate to four percent.
Its first increase since August 2023.
Eight of its nine members voted for the hike.
The ninth wanted an even bigger one.
@scene h4-waarom
Why does this hurt tech stocks so much?
Many tech companies are valued on the profits they hope to make years from now.
When interest rates rise, money in the future is worth less today.
And safe savings start to pay more.
So investors become less willing to pay a high price for a promise.
That is also why the Nasdaq, full of tech companies, usually falls harder than the Dow.
@scene h5-chips
The fourth fear comes from Washington.
It is about chips going to China.
@scene h5-documenten
Documents from the House Foreign Affairs Committee showed that the Commerce Department approved sales of more than eight hundred seventy-nine thousand AI chips to Chinese companies.
@scene h5-bessent
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent blamed the Commerce Department, and said there is "a lot of leakage in the system."
He said other departments had wanted to put a company called Megaspeed on a trade blacklist for months.
@scene h5-bonta
And this week, California's attorney general Rob Bonta sent Nvidia a subpoena.
He wants to know whether its chips reached China in violation of export rules, and whether the company misled investors.
He said the conduct of Nvidia and its former CEO "raises bright red flags."
@scene h1-week
Together, these four fears hit the market hard.
To see how fast this happened, look at the past week.
For the S&P 500, Thursday was the sixth losing day in a row.
In just four trading days, the Dow fell more than six percent.
The S&P 500 and the Nasdaq each lost about five.
@scene h1-azie
The next morning, the selling moved to Asia.
Japan's Nikkei fell five percent.
South Korea's Kospi fell even more.
Samsung dropped seven percent.
@scene h1-chips
Chip companies were hit the hardest.
Tokyo Electron fell seven percent.
Lasertec fell more than nine.
And in Taiwan, TSMC, the company that makes most of the world's advanced chips, lost almost five percent.
@scene h1-softbank
And SoftBank, the Japanese group that has invested more than sixty-four billion dollars in OpenAI, crashed fourteen and a half percent.
Its worst day since August 2024.
@scene h1-nergens
Usually, when stocks fall, investors run to safety.
This time, there was almost nowhere to run.
Gold has fallen more than eleven percent in a month.
Bitcoin has lost about a quarter of its value in the same time.
One reason for gold is simple.
Gold pays no interest.
So when interest rates are expected to rise, holding it becomes less attractive.
@scene h1-vix
Wall Street's fear gauge, the VIX, tells the same story.
It measures how much traders expect the S&P 500 to swing over the next thirty days.
A month ago, it stood around eighteen.
Now it is above twenty-eight, its highest level in months.
@scene h6-geschiedenis
So is this the start of something worse?
Nobody knows.
But history can tell us something.
@scene h6-tellen
We looked at every closing price of the Nasdaq since 1971.
Before this one, it had thirty-one corrections of ten percent or more.
Thirteen of them turned into a fall of twenty percent or more.
The rest recovered before that.
@scene h6-voorbeelden
Some recoveries were fast.
This spring, the Nasdaq fell thirteen percent, and was back at a record by the middle of April.
In early 2020, when Covid hit, it fell thirty percent, and still recovered within a few months.
In August 2024, it fell thirteen percent, and was back at a record by the end of October.
Others took much longer.
In 2022, it fell thirty-six percent, and needed more than two years to recover.
@scene h7-volgende
So what should you watch now?
First, the Fed's meeting on December eighth and ninth.
Second, the next inflation numbers.
Third, the holiday season, starting with Black Friday on November twenty-seventh.
Shoppers will show whether higher prices are starting to hurt.
And fourth, the hearings on AI chips in the Senate next month.
