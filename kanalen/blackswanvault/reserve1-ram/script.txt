# BlackswanVault reserve 1: waarom RAM zo duur is. CONCEPT KLAAR 21 nov 2026 — nog nalezen: winkelprijs $450 vs ~$100 (h0) en "almost thirty years" Crucial (h5). Eén zin per regel. Getallen voor de stem uitgeschreven.
# Titel (voorstel): Why RAM Got So Expensive. AI Is Eating Your Memory
# Opbouw: antwoord vooraan (AI-datacenters kopen het geheugen op), dan prijzen, waarom, wie verdient, wie betaalt, wanneer het stopt.
@scene open-prijs
If you tried to build a computer this year, you probably noticed something strange.
The memory, the RAM, suddenly cost more than almost anything else in the box.
A thirty-two gigabyte kit of fast memory now costs around four hundred fifty dollars.
Not long ago, the same kit cost about a hundred.
@scene open-antwoord
So what happened?
The short answer is artificial intelligence.
The companies that make memory chips are selling as much as they can to AI data centers.
And there is simply not enough left for the rest of us.
@scene open-geld
In the third quarter of this year, the whole memory industry brought in about two hundred billion dollars.
That is three hundred sixty-nine percent more than a year earlier.
But the number of chips they shipped grew by only about four percent.
Almost all of that extra money came from one thing: higher prices.
Let's see how we got here, who is making money, and when it might end.
@scene h1-wat
First, what is RAM?
It is your computer's short-term memory.
Every app you open, every browser tab, every game, lives in RAM while you use it.
Your phone has it, your laptop has it, your car has it.
And so does every AI server in the world.
@scene h1-drie
Almost all of the world's memory chips come from just three companies.
Samsung and SK Hynix in South Korea, and Micron in the United States.
When those three change their plans, the whole world feels it.
@scene h2-hbm
And this year, they changed their plans.
AI chips, like the ones Nvidia sells, need a special kind of memory.
It is called high bandwidth memory, or H B M.
It is stacked in layers, right next to the AI chip, so data can move incredibly fast.
@scene h2-ruil
H B M is much harder to make than normal memory.
It uses far more factory space for every gigabyte.
And it is far more profitable.
So the big three moved more and more of their factories to AI memory, and to memory for servers.
According to the research firm I D C, that came at the expense of the chips that go into laptops and phones.
@scene h3-prijzen
Then the prices exploded.
In the first quarter of this year, contract prices for memory almost doubled in just three months.
The research firm TrendForce called it a record for a single quarter.
And they kept climbing.
Up to twenty-three percent more in the third quarter.
And TrendForce expects up to eighteen percent more in the last quarter of the year.
@scene h3-winkel
In stores, you can see it on the price tags.
Storage is going up too.
According to Tom's Hardware, a two terabyte drive that cost eighty dollars this summer was back at one hundred thirty dollars in November.
@scene h4-winst
So who is making money?
The memory makers, more than ever before.
@scene h4-hynix
On October twenty-eighth, SK Hynix reported its results for the third quarter.
Revenue passed one hundred trillion won for the first time, about seventy billion dollars.
Its operating profit was eighty-three trillion won.
That is a profit margin of about eighty percent.
For a company that makes physical chips in giant factories, that is almost unheard of.
Compared to a year earlier, its profit was more than seven times higher.
@scene h4-geboekt
And SK Hynix said something even more striking.
Its memory for AI is already booked by customers all the way through 2029.
@scene h4-samsung
One day later, Samsung reported a record quarter for its chip business too.
For years, memory was a business of boom and bust.
Right now, it is all boom.
@scene h5-micron
For ordinary buyers, the clearest signal came from Micron.
In December last year, Micron announced it would stop selling memory and drives to consumers under its brand Crucial.
Crucial had been one of the best known names for upgrading your own computer for almost thirty years.
Micron said demand from AI data centers was simply too strong.
It moved the factory output to memory for AI and for servers.
@scene h5-pc
Computer makers are feeling it as well.
According to TrendForce, some are putting smaller drives into their normal laptops, to keep prices down.
So you might pay the same price as last year, and get less.
@scene h6-altman
And then there is Sam Altman, the head of OpenAI.
At the end of October, on a podcast, he talked about the memory sitting inside our own computers.
He said that most of it sits unused, and he called that, quote, "quite terrible" for a world that is short on memory.
His point was that a data center can use the same chips much more efficiently.
@scene h6-reactie
Many people did not like hearing that from the head of one of the biggest buyers of memory in the world.
A few weeks later, on November sixteenth, he said, quote, "I want people to have a lot of RAM."
But he kept his main argument, that AI in the cloud uses memory better.
@scene h7-wanneer
So when will it end?
Nobody knows for sure.
The good news is that the price increases are getting smaller.
From almost double in one quarter at the start of the year, to up to eighteen percent now.
@scene h7-waarom-niet
The bad news is that new memory factories take years to build.
And as long as AI companies keep spending hundreds of billions of dollars on data centers, they will keep buying first.
Some industry leaders expect prices to fall sometime in 2027.
Others think the shortage could last much longer.
@scene h7-tips
So what can you do?
If your computer still works fine, this may not be the year to upgrade.
If you do need a new one, look carefully at how much memory and storage it really has.
And watch for sales, because some memory kits have already dropped from their highest prices.
None of this is financial advice.
@scene slot
For decades, memory was one of the cheapest parts of any computer.
AI changed that in less than a year.
The chips in your laptop and the chips in a giant AI data center now come from the same few factories.
And right now, the data centers are paying more.
