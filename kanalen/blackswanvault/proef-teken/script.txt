On Friday, SpaceX stock closed at one hundred twenty-seven dollars and seventy-nine cents.
That is below the one hundred thirty-five dollars it sold for at its IPO in June.
So what is going on with the most hyped stock of the year?
And why could one date in December make it even worse?
@scene b
SpaceX went public on June twelfth.
According to CNBC, it raised a record eighty-six billion dollars.
On the first day, the stock closed at almost one hundred sixty-one dollars.
Four days later, it closed at more than two hundred eleven dollars.
That was the top.
In August, it fell to about one hundred eight dollars.
It came back, but now it is about forty percent below its peak.
@scene c
To understand why, you need to know where SpaceX really makes its money.
In twenty twenty-five, Starlink, the satellite internet business, brought in eleven point four billion dollars.
And it made a profit of four point four billion.
The rocket business brought in four point one billion, but it lost money.
And the AI business brought in three point two billion dollars, and lost about the same amount.
@scene d
And that AI business is where the worry starts.
According to Bloomberg, SpaceX put together a package of about forty billion dollars in bonds and bank loans.
The money is for leasing Nvidia chips for five years.
The Financial Times reported that lenders were nervous Musk might send those chips into space.
If something goes wrong, they would not be able to get them back.
@scene e
Now, the date.
Before a company goes public, early investors and employees already own shares.
But for about six months after the IPO, they are not allowed to sell them.
That waiting period is called the lock-up.
@scene f
For SpaceX, The Motley Fool expects that lock-up to end on December eighth.
On that day, shares that were frozen can suddenly be sold.
If a lot of people sell at the same time, the price can drop even further.
Musk and other insiders have to wait longer, until June twenty twenty-seven.
@scene g
But nobody knows if that selling will happen.
Some early investors may believe in the AI story and simply keep their shares.
That is why December eighth is the date to watch.
