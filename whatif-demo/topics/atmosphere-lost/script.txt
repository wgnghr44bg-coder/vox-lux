Imagine you are standing at a lookout above a mountain lake, on a clear and quiet morning. [pause]
Above you lies a thin blanket of air. [pause]
It presses on every square metre of you with the weight of ten tonnes, and you never feel it. [long pause]
Now imagine that Earth loses its atmosphere. Not forever. For one minute. [long pause]
Second one. The pressure starts to fall. [pause]
The blue sky fades to black, and the stars come out in the middle of the day. [long pause]
Then everything goes silent. Sound needs air to travel. [pause]
The siren down the road is still flashing. But you hear nothing at all. [long pause]
Second ten. The air in your lungs rushes out on its own. [pause]
You would have only seconds before you passed out. [long pause]
But the strangest part is still to come. [long pause]
Second thirty. The pressure drops below ten hectopascals. [pause]
At that point, even ice cold water boils. [pause]
The whole lake bubbles, and it loses heat so fast that its surface, and the dew on every tree, freezes over. [long pause] [long pause] [long pause] [long pause] [long pause]
Second sixty. The air comes back. [pause]
The sky turns blue again, and the ice slowly melts away. [long pause]
One minute without air. Do you think you would make it?
