The Messinian salinity crisis dried up the Mediterranean Sea in a cycle of partial or nearly complete desiccation from 5.96 to 5.33 million years ago, ending with the Zanclean flood when the Atlantic reclaimed the basin | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messinian_salinity_crisis
After the precursor of the Strait of Gibraltar closed about 5.96 million years ago, the dry climate of the region dried the Mediterranean basin out nearly completely within a thousand years | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messinian_salinity_crisis
The dried basin was left 3 to 5 km deep below normal sea level, so rivers carved huge canyon systems into the empty sea floor, some similar in scale to the Grand Canyon | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messinian_salinity_crisis
The Nile cut its bed down to about 2,500 metres below sea level just north of Cairo, and the Rhone also cut a deep canyon during the crisis | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messinian_salinity_crisis
About 5.33 million years ago the barrier at the Strait of Gibraltar broke one last time and the Atlantic rapidly refilled the Mediterranean in the Zanclean flood | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messinian_salinity_crisis
If the Strait of Gibraltar closes again, which is likely in the near future in geological time, the Mediterranean would mostly evaporate in about a thousand years | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messinian_salinity_crisis
Over 5 million years ago the Mediterranean dried up into a salt flat; the Strait of Gibraltar closed about 6 million years ago, evaporation lowered the sea level, and the Atlantic refilled the basin in a cataclysmic flood | https://news.cnrs.fr/articles/when-the-mediterranean-was-empty
