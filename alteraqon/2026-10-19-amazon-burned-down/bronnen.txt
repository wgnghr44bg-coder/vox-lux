The "lungs of the Earth" claim that the Amazon makes 20 percent of the world's oxygen is false: scientists estimate the Amazon's share at 6 to 9 percent, and the forest consumes nearly all of that oxygen itself, so its net contribution to the world's oxygen is effectively zero | https://www.factcheck.org/2019/09/amazon-doesnt-produce-20-of-earths-oxygen/
About half of Earth's oxygen production comes from the ocean, mostly from photosynthesising plankton, which produce more oxygen than all tropical rainforests on land combined | https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/ocean-oxygen.html
Roughly the same amount of the oxygen produced in the ocean is consumed again by marine life, so the ocean is not a net supplier of breathable air either | https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/ocean-oxygen.html
On a typical day the trees of the Amazon release about 20 billion tons of moisture into the atmosphere, and that moisture is recycled from sky to land five to six times | https://amazonaid.org/resources/about-the-amazon/the-hydrological-cycle/
Brazilian climate scientist Antonio Nobre (INPE): within the Amazon "20 billion tons of water evaporate per day" - more water than the Amazon River discharges into the Atlantic in a day (the so-called flying rivers) | https://blog.pachamama.org/flying-rivers-of-the-amazon-rainforest-a-critical-rain-generator-for-the-planet
Scientists warn the Amazon could pass a tipping point where the water-recycling pump fails and the forest collapses into savanna, which is why the loss of the water cycle - not oxygen - is the real danger | https://www.factcheck.org/2019/09/amazon-doesnt-produce-20-of-earths-oxygen/
Brazil's space agency INPE counted 74,155 active fires in Brazil between 1 January and 20 August 2019, an 84 percent rise on the same period in 2018, and NASA confirmed 2019 was the worst year for wildfires in the region since 2010 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Amazon_rainforest_wildfires
On 19 August 2019 the sky over Sao Paulo suddenly turned black when a "smoke corridor" carried smoke from the forest fires thousands of miles from the Amazon region, almost 2,800 kilometres away | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Amazon_rainforest_wildfires
In 2019 more than 906 thousand hectares of forest in the Amazon biome were lost to fires, and INPE reported over 80,000 fires across Brazil by 29 August 2019, a 77 percent increase on the same period the year before | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Amazon_rainforest_wildfires
INPE satellite data showed an 84 percent increase in fires in Brazil in 2019 compared with 2018, with more than 74,000 fires detected between January and August, the highest number since 2010 | https://www.bbc.com/news/world-latin-america-49415973
