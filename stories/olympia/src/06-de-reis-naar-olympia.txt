Early in the morning, a great procession leaves Elis. At the front walk the judges, in their purple robes. Behind them come the athletes, and their trainers, fathers and brothers. Then come the horses and chariots, for the horse races. And then a long line of people on foot, on donkeys and in carts. It is a holy procession, as well as a journey.

--

The road to Olympia runs south along the coast, for about fifty kilometres. It takes two days. The summer sun is hot, and the road is dusty. On one side, there is the sparkling sea. On the other side, there are olive groves, vineyards and fields of golden wheat. Cicadas sing loudly in the trees. Lykos walks beside his father and Dion, carrying his bundle on his back.

On the way, the procession stops at a sacred spring. There, the judges make a sacrifice of a pig, and the athletes wash themselves in the clear water. It is a way of making themselves pure before they enter the holy place of Zeus. Lykos splashes the cold water over his face and arms. It feels wonderful after the long, hot walk.

{img:sacred-spring-athletes-washing-cool-water-plane-trees-shade}
The road is crowded. Thousands of people are travelling to Olympia. There are rich men in fine chariots, with servants walking beside them. There are farmers on donkeys, and poor people on foot, carrying food in baskets. There are merchants with carts full of goods to sell. There are families, and groups of friends, singing as they walk.

There are also official delegations, sent by the cities. Each important city sends a group of its leading citizens, to make sacrifices to Zeus and to show the wealth of their city. Some of them have beautiful horses, and golden cups, and animals for the sacrifice. They want everyone to see how rich and powerful their city is.

--

{img:crowds-travelling-road-donkeys-carts-families-olive-groves-dust}
People come from much further away too. Ships from Italy and Sicily have landed at the nearby coast, full of visitors. Some have travelled for weeks across the sea. They have come from Marseille in France, from Cyrene in Africa, and from the Greek cities on the shores of the Black Sea. They speak Greek with many different accents. Lykos can hardly understand some of them.

Thanks to the sacred truce, the roads are safe. Even people whose cities are enemies walk side by side on the road to Olympia. A man from Athens shares his water with a man from Corinth. A Spartan helps a family from Thebes to fix the wheel of their cart. For these few weeks, everyone is simply a Greek, travelling to the festival of Zeus.

{img:first-view-olympia-valley-temples-roofs-glinting-sun-river-pine-hill}
On the second day, in the afternoon, the procession comes over a low hill. And there, below them, is the valley of Olympia. Lykos stops and stares. He has heard about it all his life, but he never imagined it would look like this. The river Alpheios shining in the sun. The pine-covered Hill of Kronos. And at its foot, the sanctuary, with its temples, statues and altars, gleaming white and gold among the green trees.

--

All around the sanctuary, the valley is already filling up with people. There are tents and huts everywhere, and the smoke of a thousand cooking fires. The sound of the crowd rises up from the valley, like the sound of the sea. Old Philon puts his hand on Lykos's shoulder. There it is, he says quietly. Olympia. And the old man's eyes are wet with tears.

That evening, they find a place for their little tent near the river, among the other people from Arcadia. Some of them are from villages close to their own. They greet each other like old friends, and share their bread and olives. An old woman gives Lykos a handful of dried figs, and tells him to eat them on the morning of the race. They bring luck, she says. Lykos thanks her, and puts them carefully in his bundle.
