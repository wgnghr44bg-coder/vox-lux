On the first morning of the Games, all the athletes gather in a building near the sanctuary, called the council house. Inside, there is a statue of Zeus with a thunderbolt in each hand. He is called Zeus of Oaths. Of all the statues of Zeus at Olympia, he is the one most likely to frighten anyone who might be tempted to cheat.

--

In front of the statue, a boar is sacrificed. Then the athletes swear an oath, one after another. So do their fathers and brothers and trainers. They swear that they have trained for ten months, as the rules demand. They swear that they will not cheat in any way. They swear that they will compete fairly, and accept the decisions of the judges. Lykos's voice shakes a little when it is his turn. But he says the words clearly.

The judges also swear an oath. They promise to judge fairly, and not to accept any bribes. And they promise to keep secret what they know about the athletes, such as their true ages. Then the oath is finished. The Games have officially begun.

{img:herald-contest-stadium-olympia-man-calling-loudly-crowd-grassy-bank}
On the first day, there are also two special contests. They are not for athletes, but for heralds and trumpeters. The heralds compete to see who has the loudest and clearest voice. The trumpeters compete to see who can blow the trumpet best. The winners will have important jobs during the Games. The winning herald will announce the names of the athletes and the winners. The winning trumpeter will give the signal for the start of the races.

The crowd enjoys these contests very much. One after another, the heralds stand up on a little platform and shout out a few lines. Some of them have voices like thunder. Some of them squeak a little at the end, and the crowd laughs kindly. The winner is a big man from Megara, whose voice can be heard at the other end of the stadium. He bows, and everyone applauds.

--

{img:people-praying-altars-olympia-offerings-flowers-honey-cakes-smoke}
For the rest of the first day, people visit the sanctuary, pray and make offerings. The athletes go to the altars of the gods and heroes, to ask for help. Some of them sacrifice animals. Others offer cakes, wine or honey. Some ask the priests to look into the future for them. Old Philon takes Lykos to a little altar of Hermes, the god of speed, and they offer a small honey cake together.

There are many priests and helpers in the sanctuary. They look after the altars and temples, and help people with their sacrifices. There are even special priests, whose families have been seers at Olympia for many generations. They read signs in the sacrifices, to tell what the gods want. Athletes and generals from all over Greece come to ask their advice.

{img:sunset-hill-of-kronos-pine-trees-golden-light-sanctuary-quiet}
In the late afternoon, Lykos goes back to the tent to rest. Tomorrow is the day of the boys' contests. Philon tells him to eat a good supper and go to sleep early. He eats bread and cheese and a little grilled meat. Then he lies down in the tent and closes his eyes.

--

His father sits outside the tent for a long time, looking at the evening sky. He is a simple shepherd. He never imagined that his son would one day run at Olympia. He thinks about his wife and his daughter, at home in the mountains. And he thinks that, whatever happens tomorrow, he is the proudest father in all of Greece.
