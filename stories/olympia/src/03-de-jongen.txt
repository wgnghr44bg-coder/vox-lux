High in the mountains of Arcadia, in the middle of the Peloponnese, there is a small village of stone houses. It lies on a green slope, with oak trees and chestnut trees all around. Below the village, a stream runs down through the valley. Above it, the bare mountains rise up into the sky. Sheep and goats graze on the hillsides. And the bells around their necks make a soft clinking sound, all day long.

--

This is where Lykos lives. He is sixteen years old. His father is a shepherd, and so was his grandfather. Their house is small and simple, built of rough stone, with a roof of clay tiles. There is one main room, with a fire in the middle, and a little courtyard where the chickens scratch in the dust. Lykos lives there with his father, his mother, his grandmother and his little sister, Theano.

Every morning, before the sun rises, Lykos takes the goats up into the mountains. He follows them over the rocks and through the bushes, all day long. When a goat wanders off, he runs after it. When a kid is in danger, he runs to help. He has been running in these mountains since he was a little boy. His legs are thin but very strong. And he is the fastest runner in the whole valley.

{img:boy-running-mountain-path-goats-scattering-wind-ancient-greece-sunlight}
The other boys of the village race each other on feast days, on a flat field by the stream. Lykos always wins. He wins so easily that the older men begin to talk about him. At the spring festival, he even beats the grown men. And one old man, called Philon, comes to talk to his father.

Philon was once a runner himself. Many years ago, as a young man, he ran at Olympia. He did not win. But he has never forgotten it. Now he is old and grey, and he walks with a stick. He sits by the fire in the house of Lykos, and he says that the boy should go to Olympia. To run in the race for boys. He says that he has never seen a boy run like that.

--

{img:old-trainer-and-father-talking-by-hearth-fire-stone-house-evening}
At first, the father of Lykos says no. Olympia is far away. It costs money to travel there, and to stay for many weeks. And who will look after the goats? But Philon talks to him for a long time. He says that he will train the boy himself, for nothing. He says that if Lykos wins, the whole village will be proud. And he says that Zeus himself has given the boy those fast legs, so it would be wrong not to use them.

In the end, the father agrees. Lykos can hardly believe it. That night, he lies awake on his straw mattress, looking at the dark beams of the roof. He thinks about Olympia. He has heard so many stories about it. The great temple. The crowds. The famous runners. And the olive wreath, the prize that every athlete dreams of.

{img:old-man-coaching-boy-runner-dusty-field-dawn-stick-pointing}
So the training begins. Every morning, before he takes the goats out, Lykos goes down to the flat field by the stream. Philon is already waiting for him, leaning on his stick. He makes Lykos run short races, again and again, as fast as he can. He makes him run up the steep hillside, until his legs burn. He teaches him how to start quickly, and how to lean forward at the end.

Philon also tells him what to eat. Lots of bread and porridge, cheese and figs, and a little meat when there is some. Not too much wine. And plenty of sleep. He teaches Lykos to rub his body with olive oil before he runs, to keep his muscles supple. And he tells him stories, in the evenings, about the famous runners of the past.

--

Little Theano is eleven years old. She often comes down to the field to watch her brother train. Sometimes she runs beside him, laughing, her hair flying. She is fast too, almost as fast as Lykos. But she knows that girls cannot run in the Olympic Games. Philon tells her that there is another festival at Olympia, for girls, in honour of the goddess Hera. Theano's eyes go wide. From that day on, she trains too.

The training lasts for many months, through the autumn, the winter and the spring. The days get shorter and then longer again. Snow falls on the mountains, and melts. The almond trees blossom. And slowly, Lykos becomes even faster. He is ready. Now he only has to wait for the heralds to come.
