In the middle of the sanctuary stands the greatest building of all. The Temple of Zeus. It is enormous, much bigger than any building Lykos has ever seen. It stands on a high platform of stone, with steps all around. Thirty-four huge columns hold up the roof. Each column is more than ten metres tall, and so thick that three men holding hands could hardly reach around it.

--

The temple is made of local stone, covered with a smooth layer of white plaster, so that it looks like marble. The roof is made of real marble tiles. At the corners of the roof, there are golden statues of the goddess Victory, and golden bowls, shining in the sun. At the front and back of the temple, in the triangle under the roof, there are great carved scenes. One shows the chariot race of Pelops. The other shows a battle between people and wild centaurs, half-man and half-horse.

All along the top of the walls, there are twelve carved panels. They show the twelve great labours of Heracles. Heracles fighting the lion. Heracles cleaning the stables. Heracles holding up the sky. The carvings are painted in bright colours, red, blue and gold. Lykos walks slowly around the temple, looking up at the pictures, one after another, like the pages of a storybook.

{img:temple-zeus-carved-metope-heracles-labours-painted-colours-close}
Inside the temple, it is dark and cool. As Lykos's eyes get used to the dim light, he sees it. At the far end of the hall, there is a giant statue of Zeus, sitting on a throne. It is so big that his head almost touches the roof. People say that if Zeus stood up, he would break through the ceiling.

The statue is made by the most famous sculptor in Greece, a man from Athens called Phidias. He has only just finished it. The body of Zeus is made of ivory, pale and smooth like skin. His robe is made of gold, decorated with little flowers and animals. In his right hand, he holds a small statue of Victory. In his left hand, he holds a tall staff with an eagle on top. On his head, there is a wreath of golden olive leaves.

--

{img:great-statue-zeus-gold-ivory-throne-dim-temple-light-awe-small-people}
The throne of Zeus is made of gold, ivory, ebony and precious stones. It is decorated with carvings of gods, heroes and animals. In front of the statue, on the floor, there is a shallow pool of olive oil. The oil keeps the ivory from cracking in the dry air. And it also reflects the light from the doorway, so that the golden statue seems to glow in the darkness.

Lykos stands very still and looks up at the face of Zeus. It is calm and kind, and very wise. Not angry, not frightening. Just quietly powerful, like a good king or a loving father. Around him, other visitors are standing in silence too. Some of them are praying. Some of them have tears in their eyes. Nobody speaks.

{img:pilgrims-standing-silent-before-zeus-statue-oil-pool-reflection-glow}
This statue will become famous all over the ancient world. It will be called one of the Seven Wonders of the World. People will travel for months to see it. A Greek writer will say that a man who has seen it can never be completely unhappy again. And it will stand in this temple for more than eight hundred years.

--

Lykos takes out the little clay figure of Zeus his grandmother gave him. It is rough and simple, made by a village potter. He holds it in his hand and looks from the little Zeus to the great Zeus. Then he says a quiet prayer. He does not pray to win. Philon told him not to do that. He prays to run well, and to make his family proud, and to come home safe.

When he comes out of the temple, the bright sunlight dazzles him. He blinks. The sanctuary is busy and noisy again, full of people and voices. But inside him, there is a feeling of calm, which stays with him for the rest of the day.
