So we leave Olympia tonight, in the time of the Games, as the evening falls. The sun is going down behind the hills in the west. Its last golden light is shining on the roof of the Temple of Zeus, and on the old columns of the Temple of Hera. The shadows of the plane trees are growing long across the sanctuary. And the valley is quiet now.

--

The crowds have gone home. The tents are gone. The merchants and the jugglers and the poets have all left. Only the priests remain, walking slowly between the altars, lighting the evening lamps. In the prytaneion, the sacred fire is still burning, as it always does. A small flame in the dusk, that is never allowed to go out.

{img:empty-stadium-olympia-dusk-grassy-banks-starting-line-first-stars}
The stadium lies empty in the twilight. The grassy banks are quiet. The long track is smooth and pale. At each end, the stone starting lines are waiting, with their little grooves for the toes of the runners. In four years, the crowds will come back, and the runners will stand here again. But for now, there is only the sound of the crickets, and the wind in the pine trees on the Hill of Kronos.

--

The river Alpheios flows slowly through the valley, wide and shallow and silver in the evening light. It has flowed here for thousands of years. It flowed past Koroibos the cook, and past Lykos the shepherd boy, and past all the runners and wrestlers and chariot drivers who ever came to this valley. And it flows on, quietly, toward the sea.

{img:mountain-village-arcadia-night-lamp-glow-window-olive-wreath-wall}
Far away, high in the mountains of Arcadia, the stars are coming out over a small stone village. In one of the houses, a fire is burning. An olive wreath hangs on the wall above it, slowly drying. A boy is asleep on his straw mattress, tired from a long day with the goats. And beside him, his little sister is dreaming of running, with her hair flying behind her, in the stadium of Olympia.

--

The goats are asleep in their pen. Their bells are quiet. The stream murmurs in the valley below. And over the dark mountains, the moon is rising, a little smaller than it was on the night of the great feast. It shines on the mountains of Arcadia, and on the valley of Olympia, and on the whole sleeping land of Greece.
