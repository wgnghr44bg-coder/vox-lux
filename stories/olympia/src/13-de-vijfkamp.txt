In the afternoon of the same day, the crowds go back to the stadium, for the pentathlon. Penta means five in Greek. So the pentathlon is a contest of five events. Running, jumping, throwing the discus, throwing the javelin and wrestling. The athletes who take part must be good at everything. They are not the fastest runners or the strongest wrestlers. But they are the best all-round athletes.

--

The Greeks loved the pentathlon athletes. They said their bodies were the most beautiful of all, because they were strong and fast and graceful at the same time. Sculptors liked to use them as models for their statues. One famous statue, called the Discus Thrower, shows a pentathlon athlete at the moment just before he throws.

The first event is the discus. The discus is a flat, round plate of bronze or stone. It is quite heavy. The athlete stands at a line, holding the discus in one hand. He swings it back, twists his whole body, and then spins around and lets it fly. The discus sails through the air and lands with a thud in the dust. A judge puts a little peg in the ground where it landed. Each athlete throws five times, and his best throw counts.

{img:discus-thrower-athlete-twisting-body-bronze-disc-sunlight-stadium}
The second event is the long jump. But it is different from the long jump today. The athletes hold a weight in each hand. The weights are made of stone or metal, shaped to fit the hand. When the athlete jumps, he swings the weights forward, to carry him further. Just before he lands, he swings them back again. Some people think the athletes made several small jumps in a row. Nobody is quite sure today.

To help the athletes jump in rhythm, a man plays the flute while they jump. It must have looked rather lovely. The soft music of the flute, the athlete running and swinging his weights, and then rising up into the air like a bird. When he lands, he must leave clear footprints in the soft earth. If he falls over, the jump does not count.

--

{img:long-jumper-holding-stone-weights-mid-air-flute-player-beside}
The third event is the javelin. The javelin is a long, light spear, made of wood. Around the middle of it, there is a little loop of leather. The athlete puts two fingers in the loop. When he throws, the loop makes the javelin spin through the air. So it flies further and straighter. The athletes throw at a target, or for distance, and the crowd cheers each good throw.

The fourth event is a running race, the length of the stadium. And the fifth and last event is wrestling. But often, the wrestling is not needed. Because if one athlete has already won three of the first four events, he is the winner of the whole pentathlon. Then the contest is over.

On this afternoon, the pentathlon is won by a young man from the island of Aegina. He is the best at the discus and the javelin, and he is very fast. He wins three events, so he does not have to wrestle. The crowd cheers, and the herald calls out his name. His family and friends rush onto the track, throwing ribbons and leaves over him.

--

Lykos watches the pentathlon with great interest. He has never seen a discus or a javelin before. In the mountains, there were only stones, which the boys threw at targets. He decides that, when he gets home, he will try it. He will find a flat stone for a discus, and a straight branch for a javelin. And he will teach Theano too.

{img:boy-watching-from-grassy-bank-chin-on-hands-dreaming-afternoon}
By now, the news of his victory has spread through the camp. People recognise him as he walks by. They point at him and smile. Look, there is the shepherd boy who won the boys' race. A man from his own region, Arcadia, comes to shake his hand. He says that all of Arcadia is proud of him. Lykos blushes and does not know what to say. He is still just a shepherd's son from a small village in the mountains. But now, he is also something more.
