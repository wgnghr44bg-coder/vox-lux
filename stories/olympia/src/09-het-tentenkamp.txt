Outside the walls of the sanctuary, the whole valley has become a huge camp. Tens of thousands of people have come to the Games. And there are almost no buildings for them to stay in. So they sleep in tents, in huts made of branches, or simply under the open sky, wrapped in their cloaks.

--

The rich visitors have big, colourful tents, with carpets and cushions inside. The official delegations from the cities have the best places, close to the sanctuary. Some of them bring their own cooks and servants, and give big dinners every night. The poorer visitors sleep wherever they can find a space. Along the river, under the trees, on the hillsides. Lykos, his father and Philon have a small tent of goatskin, near the river, among the people from Arcadia.

The camp is crowded and noisy. And in the middle of the summer, it is very hot. The sun beats down all day long. There is not enough shade, and not enough water. There are flies everywhere. People queue at the springs and wells with their jars. Some people even complain about it. One Greek writer later joked that a master once threatened his lazy slave, saying he would take him to Olympia as a punishment.

{img:market-stalls-olympia-camp-merchants-selling-food-wine-souvenirs}
But most people do not mind the heat and the crowds. They are here for the Games, and for the festival. And the camp is full of life. Everywhere, there are merchants selling things. Bread, cheese, olives, figs, grilled meat and wine. Little clay figures of Zeus and of athletes, as souvenirs. Pots and cups, jewellery, sandals and hats against the sun. The merchants shout out their prices, and people bargain with them.

There are also entertainers. Jugglers throw balls and knives into the air. Acrobats do somersaults. Musicians play the flute and the lyre. Fortune tellers sit on little stools, offering to tell people's futures. And there are men who sell fresh water, carried from the river in big jars on their backs. On hot afternoons, they sell a great deal of it.

--

{img:poet-reciting-crowd-listening-shade-tree-olympia-scroll}
In the shade of the trees, there are speakers and poets. Olympia is the best place in the world to become famous, because people from every Greek city are here. So writers and thinkers come to read their works aloud to the crowds. Painters show their pictures. Teachers give speeches about wisdom and politics. An old story says that the great historian Herodotus once read his history book aloud here, to a big crowd, and they loved it so much that his fame spread all over Greece.

Lykos walks through the camp with Dion, his friend from Sicily. They look at everything, with wide eyes. Dion has a little money, and buys them each a honey cake. They sit on the riverbank and eat them, with their feet in the cool water. They talk about their homes, and about the race, and about what they will do afterwards.

{img:two-boys-sitting-riverbank-feet-in-water-eating-honey-cakes-evening}
In the evening, the camp is lit up by thousands of little fires. The smell of cooking fills the air. People sit together in groups, eating and talking and singing. From the tents of the rich, there comes the sound of music and laughter. Someone is playing a sad, sweet melody on a flute, somewhere near the river. And above the valley, the moon is rising. It is almost full.

--

Lykos lies in the little goatskin tent, between his father and Philon. He is tired, but he cannot sleep. Outside, the camp is slowly getting quieter. The fires burn down. The voices fade. A dog barks far away. An owl calls from the Hill of Kronos. Lykos thinks about the race, in two days' time. His heart beats a little faster. Then he hears his father breathing slowly beside him, and old Philon snoring softly. And at last, he too falls asleep.
