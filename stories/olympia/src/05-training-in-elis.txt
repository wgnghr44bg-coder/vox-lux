After four days of walking, Lykos, his father and old Philon arrive in Elis. It is a busy town, on a wide plain near the sea, about fifty kilometres north of Olympia. In this month before the Games, Elis is full of athletes. They have come from every part of the Greek world. Lykos has never seen so many strong young men in one place.

--

All the athletes must stay in Elis for a whole month, and train together. This is one of the strict rules of the Games. During this month, they are watched by the judges. The judges are ten men from Elis, chosen for the job. They are called the Hellanodikai, which means the judges of the Greeks. They wear purple robes, and they carry long sticks. Everyone respects them, even the most famous athletes.

The judges watch every athlete carefully. They want to see that each one is good enough to compete at Olympia. They also check that the boys really are boys, and not young men pretending to be younger. They look at their size, their faces and their voices. Some boys who look too old are sent to compete with the men instead. Lykos is a little nervous when the judges look at him. But they nod, and write his name on the list for the boys' race.

{img:hellanodikai-judges-purple-robes-sticks-watching-young-runners-elis}
Elis has several training grounds. There is a big gymnasium, which is an open courtyard surrounded by covered walkways with columns. There is a running track, and a place for wrestling, with soft sand on the ground. Every day, from early morning, the athletes train there. The air is full of dust and shouting and the smell of olive oil.

Greek athletes train and compete naked. This was normal for them. They saw the healthy, strong human body as something beautiful, a gift from the gods. Before training, each athlete rubs his whole body with olive oil. Then some of them sprinkle a fine dust over the oil, to protect the skin from the sun. Afterwards, they scrape it all off with their bronze scrapers, and wash in cold water.

--

{img:athletes-oiling-skin-clay-flasks-bronze-strigil-courtyard-shade}
Lykos trains every day with the other boys. There are runners from Sparta, from Corinth, from Athens, and from the faraway Greek cities of Sicily and Italy. Some of them are friendly. Some of them are proud, and do not talk to a shepherd boy from the mountains. One boy from Sicily, called Dion, becomes his friend. Dion is the son of a rich family, and has travelled across the sea in a big ship. He has never seen a goat up close.

The training is hard. The judges are strict. They watch the athletes run and wrestle, and they shout at anyone who is lazy. Sometimes they use their sticks, to tap an athlete who breaks a rule. Philon watches Lykos from the side of the track, and gives him advice every evening. Start faster. Keep your head still. Do not look at the other runners. Just run.

In the evenings, the athletes eat together and talk. They tell stories about famous champions. There is Milo of Kroton, a wrestler who won at Olympia six times. People said he once carried a young bull on his shoulders around the whole stadium. And there are runners who won the short race and the long race at the same Games. The boys listen with shining eyes. They only dream of winning one wreath.

--

Lykos misses home. He misses his mother and his sister, and the quiet of the mountains. Elis is noisy and crowded. At night, he lies on his mat and listens to the sounds of the town. Dogs barking, people laughing, carts rolling past. He holds the little clay figure of Zeus in his hand. And he thinks of the green valley at home, and the goat bells, and the stream.

{img:boy-lying-awake-night-holding-small-clay-figure-oil-lamp-elis}
But the month passes quickly. The athletes grow stronger and faster. And at last, at the end of the month, the judges announce the final list of who may compete. Lykos is on it. The next morning, all the athletes, the judges and many others will set out together on the road to Olympia. The Games are about to begin.
