Tonight we travel back more than two thousand years, to the year 60 before Christ. We go to Gaul, the land that is now France. It is a land of great forests, wide rivers, green hills and rich farmland. And on top of a high, wooded hill, in the middle of Gaul, there stands a great walled town. It is called Bibracte. And it is the capital of a Celtic people, called the Aedui.

--

It is early morning, in spring. A thick mist fills the valleys below the hill. Only the tops of the hills rise above it, dark with forests of beech and oak. On top of the highest hill, there is a long wall of stone and timber, running around the town. Inside the wall, there are hundreds of houses, with thatched roofs, and smoke rising from them into the cool morning air. A cock is crowing. Somewhere, a blacksmith's hammer is already ringing.

The people of Bibracte are waking up. Farmers are leading their cattle out to the fields. Women are fetching water from the springs. Craftsmen are opening their workshops. And in a quiet house near the sacred spring, an old man with a long white beard, in a white robe, is standing at his door, looking at the sky. He is watching the moon fade, and the sun rise. He is a druid.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

The Celts were one of the great peoples of ancient Europe. They lived across a huge area, from Ireland and Britain in the west, through Gaul and Spain, to the lands along the Danube, and even as far as Turkey. They were not one country, but many different tribes, each with its own king or leaders. But they spoke related languages, believed in similar gods, and shared a way of life.

Tonight, we will spend a year among the Celts of Gaul, just before the Romans came. We will follow a family in the town of Bibracte. A blacksmith, his wife, and their two children. Especially their son, a boy of twelve, who has been chosen to study with the druids. We will see how the Celts lived, worked, ate and celebrated. We will visit their workshops, their sacred groves and their great feasts.

{img:white-robed-druid-oak-grove-sunlight-old-man-staff-calm-wise}
And we will meet the druids. The wise men of the Celts. Priests, judges, teachers, healers and keepers of knowledge. Very little is known about them, because they never wrote anything down. Everything we know comes from what Greek and Roman writers said about them, and from archaeology. And so, they are one of the great mysteries of the ancient world.

--

The Romans told some dark stories about the druids, and about the Celts. Some of them may be true. Some were probably exaggerated, by enemies who wanted to make the Celts look wild. We will not dwell on them. Instead, we will look at the everyday life of the Celts, their skill, their art, their love of stories, and their deep connection with the natural world. The oak trees, the springs, the moon and the stars.

{img:celtic-hillfort-sunrise-over-forested-hills-gaul-golden-light}
So let us begin, on a misty spring morning, on a hill in the middle of Gaul, in the year 60 before Christ. The sun is rising over the forests. The birds are singing in the oaks. And in the town of Bibracte, a boy of twelve is waking up, on the most important day of his life.
