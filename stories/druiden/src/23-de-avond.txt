So we leave the Celts tonight, in the year 60 before Christ, as the evening falls. The sun is setting over the forested hills of Gaul. The sky is turning red and gold. The mist is beginning to rise in the valleys. And on the high hill of Bibracte, the smoke is rising from the thatched roofs, straight up into the still evening air.

--

In the street of the craftsmen, the forge is quiet now. The fire is banked down. The hammer is resting on the anvil. In the house behind it, the family is sitting around the hearth, eating their supper. Pork and beans from the cauldron. Fresh bread. A cup of beer. The grandmother is telling a story. Senna is listening, with her chin in her hands. And the cat is curled up by the warm stones.

{img:druid-house-courtyard-old-oak-dusk-students-resting-spring-water}
Up the hill, in the druid's house, near the sacred spring, the old druid is sitting under the oak tree in the courtyard, in the last light. Around him, his students are sitting on the grass, repeating the verses they learned today, softly, in the gentle evening. Among them is a boy with red hair and an owl brooch, whose memory is like a deep well, that never runs dry.

--

The sacred spring is murmuring in the twilight, flowing out of the hillside into its stone basin, as it has for thousands of years. A ribbon tied to the branch above it moves gently in the breeze. In the stables near the gate, a white mare and her little foal are standing together, in the warm straw, half asleep. And in the forest below, an owl is calling, softly, to the coming night.

{img:night-sky-over-celtic-hillfort-milky-way-stars-oak-silhouettes-calm}
The stars come out, one by one. Then hundreds of them. Then thousands. The Milky Way stretches across the sky, the road of the souls. The moon rises over the eastern forests, a thin silver crescent, the beginning of a new month. And high on the hill, beyond the town walls, an old druid and a young student are lying on the grass, looking up, naming the stars, in the quiet dark.

--

The town grows quiet. The fires are covered. The dogs curl up by the doors. The forests are still. And under the great sky of Gaul, the people of Bibracte sleep, peacefully, on their high hill among the oaks. Not knowing what the years will bring. Only knowing that the sun will rise again tomorrow, and the moon will grow, and the seasons will turn, as they always have, in the old, slow rhythm of the Celtic year.
