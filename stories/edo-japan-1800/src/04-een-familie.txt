Let us meet the family who lives in a little shop near the river, in the old part of Edo.

The father is called Tokichi. He is a lantern maker, and he is about forty years old. He makes paper lanterns and paper umbrellas, by hand, in the front room of the shop. He learned the craft from his father, who learned it from his father. Tokichi is a calm and patient man. He likes his work, and he does it carefully. He does not talk much while he works. But in the evenings, he likes to laugh and tell jokes.

The mother is called Oyuki. She runs the shop and talks to the customers. She keeps the money in a little wooden box and writes the accounts with a brush. She also cooks and cleans and sews, and looks after the whole family. She is quick and clever, and she always has a kind word for the neighbours. Everyone in the street likes Oyuki.

--

They have two children. The daughter is called Ohana, which means flower. She is eleven years old, with a round face and bright eyes. She goes to a little neighbourhood school in the mornings, where she learns to read and write. She loves to practise her writing with a brush. And she loves to help her father paint pictures on the lanterns.

{img:girl-eleven-practising-calligraphy-brush-ink-low-desk-window-light}
The son is called Shinta, and he is eight. He is lively and curious and always asking questions. He loves boats and wants to be a boatman when he grows up. He spends hours on the riverbank, watching the boats go by. He knows the names of all the boatmen on this part of the river. He also loves sweets, especially the little rice cakes sold by the peddler at the corner.

--

The grandmother lives with them too. She is Tokichi's mother, and her name is Oume. She is about seventy, small and thin, with her grey hair tied up neatly. She has a sharp tongue and a warm heart. Her great love is the kabuki theatre. She has seen every famous actor in Edo, and she knows all the plays by heart. She keeps a little collection of prints of her favourite actors. She takes them out sometimes and looks at them, and sighs happily.

{img:old-japanese-grandmother-looking-at-kabuki-actor-prints-smiling-room}
There is also a young apprentice in the shop, a boy of fifteen called Sukeroku. He came from a village in the countryside to learn lantern making from Tokichi. He lives with the family and eats with them. He sweeps the shop and runs errands, and he is slowly learning the craft. He is a little shy and a little clumsy. But he works hard, and the family is fond of him.

--

The shop stands on a street that runs down to the river. It is a two-storey wooden house, with the shop at the front on the ground floor. Above the door hangs a short curtain of blue cloth, called a noren. On it is written the name of the shop in white letters. When the noren is hanging, the shop is open. When it is taken down, the shop is closed for the day.

{img:edo-lantern-shop-front-blue-noren-curtain-lanterns-hanging-street}
Outside the shop, there are always a few lanterns hanging on display. There are round ones and long ones, red ones and white ones. Some have the names of shops written on them. Some have pictures of flowers or birds or waves. At night, Tokichi lights one of them, so that people can see the shop from far away. The soft light glows warm on the dark street. And neighbours walking past often stop to admire it, and to chat with Oyuki at the door.

The family also has a little dog, a small white dog with brown ears called Shiro. Shiro means white. He sleeps by the kitchen door and follows Shinta everywhere. When Shinta goes to the river, Shiro goes too. He sits beside the boy on the riverbank and watches the boats, with his ears pricked up. When a boatman waves, Shiro wags his tail.

Every evening, after supper, the family sits together for a while before bed. Tokichi smokes a little pipe of tobacco. Oyuki sews or mends clothes. Oume tells a story, or sings a little song from the theatre. Ohana shows her writing to her father. Shinta plays with Shiro on the tatami. And Sukeroku sits quietly in the corner, listening to everything, slowly feeling at home.
