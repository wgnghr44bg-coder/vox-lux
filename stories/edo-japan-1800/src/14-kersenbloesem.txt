In early April, the cherry trees of Edo burst into flower. And the whole city goes out to see them.

The custom of going to look at the cherry blossoms is called hanami, which means flower viewing. It is one of the happiest times of the year. Families and friends go to the places where the cherry trees grow. They spread mats and cloths on the ground under the trees. They eat and drink, sing and dance, and look up at the flowers.

--

There are several famous places for hanami in Edo. There is the hill of Ueno, with its temple and its hundreds of cherry trees. There is the bank of the Sumida river, lined with trees for kilometres. And there is a hill called Asukayama, on the edge of the city. The shogun himself had cherry trees planted there, about eighty years ago, so that ordinary people could enjoy them.

{img:sumida-riverbank-cherry-blossoms-boats-petals-falling-water-spring}
The family goes to Asukayama on a sunny day in April. It is a long walk, about two hours. They set off early in the morning. Oyuki has prepared a beautiful picnic in a lacquered box. There are rice balls, wrapped in seaweed. There are grilled fish and boiled eggs, pickled vegetables and little sweet cakes. Tokichi carries a jug of sake, rice wine, for the grown-ups.

--

When they arrive at the hill, it is already crowded with people. Every space under the trees is taken. There are families and groups of friends, old people and young people. Some have brought musical instruments. Some are dancing. Some are writing poems about the flowers and hanging them on the branches. The family finds a little spot under a tree and spreads out their mat.

{img:family-picnic-mat-under-cherry-tree-lacquer-box-food-grandmother-laughing}
The cherry blossoms are beautiful. The trees are covered with pale pink flowers, so many that you can hardly see the branches. When the wind blows, the petals fall like soft pink snow. They fall on the mats and in the food and in people's hair. Shinta tries to catch them as they fall. Ohana collects them in her hand and lets them float away again.

--

Oume sits under the tree, looking up at the blossoms. She is quiet for a long time. Then she says something to Ohana. The cherry blossoms are beautiful because they do not last, she says. A few days, and then they are gone. That is why we must look at them now, while we can. Ohana does not quite understand. But she looks up at the flowers very carefully, so she will remember them.

{img:evening-cherry-blossoms-paper-lanterns-glowing-people-walking-home}
As the sun goes down, lanterns are lit under the trees. The cherry blossoms glow pale in the lantern light. People sing and laugh, and the music goes on. At last, the family packs up their picnic box and walks home in the dusk. Shinta is so tired that Tokichi carries him on his back. Ohana walks beside her grandmother, holding her hand. There are still cherry petals in her hair.

Before the cherry blossoms, there are the plum blossoms. They come at the end of winter, when it is still cold. Plum flowers are white or deep pink, with a sweet smell. People go to see them too, wrapped in warm coats. Oume loves the plum blossoms even more than the cherries. They are brave, she says, because they bloom in the cold, before anything else.

After hanami, the petals cover the ground under the trees like a pink carpet. They float on the river, in long drifting patches. Children scoop them up in their hands and throw them in the air. Within a week, the blossoms are all gone, and the trees are green with new leaves. People sigh and say, until next year. And they go back to their work.
