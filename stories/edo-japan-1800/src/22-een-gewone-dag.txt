Before we leave Edo, let us go back once more, to an ordinary afternoon in late spring, in the year 1800.

The cherry blossoms are over now, and the trees along the river are green with new leaves. Swallows have come back from the south, and they are building their nests under the eaves of the houses. In the lantern shop, Tokichi is working on a big order of lanterns for a temple festival. Sukeroku is helping him, splitting bamboo into thin strips. The apprentice is getting better every week. Today, Tokichi let him paste the paper on a lantern all by himself.

--

Oyuki is sitting at the front of the shop, writing in her account book with a brush. A neighbour stops at the door to chat about the weather and the price of rice. Down the street, a peddler is calling out that he has fresh bamboo shoots. Oyuki thinks she might buy some for supper. Bamboo shoots with a little soy sauce and fish flakes is Tokichi's favourite spring dish.

{img:mother-shop-front-account-book-brush-neighbour-chatting-afternoon-sun}
Oume is sitting in the little garden at the back, on a cushion in the sun. She is sewing a new cover for a cushion, with tiny stitches. Now and then, she stops and listens to the water dripping in the stone basin. A little brown sparrow comes down to drink from it. She keeps very still, so it will not fly away.

--

Ohana has come home from school. She is sitting at her little desk upstairs, by the window, practising her characters. Today she is writing the character for spring. It has many strokes, and it is hard to make it beautiful. She has written it twenty times already. The twenty-first one is the best. She holds it up and looks at it, and decides to show it to her grandmother.

{img:girl-upstairs-window-calligraphy-spring-character-afternoon-light-street}
And Shinta is down by the river, as usual. He is sitting on the wooden steps of a landing, with his chin in his hands. The boats go past, one after another. There is the vegetable boat, the fish boat, and the boat that carries rice. The old boatman who rowed them at the fireworks waves to him. Shinta waves back. One day, he thinks, that will be me.

--

In the late afternoon, the family goes to the bath together, as always. They soak in the hot water and chat with the neighbours. Then they walk home, warm and pink, through the evening street. They eat supper together around the little low tables. There is rice and soup, grilled fish and bamboo shoots with soy sauce. Tokichi says it is the best supper he has had all year. He says that every spring.

{img:family-supper-low-tables-evening-lantern-light-rice-bamboo-shoots}
After supper, Mr Yamada stops by the shop. He has written a new haiku, his own this time. He reads it to Oume. It is about a swallow, building its nest under the roof of a lantern shop. Oume listens, and then she is quiet for a moment. That one, she says, is good. Mr Yamada bows deeply, very pleased. And the evening light fades slowly over the street.

Late in the afternoon, a tofu seller comes down the street with his little horn. Oyuki buys a block of fresh tofu for supper. The tofu seller is an old friend. He asks about the children and the grandmother. He tells Oyuki that his daughter is getting married next month. Oyuki is delighted. She promises to make a special lantern for the wedding.

Later, when the children are in bed, Tokichi sits by the lamp and finishes the last lantern of the day. He paints a crane flying across a full moon, with long slow strokes of his brush. When it is finished, he holds it up and looks at it. It is good. He puts it on the shelf with the others, ready for the temple festival. Then he blows out the lamp and goes to bed.
