In the middle of July, Mary, Shelley, and Claire go on a journey to the mountains. To the valley of Chamonix, at the foot of Mont Blanc, the highest mountain in the Alps. It is about eighty kilometres from Geneva. They travel in a carriage, and then on mules, along rough mountain roads, up and up, through deep valleys, and dark forests, and past roaring rivers, and waterfalls. The baby stays at home, with his nurse.

The journey is wild, and beautiful, and a little frightening. The road climbs along the edge of deep gorges, with the river roaring far below. The mountains rise up on every side, higher, and higher, with snow on their tops, even in July. There are great waterfalls, falling hundreds of metres, down the cliffs. And there are avalanches, which they hear, far away, like thunder, as the snow crashes down the mountainsides.

--

{img:chamonix-valley-1816-village-wooden-chalets-glaciers-above-summer-meadows}
At last, they reach Chamonix. It is a small village, in a long, narrow valley, with wooden houses, and a church, and a few inns, for travellers. Above it, on every side, rise the great mountains. And above them all, Mont Blanc. Huge, and white, and silent, its peak hidden in the clouds. From the valley, they can see the glaciers. Great rivers of ice, coming down from the mountains, between the rocks, almost into the valley itself.

The next day, they climb up to the most famous glacier of all, the Mer de Glace. The Sea of Ice. It is a long, wide glacier, high in the mountains, between great peaks of rock. They climb for hours, up a steep path, through pine forests, with a guide, and mules. When they reach the top, they see the glacier, spread out below them. A great frozen river of ice, cracked, and broken, into waves, and ridges, and deep blue crevasses, stretching away, between the mountains.

{img:young-woman-standing-at-edge-of-glacier-looking-vast-ice-awe-1816}
Mary is overwhelmed. She has never seen anything so vast, and so wild, and so strange. She writes, later, that it was like a frozen sea, whose waves had been stopped, in the middle of a storm. The silence is enormous. Only the occasional crack, and groan, of the ice, moving, very slowly, down the valley. And the wind, blowing across it. She feels very small, and very far from the rest of the world.

The weather, as usual that summer, is bad. It rains, and the clouds come down, and hide the mountains. But sometimes, the clouds part, for a moment, and they see the great white peak of Mont Blanc, shining in the sun, high above them. And then the clouds close again. Shelley writes a long poem about it, called Mont Blanc. About the power of nature, and the mystery of the mountain, and the silence of the snow.

--

In the evenings, at the inn, in Chamonix, they sit by the fire, and write letters, and their journals, and talk about what they have seen. Mary thinks about her story. And she decides that the Mer de Glace must be part of it. In her book, Victor Frankenstein climbs up to the glacier, alone, to find peace, after a great sorrow. And there, on the ice, among the mountains, he meets the creature, for the first time since he made it.

The creature comes toward him, across the ice, leaping over the crevasses, with superhuman speed. Victor is terrified. But the creature does not attack him. Instead, he asks Victor to listen to him. To listen to his story. And they go together into a little hut, on the mountain, and sit by a fire, and the creature tells Victor everything that has happened to him, since the night he was made. It is one of the most powerful scenes in the book.

{img:mountain-hut-fire-two-figures-talking-glacier-outside-snow-falling}
Mary and Shelley and Claire come back to Geneva after about a week, tired, and cold, and full of wonder. They are glad to be back by the lake, in the little house, with the baby. Mary picks up Willmouse, and holds him tightly, and kisses him. And that evening, she sits down at her desk, and writes about the Mer de Glace, and the mountains, and the meeting on the ice. While it is all still fresh in her mind.
