Today, Lake Geneva is still one of the most beautiful places in Europe. The lake is still long, and deep, and blue, between the green hills, and the vineyards, and the mountains. On a clear day, you can still see Mont Blanc, white, and shining, far away, above the clouds. And the boats still sail on the lake, in the summer, white sails on the blue water, just as they did in 1816.

Geneva is a much bigger city now, of about two hundred thousand people. It is an international city, home to many great world organisations. The Red Cross was founded here, in 1863. And the United Nations has its European headquarters here. People from every country in the world live and work in Geneva. And in the middle of the lake, there is a great fountain, the Jet d'Eau, which shoots water a hundred and forty metres into the air.

{img:today-villa-diodati-cologny-private-house-hillside-trees-lake-view}
The Villa Diodati is still there, on the hillside, at Cologny. It looks much as it did in 1816, with its pale walls, and its long balcony, looking out over the lake. It is a private house now, and visitors cannot go inside. But you can walk past it, on the road, and look at it, through the gates, and imagine the young poets on the balcony, watching the storms. There is a small plaque, by the gate, which tells its story.

The little house below the villa, where the Shelleys stayed, Maison Chapuis, is gone. It was pulled down, long ago. But the vineyard between the two houses is still there, on the steep slope. And the path through the vines, which Mary and Shelley walked up and down every day, is still there too. Cologny is now one of the most expensive and exclusive places to live, in the whole of Switzerland.

{img:today-chillon-castle-visitors-lake-shore-autumn-mountains-reflection}
The castle of Chillon still stands on its rock, at the eastern end of the lake. It is one of the most visited castles in Switzerland. Visitors can go down into the dungeon, and see the stone pillars, and the iron rings, where Bonivard was chained. And on one of the pillars, they can see the name of Byron, carved in the stone. Or a name that people believe Byron carved. Many visitors take a photograph of it.

In Chamonix, the glaciers are still there. But they are much smaller than they were in 1816. In the cold years of the early nineteenth century, they reached far down into the valley. Since then, as the climate has warmed, they have melted, and shrunk, and drawn back up into the mountains. The Mer de Glace is much shorter, and thinner, than when Mary saw it. Every year, it loses a little more. Visitors can take a little red mountain train up to see it, from Chamonix.

--

In 2016, two hundred years after the famous summer, there were celebrations, and exhibitions, in Geneva, about Frankenstein, and Mary Shelley, and the Villa Diodati. A great library near Geneva held an exhibition, with rare books, and letters, and manuscripts. There were films, and talks, and plays, and readings. And people walked along the lake, to Cologny, to see the villa, and remember the summer of 1816.

Near the old city of Geneva, there is now a statue of Frankenstein's creature. It is not a frightening statue. It shows the creature as he is in the book. A big, sad figure, standing alone, in a quiet square, looking a little lost. Children sometimes climb on it, and people take photographs with it. It seems to fit, somehow. The creature, who was born in Mary's imagination, by the lake, has come home to Geneva.

{img:today-cologny-vineyard-autumn-grape-harvest-pickers-lake-below-sunshine}
And every autumn, in Cologny, the grapes are still harvested, on the steep slopes, above the lake. The pickers still carry the baskets down the hill, to the press. The new wine is still tasted, with bread, and cheese, and roasted chestnuts. And on a cold evening, when the storm clouds gather over the mountains, and the lightning flickers over the lake, some people in Cologny still remember the summer when Frankenstein was born.
