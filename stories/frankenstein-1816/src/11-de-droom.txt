Then, one night, it happens. Mary has gone to bed, late, after a long evening of conversation, by the fire, at the villa. She lies in bed, in the dark, with her eyes closed. But she cannot sleep. Her mind is full of the things she has heard, about electricity, and life, and death. And then, as she lies there, between sleeping and waking, she sees something. Not a dream, exactly. More like a vision, very clear, and very vivid, as if it were really happening, in front of her eyes.

She sees a young student, pale, and thin, kneeling beside something he has made. It is the body of a man, lying stretched out, on a table. A body made by the student himself, out of many parts. The student has worked on it for a long time, in secret. And now, he has found a way to bring it to life. Mary sees the body begin to stir, with a kind of uneasy, half living movement. And she sees the student, terrified by what he has done.

--

{img:shuttered-window-dawn-light-young-woman-sitting-up-in-bed-wide-eyed}
In her vision, Mary sees the student run away, from the thing he has made, in horror. He goes to his room, and lies down, and tries to sleep, and forget. He hopes that the spark of life will fade, and the creature will be still again. But then he opens his eyes. And there, beside his bed, holding open the curtains, and looking at him, with yellow, watery, questioning eyes, is the creature he has made.

Mary opens her eyes, with a start. Her heart is beating fast. She is frightened. She looks around the dark room, to make sure that it is really empty. She sees the moonlight, coming through the shutters. The window, the curtains, the furniture, all just as they should be. And she hears the rain, falling outside, and the lake, lapping on the shore. She is safe. It was only a vision.

{img:moonlight-on-lake-from-window-quiet-night-rain-stopped-1816-calm}
She tries to think of something else, something ordinary. Something calm. The ghost story competition. The others' stories. And then, suddenly, she realises. This is it. This is her story. What frightened her, in the night, will frighten other people too. She only needs to describe the spectre that has come to her pillow. She feels a great rush of joy, and excitement, in the dark. She has found her story.

Many years later, in 1831, Mary wrote about that night, in an introduction to a new edition of her book. She described the vision, just as she had seen it. And she described her joy, when she realised that she had found her story. That introduction is one of the most famous descriptions of how a story was born, in the whole history of literature. People still read it today, and wonder.

--

The next morning, when the others ask her, as usual, have you thought of a story yet? Mary says yes. She has. She tells them about her vision. They are very interested. Shelley especially. He encourages her to write it. And so, that morning, she sits down at her desk, by the window, in the little house below the villa, and begins to write. The first words she writes are, It was on a dreary night of November.

{img:young-woman-writing-first-page-quill-desk-window-lake-morning-grey}
At first, she thinks it will be a short story. A few pages, like the others. But as she writes, the story grows. And grows. She thinks about the student, and why he made the creature. She thinks about the creature, and what it feels, and what it wants. She thinks about what happens next. The story becomes longer, and deeper, and richer, and sadder, than any ghost story. It becomes a novel.

Élise sees her, that morning, through the window, as she walks up the path to the villa. The young English lady, with her red-gold hair, bent over her desk, writing, and writing, very fast, with her pen. She does not look up, as Élise passes. She is somewhere else, in a world of her own. Élise wonders what she is writing. She will not find out, for many years.
