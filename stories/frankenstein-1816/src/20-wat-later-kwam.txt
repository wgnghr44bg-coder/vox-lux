In December 1816, a few months after they came back from Geneva, Mary and Shelley were married, in London. It was a quiet wedding, in a church. Mary's father was there, and he was happy, at last. The next year, Mary had another baby, a little girl, called Clara. And in 1818, the family moved to Italy, where Shelley hoped that the warm climate would be good for his health.

Italy was beautiful. But there was great sadness there too. Both of Mary's young children, Clara and William, passed away, in Italy, of fevers, within a year of each other. Mary was heartbroken. For a long time, she could hardly bear to live. Then, in 1819, she had another son, Percy Florence. He grew up healthy, and strong, and lived a long life. He was a great comfort to her.

In 1822, the Shelleys were living in a house by the sea, on the coast of Italy, near Lerici. Shelley loved sailing, as he had on Lake Geneva. He had a new boat built, and named it after one of Byron's poems. In July, he sailed along the coast, to visit Byron, and some friends. On the way back, a sudden storm came up, at sea. The boat sank, and Shelley was drowned, with his friend, and a boy who sailed with them. He was twenty-nine years old.

Byron passed away two years later, in 1824, in Greece, where he had gone to help the Greeks, in their war for freedom against the Turks. He caught a fever, and died, at the age of thirty-six. He became a hero in Greece, and is still honoured there today. Polidori had passed away, three years earlier, in London, at the age of only twenty-five. Of all the friends at the Villa Diodati, in that summer of 1816, only Mary and Claire lived to grow old.

{img:quiet-cemetery-bournemouth-church-gravestone-flowers-gentle-light}
Mary went back to England, with her little son. She was a widow, at twenty-four. She had very little money. So she worked, as a writer, to support herself, and her son. She wrote more novels, and stories, and travel books, and biographies. One of her novels, The Last Man, is about the end of the human race, in a great plague, far in the future. She also edited Shelley's poems, and made sure that the world remembered him, as a great poet.

She never married again. She said that she had been married to a poet, and that was enough. She lived quietly, in London, with her son. When he grew up, he married a kind woman, who loved Mary, and looked after her. Mary passed away in 1851, at the age of fifty-three. She was buried in a churchyard, in Bournemouth, by the sea, with her mother and father, who were moved there to be with her.

--

Claire lived the longest of them all. She had her baby, a little girl, in January 1817, and called her Allegra. Byron took the little girl to Italy, to bring her up. But Allegra passed away, of a fever, when she was five. Claire never forgave Byron. She lived a long, travelling life, as a governess, in Russia, and Germany, and Italy. She passed away in Florence, in 1879, at the age of eighty. She was the last one, of that famous summer, by the lake.

{img:older-woman-in-florence-1870s-garden-chair-remembering-quietly-sunlit}
And the Morets, in Cologny? Pierre went on sailing his boat, on the lake, for many years. He carried many more English tourists, who came to see the Villa Diodati, and the places where the famous poets had stayed. He always told them about the storm, near the harbour, and the gold coin that Byron gave him. Jacques became a boatman too, and later, the owner of three boats, and a small hotel.

Élise married a young watchmaker, from Geneva, who worked with her Uncle Henri. They lived in the old town, in a tall house, with a view of the lake. She learned to read English, slowly, with the little blue book that Mary gave her. And many years later, she read Frankenstein, in English, from beginning to end. When she came to the parts about Geneva, and the lake, and the storms, and the mountains, she cried a little. Because she had been there, that summer, when it all began.
