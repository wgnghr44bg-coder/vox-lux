Tonight, we travel back more than two hundred years, to the summer of 1816. We go to Switzerland, to the shores of Lake Geneva. A long, deep, beautiful lake, shaped like a crescent moon, lying between green hills, and vineyards, with the high white mountains of the Alps rising beyond. On a clear day, you can see the snowy peak of Mont Blanc, the highest mountain in Europe, far away, floating above the clouds.

At the western end of the lake, there is the old city of Geneva, with its grey stone houses, and its cathedral, on the hill. And along the shores, there are villages, and farms, and vineyards, and the elegant country houses of rich families, with their gardens, running down to the water. It is one of the loveliest places in Europe. Travellers come from all over the world to see it.

--

But the summer of 1816 is a strange summer. It is cold, and dark, and wet. It rains almost every day. Great storms sweep across the lake, with thunder, and lightning, and wind. The sun hardly shines. In June, there is snow on the hills, where there should be flowers. The crops will not ripen in the fields. People will remember it for the rest of their lives, as the year without a summer.

{img:villa-diodati-cologny-1816-stone-house-balcony-lake-view-stormy-sky}
And in a house on the hillside above the lake, a group of young English travellers is staying. A famous poet, and another poet, and a young doctor, and two young women, and a baby. Because of the rain, they cannot go outside. So they sit by the fire, in the evenings, and talk, and read, and tell stories. And one of them, a young woman of eighteen, is about to have a dream, which will lead to one of the most famous books ever written.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

{img:swiss-boatman-family-1816-cottage-vineyard-lake-shore-father-mother-children}
In this story, we will spend that strange summer by the lake. We will meet the English travellers. Mary Godwin, the young writer. Percy Bysshe Shelley, the poet she loves. Lord Byron, the most famous poet in Europe. His young doctor, John Polidori. And Mary's stepsister, Claire. And we will meet a local family, who live in the village below the great house. The Morets. Pierre, a boatman, and a grower of grapes. His wife Louise. Their daughter Élise, who is twelve, and helps in the kitchen of the great house. Their son Jacques, who is nine. And old Grandmother Anne.

We will see the storms over the lake, and the evenings by the fire. We will hear the ghost stories, told in the candlelight, on a dark and stormy night. We will be there when Mary has her famous dream. We will travel to the mountains, to see the great glaciers of Mont Blanc. And at the end of the year, we will help with the grape harvest, very late, in the cold autumn, when the strange summer was finally over.

{img:candlelit-drawing-room-1816-young-people-by-fire-books-rain-on-windows}
It is not a frightening story. It is a story about a group of young people, far from home, in a beautiful place, in bad weather, with time on their hands, and their heads full of ideas. And about a book, which was born from a summer of rain, and storms, and conversation, and which has been read, and loved, and retold, all over the world, ever since.

So let us go to Lake Geneva, in the summer of 1816. Let us walk up through the wet vineyards, to the great house on the hill. And let us listen to the rain on the windows, and the crackle of the fire, and the voices of the young people, talking late into the night.
