When they first came to the lake, Shelley and Byron decided to buy a boat. They both loved boats, and the water. They bought a small sailing boat, together, with a single sail, and a keel, and they kept it in a little harbour, below the Shelleys' house. In the evenings, when the weather allowed, they went out on the lake, with Mary, and Claire, and sometimes Polidori.

Neither Byron nor Shelley was a very good sailor. And the lake can be dangerous, with sudden storms, and strong winds, coming down from the mountains. So they asked Pierre Moret to go with them, sometimes, as their boatman. Pierre knows the lake better than anyone. He sits at the back of the boat, at the tiller, and steers, and handles the sail, while the English talk, and look at the view.

--

{img:moonlit-lake-boat-drifting-poets-talking-boatman-at-tiller-mountains}
On calm evenings, the lake is very beautiful. The water is still, and clear, and reflects the sky, and the mountains, and the first stars. The boat drifts slowly, with the sail barely moving. The only sound is the water lapping against the boat, and the voices of the young people, talking quietly, about poetry, and life, and love, and the stars. Sometimes Byron sings an old song, or recites a poem. Sometimes Shelley reads aloud from a book.

Pierre does not understand most of what they say. But he understands enough. He knows that they are talking about important things. And he likes them, the young English. They are polite to him, and they pay him well, and they ask him about the lake, and the weather, and the fish, and the villages. Shelley especially likes to talk to him, in his careful French. He asks Pierre about his life, and his family, and listens to the answers.

{img:boatman-and-young-woman-talking-boat-lake-1816-baby-wrapped-shawl}
Mary likes Pierre too. She sometimes sits beside him, at the back of the boat, with the baby wrapped in her shawl, and asks him questions. About the lake, and the mountains, and the old stories of Geneva. Pierre tells her about the ghosts that people say walk along the shore, on stormy nights. And about the old castle of Chillon, at the other end of the lake, where prisoners were once kept in a dungeon, below the water. Mary listens, and remembers.

One evening, a sudden wind comes down from the mountains, and the lake becomes rough, very quickly. The waves grow big, and the boat rocks, and spray flies over the side. Claire is frightened. Shelley, who cannot swim, sits very still. Byron, who is a strong swimmer, laughs, and says that if the boat sinks, he will save everyone. Pierre says nothing. He just steers, calmly, and brings the boat safely back to the harbour, in ten minutes.

--

{img:boat-returning-safely-small-harbour-storm-clouds-lanterns-relieved}
When they are safely ashore, Byron shakes Pierre's hand, and gives him a gold coin. And Shelley thanks him, and says that he is the best sailor on the lake. Pierre is embarrassed. He says that it was only a little wind. But that night, he tells Louise that the English should be more careful. The lake is beautiful, he says, but it is not a pond. It can turn, very quickly.

The boat trips on the lake are some of the happiest moments of that summer, for the young English travellers. Mary wrote later about the evenings on the water, and the beauty of the lake, and the mountains, in the evening light. And many years later, she remembered them, with great love, and sadness, because of what happened later. But that is a story for another time.

For now, it is summer. Or what passes for summer, this year. And when the rain stops, for an evening, and the clouds part, and the sun sets, red, and gold, over the Jura mountains, the little boat goes out on the lake again. With Pierre at the tiller, and the young poets talking, and the stars coming out, over the dark water.
