So we leave the lake tonight, in the summer of 1816, as the evening falls. The rain has stopped, for a while. The clouds are lifting, over the mountains, and in the west, the sky is glowing, with a strange, deep red, and orange, and gold, from the faraway volcano. The lake is still, and dark, and shining. And the first stars are coming out, between the clouds.

Up on the hill, the windows of the Villa Diodati are glowing, with candlelight. The friends are gathered in the drawing room, by the fire. Byron is reading aloud, from a book of ghost stories. Shelley is lying on the sofa, listening, with his eyes closed. Polidori is writing in his diary. Claire is sitting by the window, looking out at the lake. And Mary is sitting in the corner, quietly, listening to everything.

--

{img:path-through-vineyard-dusk-lantern-girl-walking-home-cottage-lights}
Down the path, through the wet vineyard, Élise is walking home, with her lantern swinging. Her day's work at the villa is finished. She is tired, but happy. She can hear the voices, from the villa, behind her, and the soft sound of the lake, in front of her. And ahead of her, she can see the warm light in the window of her own house, where her family is waiting, with supper.

In the Morets' kitchen, the fire is burning, and the soup is bubbling, in the pot. Louise is cutting bread, and cheese. Pierre is sitting by the fire, with his pipe, which has gone out again. Grandmother Anne is knitting, in her chair. Jacques is mending a fishing line. When Élise comes in, they all look up, and smile. And she sits down, and tells them about her day.

--

{img:cottage-window-night-family-by-fire-lake-dark-outside-distant-thunder}
After supper, they sit together by the fire, for a while. Grandmother Anne tells an old story, about a water spirit, who lived in the lake, long ago, and helped the fishermen in storms. Jacques falls asleep, with his head on the table. Élise helps her mother with the dishes. And then, one by one, they go up to bed, under the steep roof, and the house grows quiet.

Outside, far away, over the mountains, there is a faint flicker of lightning, and a low, gentle rumble of thunder. Another storm is coming, slowly, across the lake. But it is far away, still. And the house is warm, and safe. The rain begins again, softly, on the roof. And the lake laps quietly on the shore, below the vineyard, in the dark. And somewhere up on the hill, by candlelight, a young woman of eighteen is still awake, listening to the rain, and thinking about a story.
