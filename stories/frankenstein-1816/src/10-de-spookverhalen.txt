In the middle of June, there is a week of terrible weather. It rains, and rains, and rains, for days. There are storms every night. Nobody can go outside. The friends are all at the Villa Diodati, gathered around the fire, in the drawing room, in the evenings, bored, and restless. They have read all the books they brought. They have talked about everything.

Then somebody finds a book, in the villa. It is a collection of German ghost stories, translated into French. It is called Fantasmagoriana. They take turns reading the stories aloud, by candlelight, in the evenings, while the rain falls, and the wind howls, and the thunder rolls over the lake. The stories are about ghosts, and phantoms, and haunted castles, and strange happenings in the night.

--

{img:group-reading-ghost-stories-aloud-candlelight-storm-window-villa-diodati}
One story is about a man who kisses all his children, one by one, as they sleep. And another is about a family portrait, and a ghostly figure, that walks at midnight. And another about a bride, who discovers that the man she has married is a ghost. They are old-fashioned, and a little silly, in places. But in the candlelight, with the storm outside, they are also quite exciting. The young people listen, wide-eyed, and shiver, a little, and laugh at themselves for shivering.

Then, one evening, after reading the stories, Byron has an idea. He says, we will each write a ghost story. A competition. Each of us will write a story, and then we will see whose is best. The others agree, at once. It sounds like a wonderful game, for the rainy evenings. Byron, and Shelley, and Polidori, and Mary will each write one. Claire says that she will not write one. She will listen.

{img:writers-at-desks-different-rooms-villa-night-candles-quills-rain}
So they all go off, to think about their stories. Byron begins a story about a mysterious gentleman, who travels to the East, and dies, and seems to come back to life. He writes a few pages, and then gets bored, and stops. He is not really interested in prose stories. He prefers poetry. Shelley begins a story about his own childhood, and his strange experiences. But he too soon gives up. He finds it too hard.

Polidori begins a story about a lady with a skull for a head, who has been punished for looking through a keyhole. Even Polidori admits that it is not very good. But later, he takes Byron's unfinished story, about the mysterious gentleman, and turns it into a story of his own. It is called The Vampyre. It is published, three years later, and it is a great success. It is the first modern vampire story in English. And it inspired many of the vampire stories that came after, including, much later, the story of Dracula.

--

And Mary? Mary tries, and tries, to think of a story. Every morning, the others ask her, have you thought of a story yet? And every morning, she has to say no. She thinks, and thinks, but nothing comes. She wants to write a story that will make people afraid to look around them. That will make their blood run cold, and their hearts beat faster. But she cannot think of one. She feels stupid, and frustrated.

{img:young-woman-at-window-rain-night-thinking-chin-in-hand-candle-behind}
Days pass. The others have given up on their stories. But Mary keeps thinking. In the evenings, she listens to Byron and Shelley talking, by the fire, about electricity, and life, and death. And at night, she goes to bed, in the little house below the villa, and lies awake, listening to the rain, and thinking. Her baby sleeps in his cradle, beside the bed. Shelley sleeps beside her. And Mary lies awake, in the dark, waiting for a story to come.

Élise hears about the competition from the cook, who heard it from Byron's servant. She tells her family, at supper. A ghost story competition! Jacques thinks it is a wonderful idea. That evening, by the fire, he tells a ghost story of his own, about a headless boatman, who rows across the lake, on stormy nights. It is not very frightening. But Grandmother Anne pretends to be terrified, and everyone laughs.
