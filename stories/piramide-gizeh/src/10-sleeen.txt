How do you move a stone as heavy as two cars, with no wheels and no machines?

You put it on a sledge. A sledge is a big flat wooden frame with two long runners underneath, a bit like a sled for snow. The block is tied onto the sledge with ropes. Then a team of men pulls the sledge by long ropes.

--

But pulling a heavy sledge over sand is very hard. The runners dig into the sand. It is like trying to drag something heavy through a sandbox. The sand piles up in front of the runners. And the sledge gets stuck.

The Egyptians found a clever trick. They poured water on the sand in front of the sledge. Not too much and not too little. Just enough to make the sand damp. Damp sand is firm. It does not pile up. And the sledge slides over it much more easily.

{img:egyptian-tomb-painting-style-statue-on-sledge-man-pouring-water}
How do we know? There is an old Egyptian painting in a tomb. It is from a bit later than Khufu's time. It shows a huge statue on a sledge. Many men are pulling it with ropes. And at the front, a man stands on the sledge pouring water onto the sand from a jar. For a long time, people thought this was part of a ceremony. Then, a few years ago, scientists tested it. They found that wet sand really does make pulling much easier. About half as hard. The Egyptians knew exactly what they were doing.

--

The builders also made special roads for the sledges. They laid wooden beams across the path, or packed hard clay. That way the sledges slid smoothly. Some of these roads have been found. One has wooden beams still in place after four thousand years.

{img:team-pulling-in-rhythm-overseer-clapping-song-dust-afternoon}
Pulling a sledge is teamwork. Twenty or thirty men pull together. If they pull at different times, the sledge jerks and stops. So they sing. One man calls out a line. The others answer and pull at the same moment. Pull and rest. Pull and rest. The songs are simple and loud. Some are funny. Some are about their homes or their families or the king.

--

At the end of a long pull, the team stops. The block is in place at the foot of the ramp. The men drop the ropes and sit down in the sand, panting. Someone brings water. Someone tells a joke. Everyone laughs. And then the overseer calls. Another block is waiting.
