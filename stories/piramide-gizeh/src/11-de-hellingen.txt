The biggest question of all. How did they get the stones up?

The bottom of the pyramid is easy. You just slide the blocks into place. But the pyramid rises higher and higher. Fifty metres. A hundred metres. How do you get a block weighing two tons all the way up there?

--

The answer is ramps. A ramp is a slope you can drag a sledge up. The Egyptians built ramps of mud brick and rubble and limestone chips. They leaned them against the pyramid. And they pulled the stones up.

But what kind of ramps exactly? Nobody knows for certain. People have argued about it for a long time. And there are several ideas.

{img:straight-long-ramp-toward-pyramid-side-many-teams-climbing}
One idea is one long straight ramp, running up one side. But to reach the top, that ramp would have to be enormous. Almost as big as the pyramid itself. And every time the pyramid grows, the ramp must grow longer too.

--

Another idea is a ramp that winds around the outside of the pyramid. Like a path spiraling up a mountain. It would be shorter. But it would cover the sides, so the builders could not check if the corners were straight.

{img:spiral-ramp-winding-around-pyramid-teams-on-path-evening-light}
A newer idea says there may have been ramps inside the pyramid. A tunnel-like ramp that spirals up just inside the outer wall. Some scientists think they have found signs of this. But it is not proven.

--

And in 2018, archaeologists found something exciting. In an old quarry far away, from the time of Khufu, they found a steep ramp. On each side of it there were stairs. And there were post holes, where big wooden posts once stood. The workers may have used the posts like a pulley. They wrapped the ropes around the posts. Then they could pull the sledge up a much steeper slope, with less effort. Maybe the pyramid builders used the same trick.

{img:steep-quarry-ramp-stairs-both-sides-wooden-posts-ropes-sledge}
Perhaps the truth is a mix of all these ideas. Big ramps at the bottom. Smaller steeper ramps higher up. And clever levers and posts at the very top. The builders were practical people. They used whatever worked.

--

One thing is sure. Every block was pulled up by people. Team after team. Day after day. Rope in hand, feet in the dust, voices singing. And every evening, when the sun went down behind the desert, the pyramid was a little taller than in the morning.

It was dangerous too. A rope could snap. A sledge could slide back. So the overseers watched every pull with sharp eyes. And when a block finally reached the top and slid into its place, the whole team cheered.

Nobody knows exactly what the ramps looked like. Perhaps there was one long straight ramp, leading up one side. Perhaps the ramp wound around the pyramid like a path around a hill. Perhaps there were small ramps inside the pyramid itself. Scientists and builders still argue about it today. They draw pictures and build models and test their ideas. And every few years, someone finds a new clue in the sand. It is one of the great puzzles of the pyramid.

From the top of the ramps, the workers can see a very long way. They can see the green river valley stretching north and south. They can see the white walls of Memphis in the distance. They can see the boats on the river, with their sails like little white wings. On a clear day, some say, you can see the edge of the desert shimmering in the heat. For a moment, the tired workers stop and look. Then they turn back to their work.
