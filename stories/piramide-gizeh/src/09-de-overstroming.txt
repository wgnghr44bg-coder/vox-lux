In the middle of summer, the Nile begins to rise. It is the start of the flood. And at Giza, the busiest season of the year begins.

The water creeps up the river banks. It spills into the canals. It covers the fields. Day by day, the green land disappears under brown water. Villages stand on their mounds like little islands. Palm trees stick up out of the water. Birds fly over it in great flocks.

--

For the farmers, this is a time of waiting. They cannot plough or plant. Their fields are under water. So many of them are called to work for the king. They leave their villages and come to Giza. They join a work team. They get a place to sleep in the long halls of the workers' town. And they start work on the pyramid.

For some young men, it is their first time away from home. They have never seen anything like Giza. The huge pyramid. The crowds of workers. The noise and the dust. At first they are scared. Will they be strong enough? Will they make friends in their team? But soon they learn the work songs. They find a place in their team. And they start to feel part of something great.

{img:young-farmer-arriving-giza-first-time-staring-at-pyramid-bundle}
The flood brings the water close to the pyramid. And that matters a lot. Because now boats can sail right up to the harbour at the foot of the hill. They can bring white stone from Tura and granite from far to the south. And food and wood and copper. Everything comes by water.

--

So the flood season is a race. For about three months, the boats come and go day and night. The harbour is crowded. Workers unload stone from dawn to dusk. When the flood goes down, the boats will not be able to come so close. The heavy stones must arrive before the water drops.

{img:busy-harbour-giza-many-boats-unloading-stone-workers-flood-season}
Every year, Hemiunu watches the flood with worry. A good flood means plenty of water for the boats. And plenty of food next year. A low flood means trouble. Fewer boats. Less food. Slower work. So when the river rises high and steady, everyone at Giza is glad. And the work goes faster than ever.

--

One year, the flood comes late. The river stays low for weeks after it should have risen. At Giza, the boats wait at Tura with their white stones. They cannot get across. The harbour is half dry. People in the villages begin to pray. Priests make offerings at the river. Every morning, the measurers look at the marks on the stone steps. Has it risen? Not yet. Then, one morning, there is a new mark. And the next day another. The river is coming. Everyone breathes again. And within a week, the boats are sailing across once more.
