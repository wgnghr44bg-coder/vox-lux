On the east bank of the Nile, across from Giza, there are cliffs of fine white limestone. This is Tura.

The stone at Tura is special. It is very fine and very even. When it is cut and polished, it is almost pure white. It shines in the sun. That is exactly what Khufu wants for the outside of his pyramid. A smooth white skin that glows like light.

--

The best stone at Tura lies deep inside the cliffs. So the workers dig tunnels into the rock. They follow the good layer of stone. Some tunnels go far into the hill. They cut the blocks out by lamplight. It is cool and dim in the tunnels, but full of dust. The workers cough and wipe their eyes.

The blocks are cut carefully, almost to their final shape. Then they are dragged out of the tunnels on sledges. Down to the river bank. And loaded onto the boats of men like Merer.

{img:workers-dragging-white-block-out-of-tunnel-tura-lamplight-sledge}
It is a long way, and a risky one. A big white block can weigh several tons. Loading it onto a boat is tricky. If it slips, it could sink the boat. Or crush someone's foot. So the workers move slowly and carefully. They use ramps of earth and wooden rollers. They tie the stone with thick ropes made of palm fibre.

--

Then the boats push off. They cross the wide river. In the flood season, the water spreads far over the fields. The boats can sail straight across toward the desert edge. The boatmen pole and paddle and set their sails. Merer stands at the front and watches the water. He knows where the shallow places are. And where the current is strong.

{img:boat-crossing-wide-flood-water-toward-giza-pyramid-on-horizon}
As they come closer to Giza, they can see the pyramid on its hill. Each time it is a little higher than the last time. The boatmen look up at it. Some of them have worked on it for years. And they feel proud. Their white stones will cover that great shape. And they will shine for thousands of years.

--

The Tura quarry men have a saying. The pyramid is only as beautiful as its skin. Inside, nobody will ever see the rough blocks. But the white casing will be seen by everyone, for as long as the pyramid stands. So they choose their stone with great care. Any block with a crack or a dark stain is put aside. Only the purest white goes on the boats. And sometimes the overseer rejects a block that took days to cut. The workers groan. But they know he is right.
