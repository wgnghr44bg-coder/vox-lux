The king who ordered the Great Pyramid is called Khufu. The Greeks, much later, will call him Cheops.

He comes from a family of builders. His father was a king called Sneferu. Sneferu built not one pyramid, but three. His first one partly fell down. His second one had a problem. The builders had to change the angle halfway, so it is bent. People still call it the Bent Pyramid. And his third one was a true smooth pyramid. Khufu grew up watching these huge building sites. He saw what went wrong and what went right.

--

When his father passed away, Khufu became king. He was still fairly young. And almost at once, he began planning his own pyramid. It had to be bigger and better than his father's. That was a big promise to make.

We know very little about Khufu as a person. Strangely, for the man who built the biggest pyramid, only one small statue of him has been found. It is made of ivory. It is only about seven centimetres tall, smaller than your hand. It shows him sitting on his throne, wearing a red crown. His face is calm and a little stern.

{img:sneferu-bent-pyramid-dahshur-desert-changed-angle-sunset}
Later stories, written long after he passed away, say he was a harsh king. They say he made everyone work for his pyramid and closed the temples. But those stories were written two thousand years later, by people who never knew him. In his own time, people seem to have honoured him. His name was spoken in prayers for centuries after.

--

Khufu rules for about twenty-five years. And building his pyramid takes about twenty years. That means a race against time. A king's tomb must be ready when he passes away. If he dies before it is finished, the work will have to be rushed. Or left half done. So from the very first day, everyone knows there is no time to waste.

{img:royal-court-old-kingdom-khufu-viewing-plans-officials-bowing}
Each year, the royal officials report to the king on the work. How many blocks have been laid. How high the pyramid has risen. How many workers there are. How much bread and beer they need. Khufu listens carefully. Year by year, the pyramid grows. And year by year, the king grows older.

--

Some evenings, the stories say, the king sails across the river to see his pyramid for himself. He stands at the foot of the ramps and looks up. The workers bow low. Hemiunu walks beside him and explains everything. Khufu says little. He looks at the great rising shape for a long time. Then he nods and goes back to his boat. And the next morning, the work goes on a little faster.

In the palace at Memphis, the king's day is full of meetings and ceremonies. But even a king has quiet moments. In the evening, he may sit in his garden by a pool of blue water lilies. Servants wave large fans of ostrich feathers to keep him cool. A musician plays softly on a harp. And the king looks out over the river toward Giza, where his great tomb is slowly rising against the sky.
