Khufu was laid to rest in his pyramid. The priests made offerings at his temple for many years. And slowly, the age of the great pyramids came to an end.

Later kings still built pyramids. But they were smaller and less well made. Egypt changed. It had good times and hard times. It was ruled by many kings over thousands of years. But nobody ever built anything like the Great Pyramid again.

--

About two thousand years after Khufu, a Greek traveller called Herodotus visited Egypt. He saw the pyramids and wrote about them. He was told they were built by a hundred thousand men, working in shifts of three months. And he was told the workers ate huge amounts of radishes and onions and garlic. He even wrote down how much it cost. Some of what he wrote was wrong. But he was amazed, like everyone after him.

The Greeks made a list of the seven wonders of the world. The Great Pyramid was on it. It was already very old then. All the other wonders on that list have disappeared. Only the Great Pyramid is still standing.

{img:medieval-cairo-people-taking-casing-stones-camels-pyramid-distance}
Over the centuries, robbers broke into the pyramid. They took everything they could find. Later, people took the white casing stones to build the new city of Cairo. The smooth white skin was stripped away. What was left were the rough steps we see today.

--

Then, in the last two hundred years, scientists and explorers came. They measured the pyramid. They crawled into its passages. They found the workers' marks in the small rooms. They dug up the workers' town. They found Merer's diary. They rebuilt the boat. And bit by bit, the story of how the pyramid was built came back to light.

There are still mysteries. In 2017, scientists used tiny particles from space that can pass through stone. With them, they looked inside the pyramid without breaking anything. And they found a big empty space above the Grand Gallery. Nobody knew it was there. What is it? A hidden room? A space left to lighten the weight? Nobody knows yet.

{img:scientists-instruments-inside-pyramid-passage-lights-measuring-today}
So the Great Pyramid still has secrets. After four and a half thousand years, it is still teaching us. And maybe it always will.

Over the centuries, many travellers came to see the pyramids. Greek and Roman visitors came and wrote about them in their books. Some carved their names into the stones, just to say they had been there. Later, explorers came with measuring tapes and notebooks. They climbed up the outside and crawled through the narrow passages inside. And every one of them stood at the foot of the Great Pyramid and looked up in wonder.

Today, scientists use very modern tools to study the pyramid. They use special cameras that can see through stone, using tiny particles from space. A few years ago, they found a hidden empty space inside the Great Pyramid, above the Grand Gallery. Nobody knows what it is for. Perhaps it helped the builders in some way. Perhaps it is simply a space that was left empty. They are still studying it, carefully and patiently.
