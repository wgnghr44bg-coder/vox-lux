The Great Pyramid is not just a solid pile of stone. Inside it there are passages and rooms.

The entrance is on the north side, high up the wall. From there, a narrow passage slopes down deep into the rock under the pyramid. It ends in a rough room that was never finished. Perhaps the builders changed their minds about where to put the king.

--

Another passage leads up instead. It is low and narrow. You have to bend over to walk in it. It climbs up through the stone. And then suddenly it opens into something amazing. A great hall.

It is called the Grand Gallery. It is long and very tall. Nearly fifty metres long and almost nine metres high. The walls are made of smooth limestone. They step inward as they go up, so the roof is narrow at the top. It is like walking into a tall stone canyon inside the pyramid. Even today, people who visit it stop and stare.

{img:grand-gallery-interior-tall-stepped-walls-sloping-floor-lamplight}
Why did they build it? Nobody is quite sure. Perhaps to hold big granite blocks that would later slide down and seal the passage. Perhaps to help move stones. Perhaps simply because it was grand and beautiful. A fitting road for a king.

--

From the Grand Gallery, a short low passage leads to another room. It is called the Queen's Chamber. But no queen was ever buried there. That name was given much later, by mistake. It is a quiet room with a pointed roof. Nobody knows what it was really for.

From its walls, two very narrow shafts lead into the stone. They are only about twenty centimetres wide. Too small for a person. In our own time, scientists sent a little robot up one of them. After sixty metres it found a small stone door with two copper handles. Behind it, another door. What lies beyond? Nobody knows yet.

{img:small-robot-narrow-shaft-stone-door-copper-pins-camera-light}
Some people think these shafts point toward certain stars. The Egyptians believed the king's spirit would rise to the stars in the north. Stars that never set. And so, perhaps, the shafts were roads for his spirit. Paths through the stone, pointing to the sky.

--

All these passages had to be planned before the stone was laid around them. Every slope. Every turn. Every room. Hemiunu and his builders had to think everything through from the start. There was no going back. Once the stones were in place, they could not be moved.
