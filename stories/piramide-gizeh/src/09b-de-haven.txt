At the foot of the Giza hill, there is a harbour.

The builders dug it out of the land at the edge of the desert. They also dug a canal to connect it with the river. During the flood, the water fills the harbour. Boats can sail in from the Nile and tie up there. Close to the building site.

--

The harbour is a busy place. Boats arrive loaded with stone and grain and cattle. Workers unload them. They drag the stones up onto the bank on sledges. Scribes count everything. Officials shout orders. Boatmen rest in the shade and eat and wait to go back.

Near the harbour there are store rooms and workshops. There are pens for the cattle. There are offices for the scribes. And there is an office for the man in charge of the harbour. In Khufu's time, that is Ankhhaf, the king's half-brother. Merer mentions him in his diary.

{img:ankhhaf-official-bust-old-kingdom-serious-face-harbour-office}
A famous statue of Ankhhaf has been found. It is a bust, showing his head and shoulders. It is made of limestone covered with painted plaster. His face is serious and tired. He has bags under his eyes and lines on his forehead. He looks like a man with a lot of responsibility. Like a man who has not slept well in a long time.

--

From the harbour, a long stone road leads up the hill to the pyramid. Workers drag the stones up this road on sledges. It is a slow climb. The road is steep in places. Teams of men pull together on the ropes. One man pours water in front of the sledge. Another calls out the rhythm. Pull. Pull. Pull.

{img:stone-causeway-up-hill-sledges-teams-pulling-harbour-behind}
At the end of the flood season, the water drops. The harbour starts to dry out. Mud appears at the edges. The last boats hurry to leave before they get stuck. And then the harbour is quiet until next year. The workers who came for the flood go home to their villages. They will plant their fields in the fresh black mud. And next summer, when the river rises again, many of them will come back.

--

Some nights, when the moon is full, the work at the harbour goes on after dark. Torches burn along the quay. The boats keep coming. Nobody wants to waste a single day of high water. The workers take turns to sleep for a few hours on the warm stones. Then they get up and unload the next boat. It is tiring. But the flood will not wait for anyone.
