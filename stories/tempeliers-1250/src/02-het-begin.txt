The story of the Templars began more than a hundred and thirty years before our story, in Jerusalem. In the year 1099, an army from western Europe had taken the city, after a long journey and a hard war. This was the First Crusade. The crusaders set up a new kingdom, called the Kingdom of Jerusalem. And after that, thousands of pilgrims from Europe began to travel to the Holy Land, to visit the places where Jesus had lived.

--

But the journey was dangerous. The roads from the coast to Jerusalem passed through hills and deserts, where robbers attacked travellers. Many pilgrims were robbed, or worse. So, around the year 1120, a knight from Champagne, called Hugues de Payens, had an idea. Together with a few friends, he decided to protect the pilgrims on the roads. And they would live like monks, in poverty and prayer, while they did it.

The king of Jerusalem liked the idea. He gave the knights a place to live, in a part of his palace. The palace was on the Temple Mount, the hill where the ancient Temple of Solomon had once stood. The crusaders thought that the great mosque on the hill, the al-Aqsa Mosque, was the Temple of Solomon itself. So the knights became known as the Poor Fellow-Soldiers of Christ and of the Temple of Solomon. Or simply, the Templars.

{img:hugues-de-payens-few-knights-poor-simple-clothes-sharing-horse-legend}
At first, there were only nine of them, the story says. They were so poor that, according to a legend, two knights had to share one horse. This picture, two knights riding one horse, became the symbol on the seal of the order. It showed their poverty and their brotherhood. In reality, there may have been a few more than nine. But they were certainly few, and poor.

For about ten years, the little group grew slowly. Then, in 1129, Hugues de Payens travelled back to Europe, to ask for support. At a great meeting of churchmen in the city of Troyes, in Champagne, the Templars were officially recognised by the Church. They were given a rule of life, a list of rules for how they should live, pray, eat and dress. From then on, they were a real religious order.

--

{img:bernard-of-clairvaux-monk-writing-abbey-cell-window-quill}
They had a powerful friend. His name was Bernard of Clairvaux. He was a monk, the most famous churchman of his time, and the nephew of one of the first Templars. Bernard wrote a letter in praise of the new knighthood. He wrote that the Templars were a new kind of knight, gentle as lambs in their own house, and brave as lions on the road. Because of Bernard, the Templars became famous all over Europe.

After that, gifts began to pour in. Kings, nobles and ordinary people gave the Templars land, money, horses, farms, houses and mills. They believed that helping the Templars was a way to help the Holy Land, and to earn a blessing for their souls. Young men from noble families came to join the order. Within twenty years, the Templars had houses all over Europe, from Portugal to England, and from Spain to Hungary.

{img:templar-houses-across-europe-map-feeling-farms-chapels-countryside}
The Templars used the money from their lands in Europe to support their brothers in the Holy Land. They bought horses, weapons and food. They built castles. They paid for ships. Every year, a part of the income of every Templar house in Europe was sent to the east. So for every Templar knight in the Holy Land, there were many brothers in Europe, working on farms and in houses, to support him.

--

By 1250, the Templars have existed for more than a hundred and twenty years. They are rich and famous. They own thousands of houses and farms. But the Holy Land has changed. Jerusalem has been lost again, six years ago. The crusaders now hold only a strip of land along the coast, with the great port of Acre as their capital. And the Templars have moved their headquarters there, to a fortress by the sea.
