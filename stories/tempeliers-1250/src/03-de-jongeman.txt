Guillaume was born in a small manor house in Champagne, not far from the city of Troyes. His father is a knight, but not a rich one. He owns a little land, a few farms, a mill and a small stone house with a tower. The family is proud of its name. But there is not much money. And Guillaume is the third son.

--

In those days, the oldest son usually inherited the family land. The younger sons had to find their own way in the world. Some became priests or monks. Some went to serve a great lord, as knights. Some went on crusade. And some joined the military orders, like the Templars or the Hospitallers. For a younger son of a poor knight, the Templars offered a respected life, a purpose, and a place in a famous brotherhood.

Guillaume has grown up learning the skills of a knight. Since he was seven, he has learned to ride a horse, to use a sword and a lance, and to look after weapons and armour. He served as a page and then as a squire in the house of a richer lord, where he also learned good manners, how to serve at table and how to behave at court. He can ride better than almost anyone his age. And he loves horses more than anything.

{img:squire-training-lance-quintain-tilting-yard-young-man-horse-1250}
But Guillaume is also a thoughtful young man. He goes to church every day, and he likes the quiet of the chapel. He has listened to the stories of the old knights, about the Holy Land, about Jerusalem and the Holy Sepulchre, the church built over the place where Jesus was buried. He has heard about the Templars, the knights who are also monks. And slowly, a wish has grown in him, to join them.

His family has a connection with the Templars. His great-uncle was a Templar, many years ago, and gave the order a small piece of land. The Templar house nearby, called a commandery, stands on that land. Guillaume has visited it many times as a boy. He has seen the brothers praying in their chapel, and working in their fields, and riding out on their horses. He has always admired them.

--

{img:father-and-son-talking-by-fireplace-stone-hall-evening-1250}
When Guillaume tells his father that he wants to become a Templar, his father is quiet for a long time. Then he says that it is a good choice, and an honourable one. But he also says that it is a choice for life. Once he has made his vows, Guillaume can never leave the order. He will own nothing. He will not marry. He will obey every order of his superiors. And he may be sent anywhere in the world, even to the Holy Land, and never come back.

Guillaume's mother cries. She does not want her son to go far away. But she also knows that he has thought about it carefully. She sews him a new linen shirt, and gives him a little wooden cross that belonged to her own mother. His sister, who is twelve, makes him promise to write. His older brothers slap him on the back and wish him good luck. They are a little jealous, perhaps.

{img:young-man-riding-to-commandery-gate-spring-morning-fields-chapel-bell}
One spring morning, Guillaume rides to the Templar commandery, with his father. He is wearing his best clothes. He carries nothing with him, because he knows that a Templar must give up all his possessions. At the gate, they are met by a sergeant, who takes their horses. And they are led into the courtyard, where the commander of the house is waiting.

--

The commander is called Brother Hugues. He is about fifty years old, with a long grey beard, a calm face and kind eyes. He has served in the Holy Land for many years, and has come back to France to run this house. He greets Guillaume's father as an old friend. Then he looks at Guillaume for a long time. He asks him why he wants to join. Guillaume answers as honestly as he can. Brother Hugues nods slowly. Then he says that the brothers will meet in the chapel tonight, to decide.
