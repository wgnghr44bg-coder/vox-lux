The Templars have their own ships. They need them to carry brothers, pilgrims, horses, weapons, food and money across the sea to the Holy Land. Every spring and every autumn, a fleet of ships sails from the ports of France, Italy and Spain to the east. The journey is called a passage. And Guillaume will sail on the autumn passage.

--

The ship is big and round, with two masts and large sails, and high wooden castles at the front and the back. Below the deck, there are special places for horses, with slings to hold them up when the sea is rough. Loading the horses is difficult. They are led down a wooden ramp into the hold, through a door in the side of the ship, which is then sealed shut. Ferrant does not like it. Guillaume talks to him softly all the way, until the big horse calms down.

On board, there are many people. Templar brothers, knights and sergeants, going to the Holy Land. Pilgrims, rich and poor, going to visit the holy places. Merchants with their goods. Sailors. There are men and women, old and young. Some are excited. Some are afraid. Many of them have never been to sea. They sleep on deck, or in small, crowded spaces below, on straw mattresses.

{img:ship-sailing-open-mediterranean-blue-sea-sails-full-sunny-day}
The ship sails out of Marseille on a calm morning. The wind fills the sails. The city gets smaller and smaller, and then disappears. And soon there is nothing around them but the blue sea and the blue sky. The voyage to Acre takes about five or six weeks, depending on the wind. They sail along the coast of Italy, past Sicily, past the Greek islands, to Cyprus, and then to the coast of the Holy Land.

The first days are hard. Many people are seasick, including Guillaume and Thibaut. They lie on the deck, pale and miserable, while the ship rolls up and down. But after a few days, they get used to it. And then the voyage becomes quite pleasant. The weather is warm. The sea is blue. Dolphins swim beside the ship. And at night, the stars are brighter than Guillaume has ever seen them.

--

{img:pilgrims-praying-on-ship-deck-evening-sunset-priest-sailors}
On board, the Templars keep their Rule, as best they can. They say their prayers at the right hours, standing on the deck. They eat their meals together. The pilgrims join them for Mass on Sundays, which a priest says on deck. In the evenings, the pilgrims sing hymns, and the sailors sing their own songs, and sometimes they all sing together, under the stars.

Guillaume makes friends with an old pilgrim from Flanders, a merchant called Jan. He is going to the Holy Land for the third time, to pray for his wife, who passed away last year. He tells Guillaume about Acre, about the markets and the churches, and about the people of the east. He teaches him a few words of Arabic. Peace be with you. Thank you. How much does it cost. Guillaume repeats them, until he can say them properly.

{img:storm-at-sea-sailors-lowering-sails-ship-rolling-grey-waves-distance}
Halfway across, near Crete, there is a storm. The wind howls and the waves are huge. The ship rolls so much that people are thrown from one side to the other. Guillaume goes down into the hold, to calm Ferrant, who is kicking and neighing. He stays with him all night, holding his head, talking to him, while the ship groans and creaks around them. In the morning, the storm passes. Everyone is safe. Ferrant is tired, but unhurt.

--

They stop at Cyprus for a few days, to take on fresh water and food. Cyprus is a beautiful island, with mountains and orange groves, ruled by a Christian king. The Templars once owned the whole island, for a short time. Then they sail on, toward the east. And one morning, at dawn, a sailor at the top of the mast shouts out. Land! There, low on the horizon, is a pale line of coast. The Holy Land. Guillaume falls to his knees on the deck, and prays.

In the evenings on the ship, the sea is calm and the sky is full of colour. The sailors sit together on the deck, mending ropes and telling stories. The pilgrims pray quietly. Guillaume sits at the rail, looking out over the darkening water, with the warm wind on his face. Sometimes, he goes down into the hold to visit Ferrant, and stands with him for a while in the creaking darkness.
