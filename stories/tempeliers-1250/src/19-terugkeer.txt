Guillaume sails back to France in the spring, on a Templar ship, from Acre to Marseille. He stands at the rail as the ship leaves the harbour, and watches the city get smaller. The towers of the Temple, with their golden lions, shining in the morning sun. The walls, the churches, the hills of Galilee behind. He has lived here for fifteen years. Half of his life as a grown man. He does not know if he will ever see it again.

--

Thibaut stands beside him. He is staying in the Holy Land, at Château Pèlerin, with his horses. He has come to the harbour to say goodbye. The two men embrace on the quay, for a long time. Thibaut says that he will look after Ferrant. Guillaume says that he knows. Then Thibaut stands on the quay and waves, until the ship is far out at sea, and he is only a little dot among the crowd.

The voyage home is calm. The sea is blue. The wind is kind. Guillaume says his prayers on deck, and talks with the other passengers. Pilgrims going home, merchants, and a few other brothers returning to Europe. He thinks about the past fifteen years. About everything he has seen and learned. And about the little commandery in Champagne, which he left as a young man, and to which he now returns as its commander.

{img:knight-riding-north-through-france-spring-fields-poppies-vineyards}
From Marseille, he rides north, through the whole of France, along the same roads he took fifteen years before. Past the olive trees and the cypresses of the south. Along the river Rhône. Through the vineyards of Burgundy. Everything looks the same, and yet different. Or perhaps it is he who has changed. He sees things he never noticed before. He notices how green everything is, after the dry hills of the Holy Land.

At last, one evening in late spring, he rides over a low hill, and sees the commandery below him, in the valley. The little chapel with its bell tower. The great barn. The dovecote. The fields of young wheat, green and waving. The mill on the river. It looks exactly the same as when he left. He stops his horse on the hill, and looks at it for a long time, with tears in his eyes.

--

{img:old-commander-greeting-returning-knight-gate-embrace-evening-light}
At the gate, old Brother Hugues is waiting for him. He is very old now, white-haired and bent, leaning on a stick. But his eyes are still kind and clear. Guillaume gets off his horse and kneels before him. Brother Hugues raises him up, and embraces him. Welcome home, my brother, he says. Welcome home.

The brothers of the commandery have gathered in the courtyard to greet him. Some of them he knows from before. Some are new, young men who have joined since he left. They look at him with curiosity and respect. A knight who has served fifteen years in the Holy Land. Who has seen Acre and Château Pèlerin, and Nazareth and the Sea of Galilee. To them, he is almost a legend.

{img:returning-templar-in-chapel-kneeling-familiar-candles-evening-vespers}
That evening, Guillaume goes to the little chapel for Vespers. He kneels in the same place where he knelt as a young man, on the night he was received into the order. The same painted walls. The same candles. The same smell of incense and old stone. The brothers sing the same psalms. And Guillaume closes his eyes, and listens, and feels that he has come home.

--

After Compline, he walks out into the courtyard. The stars are coming out. The air smells of cut grass and wood smoke. The pigeons are cooing softly in the dovecote. An owl is calling from the woods, just as it did on his first night here, so many years ago. And Guillaume stands in the dark courtyard, under the stars, and thinks of Elias on his rooftop in Acre, naming the stars in Arabic. They are the same stars, here and there. That thought comforts him.
