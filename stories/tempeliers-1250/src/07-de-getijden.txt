The day of a Templar is divided by prayer. Like other monks, the Templars pray at set times, day and night. These times are called the hours. They are announced by the bell of the chapel. When the bell rings, every brother must stop what he is doing, and go to pray. Even in the middle of the night.

--

The first prayer of the day is called Matins. It is said in the middle of the night, around two or three o'clock. The bell rings, and the brothers get up from their beds in the dark, put on their shoes and mantles, and go to the chapel. They sing psalms and say prayers, for about an hour. Then they go to check on their horses, in the stables. And then they may go back to bed, until dawn.

At dawn, there is the prayer called Prime. Then the brothers hear Mass. Then they go to work. At about nine o'clock, there is a short prayer called Terce. At midday, there is Sext. In the afternoon, there is None. In the late afternoon, there is Vespers. And at the end of the day, before going to bed, there is Compline. Seven times a day, and once at night, the brothers pray.

{img:templar-saying-paternosters-stables-horse-quiet-prayer-morning-light}
But many of the Templars cannot read. They cannot follow the long Latin prayers in the books. So the Rule allows them to say a simpler prayer instead. They say the Lord's Prayer, the Paternoster, a certain number of times for each hour. Thirteen times for Matins. Seven times for each of the other hours. Nine times for Vespers. They can say them anywhere. In the chapel, in the stables, or on the road.

Guillaume can read a little. But he prefers to say the Paternosters, like most of the brothers. He counts them on his fingers. Sometimes he loses count, especially at Matins, when he is still half asleep. Then he starts again, just to be sure. Brother Raoul says that God does not mind if you lose count, as long as you mean the words.

--

{img:templar-brothers-working-fields-afternoon-wheat-horses-cart-sunlight}
Between the prayers, there is work. In the morning, the brothers look after their horses, their weapons and their equipment. They train with the sword and the lance, in a field behind the commandery. Then they work on the farm, or in the house, according to their job. Some check the fields and the workers. Some go to the market, to buy and sell. Some keep the accounts. Some travel to other commanderies with messages.

In the afternoon, there is more work, and more training. The knights practise riding together in formation, because in battle, Templars always ride together, in close order, like a wall. They must never break the formation, without permission. Guillaume is very good at this. He has always been a fine rider. The other brothers soon notice it.

{img:templar-chapel-vespers-evening-golden-light-through-window-brothers}
In the evening, after Vespers, the brothers eat their supper. Then there is Compline, the last prayer of the day. After Compline, there is silence. The brothers must not speak, except for something urgent. They go to check on their horses one last time. Then they go to the dormitory, and lie down on their beds, in their shirts and breeches. The lamp is left burning. And they sleep, until the bell rings for Matins.

--

At first, Guillaume finds it hard to get up in the middle of the night. He is always tired. But after a few weeks, his body gets used to it. And he begins to love Matins most of all. The chapel is dark, lit only by a few candles. Outside, the world is silent. The brothers sing softly together, their voices rising up into the dark roof. It feels as if they are the only people awake in the whole world, keeping watch over everyone else, while they sleep.

On feast days, there is more singing, and the chapel is decorated with flowers and candles. The chaplain wears his finest robes. Sometimes, people from the village come to Mass in the little chapel too, standing at the back in their Sunday clothes. And afterwards, the brothers have a better meal than usual, and a little extra wine.
