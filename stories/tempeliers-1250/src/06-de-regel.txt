The life of a Templar is governed by the Rule. It is a long book of rules, which tells the brothers how to live, every hour of the day. It was first written in Latin in 1129, at the council of Troyes. Later, it was translated into French, so that the brothers, who mostly could not read Latin, could understand it. And many new rules were added over the years. By 1250, it has hundreds of articles.

--

The Rule tells the brothers what to wear. Their clothes must be simple and plain, without decoration. The knights wear white mantles, as a sign of purity. The sergeants wear black or brown. All brothers wear the red cross on their mantle, on the left shoulder, over the heart. Their clothes must not be too long or too short. Their shoes must have no points or laces. And they may not wear fur, except lambskin or sheepskin in winter.

The Rule tells them how to look. Their hair must be cut short. But they must not shave their beards. So all Templars have beards. The Rule says they must not be proud of their appearance, or look at themselves too much. They must not decorate their horses or their weapons with gold or silver. Everything must be simple. Their beauty, the Rule says, should be inside them, not outside.

{img:templar-brothers-bearded-short-hair-plain-mantles-walking-cloister}
The Rule also tells them how to behave. They must not speak too much, or laugh loudly. They must not gossip or boast. They must not go anywhere without permission. They must not write or receive letters without the commander reading them. They must not gamble, or hunt with hawks, or go to parties. The only animal they may hunt is the lion. Since there are not many lions in Champagne, this is not a problem.

And the Rule tells them how to treat each other. They must be gentle and patient with each other, like brothers. They must look after the sick and the old. They must share everything. If a brother is in trouble, the others must help him. If a brother does something wrong, he must confess it in front of all the brothers, at the weekly meeting called the chapter. And then he receives a punishment.

--

{img:chapter-meeting-brothers-seated-benches-commander-speaking-chapel}
The punishments are sometimes strict. For small faults, a brother might have to eat his meals sitting on the floor, for a few days. Or eat only bread and water. For bigger faults, he might lose his mantle for a time, and have to work with the servants. For the most serious faults, like leaving the order or running away in battle, he might be expelled forever. But most punishments are mild. And most brothers try very hard to follow the Rule.

Each week, on Sunday, the brothers hold their chapter meeting. They sit on benches around the walls of the chapel. The commander speaks to them about the Rule and about the business of the house. Then brothers who have broken a rule stand up, kneel, and confess. The others listen. The commander decides the punishment. It is a strange custom. But it keeps the brothers honest, and humble.

{img:templar-brother-reading-aloud-to-others-refectory-meal-silence}
Guillaume finds the Rule hard at first. He likes to talk, and to laugh, and to ride where he likes. Now he must ask permission for everything. He must be silent at meals. He cannot even give a gift to his own sister without asking. But slowly, he gets used to it. And he begins to find something peaceful in it. He does not have to decide things for himself any more. He only has to follow the Rule, and do his best.

--

One old knight in the commandery, called Brother Raoul, has been a Templar for forty years. He has a long white beard and bright blue eyes. He served in the Holy Land for twenty years, and has many scars. He tells Guillaume that the Rule is like a good saddle. At first, it is stiff and uncomfortable. But after a while, it fits you perfectly, and you cannot ride without it. Guillaume laughs. But later, he finds that the old man was right.
