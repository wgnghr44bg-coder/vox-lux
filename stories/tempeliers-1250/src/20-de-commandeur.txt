Guillaume becomes the commander of the house. Brother Hugues stays with him, as an adviser, for two more years, until he passes away peacefully, one winter night. He is buried in the chapel, next to old Brother Raoul. Guillaume holds a long vigil for him. And then he takes up the work that the old man taught him.

--

Being a commander is a big responsibility. Guillaume must run the whole estate. The fields, the vineyards, the mill and the farm. He must manage the workers and the servants, and deal with the peasants who rent the land. He must keep the accounts, and send money to the Holy Land every year. He must welcome guests and travellers, and give food to the poor at the gate. And he must look after the brothers, and make sure they follow the Rule.

Guillaume is a good commander. He is fair and patient. He listens before he decides. He uses what he learned in the east. He plants new crops in the garden, from seeds he brought back. He grows a few lemon trees in pots, which must be carried indoors in winter. They never give much fruit, in the cold of Champagne. But the brothers like to smell the flowers in spring.

{img:commander-teaching-young-squires-horses-meadow-commandery-afternoon}
He takes a special interest in the young brothers and squires. He teaches them to ride, and to look after their horses, as Brother Raoul once taught him. He tells them stories about the Holy Land. About Acre and its markets, about the castle by the sea, about the pilgrims and the stars. He tells them that the people of the east are people like them. And he tells them about a big grey horse called Ferrant, who loved oranges and the sea.

He also takes care of the poor. Every day, after the meals, he makes sure that the bread and the food are given out at the gate. He knows the poor people of the village by name. When the harvest is bad, he opens the granaries, and gives grain to the families who need it. The people of the village come to love the commander, with his grey beard and his brown face, who speaks a little Arabic and grows lemon trees in Champagne.

--

{img:commander-giving-grain-to-villagers-barn-door-bad-harvest-winter}
News comes from the Holy Land, from time to time, carried by brothers returning to Europe. It is not always good news. The Christians are losing more castles and towns. The great castle of Safed has fallen. Jaffa and Antioch have fallen. Acre is still standing, and Château Pèlerin. But the Holy Land of the crusaders is getting smaller and smaller. Guillaume prays for his brothers there every day.

A letter comes, one autumn, from Thibaut. It is written by a clerk, because Thibaut never learned to write well. It says that Ferrant has passed away, peacefully, in his meadow by the sea, at a great age. Thibaut buried him on the hillside, under an olive tree, where he can see the waves. Guillaume reads the letter alone in his room. Then he goes to the stables, and stands for a long time with his hand on the neck of a young grey horse, who reminds him a little of the old one.

{img:old-commander-walking-commandery-garden-lemon-trees-pots-evening}
Guillaume grows old in the commandery. His beard turns white. His back grows stiff. He walks with a stick, like Brother Hugues once did. But he still gets up for Matins every night. He still checks the horses. He still welcomes the poor at the gate. And on summer evenings, he still sits in the meadow behind the commandery, watching the horses graze, and the swallows dive over the grass, as the sun goes down.

--

He passes away peacefully, in his bed, at a great age, with his brothers around him, praying. He is buried in the little chapel, next to Brother Hugues and Brother Raoul. On his grave, there is a simple stone, with his name, and a red cross. And in his hand, the brothers place a small lead badge from Nazareth, and a little piece of embroidered cloth, made long ago by his sister.
