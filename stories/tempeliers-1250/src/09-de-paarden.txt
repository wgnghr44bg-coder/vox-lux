For a Templar knight, horses are the most important thing of all. A knight without a horse is not a knight. So the Templars take great care of their horses. Every knight has three or four horses. And they are looked after with as much care as the brothers themselves.

--

Each knight has a big, strong war horse, called a destrier. It is trained for battle, to stay calm in noise and crowds, and to carry a knight in armour. It is the most expensive horse of all. A good destrier can cost as much as a small farm. Each knight also has one or two riding horses, for travelling, which are lighter and more comfortable. And there is a pack horse or a mule, for carrying his equipment.

Each knight also has a squire. The squire is a young man who helps the knight. He looks after the horses, cleans the armour and the weapons, carries the equipment, and helps the knight to dress. Some squires are young men who hope to become Templars themselves one day. Others are servants, paid for their work. Guillaume's squire is a cheerful boy of sixteen called Thibaut, from a village nearby.

{img:knight-and-squire-checking-horse-hooves-stable-yard-friendly}
The Rule says a lot about horses. The brothers must check on their horses every day, and several times. After Matins in the night, they go to the stables. After Prime in the morning, they go again. They make sure the horses have enough food and water, that they are healthy, and that their saddles and bridles are in good order. If a horse is ill, they must tell the brother in charge of the horses at once.

The horses are fed well. Barley, oats and hay, and sometimes beans. The Rule says exactly how much each horse should receive. The brothers may not give their own horse more than the others, without permission. They may not race their horses, or ride them too fast without reason. They may not lend them to anyone. And they may not let them be damaged, through carelessness.

--

{img:destrier-war-horse-white-caparison-red-cross-standing-calm-meadow}
Guillaume's war horse is a big grey stallion, called Ferrant, which means iron grey. He is strong, calm and very intelligent. When Guillaume first meets him, the horse looks at him for a long time, and then pushes his nose into Guillaume's chest. From that moment, they are friends. Every morning, Guillaume brushes him, talks to him, and gives him a piece of bread from his own breakfast, when nobody is looking.

The Templars also keep the armour and weapons of the knights. Each knight has a coat of mail, made of thousands of little iron rings. A helmet. A shield, painted with the colours of the order, black and white. A sword, a lance, a dagger and a mace. Thibaut keeps them all clean and oiled, in a wooden chest in the armoury. In battle, the knights also wear a white surcoat over the mail, with the red cross on the front.

{img:templar-banner-black-white-beauseant-flag-wind-blue-sky}
The banner of the Templars is a simple flag, divided into two parts, black and white. It is called the Beauséant. In battle, it is carried by a special brother, and it must always stay raised. As long as the banner is flying, the brothers must stay and gather around it. It is the heart of the Templars in any battle. But we will not follow them into battle tonight.

--

On quiet summer evenings, after Vespers, Guillaume sometimes takes Ferrant out to the meadow behind the commandery, to let him graze. He sits on a stone in the grass, and watches the big horse eat, slowly, with his tail swishing at the flies. The sun goes down over the fields. The swallows dive over the meadow. And Guillaume thinks that a horse is perhaps the best friend a man can have. Ferrant lifts his head, looks at him, and seems to agree.

In winter, the horses are given extra hay and warm blankets. The stables are cosy, full of the smell of straw and the soft sounds of horses chewing. On the coldest nights, Guillaume goes to the stables after Matins, and stands for a while beside Ferrant, warming his hands on the horse's neck. The big horse breathes out slowly, in clouds of warm steam, and leans gently against him.
