That evening, the brothers of the commandery gather in the chapel. There are about twelve of them. Some knights, some sergeants, and a chaplain, a priest who is also a member of the order. They stand in a circle, in the candlelight. Brother Hugues asks them if anyone knows a reason why Guillaume should not be received. Nobody does. So he sends two brothers to fetch the young man.

--

Guillaume is waiting in a small room near the chapel. The two brothers come to him. They speak to him seriously. They tell him that he must think carefully about what he is asking. They say that he sees the Templars in their fine mantles, riding fine horses, eating good food. But he does not see the hard rules inside. When he wants to sleep, he will be woken to pray. When he wants to eat, he may be sent to work. When he wants to be in one place, he will be sent to another.

They ask him a series of questions. Is he a knight, the son of a knight, and born of a lawful marriage? Is he married or engaged? Has he made vows to another religious order? Does he owe money that he cannot pay? Is he healthy? Is he anyone's servant? Guillaume answers each question truthfully. Then the brothers go back to the chapel and report his answers.

{img:young-man-kneeling-before-commander-chapel-candlelight-reception-vows}
At last, Guillaume is brought into the chapel. He kneels in front of Brother Hugues, with his hands together. And he says the words he has prepared. Sire, I have come before God and before you and before the brothers, and I ask you, for God and Our Lady, to welcome me into your company. Brother Hugues asks him again if he is sure. He tells him once more about the hardships. Guillaume says yes. He is sure.

Then Guillaume makes his vows. He promises, before God and the Virgin Mary, to obey the master of the Temple and his superiors, all the days of his life. He promises to live in chastity, without a wife. He promises to own nothing of his own. He promises to follow the customs of the house. And he promises to help to defend the Holy Land, and never to leave the order, unless he is given permission.

--

{img:white-mantle-red-cross-placed-on-shoulders-kneeling-man-chapel-brothers}
Then Brother Hugues takes a white mantle, with a red cross on the shoulder, and places it around Guillaume's shoulders. He fastens the cord at his neck. The chaplain says a prayer and a psalm. The brothers sing. Brother Hugues raises Guillaume to his feet, and gives him the kiss of peace. And then each of the brothers comes forward, and welcomes him as a brother.

The white mantle is a sign of purity. Only knights may wear it. It is the most precious thing that Guillaume will now have. And yet, it is not really his. Nothing belongs to him any more. Everything he uses, his clothes, his bed, his horse, his sword, belongs to the order. He is only allowed to use them. If he loses or damages them, he will be punished.

{img:new-templar-brother-sitting-with-commander-learning-rule-book-candle}
After the ceremony, Brother Hugues sits down with Guillaume and explains the most important rules. How to behave in chapel and at table. How to ask permission for things. What punishments there are for breaking the rules. Which bells he must listen for, day and night. It is a lot to remember. Guillaume listens carefully. He has many questions. But a good Templar does not ask too many questions. So he just nods.

--

That night, Guillaume sleeps for the first time in the dormitory of the commandery, with the other brothers. He lies on a simple bed, with a straw mattress, a sheet and a blanket. A lamp burns all night in the dormitory, as the rule demands. He does not sleep much. He lies awake, listening to the breathing of the other brothers, and thinking about his new life. Outside, an owl is calling. And far away, a dog is barking. He feels very small, and very happy, and a little afraid.
