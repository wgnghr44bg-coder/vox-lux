Tonight we travel back almost eight hundred years, to the year 1250. We go to the countryside of Champagne, in the east of France. It is a land of gentle hills, wheat fields, vineyards and small rivers. And on the edge of a quiet village, there stands a group of stone buildings around a courtyard, with a little chapel in the middle. It is a house of the Knights Templar.

--

It is very early in the morning, long before sunrise. The sky is still dark, with a few stars. The fields are silent. And then, from the little chapel, a bell begins to ring. Softly at first, and then more clearly, across the sleeping countryside. In the dormitory beside the chapel, a group of men are getting up from their narrow beds, in the darkness. They do not speak. They put on their mantles, and walk quietly to the chapel, to pray.

Some of them wear white mantles, with a red cross on the shoulder. They are the knights. Others wear black or brown mantles, also with a red cross. They are the sergeants, the brothers who are not knights. They stand together in the dim chapel, lit by a few candles. And they begin to sing, softly, in Latin, the first prayers of the day. Outside, the first birds are waking up.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

{img:templar-knight-white-mantle-red-cross-standing-chapel-door-candlelight}
The Knights Templar were one of the most famous groups of the Middle Ages. They were monks, who had promised to live in poverty, obedience and chastity, like other monks. But they were also knights, who carried swords and rode horses. They were founded to protect pilgrims on their way to Jerusalem. And over time, they became one of the richest and most powerful organisations in Europe.

Today, there are many legends about them. Stories about secret treasures, hidden knowledge and mysterious rituals. Most of these stories were invented much later. Tonight, we will try to see the real Templars. Not as legends, but as people. Men who prayed, worked, farmed, kept accounts, looked after horses, and travelled far across the sea.

{img:young-man-1250-champagne-fields-looking-at-commandery-walking-road}
We will follow a young man from Champagne, called Guillaume. He is nineteen years old, the son of a minor knight. He joins the Templars, and we will see how he is received into the order. We will learn the rules of their life, what they ate, how they prayed and how they slept. We will visit the farms and the mills that the Templars owned, and see how they became the bankers of kings.

--

Then we will travel with Guillaume across the sea, to the Holy Land. We will see the great city of Acre, with its crowded markets and its many peoples. We will visit the Templars' fortress by the sea, and their mighty castle on the coast. And we will meet the people who lived there, Christians, Muslims and Jews. There were wars in those days, and we will look at them only from a distance.

{img:acre-1250-harbour-ships-city-walls-towers-mediterranean-sunlight}
So let us begin. Not yet in the Holy Land, but in the quiet countryside of France, on a spring morning in 1250. The bell of the little chapel is ringing. The brothers are singing their prayers. And at the gate of the house, a young man is standing, waiting, his heart beating fast. He has come to ask if he may become a Templar.
