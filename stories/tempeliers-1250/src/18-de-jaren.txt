The years pass. Guillaume serves in the Holy Land for more than fifteen years. He lives in Acre, at Château Pèlerin, and at other Templar castles. He rides on patrols and escorts. He guards walls and gates. He sits on the council of the house. And slowly, he becomes an experienced, respected brother of the order.

--

There are difficult years. Wars break out, and then truces are made, and then wars break out again. Some of Guillaume's brothers are lost. The Christians lose some of their castles and towns. Each time, the brothers pray for those who have passed away, and carry on. We will not follow the battles. It is enough to say that Guillaume was brave, and fair, and that he came through them.

Thibaut, his squire, becomes a sergeant brother of the order, with a brown mantle and a red cross. He is very proud. He becomes the brother in charge of the horses at Château Pèlerin, because he knows more about horses than anyone. Guillaume and Thibaut remain close friends, all their lives. They are like brothers, which, of course, they are.

{img:old-grey-war-horse-retired-grazing-meadow-castle-walls-evening-light}
Ferrant, the big grey horse, grows old. He carries Guillaume for many years, faithfully. But at last, he is too old to work. Guillaume asks permission for him to be retired, and to spend his last years in a meadow near Château Pèlerin, by the sea. Permission is given. The old horse grazes there in the sun, and walks in the shallow waves in the mornings. When Guillaume visits, Ferrant still lifts his head and trots over to him, and pushes his nose into his chest, as he did on the first day.

Guillaume learns many things in these years. He learns Arabic well enough to talk with the local people and with merchants. He learns to read and write better, and helps the brothers with their accounts and letters. He learns to judge people, and to listen before he speaks. And he learns patience, which, Brother Raoul told him long ago, is the most important virtue of a Templar.

--

{img:templar-letter-from-france-sealed-reading-by-window-sea-light}
Sometimes letters come from France. Letters from the commandery, from Brother Hugues, telling him the news. The harvest was good. The mill has been repaired. Old Brother Raoul passed away peacefully, in his sleep, at a great age, and was buried in the little chapel. Guillaume reads that letter many times. He prays for the old man, who taught him so much. And he remembers his words about the Rule being like a good saddle.

Letters also come from his family. His father has passed away. His older brother now runs the family lands. His sister has married, and has three children. His mother is old, but still well, and still prays for him every day. Guillaume writes back whenever he can, with the permission of his commander. He tells them about the Holy Land, about oranges and sugar, the castle by the sea, and the stars.

{img:templar-knight-praying-alone-chapel-acre-candle-quiet-evening}
In the evenings, in the chapel of the Temple of Acre, Guillaume often stays a little longer after Compline, when the other brothers have gone to bed. He kneels in the dim light of a single candle, and prays. For his family. For the brothers who have passed away. For the people of the Holy Land, of every faith. For peace. And for Ferrant, in his meadow by the sea.

--

And then, one day, after fifteen years in the east, a letter comes from the Grand Master. Guillaume is to return to France. Brother Hugues, the commander of his old commandery in Champagne, has grown too old to run the house. And the order has chosen Guillaume to take his place. He is to be the new commander. Guillaume reads the letter twice. Then he goes out to the meadow by the sea, to say goodbye to an old grey horse.
