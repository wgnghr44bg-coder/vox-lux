So we leave the Templars tonight, in the year 1250, as the evening falls. In the countryside of Champagne, the sun is setting over the fields. The wheat is golden. The vineyards are green on the hills. And the little chapel of the commandery is glowing in the last warm light. The bell is ringing for Vespers, softly, across the quiet fields.

--

In the courtyard, the brothers are walking slowly to the chapel, in their white and brown mantles. The workers are coming back from the fields, with their tools on their shoulders. The pigeons are flying home to the dovecote. In the stables, the horses are munching their hay. And at the gate, an old man and a little boy are waiting for their bread, sitting on the grass in the evening sun.

{img:templar-brothers-singing-vespers-chapel-candles-golden-evening-light}
In the chapel, the candles are lit. The brothers stand in their places, and begin to sing. Their voices rise softly into the dark roof, old and young together. The same psalms that monks have sung for hundreds of years. Among them is a young man of nineteen, in a new white mantle, singing the words for the first time, a little unsure, a little out of tune, but with all his heart.

--

Far away, across the sea, the same evening is falling over Acre. The sun is setting over the water, turning the walls of the Temple golden. The lions on the tower are shining. The ships in the harbour are rocking gently. In the markets, the merchants are packing up their spices and their silks. And in the chapel of the Temple, other brothers are singing the same Vespers, in the same words, at the same hour.

{img:moon-rising-over-acre-sea-templar-tower-silver-reflections-calm}
The night comes softly over both places. Over the green fields of Champagne, and the dry hills of Galilee. Over the commandery with its dovecote, and the castle by the sea. The stars come out, one by one. The same stars, over France and over the Holy Land. Aldebaran, Betelgeuse and Vega. The follower, the hand of the giant, and the falling eagle.

--

In the dormitory of the commandery, the lamp is burning softly, as the Rule demands. The brothers are asleep on their narrow beds. Outside, an owl is calling from the woods. A horse shifts in its stall. The bell is silent now, until Matins. And the young man in the new white mantle lies awake for a little while, listening to the night, before he too falls asleep, under the quiet stars.
