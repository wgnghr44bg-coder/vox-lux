The Templar house where Guillaume now lives is called a commandery. There are hundreds of commanderies all over Europe. Most of them are not castles. They are more like big farms, with a chapel. They are run by a commander, with a small group of brothers, and many workers and servants. Their main job is to make money and supplies for the order, to send to the Holy Land.

--

Guillaume's commandery stands around a big square courtyard. On one side, there is the chapel. It is a simple building of pale stone, with a round window above the door, and a little bell tower. Inside, it is plain and quiet, with painted walls showing the life of Christ, and Templar knights on horseback. The brothers spend many hours here every day.

Next to the chapel, there is the main house. On the ground floor, there is a hall, where the brothers eat their meals, and a kitchen. Upstairs, there is the dormitory, where they sleep. There is also a small room for the commander, where he keeps the accounts and the important documents in a wooden chest. And there is a room for guests, because the Templars often give shelter to travellers.

{img:huge-medieval-barn-timber-roof-grain-stored-carts-templar-farm}
On the other sides of the courtyard, there are the farm buildings. There is a huge barn, with a high wooden roof, for storing grain and hay. Some Templar barns were so well built that they still stand today, almost eight hundred years later. There are stables for the horses and the oxen, a cowshed, a sheepfold and a pigsty. There is a bakery, a brewhouse, a blacksmith's forge and a workshop for repairing tools.

In one corner of the courtyard, there is a round stone tower, with hundreds of little holes in its walls. It is a dovecote, where pigeons nest. Only nobles and religious houses are allowed to keep a dovecote. The pigeons give meat and eggs. And their droppings are very good fertiliser for the fields. There is also a well, in the middle of the courtyard, and a fishpond behind the barn, full of carp.

--

{img:templar-commandery-fields-workers-harvest-vineyards-mill-river-landscape}
Around the commandery, there are fields, meadows, woods and vineyards. The Templars own a lot of land here, given to them by local families over many years. Some of the land is farmed by the commandery itself, with hired workers. Other land is rented out to peasants, who pay the Templars rent, in money or in grain, chickens and wine. The Templars also own a watermill on the river, where the peasants must bring their grain to be ground.

Only a few of the people at the commandery are actual Templar brothers. Most are workers. Ploughmen, shepherds, cowherds, millers, bakers, cooks and servants. Some are local villagers, who come to work each day. Some live at the commandery, as servants. And there are also people called confratres, friends of the order, who are not brothers, but who have given gifts and receive the prayers of the brothers in return.

{img:templar-brothers-walking-across-courtyard-workers-greeting-morning-sun}
Brother Hugues, the commander, runs everything. He decides what to plant and when to harvest. He checks the accounts. He settles disputes between the workers and the peasants. He welcomes visitors. And once a year, he sends a sum of money to the provincial master of the Templars, for the Holy Land. It is a big responsibility. He is helped by the other brothers, each with his own job.

--

At first, Guillaume is surprised by all this. He thought that being a Templar would mean riding into battle, and praying in great churches. Instead, he finds himself checking the sheep, counting sacks of grain and helping to repair the mill. But Brother Hugues tells him that this work is just as important. Without the farms of Europe, he says, there would be no knights in the Holy Land. Every sack of grain is a small piece of the defence of Jerusalem. Guillaume thinks about this, and decides that it is true.

In the evening, the courtyard of the commandery is a peaceful place. The workers have gone home to the village. The animals are in their stables. The brothers walk slowly across the courtyard to Vespers. The pigeons coo in the dovecote. And the smell of fresh bread drifts from the bakery, where tomorrow's loaves are already being prepared.
