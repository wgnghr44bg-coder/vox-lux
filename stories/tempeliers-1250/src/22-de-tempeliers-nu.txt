Today, there are still many places where you can see the world of the Templars. One of the most beautiful is in London. It is the Temple Church, near the river Thames. It was built by the Templars in the twelfth century, as the church of their headquarters in England. Its main part is round, like the Church of the Holy Sepulchre in Jerusalem.

--

Inside the Temple Church, on the floor, there are stone figures of knights, lying on their backs, with their swords and their shields, and their legs crossed. They are tombs of knights who were friends of the Templars, from the twelfth and thirteenth centuries. The church was badly damaged by bombs in the Second World War. But it has been carefully restored. And it is still a working church today, with beautiful music.

In Portugal, in the town of Tomar, there is a great castle and convent of the Templars, called the Convent of Christ. In the middle of it, there is the Templar church, round and full of paintings and gold. It is one of the most beautiful buildings in Portugal. Visitors walk through its cloisters and its halls, and imagine the knights who once prayed there.

{img:acre-today-old-city-walls-sea-harbour-fishing-boats-sunset}
In Acre, which is now in Israel, the old city still stands by the sea, with its walls and its harbour. Much of it was rebuilt after the time of the crusaders. But under the streets, there are still the great vaulted halls of the Hospitallers, which have been dug out and can be visited. And in 1994, a man trying to fix a blocked drain discovered something extraordinary. The Templar tunnel.

The tunnel, which once ran from the Templar fortress to the harbour, had been forgotten for seven hundred years. Today, visitors can walk through it, with lights along the way. It is narrow and cool, with a curved stone roof, cut into the rock. Walking through it, you can imagine the brothers hurrying along it, carrying messages, money or supplies, in the time of Guillaume. The fortress itself is gone. It fell into the sea long ago. But the tunnel remains.

--

{img:atlit-castle-ruins-today-peninsula-sea-waves-sunset-quiet}
Château Pèlerin, at Atlit, is still there too, standing in ruins on its little peninsula by the sea. Its great walls are broken now, and its towers have fallen. The place is used by the army today, and is not open to visitors. But from the beach nearby, you can see its tall, broken walls, rising against the sky, with the waves breaking at its feet. Seabirds nest in its stones. And the sea still shines around it, as it did when Guillaume rode along the beach with Ferrant.

In France, many Templar commanderies still stand in the countryside. Some have been restored, and can be visited. Their chapels, their barns and their dovecotes. In some of them, there are little museums. In Champagne, near Troyes, in the village of Payns, where Hugues de Payens was born, there is a small museum about the Templars. And in Troyes itself, the old city where the order was recognised, you can still walk through the narrow medieval streets.

{img:restored-templar-commandery-france-today-chapel-dovecote-visitors}
In Paris, the great Temple fortress is gone. It was pulled down in the early eighteen hundreds. But the area is still called the Temple. There is a little park, called the Square du Temple, where the fortress once stood. And there is a metro station called Temple. Most Parisians who use it do not think about the knights. But the name has survived, for more than eight hundred years.

--

And all over the world, people still read and write about the Templars, and visit their places. Some for the legends. Some for the history. Some simply because they are moved by the story of these men, who promised to live simply, to pray, to share, and to protect travellers on dangerous roads. It is a good promise. And it is a promise that people still understand today.
