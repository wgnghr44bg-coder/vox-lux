In the spring, Guillaume is sent south along the coast, to the greatest castle of the Templars. It is called Château Pèlerin, which means the Pilgrims' Castle. It stands on a small rocky peninsula, sticking out into the sea, about thirty kilometres south of Acre. Today, the place is called Atlit.

--

The Templars built the castle about thirty years before our story, in 1218. Pilgrims helped them to build it, which is how it got its name. While digging the foundations, the builders found an old wall, and a lot of ancient coins. So they had money to help pay for the castle. And they found a spring of fresh water, inside the walls. That was a great blessing. A castle with its own water can never be forced to surrender by thirst.

The castle is enormous. On the land side, there are two huge walls, one behind the other, with great square towers. The inner wall is about thirty metres high, and twelve metres thick. Nothing could break through it. On the other three sides, there is the sea. The castle has its own little harbour, where ships can bring supplies. In all its history, Château Pèlerin was never taken by force.

{img:inside-castle-atlit-courtyard-church-twelve-sided-great-hall-vaults}
Inside the walls, there is a whole little town. There is a church with twelve sides, like the Church of the Holy Sepulchre in Jerusalem. There are great vaulted halls, stables, storerooms and kitchens. There are baths. There are gardens, orchards and even fishponds. The castle can hold thousands of people, if needed. In times of danger, the people from the villages around come inside the walls for safety.

Around the castle, the Templars own villages, fields, orchards and vineyards. Local peasants live in the villages and farm the land. Many of them are Arabic-speaking Christians, and some are Muslims. There are also salt pans by the sea, where salt is made by letting seawater dry in the sun. And there are fields of sugar cane. The castle is like a big commandery, but by the sea, in a much warmer land.

--

{img:sugar-cane-fields-coast-holy-land-workers-mill-sea-in-distance}
Guillaume is amazed by the sugar cane. It grows tall, like giant grass, in the warm, wet fields near the coast. The workers cut it, and take it to a mill, where it is crushed, and the sweet juice is boiled in big copper pots, until it turns into brown sugar. In Europe, sugar is very rare and expensive, a luxury for the rich. Here, the brothers can taste it every day. Thibaut eats so much of it that he gets a stomach ache.

The work at the castle is calm, most of the time. The brothers guard the walls, look after the horses, check the fields and the villages, and patrol the coast road. Sometimes there is news of danger, and everyone must be ready. But most days are peaceful. Guillaume rides along the beach in the morning, with Ferrant, along the edge of the waves. The horse loves the sea. He splashes in the shallow water, like a young colt.

{img:knight-riding-horse-along-beach-waves-splashing-morning-castle-behind}
In the evenings, Guillaume likes to sit on the wall of the castle, looking out over the sea. The sun sets in the west, over the water, turning everything red and gold. Fishing boats come back to the little harbour. The brothers sing Vespers in the twelve-sided church. And Guillaume thinks that this castle, standing between the sea and the hills, is one of the most beautiful places he has ever seen.

--

He stays at Château Pèlerin for two years. He learns to speak some Arabic, from the local workers. He learns to ride in the heat, and to live in a land that is so different from his own. He becomes friends with some of the local people. And he grows from a young man into a man. When he leaves, to return to Acre, he looks back at the castle from the coast road for a long time, until it disappears behind the dunes.
