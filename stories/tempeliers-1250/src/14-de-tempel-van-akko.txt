At the southwestern corner of Acre, right by the sea, there stands the great fortress of the Templars. It is the headquarters of the whole order, the home of the Grand Master. It is one of the strongest buildings in the city. And it is the place where Guillaume will now live.

--

The fortress is called the Temple. Its walls are very thick, built of big blocks of pale stone. At the entrance, there is a huge tower. On top of each of its corners, there is a small turret, and on each turret, there is a lion, made of gilded copper, shining in the sun. The waves of the sea break against the walls on one side. And on the other side, the fortress looks out over the city.

Inside, there is a big courtyard, with buildings all around it. There is a church, the most important Templar church in the world. There are halls and dormitories for the brothers, stables for hundreds of horses, storerooms, kitchens and workshops. There is a treasury, where the money of the order is kept. And there is a palace for the Grand Master, with fine rooms looking out over the sea.

{img:templar-grand-master-elderly-white-mantle-council-hall-brothers}
The Grand Master is the head of all the Templars, everywhere in the world. He is elected by the brothers, for life. He has great power. But he must also obey the Rule, and listen to the advice of his council of senior brothers. He has his own household, with a chaplain, a clerk, servants, and several horses. Guillaume sees him only from a distance, a tall, grey man in a white mantle, walking slowly across the courtyard, with other brothers around him.

Life in the Temple of Acre is much like life in the commandery in Champagne. The bells ring for the hours. The brothers pray, eat in silence, and look after their horses. But there are many more brothers here. Hundreds of them. Knights, sergeants, chaplains, squires and servants. And there are more duties. Guarding the walls. Escorting travellers and merchants. Patrolling the countryside around the city.

--

{img:secret-tunnel-templar-acre-stone-vaulted-passage-lantern-light}
The Templars of Acre have a secret. Underneath the city, there is a tunnel. It runs from the Templar fortress, under the streets, all the way to the harbour on the other side of the city. It is about three hundred and fifty metres long, cut into the rock and roofed with stone. The brothers can use it to move people, money and goods from the fortress to the port, safely and secretly. Very few people know about it.

Guillaume is given a place in the dormitory of the young knights. His bed is near a window, through which he can hear the sea. At night, he listens to the waves breaking against the walls, below. It is a soothing sound. It reminds him of the wind in the trees at home. Ferrant is given a stall in the great stables, among hundreds of other horses. Every morning, Guillaume visits him, and brings him an orange peel, which the horse loves.

{img:young-templar-looking-out-window-at-sea-night-moon-reflection}
On his first night in Acre, Guillaume cannot sleep. He gets up quietly, and goes to the window. The moon is shining on the sea, making a silver path to the horizon. Somewhere out there, far to the west, is France, and Champagne, and the little commandery with its chapel and its dovecote. And somewhere to the east, beyond the hills, is Jerusalem, which he may never see. He thinks about both. Then he says a Paternoster, thirteen times, and goes back to bed.

--

In the morning, the bell rings for Prime. The sun rises over the hills of Galilee, and turns the walls of the fortress golden. The gulls are crying over the harbour. The city is waking up. And Guillaume puts on his white mantle, and goes to the chapel, to begin his first day as a Templar in the Holy Land.

Before long, Guillaume knows every corner of the Temple. The cool, dark storerooms, smelling of spices and grain. The busy stables. The kitchens, where the cooks make dishes he has never tasted before, with rice, lentils, dates and lemons. And the high wall by the sea, where the brothers stand guard at night, watching the lights of the ships out on the dark water.
