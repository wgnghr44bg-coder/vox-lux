In the autumn of 1250, a letter arrives at the commandery. It comes from the master of the Templars in France. It says that the Templars in the Holy Land need more brothers. Many have been lost, in the war in Egypt. Young knights are needed, to go east. And Guillaume's name is on the list.

--

Brother Hugues calls Guillaume into his room, and reads him the letter. Guillaume listens in silence. He has been at the commandery for less than a year. He has grown to love it. The chapel, the fields, the horses, the brothers. But he knew, when he made his vows, that this could happen. He knew that he might be sent to the Holy Land. And part of him has always wanted to go.

He asks Brother Hugues one question. May Ferrant come with him? Brother Hugues smiles. He says that horses are always needed in the Holy Land. And that Ferrant will go with him, if the ships have room. Guillaume is so relieved that he almost forgets to bow before he leaves the room.

{img:young-knight-saying-goodbye-mother-sister-manor-gate-autumn}
Before he leaves, Guillaume is allowed to visit his family one last time, with another brother as his companion. His mother holds him for a long time. His father tells him to be brave, and to be wise, which is harder. His sister gives him a little piece of embroidered cloth, with a cross on it, which she made herself. He asks Brother Hugues if he may keep it. Brother Hugues says yes, this time.

On his last evening at the commandery, the brothers hold a little chapter for him. They pray for him, and give him their blessing. Brother Raoul, the old knight, gives him advice about the Holy Land. Drink only clean water, he says. Keep your head covered in the sun. Be patient with the people there, and learn their ways. And never forget why you went. Then the old man embraces him, and turns away quickly, so that nobody can see his eyes.

--

{img:small-group-templars-riding-south-road-france-autumn-pack-horses}
Early the next morning, Guillaume rides out of the gate, on Ferrant, with Thibaut riding behind him on the pack horse. Two other young brothers from nearby commanderies are going with them. They ride south, through the heart of France. Through Burgundy, with its vineyards turning red and gold. Along the great river Rhône, past old Roman cities. Every night, they stay at a Templar house along the way.

It takes them almost three weeks to reach the sea. Guillaume has never travelled so far. He sees mountains for the first time, far away, with snow on their tops. He sees the country change, from the green fields of the north, to the dry, rocky hills of the south, with olive trees, cypresses and the smell of wild herbs. The light becomes brighter. The air becomes warmer. And one afternoon, from the top of a hill, he sees the sea.

{img:first-view-mediterranean-sea-from-hill-blue-water-young-knight-awe}
He stops Ferrant and stares. He has never seen the sea before. It is enormous, blue and shining, stretching to the horizon. The sunlight sparkles on the water like a thousand stars. Little boats with white sails are moving on it. And somewhere, beyond that horizon, very far away, is the Holy Land. Thibaut comes up beside him, and stares too. Neither of them says a word.

--

That evening, they ride into the port of Marseille. It is a busy, noisy city, full of sailors, merchants and pilgrims, speaking many languages. In the harbour, there are dozens of ships. And among them, there are ships with a red cross on their sails. They belong to the Templars. In a few days, Guillaume will board one of them. His long journey to the east is about to begin.
