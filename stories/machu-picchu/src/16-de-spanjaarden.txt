Machu Picchu was lived in for less than a hundred years. Then everything changed.

In 1532, strangers arrived on the coast of the Inca Empire. They came from Spain, across the ocean. They had horses, which nobody in the Andes had ever seen. They had armour and weapons of steel. Their leader was called Francisco Pizarro. There were fewer than two hundred of them.

--

They came at a bad time for the Inca. A terrible sickness had swept through the empire a few years before. It had come from Europe, ahead of the Spanish themselves. Nobody in the Americas had ever had it before. Their bodies had no defence. Many people died. Among them was the emperor and his heir.

After that, two of the emperor's sons fought over who should rule. The empire was split. Just as one of the brothers won, the Spanish arrived. They captured him. They demanded a room full of gold and silver. The Inca filled it. But the Spanish did not let him go.

{img:inca-gold-objects-melted-down-room-full-sorrow-gentle-later}
We will leave those hard years there, gently, in the past. They were full of sorrow. The great Inca Empire fell. The Spanish took Cusco. The golden garden of the Sun temple was melted down into bars. Much was lost that can never be found again.

--

Some Inca kept fighting. They retreated into the warm forests and mountains north of Cusco. For forty years, they kept a small free Inca kingdom there. Not far from Machu Picchu. But in the end, it too was taken.

And Machu Picchu? The Spanish never found it. It is not in any of their papers about places they visited. It seems they did not know it was there. Hidden on its ridge in the clouds. Far from the main roads.

{img:machu-picchu-empty-quiet-mist-last-people-leaving-path-dusk}
But the people of Machu Picchu left anyway. The emperor's family was gone. Nobody came to visit anymore. Nobody sent food up the mountain. The estate had no more reason to exist. So, slowly, the servants and weavers and farmers went away. Back to their villages. Or to new lives. The fires went out. The fountains kept flowing. But there was nobody to drink from them.
