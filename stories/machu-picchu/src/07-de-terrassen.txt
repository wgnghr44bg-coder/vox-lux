On the steep sides of Machu Picchu, there are terraces. Long narrow steps of flat land, held up by stone walls. They climb up the mountain like a giant green staircase.

The Inca are masters of terraces. They build them all over the Andes. On mountainsides so steep that nothing could grow there otherwise. With terraces, they turn whole mountains into farmland.

--

A terrace is more than just a flat step. It is carefully built in layers. At the bottom are big stones. Then smaller stones. Then gravel. Then sand. And on top, rich soil. Some of the soil was carried up from the valley below. Basket by basket. On people's backs.

The layers let water drain away slowly. Not too fast, not too slow. The soil stays damp but not soaked. And the stone walls soak up the heat of the sun during the day. At night, they give the warmth back. That keeps the plants a little warmer on cold nights. It can make the difference between a good harvest and a frozen one.

{img:terrace-cross-section-layers-stones-gravel-sand-soil-diagram-like}
On the terraces of Machu Picchu, the people grow maize and potatoes and other plants. Enough to feed some of the people who live there. But not all. More food has to come from the valley below.

--

The terraces also protect the mountain. Without them, the heavy rains would wash the soil down the slopes. The mountainside could slide. With them, the rain is slowed down and held. The terraces are like a strong net holding the whole ridge together.

Near Machu Picchu, in another place, the Inca built strange round terraces. They go down into the ground like a bowl. Each ring is a little warmer or cooler than the next. Some experts think the Inca used them to test which crops grow best at which temperature. Like an outdoor laboratory.

{img:moray-circular-terraces-bowl-rings-green-grass-andes-later}
The terraces of Machu Picchu have lasted for more than five hundred years. Heavy rains, earthquakes, and centuries of nobody caring for them. And still they stand. Green steps on a green mountain. Holding the ridge in place.

--

One night during the building, there is a terrible storm. Rain pours down for hours. Water rushes down the mountain. The workers lie awake in their houses, listening. Will the new terraces hold? In the morning, they hurry out to look. One small wall near the edge has slipped. Stones and soil lie on the slope below. But all the others stand firm. The drains have done their work. The builders sigh with relief. They rebuild the fallen wall, deeper this time. And it never falls again.
