At every feast in the Andes, there is chicha.

Chicha is a drink made from maize. It is a kind of beer. Thick and yellowish and a little sour. Not very strong. People drink it every day. And at feasts, they drink a lot of it.

--

Chicha is usually made by women. They boil maize in big clay pots. They let it ferment for a few days in huge jars. Some jars are taller than a child. The chicha bubbles and changes. Then it is ready. It is drunk from cups of wood or clay. Often in pairs. The host fills two cups. One for himself and one for his guest. They drink together. That is how friendships are made and kept.

The Inca state uses chicha too. When workers come to do their turn of work, they are given food and chicha. At the end, there is a feast. The more food and chicha the state gives, the happier the workers are. Huge amounts of chicha are made for the emperor's feasts. Whole houses of women do nothing but make chicha for the state.

{img:paired-wooden-cups-kero-painted-toasting-two-men-feast-andes}
Before anyone drinks, a little chicha is poured on the ground. That is for Pachamama, the Earth Mother. She must drink first. People in the Andes still do this today.

--

What else do the people eat? Potatoes, always. Boiled or roasted or dried. Maize, roasted on the cob or ground into flour. Quinoa, cooked like porridge. Beans and squash and peppers. Llama meat, sometimes, often dried into strips. Guinea pig on feast days. Fish from the lakes and rivers. And fruit from the warm valleys.

There is one dish cooked in the ground. People dig a pit and fill it with hot stones. They put potatoes and meat and maize on the stones. They cover it all with grass and earth. And they wait. A few hours later, they dig it up. The food is soft and smoky and delicious. It is called pachamanca. It means earth pot. People in the Andes still cook it today, especially on feast days.

{img:pachamanca-earth-oven-family-uncovering-steaming-food-hot-stones}
At Machu Picchu, the workers eat simply. Potatoes and maize and chicha, mostly. Food comes up the mountain every day on the backs of llamas and people. The kitchen fires burn all day. And when the emperor visits, there are great feasts. With roasted meat and fruit and the finest chicha. And the workers get a share too.
