Today Machu Picchu is one of the most famous places in the world.

It is a World Heritage Site. In 2007, people around the world voted it one of the new seven wonders of the world. Every year, more than a million visitors come. They come by train up the valley. Then they take a bus up the winding road to the ridge. Or they walk the Inca Trail for four days and arrive through the Sun Gate at dawn.

--

They walk on the stairs the Inca built. They touch the perfect walls. They drink from the fountains, which still flow. They watch the clouds rise out of the valley in the morning. And they stand looking at the view for a long time. Many say it is even more beautiful than in the pictures.

So many visitors come that Machu Picchu must be protected. The number of people each day is limited. Visitors must walk on set paths. Guards make sure nobody climbs on the walls. Because the stones that have stood for five hundred years must stand for five hundred more.

{img:quechua-family-today-traditional-clothes-llamas-andes-village-market}
And the people of the Andes are still here. Millions of them still speak Quechua, the language of the Inca. They still grow potatoes of a hundred colours. They still make chuño in the frost. They still keep llamas with ribbons in their ears. They still weave on looms tied around their waists. They still pour a little chicha for Pachamama. And every year, four villages still rebuild a grass bridge over a river gorge.

--

So we leave Machu Picchu tonight, in the time of the emperor Pachacuti, as the evening falls.

The sun goes down behind the peaks. The light turns from gold to pink to deep blue. Clouds rise slowly out of the valley, soft and white. They fill the gorges and creep up the terraces. The fountains trickle in the dusk. In the workers' houses, little fires glow. Amaru sits outside with Kusi, his tired hands resting on his knees. In the weavers' house, Kantu folds her new cloth carefully. On the terraces, the llamas lie down in the grass. The white one with the red tassel is the last to settle. And above the ridge, the stars come out one by one. Over the snowy peaks. Over the sleeping city. Over the whole quiet empire of the mountains.
