Let us visit a village in the valley below Machu Picchu.

It is a small village. Maybe thirty families. The houses are built of stone and mud, with roofs of thick grass. They stand around small courtyards. The village sits on a slope above the river. Terraces climb the hill behind it. Above them, the mountain disappears into the clouds.

--

The family we meet has a father, a mother, three children and a grandmother. The father farms. He also does his turn of work for the state. Last year, he carried stone at Machu Picchu for two months. The mother farms and weaves and cooks. The children help. The grandmother tells stories and keeps an eye on the guinea pigs.

Yes, guinea pigs. In the Andes, people keep guinea pigs in the house. They run around the kitchen floor and squeak. They eat potato peels and grass. They keep the house warm. And on special days, they are eaten. It is a treat.

{img:guinea-pigs-running-kitchen-floor-stone-house-hearth-pots-woman}
The day starts before sunrise. It is cold. The mother lights the fire in the little clay stove. She warms soup from yesterday. Potatoes and quinoa and a few herbs. Everyone eats it hot, in bowls of clay. Then the father and the eldest son go to the fields. They carry the foot plough and a little bag of coca leaves.

--

Coca leaves are important in the Andes. People chew them slowly. The leaves give a little energy and help with the cold and the hunger and the thin air. They are also sacred. People offer them to the mountains and the earth.

The girls help their mother. They fetch water from the stream. They spin wool on spinning sticks as they walk. In the Andes, women are always spinning. Walking to the fields, talking with friends, watching the llamas. Their hands never stop.

{img:andean-girls-spinning-wool-while-walking-path-llamas-mountains}
In the afternoon, the mother weaves on her loom. One end is tied to a post. The other end is tied around her waist. She leans back to make the threads tight. She weaves in red and yellow and black. Patterns of zigzags and diamonds. Her mother taught her. And her mother before that.

--

In the evening, the family eats together around the fire. Potatoes. Maize. A little dried llama meat. Chili sauce. The grandmother tells a story about the fox and the condor. The children laugh. Outside, it is dark and cold. Stars fill the sky. And high above, on the mountain they cannot see, the fires of Machu Picchu are burning.
