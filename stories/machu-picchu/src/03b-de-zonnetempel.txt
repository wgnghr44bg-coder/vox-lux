In Cusco stands the most important temple of the empire. It is called the Coricancha. That means the golden enclosure.

It is the temple of the Sun, the great god of the Inca. Its walls are built of the most perfect stone in all of Cusco. Smooth and grey and fitted like glass. And they were once covered with gold.

--

The old Spanish writers describe it with amazement. Sheets of gold covered the walls of the main rooms. Seven hundred sheets, they say, each one heavy. In the room of the Sun, a great golden image of the Sun hung on the wall. When the morning light came through the door, it shone so brightly people could hardly look at it.

There were rooms for other gods too. For the Moon, covered in silver. For the stars and the rainbow and thunder. Each room had its own colour and its own metal and its own priests.

{img:inca-golden-garden-life-size-maize-llamas-gold-silver-courtyard}
And there was a garden. A garden made of gold. Life-size plants of maize made of gold and silver. With golden leaves and silver cobs. Golden llamas with their shepherds. Golden butterflies and birds. Golden flowers. All shining in the sun. It must have been the most amazing garden in the world.

--

Why so much gold? For the Inca, gold is not money. They do not use money at all. Gold is the sweat of the Sun. Silver is the tears of the Moon. They are holy metals. They belong to the gods and to the emperor. They are used to honour the gods. Not to buy things.

The priests of the Sun live near the temple. They lead the great ceremonies. They watch the movements of the sun through the year. They know when to plant and when to harvest. And they keep the calendar of feasts.

{img:priests-coricancha-watching-sunrise-solstice-window-cusco-mountains}
Today almost all the gold is gone. But the walls are still there. A church was later built on top of the old temple. Earthquakes have shaken the church. Parts of it have fallen down. But the Inca walls underneath have never moved. They are still standing, smooth and perfect, five hundred years later.
