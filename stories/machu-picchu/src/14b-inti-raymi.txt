The greatest festival of the Inca year is Inti Raymi. The festival of the Sun.

It takes place in June. In the Andes, south of the equator, June is the middle of winter. The days are short. The sun is low in the sky. On the shortest day, the sun seems to stop moving north. People fear that it might go away for good. So there must be a great festival to call it back.

--

For three days before, nobody lights a fire. Nobody eats much. Everyone waits. Then, on the morning of the shortest day, the whole of Cusco gathers in the great square before dawn. The nobles and the priests and the people. Everyone wears their finest clothes. The emperor himself is there. Everyone faces east. And they wait for the sun.

The sky grows pale. Then pink. Then gold. Everyone is silent. And then, over the mountains, the first ray of the sun appears. A great shout goes up from the crowd. The emperor raises a golden cup of chicha toward the sun. He greets it. He thanks it. And he pours some chicha out as an offering.

{img:priest-lighting-new-fire-mirror-sunlight-cotton-cusco-ceremony}
Then a priest takes a curved mirror of polished metal. He holds it up to catch the first sunlight. He focuses the light on a little pile of dry cotton. The cotton begins to smoke. And then it catches fire. It is a new fire, lit by the sun itself. From it, all the fires in the city are lit again. The sun has come back. The new year can begin.

--

Then there are days of feasting. Music and dancing in the square. Drums and flutes and singing. Huge amounts of food and chicha. Offerings of llamas and cloth to the Sun. The bodies of the old emperors are carried out in their fine clothes. They sit in the square with the living, as honoured guests.

{img:inti-raymi-today-sacsayhuaman-reenactment-costumes-crowd-later}
Inti Raymi is still celebrated today. Every year on the twenty-fourth of June, a great show takes place in Cusco. At the old fortress above the city. Thousands of people come to watch. Actors play the emperor and the priests and the nobles. They speak in Quechua, the language of the Inca. And they greet the sun, just as people did five hundred years ago.
