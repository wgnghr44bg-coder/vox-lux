Who built Machu Picchu? For a long time, that question had many strange answers.

When the outside world learned about Machu Picchu, about a hundred years ago, people were amazed. How could anyone build such a place, so high, so perfect, without iron tools or wheels or big animals to pull loads? Some people thought it must be much older than the Inca. Some thought it was built by a lost people. Some even made up wild stories about giants or visitors from the stars.

--

But the archaeologists kept looking. And slowly, they found the real answer. It was built by the Inca. And it was built by people. Thousands of them.

How do they know? The style of the walls is pure Inca. The same as in Cusco and other Inca places. The pots and tools found there are Inca. Old Spanish papers mention an estate of Pachacuti in exactly this area. And scientists have measured the age of bones and plants found there. They show that people lived at Machu Picchu from around 1420 to around 1530. The time of the Inca Empire.

{img:archaeologist-studying-inca-wall-joints-notebook-machu-picchu-light}
The graves at Machu Picchu tell another part of the story. More than a hundred people were buried there. Scientists studied their bones and teeth. Your teeth hold a record of where you grew up. Of the water you drank and the food you ate as a child. And the teeth showed something surprising.

--

The people buried at Machu Picchu came from all over the empire. From the coast. From the high grasslands. From the lake lands in the south. From the forest. They were not all from one place. They were brought here from far away to live and work. Servants of the emperor. Weavers and farmers and builders and cooks. People from many peoples, living together on a mountain ridge.

{img:diverse-andean-people-arriving-mountain-path-bundles-different-clothes}
So the answer to who built Machu Picchu is this. The emperor Pachacuti ordered it. His master builders planned it. And thousands of ordinary people from all over the Andes built it. Stone by stone. With their hands. Let us go and meet them.
