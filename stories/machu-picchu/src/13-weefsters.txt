For the Inca, cloth is more precious than gold.

That sounds strange. But it is true. Gold belongs to the gods. But cloth is what people give each other. It is given at weddings and births and funerals. The emperor gives fine cloth as his highest reward. Soldiers are paid in cloth. Gods are dressed in cloth. Cloth is even burned as an offering. A rich family is a family with lots of fine cloth.

--

There are two kinds of cloth. The everyday cloth is called awasqa. It is woven by families at home, on simple looms. It is used for clothes and blankets and bags.

The finest cloth is called cumbi. It is made of the softest alpaca and vicuña wool. It has very tight weaving. So tight that it is smooth and shiny like silk. Some cumbi cloth has more than a hundred threads in a single centimetre. It is woven with patterns of tiny squares in many colours. Each little square means something. A family. A people. A message. Nobody can read them all today.

{img:cumbi-tunic-tiny-squares-patterns-many-colours-museum-light}
Cumbi is made by special weavers. Some are men. But many are women called the chosen women. These are girls chosen from villages all over the empire for their skill and beauty. They live together in special houses in the cities. They weave and they make chicha for the emperor and the gods. Some stay there all their lives. Others are given in marriage to nobles and officers.

--

At Machu Picchu too, there were weavers. Women from many lands, living together on the mountain. Their spindle whorls have been found there. Little round weights of stone or clay, with a hole in the middle. They helped the spindle turn evenly as the women spun the wool.

{img:spindle-whorls-clay-stone-small-round-found-machu-picchu-hands}
One of the women at Machu Picchu is called Kantu. She comes from the shores of a great lake far to the south. She was chosen when she was twelve. She has woven for the emperor for six years. Her fingers are quick and light. She is weaving a tunic for the emperor himself. It has taken her four months so far. She is nervous. He will come to Machu Picchu soon. What if he does not like it? She checks every square again and again. Every colour. Every line. It must be perfect.
