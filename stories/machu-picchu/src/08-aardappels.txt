The most important food in the Andes is the potato.

The potato comes from these mountains. People here grew it for thousands of years before anyone else in the world had ever seen one. And the Inca do not grow just one kind. They grow hundreds. Small and big. Round and long and curly. Yellow and white and purple and red and black. Some are sweet. Some are bitter. Some grow in the warm valleys. Some grow high up where nothing else will.

--

Farmers plant potatoes in small mounds of earth. They use a foot plough called a chakitaqlla. It is a long wooden pole with a pointed end and a step for your foot. The farmer pushes it into the ground with his foot and turns the earth over. The women follow behind. They drop seed potatoes into the holes. Farmers often work in groups, singing as they go.

The Inca have a very clever way of keeping potatoes. It is called chuño. In the cold dry season, high up, the nights are freezing and the days are sunny. So the people spread potatoes on the ground. At night, they freeze. In the day, they thaw. Then people walk on them with bare feet. They squeeze out the water.

{img:families-stomping-potatoes-chuno-frost-morning-high-plateau}
They do this for several days. Freeze, thaw, stomp. In the end, the potatoes are dry and light, like little stones. They can be kept for years without going bad. When you want to eat them, you soak them in water and cook them. Chuño is like a store cupboard that never empties. In a bad year, it can save lives.

--

The Inca also grow maize. Maize does not like the cold high places. It grows in the warm valleys. It is precious. It is used for chicha, the maize beer. It is given at feasts. The emperor's fields grow lots of maize.

And there is quinoa, a small round seed that grows high up. It is full of goodness. And there are beans and squash and chili peppers. And many other roots and grains that are still eaten in the Andes today.

{img:inca-storehouses-qollqa-hillside-row-stone-buildings-food-stored}
All over the empire, along the roads, there are storehouses. Rows of small stone buildings on the hillsides. They are full of food. Chuño and maize and dried meat. Cloth and tools. If a harvest fails, the storehouses can feed the people. If an army marches past, it can be fed. The Inca plan ahead. Nobody in the empire should go hungry. At least, that is the idea.
