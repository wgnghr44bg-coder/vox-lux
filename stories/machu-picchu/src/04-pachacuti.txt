The man who ordered Machu Picchu is the emperor Pachacuti. The one who turns the world around.

We met him before. As a young prince, he saved Cusco when everyone else had fled. Then he became emperor. And he changed everything. He conquered new lands. He rebuilt Cusco in stone. He reorganised the whole empire. He set up the roads and the storehouses and the rules for work. Much of what we think of as Inca began with him.

--

The Sapa Inca wears a special sign of his rank. A woven band around his head, with a red fringe hanging down over his forehead. It is called the mascaypacha. Only the emperor may wear it. It is like a crown. When people see the red fringe, they bow down low.

Pachacuti is a great warrior. But he is also a builder and a thinker. Old stories say he wrote poems and prayers. He loved the Sun god and built temples to him. He watched the stars. And he loved the mountains and the valleys of his land.

{img:inca-royal-estate-planning-pachacuti-architects-model-clay-ridge}
Like other emperors, Pachacuti owns royal estates. These are like country palaces. Places to rest and hunt and hold feasts, away from the busy capital. Each estate has houses and temples and fields and servants. And when an emperor passes away, his estate belongs to his family forever.

--

Pachacuti wants a very special estate. In a valley to the north of Cusco, along the sacred river, there is a mountain ridge. It is beautiful beyond words. Surrounded by peaks and clouds. Above a roaring river. Close to the warm forest and its birds and flowers. And he wants to build there. A place for the emperor and his family. A place close to the gods of the mountains and the sun.

{img:pachacuti-standing-on-ridge-looking-at-peaks-clouds-advisers}
His advisers look at the ridge. They are worried. It is narrow and steep. There is no flat land. The rock is cracked. It rains a lot. How could anyone build there? But the emperor has decided. And when the Sapa Inca decides, it will be done. So the planners go home and begin to think. How? It will be the hardest building job of their lives.

--

There is an old story about Pachacuti from his youth. Before the great battle for Cusco, he went to pray at a spring. As he looked into the water, he saw a shining crystal fall in. And in the crystal, he saw a strange figure. It had rays like the sun coming from its head. And it spoke to him. You will be a great ruler, it said. You will conquer many lands. Pachacuti kept the crystal all his life, the story says. And whenever he looked into it, he saw what was to come.
