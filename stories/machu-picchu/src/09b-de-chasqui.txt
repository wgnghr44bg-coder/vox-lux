How do you send a message across an empire four thousand kilometres long? Without horses, without writing on paper, without wheels?

The Inca use runners. They are called chasquis.

--

Along the roads, every few kilometres, there are small huts. In each hut, two or more young men wait. They are chasquis. They are chosen for their speed and their strong lungs. They have trained since they were boys. And they are always ready.

When a message comes, a runner sprints along the road toward the next hut. As he gets close, he blows a trumpet made of a big sea shell. Tooot! The next runner hears it and comes out. He starts running beside the first one. The first runner tells him the message, out loud, as they run together. He also hands him a quipu, a string with knots. And then the second runner speeds off alone. The first runner stops, panting. His part is done.

{img:two-chasqui-runners-passing-message-quipu-running-together-road}
Like a relay race, the message goes on. From runner to runner, hut to hut. Each runner only runs a short distance. But he runs it as fast as he can. Day and night. In rain and snow and sun. And so the message travels incredibly fast. Up to two hundred and fifty kilometres in one day. A message from Cusco can reach the far north in about a week. For a walker, it would take months.

--

The runners do not only carry messages. They carry small things too. Old stories say the emperor in Cusco could eat fresh fish from the sea. Two days after it was caught. The runners brought it up from the coast, over the mountains, in relays.

Being a chasqui is an honour. But it is also hard. The runners must be ready at any moment. Even in the middle of the night. If they are too slow, they will be in trouble. The message must always get through.

{img:chasqui-hut-roadside-runner-waiting-blanket-night-stars-mountains}
At Machu Picchu, Amaru sometimes hears the conch trumpet echo across the mountains. Faint and far away. Somewhere on the road, a runner is coming. With news from Cusco. Is the emperor coming to see his estate? Has something happened? Everyone looks up. And waits to hear.
