In 1911, an American explorer came to the valley. His name was Hiram Bingham. He was a teacher at a university in America. And he was looking for the lost last city of the Inca. The place where they had fought on after the Spanish came.

He travelled down the sacred valley with a small team and mules. At a camp by the river, he met a local farmer called Melchor Arteaga. Bingham asked him about ruins. Arteaga said yes. There were ruins. Up on the mountain. On the ridge. He called it Machu Picchu.

--

The next morning, it was raining. Bingham's companions did not want to climb. Too steep. Too wet. So Bingham went with Arteaga and a soldier. They crossed the river on a wobbly bridge of logs. Bingham crawled across on his hands and knees. Then they climbed. Up and up. Through thick wet forest. Holding on to roots and branches. It took hours.

Near the top, they found a little hut. Two farming families lived there. They grew crops on the old terraces. They gave the visitors cool water and boiled potatoes. And then a small boy, about ten years old, offered to show Bingham the ruins. His name was Pablito.

{img:local-boy-guiding-explorer-ruins-machu-picchu-1911-overgrown-walls}
Pablito led Bingham along the terraces and into the forest. And there, among the trees and vines, Bingham saw the walls. Perfect Inca walls. Temples. Stairs. Houses. A whole city, half hidden in the green. He wrote later that it took his breath away.

--

Bingham was not the first person to see Machu Picchu. The local families had always known about it. But he was the one who told the world. He came back with teams to clear the forest and dig. He took hundreds of photographs. In 1913, a famous magazine filled a whole issue with his story and his pictures. And suddenly, everyone wanted to know about the lost city in the clouds.

{img:workers-clearing-forest-machu-picchu-1912-walls-revealed-photographs}
Bingham thought he had found the last Inca city. He was wrong about that. We now know it was Pachacuti's estate. Bingham also took many objects from Machu Picchu back to America. For a hundred years, they stayed there. Then, after many requests from Peru, they were finally sent home. Today they are in a museum in Cusco. Back where they belong.
