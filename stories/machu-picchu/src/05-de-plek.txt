The ridge where Machu Picchu will be built is narrow and steep. On one side, the mountain falls away almost straight down to the river, hundreds of metres below. On the other side, too. It is like a saddle between two peaks.

To build on a place like this, you first have to make it safe. And that means work you will never see.

--

The ridge sits on top of cracks in the earth. Deep below, two great breaks in the rock cross each other. That sounds dangerous. And in a way it is. But it also helped the builders. The cracks have broken the granite rock into pieces. Many pieces are already loose. Easy to pick up and shape. A ready-made quarry.

But the cracks also mean water. Lots of it. This is a place of heavy rain. Almost two metres of rain fall every year. If all that water soaked into the ground, the whole mountain could slide away. Buildings would crack. Walls would fall. So before anything else, the builders had to solve the water.

{img:inca-workers-digging-deep-foundations-crushed-stone-layers-ridge}
They dug deep. Under the city, they laid thick layers of crushed stone and gravel. Then smaller stones. Then soil. Water can run down through these layers quickly. It drains away into channels and out of the city. Some experts say that more than half of the work at Machu Picchu is under the ground. Hidden. Nobody sees it. But without it, nothing would still be standing.

--

Then the builders had to make flat land. There was none. So they built walls and filled them in. Step after step, up the mountainside. Each step is a flat strip of land held up by a stone wall. These are called terraces. On the steep sides, they go up like a giant staircase. Some are for buildings. Some are for growing food.

{img:terraces-being-built-mountainside-workers-carrying-soil-baskets}
It took years just to prepare the ridge. Year after year, workers dug and carried and laid stone. And slowly, the steep wild ridge became something new. A safe place to build. Ready for the stonemasons.
