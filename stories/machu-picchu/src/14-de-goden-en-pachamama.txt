The Inca have many gods. And the greatest is the Sun.

The Sun is called Inti. He gives light and warmth. He makes the crops grow. Without him, there would be nothing. And the emperor is his son. That is why the emperor is so important. He is the link between the people and the Sun.

--

There is also the Moon, Mama Killa. She is the wife of the Sun. She watches over women and the calendar. There is Illapa, the god of thunder and lightning and rain. He carries a sling. When he swings it, there is thunder. When he throws it, there is lightning. And there is Viracocha, the creator. The one who made everything at the very beginning, and then walked away across the sea.

But the god people pray to most in their daily lives is Pachamama. The Earth Mother. She is the earth itself. She gives the potatoes and the maize and the grass for the llamas. She must be thanked and cared for. People give her coca leaves and chicha and little gifts. They bury small offerings in the fields. In August, before planting, they give her special gifts. Because she is hungry then, and must be fed before she can give.

{img:andean-farmers-offering-coca-leaves-earth-pachamama-field-kneeling}
Then there are the apus, the spirits of the mountains. And the huacas. A huaca is any holy place or thing. A spring. A cave. A big rock. A strange tree. All over the Andes, there are thousands of huacas. People visit them and leave offerings and ask for help.

--

At Machu Picchu, there are many holy places. There is a great carved rock on top of a small hill. It is called the Intihuatana. That means the place where the sun is tied. People think it was used to watch the sun and mark the seasons. There is a temple shaped like a curve, with windows that catch the sunrise on the shortest and the longest days. There is a cave under it, carved with great care. And there is a rock shaped like the mountain behind it, as if it is the mountain's little twin.

{img:intihuatana-carved-stone-hilltop-machu-picchu-shadow-sun-peaks}
The Inca did not separate the holy from the everyday. The whole land is alive with spirits. Every stone and river and mountain is someone. When Amaru shapes a stone, he says a little prayer to it first. When the farmer plants a potato, he gives a few coca leaves to the earth. And when the sun rises over the peaks in the morning, everyone at Machu Picchu turns toward it. And greets it.
