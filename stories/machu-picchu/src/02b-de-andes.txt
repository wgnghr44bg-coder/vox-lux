The Andes are the longest mountain chain in the world. They run all the way down the west side of South America. Some of their peaks are more than six thousand metres high. Covered in snow and ice all year.

It is a land of huge differences. Very close together. High up, there are cold windy grasslands where only tough grass grows. Lower down, there are warm valleys with rivers and fields. And on the eastern side, the mountains fall steeply into a hot wet forest full of trees and birds and mist. All of this can be just a day's walk apart.

--

Living here is hard. The air high up is thin. There is less of it to breathe. Visitors from the lowlands get tired and dizzy and short of breath. But the people of the Andes are used to it. Their bodies have adapted over thousands of years. They have bigger chests and stronger hearts. They can work hard at heights where others can barely walk.

The weather can be harsh. Freezing nights and burning sun by day. Sudden storms and hail. Earthquakes shake the ground. Landslides wipe out paths. Rivers flood in the rainy season. Nothing here is easy.

{img:condor-soaring-over-andes-valley-wings-wide-clouds-below}
But the people of the Andes have learned to live with all of it. And to use it. They grow different crops at different heights. Potatoes high up. Maize in the warm valleys. Coca and fruit in the forest edges. Families and villages trade with each other up and down the mountain. Everyone has a little of everything.

--

Above the mountains flies the condor. It is one of the biggest birds in the world. Its wings stretch more than three metres from tip to tip. It glides for hours on the wind without flapping. For the people of the Andes, the condor is sacred. It is the messenger between the world of people and the world above.

{img:apus-mountain-spirits-snow-peak-offering-stones-andean-pass}
And the mountains themselves are sacred. The great snowy peaks are called apus. People believe they are powerful spirits. They watch over the valleys. They send the water that flows down from their snow. They must be honoured and thanked. When people cross a high mountain pass, they leave a little stone or a leaf on a pile, as a gift to the apu. The piles grow over the years into small hills of stone.
