The years go by, on the island. The seasons turn. The puffins come and go. The storms come and go. The supply boat comes and goes. And the children grow up. Slowly, life on the island changes, as it always does.

--

The next year, Isla goes to the mainland, to the school in the town, to continue her education. It is very hard to leave the island. She cries all the way across the sea, on the relief boat, looking back at the lighthouse, getting smaller and smaller. But she does very well at school. She is the best pupil in her class. And she writes long letters home, every week, which arrive with the relief boat, in bundles of four or five.

Isla becomes a teacher. She teaches in a small school on the island of Skye, where her parents came from. She teaches the children about the sea, the birds and the stars, as her parents taught her. And she paints. Beautiful watercolours of sea birds, which she sells, and which later hang in a museum in Edinburgh. She never forgets the island, and the lighthouse, and the light sweeping over her window at night.

{img:grown-sailor-on-ship-deck-looking-at-lighthouse-on-island-horizon}
Calum becomes a sailor, as he always dreamed. At fifteen, he goes to sea, as a deck boy on a cargo ship. He sails all over the world. To America, to India, to Australia, to China. He sees all the places he read about, in the books from the library box. And whenever his ship passes the coast of Scotland, at night, he stands on the deck, watching for the lights of the lighthouses. And when he sees the light of his island, flashing white, once every thirty seconds, he knows that his father is there, keeping watch.

James and Mairi stay on the island for five more years. Then they are moved to another lighthouse, on the mainland, near a village, as keepers often are. Mairi is glad to be near a church and a shop again. But she misses the island. They both do. James keeps lighthouses for twenty-five more years, at four different stations, and in all that time, the light never goes out. Not once. When he retires, the Board gives him a silver watch, with his name engraved on it.

--

{img:old-couple-retired-cottage-skye-garden-view-of-sea-distant-lighthouse}
In their old age, James and Mairi live in a small cottage on Skye, near the sea, with a garden, and a view of a lighthouse on a distant headland. In the evenings, James sits by the window, and watches its light flashing, out over the water. And he counts the flashes, out of old habit. Mairi knits by the fire. And their grandchildren come to visit, and ask for stories about the island, the storm, the fog and the rescue of the fishermen.

Donald and Effie stay on the island for many years more. Donald becomes the principal keeper, when James leaves. Their son Angus, the baby who was just learning to walk, grows up on the island, and becomes a lighthouse keeper himself, like his father. Many families of lighthouse keepers were like that. Generation after generation, keeping the lights of Scotland burning, on islands and headlands and rocks, all around the coast.

{img:keepers-family-generations-photograph-style-in-front-of-lighthouse-tower}
And the light on the island goes on shining. Year after year. Decade after decade. Every night, from sunset to sunrise. Flashing white, once every thirty seconds, out over the sea. Different keepers come and go. Different families live in the white houses. Different children play in the rock pools, and watch the puffins, and polish the brass. But the light is always the same.

--

Calum's old fishing knife, with the handle of deer horn, is still kept by his family today, more than a hundred years later. And Isla's book about the lighthouses of Scotland, the one the old judge gave her, is still on a shelf, in the house of her great-granddaughter. With a pressed sea pink flower inside the front cover, picked on the cliffs of the island, a long time ago.
