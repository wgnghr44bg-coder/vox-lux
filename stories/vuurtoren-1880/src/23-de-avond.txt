So we leave the lighthouse tonight, in the year 1880, as the evening falls. The sun is setting over the Atlantic, in the west. The sky is orange and pink and gold. The sea is calm, and shining like a mirror. The sea birds are flying home to the cliffs, calling softly. And on the highest point of the little island, the white tower stands, glowing in the last light.

--

In the kitchen of the keeper's house, the paraffin lamp is lit. Mairi is making supper. Oatcakes, cheese, a pot of soup, and fresh fish, caught that afternoon. Calum is setting the table, very badly. Isla is sitting by the window, painting a puffin, in her new watercolours. And the kettle is singing on the range.

{img:keeper-climbing-spiral-staircase-tower-sunset-light-through-small-windows}
James looks at his watch. It is almost sunset. He puts on his uniform jacket and his cap, and walks out across the yard, to the tower. He opens the door, and begins to climb the spiral stairs. Round and round, up and up. At the top, he draws back the curtains. He lights the lamp. The flame grows. The great lens fills with light. He starts the clockwork. And the first beam sweeps out across the darkening sea.

--

Out on the sea, far away, a ship is passing. A small cargo steamer, on its way north. On the bridge, the captain sees the light, flashing white, once every thirty seconds. He checks his book. He knows where he is. He adjusts his course, a little. And he thinks, as sailors often do, of the keeper in the tower, whom he will never meet, keeping watch for him, through the night.

{img:lighthouse-beam-night-calm-sea-stars-ship-lights-far-away-peaceful}
The night comes down over the island. The stars come out, one by one, and then in thousands. The moon rises over the mountains of the mainland, far away to the east. The seals are lying on the rocks, half asleep. The puffins are in their burrows. The cow is chewing in her shed. And in the houses, the lamps go out, one by one, as the families go to bed.

--

Isla lies in her bed, under the warm quilt, watching the beam pass across her window. Light. Dark. Light. Dark. Every thirty seconds. Slow and steady, like a heartbeat. She thinks of her father, up in the tower, keeping watch. She thinks of the ships, passing safely in the night. She thinks of the puffins, and the seals, and the stars. And slowly, counting the flashes, she falls asleep, as the light keeps turning, all night long, over the dark and quiet sea.
