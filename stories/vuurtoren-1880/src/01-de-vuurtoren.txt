Tonight we travel back almost a hundred and fifty years, to the year 1880. We go to the west coast of Scotland, to a small, rocky island, far out in the Atlantic Ocean. There are no trees on the island. Only grass, heather, rocks and sea birds. And on the highest point of the island, there stands a tall white tower, with a lantern of glass at the top. It is a lighthouse.

--

It is evening, in late summer. The sun is going down over the sea, in the west. The sky is turning orange, then pink, then violet. The sea is calm, and shining like silver. Gulls are gliding over the cliffs, calling. And at the foot of the tower, a man in a dark blue uniform, with brass buttons, is walking up the path from the little white house beside it. He takes out his watch, and looks at it. It is almost sunset. Time to light the lamp.

He opens the door of the tower, and begins to climb the spiral staircase inside. Round and round, up and up, more than a hundred steps. At the top, he comes into a small room, with windows of glass all around, looking out over the sea in every direction. In the middle of the room, there is a great glass lens, taller than a man, shining like a giant jewel. And inside it, there is the lamp.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

{img:keeper-lighting-lamp-inside-fresnel-lens-lantern-room-sunset-glow}
The keeper lights the lamp. A flame appears, small and yellow at first, then bigger and brighter, as he adjusts the wicks. The great lens catches the light, and bends it, and gathers it into a powerful beam. He sets the clockwork going, and the lens begins to turn, slowly. And out over the darkening sea, the beam of light begins to sweep around, like a great white arm, flashing every half minute, across the waves.

Tonight, we will spend a year on this island, with the family of a lighthouse keeper. We will see how they keep the light burning, every night, from sunset to sunrise, without ever missing a single night. How they clean, polish and care for the lamp and the lens. How they live on their lonely island, far from the mainland, with the sea all around them.

{img:lighthouse-keeper-family-island-garden-children-washing-line-summer}
We will watch the sea birds and the seals and the whales. We will wait for the supply boat, which brings food and letters from the mainland. We will sit by the fire on long winter evenings, reading books from the travelling library. We will see a great storm, and a thick fog. And we will meet the engineers who built these towers, on rocks in the middle of the sea.

--

Life on a lighthouse island was lonely, and sometimes hard. But it was also peaceful, simple and beautiful. And the keepers knew that their work was important. Every night, ships were passing in the darkness, along the dangerous rocky coast. And the light, flashing from the tower, told them where they were, and kept them safe.

{img:lighthouse-beam-sweeping-over-dark-sea-ship-lights-distance-night}
So let us begin, on a calm summer evening in 1880, on a little island off the coast of Scotland. The sun has set. The first stars are coming out. And high up in the tower, the great light is turning, slowly, flashing out over the sea, as it will all night long, until the sun rises again.
