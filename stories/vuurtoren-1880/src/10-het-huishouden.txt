Living on an island, far from shops and markets, the families must look after themselves. Food, water, fuel, clothes. Everything must be grown, made, collected, or brought by the supply boat, every few weeks. And it is Mairi who manages all of it, with great skill and energy.

--

The day begins early, for Mairi. She is up at six, to light the fire in the kitchen range, and put the kettle on. Then she goes out to milk the cow, a gentle brown Highland cow called Morag, with long shaggy hair and curved horns. She feeds the hens, and collects the eggs. Then she comes back and makes breakfast. Porridge, of course, made with oatmeal, salt and water, and eaten with fresh milk. Bread and butter. Eggs. Tea.

Water comes from a tank, which collects the rain from the roofs of the houses. On an island in the Atlantic, there is plenty of rain. But in dry summers, the water must be used carefully. Mairi uses it for drinking and cooking. For washing clothes, she uses water from a little stream that runs down the hill, behind the houses. Washing day is on Monday. And on a windy island, the clothes dry very quickly on the line.

{img:walled-garden-island-potatoes-cabbages-woman-and-boy-digging-wind}
The garden, inside its stone wall, gives the families vegetables. Potatoes, which are the most important. Cabbages, kale, carrots, turnips, onions and peas. There are also a few rows of strawberries, and a gooseberry bush, which Calum guards jealously from the birds. Mairi and the children work in the garden every day, in the summer, digging, weeding and watering. In the autumn, the potatoes are dug up and stored in a dark shed, for the winter.

The sea gives food too. James and Donald fish from the rocks, and from a small rowing boat. They catch mackerel, cod, haddock, pollack and lobsters, in creels, which are wicker baskets that they put out on the sea floor. Mairi smokes and salts some of the fish, to keep it for the winter. The children collect mussels and winkles from the rocks at low tide. And in early summer, they collect the eggs of some of the sea birds, which taste very good, as islanders have done for centuries.

--

{img:peat-cutting-moorland-family-stacking-peat-bricks-summer-sky}
For fuel, the families burn coal, which comes on the supply boat, and peat. Peat is a kind of earth, made of plants that have rotted slowly, for thousands of years, in wet ground. On the island, there is a peat bog, on the north side. In late spring, the families cut the peat, into bricks, with special spades. They stack the bricks to dry in the sun and the wind, all summer. And in the autumn, they carry them home, and build a great peat stack. Peat burns slowly, with a lovely smell, warm and smoky.

Mairi cooks on the kitchen range, an iron stove with an oven, heated by coal and peat. She bakes bread, scones and oatcakes. She makes soups and stews, of mutton, vegetables and barley. She makes fish pies, and potato cakes, and porridge every morning. On Sundays, there is a roast, of mutton or chicken, and a pudding. And for special days, a clootie dumpling, a rich pudding of flour, fruit, spices and suet, boiled for hours in a cloth.

{img:scottish-cottage-kitchen-iron-range-scones-baking-woman-children-table}
Everything else comes on the supply boat. Flour, oatmeal, sugar, tea, salt, coffee, soap, candles, paraffin for the house lamps, clothes, boots, and anything else that is needed. Mairi writes a list, every month, and sends it with the boat to a shop on the mainland. The goods arrive on the next boat. If she forgets something, she must wait another month. So she is very careful with her lists. And she always keeps a good store of everything, in case the boat cannot come, because of bad weather.

--

It is a busy life. Mairi works from morning until night, every day. But she likes it. She likes the freedom, the fresh air, the sea and the peace. She likes being her own master, in her own home, with nobody to tell her what to do, except the weather. And in the evenings, when the work is done, she sits by the fire, with her knitting, and her family around her, and listens to the wind outside, and the rain on the windows, and feels content.
