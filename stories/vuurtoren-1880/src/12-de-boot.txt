Every few weeks, a boat comes to the island. It is the most exciting day of the month. The relief boat, as it is called, brings everything the families need from the mainland. Food and supplies. Oil for the light. Coal. Letters and newspapers. And sometimes, visitors. And it takes away the letters, the reports, the log books, and anything that needs to go to the mainland.

--

The boat is a sturdy sailing boat, with two masts, rented by the Board from a boatman on the mainland. It takes about three hours to sail to the island, if the wind is good. But it can only come when the weather is calm. If there is a storm, or a heavy swell, it cannot land safely at the jetty. And so, sometimes, the families must wait for weeks, watching the sea, hoping for a calm day.

On the morning when the boat is expected, everyone is up early, watching the sea. The children run up to the top of the lighthouse, with the telescope, to be the first to see it. And when Calum spots the white sails, far away on the horizon, he shouts so loudly that everyone on the island hears him. Boat! Boat! And everyone runs down to the jetty, to wait.

{img:boatmen-unloading-barrels-sacks-crates-stone-jetty-island-busy}
When the boat arrives, there is a great bustle. The boatmen throw ropes to the keepers, who tie the boat to the jetty. And then the unloading begins. Barrels of paraffin oil for the light. Sacks of coal. Sacks of flour and oatmeal. Boxes of tea, sugar and soap. Crates of tinned food. Parcels and packages. All of it must be carried up the path to the lighthouse, on the little cart, or on people's backs. It takes hours.

And then, the most important thing. The mail bag. James opens it, at the kitchen table, and hands out the letters. Letters from Mairi's sister on Skye, with all the news of the family. Letters from Donald's parents on Lewis. Official letters from the Board, in Edinburgh. Newspapers, a few weeks old. Magazines. And sometimes, a parcel, with a present inside. Everyone reads their letters at once, and shares the news.

--

{img:family-reading-letters-kitchen-table-mail-bag-excitement-newspapers}
The boatmen stay for a cup of tea, and a bowl of soup, before they sail back to the mainland. They bring news and gossip from the villages. Who has married. Who has had a baby. Who has passed away. What the price of fish is. What is happening in Glasgow, and in London, and in the wider world. The families listen eagerly. It is their only contact with the outside world, apart from the letters.

On this visit, the boat brings something special for Isla. A parcel, from her aunt on Skye. Inside, there is a new book. A book about birds, with beautiful coloured pictures of every bird in Britain. Isla holds it as if it were made of gold. She turns the pages slowly, looking at the pictures. Many of the birds, she has seen on the island. And she reads their names, in English and in Latin, under her breath.

{img:girl-holding-new-bird-book-coloured-plates-joy-cottage-window}
For Calum, the boat brings a model ship, carved from wood, with sails of white cloth, sent by his uncle, who is a sailor. Calum is speechless. He carries it everywhere for weeks. He sails it in the rock pools, and in the stream, and in the washtub, until his mother tells him to stop. Then he puts it on the windowsill of his room, where he can see it from his bed, with the real sea behind it.

--

In the afternoon, the boat sails away again, back to the mainland, with the letters and the log books. The families stand on the jetty and wave, until it is just a white speck on the horizon. Then they walk back up the path, to the lighthouse, carrying the last parcels. The island is quiet again. Just the two families, the birds, the seals, and the sea. Until the next boat comes.
