The principal keeper of the lighthouse is called James MacLeod. He is about forty-five years old. He has a grey beard, kind blue eyes, and a weathered face, brown from the wind and the sun. He was born on the island of Skye, the son of a fisherman. He went to sea as a young man, as a sailor. And then, at twenty-five, he joined the Northern Lighthouse Board. He has been a keeper for twenty years, at five different lighthouses.

--

James is a quiet, careful man. He speaks both English and Gaelic, the old Celtic language of the Highlands and Islands. He reads a lot, especially history and books about the sea. He plays the fiddle, and sings old Gaelic songs. And he takes his work very seriously. In twenty years, he has never once let the light go out. He is proud of that. The light is his responsibility, and he looks after it as if it were his own child.

His wife is called Mairi. She is also from Skye. She is a strong, cheerful woman, with dark hair and rosy cheeks, who is never still. She runs the house, cooks, bakes, sews, knits, looks after the cow and the hens, works in the garden, and teaches the children. She also helps with the light, when it is needed. Keepers' wives often did. Mairi knows how to trim the wicks and wind the clockwork, as well as her husband.

{img:keepers-wife-knitting-by-window-sea-view-cottage-kitchen-warm-light}
They have two children. Isla, their daughter, is twelve. She is tall and thin, with her mother's dark hair and her father's blue eyes. She is clever and curious, and she loves the sea, the birds and the stars. She wants to know everything. She reads every book she can find. She can name every bird on the island, and every star in the sky. And she helps her father in the lighthouse, polishing the brass and cleaning the windows.

Their son, Calum, is eight. He is small and freckled, with sandy hair that never lies flat. He is full of energy, always running, climbing and exploring. He loves the rock pools on the shore, full of crabs, starfish and little fish. He loves the seals, which come to lie on the rocks near the beach. And he loves the boats. He dreams of being a sailor, like his father was, and sailing to faraway places.

--

{img:two-children-rock-pools-island-shore-crabs-starfish-bucket-summer}
In the other house, next door, lives the assistant keeper, with his wife. His name is Donald Morrison. He is about thirty, a big, cheerful man from the island of Lewis, who laughs a lot and tells funny stories. His wife is called Effie. She is young and shy, and she and Mairi have become good friends. They have a baby, a little boy called Angus, who is just learning to walk. Isla loves to look after him.

The two families live very close together, on their little island. They share everything. The work, the food, the cow's milk, the books, the news. They help each other. They eat together on special days. And they know each other very well. Sometimes, of course, they get on each other's nerves. On a small island, with nobody else to talk to, this is natural. But mostly, they are good friends, and good neighbours.

{img:two-families-eating-together-kitchen-table-lamp-evening-laughter}
The keepers have a strict timetable. One of them is always on duty, day and night. During the night, they take turns to keep watch, in the lantern room, in shifts of four hours. During the day, they clean and maintain the light, and do the other work of the station. They keep a log book, where they write down everything that happens. The weather, the ships that pass, any problems with the light. And once a month, they send a report to the Board, in Edinburgh.

--

James and Mairi have lived on this island for three years. Before that, they were at a lighthouse on the mainland, near a village, with a school and a church and a shop. Here, it is much lonelier. The nearest village is on the mainland, about fifteen kilometres away, across the sea. They only see other people when the supply boat comes. But they have come to love the island. The quiet. The sea. The birds. The light. And the children say they never want to leave.
