One summer evening, the fog comes. It comes in from the sea, very quickly, like a great grey wall, rolling over the waves. One moment, the sun is shining, and the sea is blue, and you can see the islands on the horizon. The next moment, everything is grey and white and silent. You cannot see the sea. You cannot see the beach. You can hardly see the houses, a few metres away.

--

Fog is one of the most dangerous things for ships. In fog, the light of the lighthouse cannot be seen. Not from far away. The sailors cannot see the coast, or the rocks, or each other. They cannot see where they are going. And many ships are wrecked in fog, on rocks that the captain did not know were there. So when the fog comes, the lighthouse must use another way to warn the ships. Sound.

In 1880, the island has a fog bell. It is a big bronze bell, hanging in a little tower, next to the lighthouse. It is rung by a machine, driven by clockwork, like the lens. A heavy hammer strikes the bell, every twenty seconds, day and night, as long as the fog lasts. The sound is deep and loud, and travels far over the water. Ships that hear it know that they are near the island, and must be careful.

{img:fog-bell-tower-bronze-bell-clockwork-hammer-keeper-winding-mist}
James starts the fog bell as soon as the fog comes in. He winds the clockwork, and the hammer begins to strike. Dong. And twenty seconds later, dong. And again. The sound echoes across the island, muffled by the fog. The families inside the houses can hear it clearly, through the walls. It is a lonely, slightly sad sound. But it is also a comforting one. It means that the keepers are doing their job.

Fog can last for hours, or even for days. And all that time, the bell must keep ringing. The keepers must wind the clockwork every few hours, and check that the hammer is working. At night, they must also keep the light burning, even though it can hardly be seen in the fog. Because perhaps, somewhere, the fog is thinner, and a ship can see it. It is tiring work. The keepers hardly sleep, when there is fog.

--

{img:ship-lights-faint-in-fog-distance-sea-bell-sound-careful-sailors}
On this evening, the fog lasts all night. James and Donald take turns at the fog bell and the light. Mairi brings them hot tea and bread. The children lie in their beds, listening to the bell, and the soft sound of the sea, which they cannot see. Calum cannot sleep. He asks his mother if there are ships out there, in the fog. She says that there probably are. But that they can hear the bell, and they will be careful.

In the middle of the night, James hears something, through the fog. A ship's horn. Far away, then closer. A steamer, out in the fog, sounding its horn, to warn other ships. It is coming closer to the island. James rings the bell harder. And then, faintly, through the grey mist, he sees the lights of the ship, red and green, very close. Too close. He holds his breath.

{img:lighthouse-beam-blurred-in-fog-keeper-on-balcony-watching-ship-turn-away}
And then, slowly, the ship's lights begin to turn. Away from the island. Out to sea. The captain has heard the bell. He has understood. He has changed his course. The ship's horn sounds again, three times, as if to say thank you. And then it fades away into the fog, and the lights disappear. James lets out his breath. He finds that his hands are shaking. He sits down on the step of the fog tower, and says a quiet prayer of thanks.

--

In the morning, the fog lifts, as suddenly as it came. The sun comes out. The sea is blue again, and calm. The islands appear on the horizon, as if nothing had happened. James stops the fog bell, and writes in the log book. Fog from eight in the evening until six in the morning. Fog bell sounded. Steamer passed close to the east at two in the morning, altered course. All well. And then he goes to bed, and sleeps until noon.
