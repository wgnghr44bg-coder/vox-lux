In spring and summer, the island is full of birds. Thousands and thousands of them. They come from the open sea, to nest on the cliffs and in the grass of the island, where they are safe from foxes and other animals that live on the mainland. The air is full of their cries, and the cliffs are white with their wings. It is one of the most wonderful sights of the year.

--

The most popular birds on the island are the puffins. Small, round, black and white birds, with bright orange feet, and big colourful beaks, striped red, yellow and grey. They look like little clowns. They nest in burrows, in the soft earth at the top of the cliffs, like rabbits. They stand at the entrances of their burrows, looking around, very seriously. And they fly out to sea, and come back with their beaks full of little silver fish, for their chicks.

Calum loves the puffins. He lies on his stomach at the top of the cliff, very still, and watches them for hours. They are not afraid of people. They come quite close, and look at him with their funny, sad-looking eyes. He has given names to some of them. The Captain, who has a very big beak. The Fat Lady, who is very round. And Grumpy, who always seems to be in a bad mood. Isla says that all puffins look the same. Calum says that she is completely wrong.

{img:gannets-diving-into-sea-white-wings-folded-splash-fishing-sunlight}
On the cliffs, there are other birds. Guillemots and razorbills, black and white, standing in long rows on the narrow ledges, like little soldiers. Kittiwakes, small gulls, who build their nests of seaweed on tiny ledges, and call out their own name, kitti-wake, kitti-wake. Fulmars, who glide along the cliffs on stiff wings, without ever flapping. And shags, black and green, with crests on their heads, nesting on the rocks near the water.

Out at sea, there are the gannets. Big white birds, with black wing tips and golden heads. They fly high over the sea, looking for fish. And when they see one, they fold their wings, and dive straight down into the water, like arrows, from thirty metres high, with a great splash. Isla loves to watch them diving. She says that they are the most beautiful birds in the world.

--

{img:grey-seals-lying-on-rocks-island-shore-pup-white-fur-autumn}
There are also seals. Grey seals, big and heavy, with long faces, who lie on the rocks near the beach, basking in the sun. And in the autumn, the mothers come to the beach to have their pups. The seal pups are covered in soft white fur, with big dark eyes. They lie on the beach, crying for their mothers, who come in from the sea to feed them. The children watch them from a distance, so as not to disturb them.

The seals are curious. When the children walk along the shore, the seals in the water swim along beside them, with their heads up, watching them, with their big dark eyes. Sometimes they sing. A strange, sad, beautiful sound, like a moaning or a wailing, which drifts across the water on calm evenings. The old islanders say that seals are people of the sea, called selkies, who can take off their skins and become human, on the land.

{img:whale-surfacing-near-island-spout-calm-sea-family-watching-from-cliff}
And sometimes, there are whales. Minke whales, and sometimes bigger ones, passing by the island, far out at sea. You can see their spouts, a puff of white mist, rising from the water, and their dark backs, curving through the waves. Once, a great pod of dolphins swims past the island, leaping and playing in the waves, dozens of them. The whole family runs to the cliffs to watch them, cheering, until they disappear.

--

In the autumn, the sea birds leave. They fly away, out to sea, where they will spend the winter, far from land. The cliffs become quiet and empty. The puffins disappear, all at once, one day in August. Calum is sad. He stands at the top of the cliff, looking at the empty burrows. But his father tells him that the puffins will come back, in the spring, to the very same burrows. And they always do. Every spring, Calum is waiting for them. And every spring, the Captain, with his big beak, is the first to come home.
