All night long, one of the keepers is on watch. The night is divided into watches, of about four hours each. The two keepers take turns. When one keeper is on watch, the other sleeps. Then they change. So the light is never left alone, not even for a minute, from sunset to sunrise.

--

The keeper on watch sits in a small room, called the watch room, just below the lantern. It has a little window, a desk, a chair, a stove for warmth, and a clock. From here, he can hear the clockwork ticking, and see the glow of the light above. Every hour, he climbs up into the lantern, to check the lamp, trim the wicks if needed, and wind the clockwork. And then he comes back down to the watch room, and waits.

The wicks of the lamp must be trimmed during the night. As they burn, they get charred at the top, and the flame becomes smoky and dim. So the keeper must cut off the charred part, very carefully, with special scissors, to keep the flame bright and clean. That is why lighthouse keepers were sometimes called wickies. It is a delicate job, especially with cold fingers in the middle of a winter night.

{img:keeper-trimming-wicks-lamp-small-scissors-careful-lantern-night}
The keeper must stay awake all through his watch. It is a strict rule. A keeper who is found asleep on watch can be dismissed. So he must find ways to stay awake. Some keepers read, by the light of a small lamp. Some write letters. Some carve wood. Some make little models of ships. Some knit, or mend clothes, or make rugs. James reads. He has read hundreds of books, on his night watches, in twenty years.

But he also looks out of the window. The keeper must watch the sea, and the sky, and the weather. He must notice if a ship is in trouble, or if fog is coming. On clear nights, the sky is full of stars, more than anyone in a town has ever seen. Sometimes, in the north, the sky is lit up with the northern lights, great curtains of green and pink light, moving slowly across the sky. James never gets tired of watching them.

--

{img:northern-lights-green-curtains-over-lighthouse-sea-keeper-balcony}
On calm nights, the lighthouse is very peaceful. The only sounds are the ticking of the clockwork, the soft hiss of the lamp, and the waves breaking gently on the rocks below. Sometimes, a sea bird calls. Sometimes, far away, a ship passes, with its little lights, red and green, moving slowly along the horizon. The keeper watches it, and knows that the captain is watching his light. And he feels that he is not alone.

At midnight, James's watch ends, and Donald comes up the stairs, yawning, with a mug of tea. They talk for a few minutes. James tells him about the weather, the ships he has seen, and anything else that has happened. He writes in the log book. Then he goes down the long spiral stairs, and across the yard, under the stars, to his house. And Donald takes over, until four in the morning.

{img:keeper-walking-across-yard-night-stars-to-cottage-lantern-in-hand}
In the house, everything is dark and quiet. Mairi and the children are asleep. James takes off his boots, quietly, and climbs into bed, beside his wife. Through the window, he can see the beam of the lighthouse, sweeping across the sea, every thirty seconds. It lights up the bedroom for a moment, very faintly, and then is gone. And then again. Like a slow heartbeat. James falls asleep, counting the flashes, as he always does.

--

Isla sometimes wakes up in the night, and sees the beam, passing across her window. It is a comforting sight. She knows that her father, or Donald, is up there, keeping watch. She knows that the ships are safe. She knows that, as long as the light is turning, everything is all right. And she turns over, and pulls the blanket up to her chin, and goes back to sleep, with the beam passing softly over her, again and again.
