On the morning after the storm, Isla goes up to the top of the lighthouse with the telescope, to look at the sea. The waves are still big, rolling in from the west. And then she sees something. On the rocks at the north end of the island, there is a small boat. A fishing boat, with a broken mast, stuck between two rocks, with the waves breaking over it. And on the boat, there are people, waving.

--

She runs down the stairs, shouting for her father. James is asleep, but he wakes at once. He grabs the telescope, and looks. Three men, on a fishing boat, wrecked on the rocks in the night. They must have been caught in the storm, and blown onto the island in the darkness. They are holding on to the boat, which is slowly breaking up, as the waves hit it. They will not last long.

James and Donald run to the north end of the island, with ropes, a life belt and a boat hook. Mairi and Effie follow, with blankets. Isla runs with them. The children have been told to stay at home, but Isla is twelve, and she refuses. When they reach the rocks, they see the boat, about thirty metres from the shore, with the three men clinging to it. Between them and the shore, there is a channel of rough water, with waves crashing through it.

{img:keepers-throwing-rope-from-rocks-to-wrecked-boat-waves-crashing}
James takes the rope, and throws it, as hard as he can, toward the boat. It falls short, into the water. He pulls it back, and throws again. Short again. The wind is blowing it back. Donald tries. He is bigger and stronger. His rope reaches the boat. One of the men grabs it, and ties it around himself. And then, slowly, carefully, James and Donald pull him through the water, toward the rocks. He is battered by the waves, but he holds on. And at last, they pull him up onto the rocks, coughing and shivering, but alive.

They do it again, for the second man. And then the third, who is the oldest, and very tired, and cannot hold on very well. The waves knock him under the water, twice. Everyone holds their breath. But James and Donald pull and pull, and at last he comes up, and they drag him onto the rocks. All three are safe. Mairi and Effie wrap them in blankets. Isla bursts into tears of relief. And the three fishermen sit on the rocks, too exhausted to speak.

--

{img:rescued-fishermen-wrapped-blankets-by-fire-keepers-cottage-hot-tea}
They carry the fishermen back to the houses, and put them by the fire, with dry clothes, hot tea and hot soup. Slowly, the men recover. They are from a village on the mainland. Their boat was caught by the storm, the evening before, on their way home. Their mast broke. They drifted all night, in the dark and the wind. And then, in the early morning, they were thrown onto the rocks of the island. They thought they would never survive.

The oldest fisherman, a man with a white beard, called Old Angus, takes James's hand, and holds it for a long time, without saying anything. Then he says that he saw the light, all through the night, as they drifted. And that it was the only thing that gave them hope. He says that he will never forget the light of this island. Or the people who keep it.

{img:old-fisherman-shaking-keepers-hand-gratitude-cottage-doorway-sea}
The fishermen stay on the island for a week, until the sea is calm enough for the relief boat to come and take them home. They help with the work. They mend the garden wall. They tell stories by the fire in the evenings, and sing old songs. Calum follows them everywhere. And when they leave, Old Angus gives Calum his fishing knife, with a handle of deer horn. Calum keeps it for the rest of his life.

--

There is a famous story, from England, about a lighthouse keeper's daughter called Grace Darling. In 1838, at the age of twenty-two, she helped her father to row a small boat through a terrible storm, to rescue nine people from a wrecked ship. She became a national heroine. Isla has read the story many times. She has always wanted to be like Grace Darling. And now, she thinks, she has helped to save three lives too. Not by rowing, but by watching. By being the first to see.
