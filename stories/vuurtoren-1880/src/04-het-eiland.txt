The island is small. You can walk all the way around it in about two hours. It is made of grey rock, covered with short grass and heather, and patches of wild flowers in summer. There are cliffs on the west side, facing the open Atlantic, where the waves crash and the sea birds nest. And on the east side, there is a little bay, with a sandy beach and a stone landing jetty, where the boats come in.

--

There are no trees on the island. The wind is too strong. Only a few small bushes grow, in sheltered places, bent over by the wind. But there is grass, enough for a cow and a few sheep. And in summer, the island is covered with flowers. Pink sea thrift, on the cliffs. Yellow primroses and buttercups. Purple heather. White daisies. It is very beautiful.

The lighthouse stands on the highest point of the island, near the western cliffs. It is a round tower, of stone, painted white, about thirty metres tall. At the top, there is the lantern, a room of glass, with a little balcony around it, and a dome on top, with a weather vane. Next to the tower, there is a long, low building, also painted white, with the houses of the keepers. Two houses, side by side, one for each keeper and his family.

{img:keepers-cottages-white-walls-slate-roof-chimneys-smoke-walled-yard}
Around the houses, there is a high stone wall, to protect them from the wind. Inside the wall, there is a yard, with a washing line, a coal shed, a store room for the oil, and a workshop. There is also a walled garden, where the families grow vegetables. Potatoes, cabbages, carrots, turnips and onions. The walls protect the plants from the salty wind. Without them, nothing would grow.

There is also a little building for the fog signal. In 1880, it is a big bell, which is rung by a machine, driven by clockwork, when there is fog. In later years, it will be replaced by a great foghorn, driven by compressed air, which can be heard for many kilometres. And there are two little huts, one for the cow, and one for the hens. And a peat stack, a big pile of dried peat, which the families burn in their fires.

--

{img:island-landing-jetty-stone-steps-small-bay-sandy-beach-boat-moored}
From the lighthouse to the landing jetty, there is a path, about a kilometre long, across the island. It is the only road. Everything that comes to the island must be carried along it. Food, coal, oil, letters, furniture and people. There is a little cart, pulled by hand, or by the pony that belongs to the station. In bad weather, the path is muddy and slippery. In good weather, it is a lovely walk, with larks singing overhead.

There are no other people on the island. Only the two keepers and their families. Long ago, there was a small farming village on the island. But the people left, more than fifty years ago, to go to the mainland, or to America. The ruins of their houses are still there, on the east side, near the beach. Little stone walls, overgrown with grass and nettles. The keepers' children like to play there, and imagine the people who lived there long ago.

{img:ruined-crofters-houses-stone-walls-grass-island-sea-view-evening}
From the top of the lighthouse, you can see far out to sea, in every direction. To the west, the open Atlantic, stretching away to the horizon, and beyond it, America. To the east, the mountains of the mainland, blue and purple in the distance, often with clouds on their tops. And to the north and south, other islands, some big, some small, scattered across the sea. On a clear day, it is one of the most beautiful views in the world.

--

The island is a world of its own. The keepers and their families know every rock, every pool, every bird's nest and every patch of flowers on it. It is small. But it is their home. And to them, it is the most important island in the world. Because on it stands the light. And the light must never go out.
