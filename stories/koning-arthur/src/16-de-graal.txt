The greatest adventure of the knights of the Round Table is the quest for the Holy Grail. It is the most mysterious part of the whole legend. And it is the adventure that changes Camelot forever.

--

The Grail first appeared in a story by the French poet Chrétien de Troyes, around the year 1190. He wrote about Percival, the young knight from the forest. One evening, Percival comes to a strange castle, ruled by a king who is wounded and cannot walk. The king spends his days fishing in a boat on the river, so he is called the Fisher King. That night, during the meal, Percival sees a strange procession. A young man carrying a bleeding lance. And a maiden carrying a shining dish, made of gold and jewels, which fills the whole hall with light. It is the Grail.

Percival is curious. But he has been taught not to ask too many questions. So he says nothing. In the morning, the castle is empty, and then it vanishes. Later, Percival learns that if he had only asked one question, whom does the Grail serve, the wounded king would have been healed, and the land around him, which was dry and barren, would have become green again. Chrétien never finished his story. He passed away, and left the mystery unsolved.

{img:fisher-king-boat-river-mist-wounded-old-king-fishing-quiet-castle}
Other writers continued the story. And over time, the Grail became something holy. Writers said it was the cup that Jesus used at the Last Supper, with his disciples. They said that a man called Joseph of Arimathea brought it to Britain, long ago. And they said that it was hidden in a secret castle, and that only the purest knight in the world could find it.

In the later stories, the Grail appears at Camelot, at the feast of Pentecost. The doors and windows close by themselves. A great light fills the hall, brighter than the sun. And the Grail floats through the hall, covered with a white cloth, so that nobody can see it. Every knight is filled with joy, and finds in front of him the food he loves most. Then the Grail disappears. And all the knights stand up and swear to search for it, for a year and a day.

--

{img:knights-riding-out-from-camelot-different-directions-forest-dawn-quest}
So the knights ride out from Camelot, each alone, in different directions, into the great forests. Arthur is very sad. He knows that many of them will never come back to the Round Table. The quest is not like other adventures. It is not about strength or bravery. It is about goodness, honesty and purity. The knights must look inside themselves, as well as in the forests.

Many knights fail. They are good men, but not good enough for the Grail. Gawain fails, because he cares more for adventures than for prayer. Lancelot comes very close. He sees the Grail, in a vision. But he cannot reach it, because of his love for the queen. He is sad, but he accepts it. Only three knights succeed. Galahad, the purest knight of all. Percival, the boy from the forest, who has learned to ask the right questions. And Bors, a humble and good knight.

{img:three-knights-kneeling-before-light-chapel-grail-galahad-percival-bors}
The three knights find the Grail in a holy city by the sea, far away. Galahad looks into the Grail, and sees heavenly things that cannot be told. He is so happy that he asks to leave this world. And a hand comes down from heaven, and takes the Grail up into the sky, and Galahad's soul with it, gently. Percival becomes a hermit, and lives a quiet life of prayer. And Bors, alone, rides home to Camelot, to tell Arthur everything.

--

What does the Grail mean? People have wondered about this for eight hundred years. Some say it is about the search for God. Some say it is about looking for the best part of yourself. Some say it is about healing, of people and of the land. And some say that the important thing is not to find the Grail at all, but to keep searching, and to ask the right questions. Perhaps that is the real lesson of Percival. Always ask. Whom does it serve?
