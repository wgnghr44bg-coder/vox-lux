Tonight we travel back about fifteen hundred years, to a time of mist and legend. We go to the west of Britain, around the year 500. It is a land of green hills, deep forests, rocky coasts and quiet lakes. The Romans have gone. Their towns are crumbling. And in the hills, on top of old earthen forts, new lords are building wooden halls. Somewhere in this world, the stories say, there was a king called Arthur.

--

It is early morning, and a thick white mist lies over the valleys. Only the tops of the hills rise above it, like islands in a pale sea. On one of these hills, there are rings of grassy banks and ditches, one inside the other, made long ago by people who lived here before the Romans. And on top of the hill, inside the banks, there is a long hall of timber, with smoke rising from its roof.

The sun is rising behind the hills. It turns the mist pink and gold. A cock is crowing. Somewhere, a horse is neighing. A man in a woollen cloak walks along the top of the bank, looking out over the sea of mist. And far below, in the valley, a bell is ringing in a little church. It is a quiet morning, in a quiet land, in a time we know very little about.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

King Arthur is one of the most famous figures in the world. Everyone has heard of him. The king who pulled a sword from a stone. Who ruled from his castle at Camelot. Who sat with his knights at the Round Table. Who had a wise wizard called Merlin, and a magic sword called Excalibur. And who, at the end, was carried away to a magical island called Avalon, where he sleeps, until Britain needs him again.

But was there ever a real King Arthur? Nobody knows for sure. Historians have argued about it for hundreds of years. There is very little written evidence from the time when he is supposed to have lived. And the stories about him were written down much later, and changed and grew with every telling. Like a little stream that becomes a great river, the legend of Arthur grew over the centuries.

{img:hill-fort-great-hall-timber-firelight-warriors-bard-listening-dark-age}
Tonight, we will follow both the history and the legend. First, we will visit the real Britain of around the year 500. We will see how people lived after the Romans left. We will visit a hill fort with a great wooden hall, and a rocky castle by the sea, where ships came from far away. We will listen to the bards, the poets who sang about heroes, by the fire.

--

Then we will follow the legend, as it grew. We will meet Merlin, and the sword in the stone, and the Lady of the Lake. We will visit Camelot, and sit at the Round Table. We will meet the knights, Gawain and Lancelot and Percival, and follow them on their quest for the Holy Grail. And at the end, we will travel to Avalon, the island of apples, hidden in the mist.

{img:avalon-island-mist-lake-apple-trees-boat-dawn-mysterious-calm}
There are a few battles in the stories of Arthur. We will see them only from a great distance, like a storm far away over the hills. Because most of all, the legend of Arthur is about other things. About friendship and loyalty. About kindness and courage. About a dream of a good kingdom, where the strong protect the weak. So let us begin, in the mist, on a hill in the west of Britain, around the year 500.
