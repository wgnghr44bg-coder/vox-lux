The Round Table was famous for its knights. There were many of them, and each had his own stories. Let us meet a few of the most famous ones, gently, as if we were sitting in the great hall of Camelot, watching them come in to the feast.

--

First, there is Sir Kay. He is Arthur's foster brother, the son of Sir Ector, who raised Arthur as a boy. He is the steward of Camelot, in charge of the household and the kitchens. In the old Welsh stories, he is a great hero, with magical powers. But in the later stories, he is often a bit grumpy and sharp-tongued. He makes fun of the young knights. But he is loyal to Arthur, always.

Then there is Sir Gawain. He is Arthur's nephew, the son of Arthur's sister. He is one of the oldest knights in the stories. He is brave, strong and very courteous, especially to ladies. In some stories, his strength grows during the morning, and is greatest at noon, like the sun, and then fades in the afternoon. He has a famous horse called Gringolet. Many stories were written about Gawain, and he was one of the most popular knights of all.

{img:sir-gawain-riding-horse-gringolet-green-forest-pentangle-shield}
Then there is Sir Lancelot of the Lake. He is the greatest knight of all, in the later stories. He was raised by the Lady of the Lake, in her palace under the water, which is how he got his name. He is the bravest and the strongest. But he is also gentle and sad, because he loves Queen Guinevere, though he knows he should not. That love causes great sorrow in the end. But the stories always say that Lancelot was the truest friend Arthur ever had.

Then there is Sir Percival. He grew up in a forest, far from other people, with his mother, who wanted to keep him away from knights and danger. He knew nothing about the world. One day, he saw some knights riding through the forest, in their shining armour. He thought they were angels. And he decided to become a knight himself. He came to Camelot as a simple country boy, and everyone laughed at him. But he became one of the best knights of all.

--

{img:young-percival-forest-boy-seeing-knights-first-time-amazed-sunbeams}
Then there is Sir Gareth. He came to Camelot as a young man, without telling anyone his name. He asked Arthur to let him work in the kitchens for a year. Sir Kay made fun of him, and gave him a nickname, Beaumains, which means beautiful hands, because his hands were so fine. After a year, Gareth went on an adventure, and showed that he was a great knight. And then it was revealed that he was actually Gawain's younger brother, a prince.

There is Sir Bedivere, who was always loyal to Arthur, and who will be with him at the very end. There is Sir Tristan, a sad young knight from Cornwall, who played the harp beautifully and loved a lady called Isolde. There is Sir Galahad, the son of Lancelot, who is so pure and good that he is the only one who can sit in the dangerous seat. And there are many, many others.

{img:knights-and-ladies-courtly-scene-garden-harp-tristan-playing-gentle}
Each knight has his own character. Some are brave and wild. Some are gentle and wise. Some are funny. Some are sad. Some make mistakes, and learn from them. That is one of the reasons why the stories of the Round Table are so loved. They are not about perfect heroes. They are about people, with their strengths and their weaknesses, trying to live up to a beautiful promise.

--

In the Middle Ages, real knights read or listened to these stories, and tried to be like the knights of the Round Table. Children played at being Lancelot or Gawain. Ladies embroidered their stories on cushions and tapestries. And in castles all over Europe, people sat by the fire in the evenings, listening to a storyteller, and imagining themselves at the Round Table, in the great hall of Camelot.

And of all the knights, the one Arthur trusts most is perhaps Sir Bedivere. He is not the strongest or the most famous. He has few great adventures of his own. But he is always there, quietly, at the king's side. In the oldest Welsh stories, he is one of Arthur's first companions. And at the very end of the legend, it is Bedivere who stays with the king, when everyone else has gone.
