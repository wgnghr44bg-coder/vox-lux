On a summer day, in 1888, many Londoners go up the river.

From the piers, at Westminster, and Charing Cross, the little passenger steamboats leave, every few minutes, crowded with families, and couples, and children, in their summer clothes, and straw hats, going up the river, to Chelsea, and Putney, and Kew, and Richmond, and Hampton Court.

--

As the boat goes upriver, the city slowly falls behind. The great buildings, and the warehouses, and the factories, give way to houses, with gardens, running down to the water, and boathouses, and willow trees, and meadows, and the river becomes narrower, and quieter, and greener.

At Kew, there are the Royal Botanic Gardens: wide lawns, and great trees, from all over the world, and flower beds, and lakes, and the great glasshouses, of iron, and glass, the Palm House, warm, and steamy, inside, full of tropical palms, and plants, and the Temperate House, and the Chinese Pagoda, rising above the trees.

{img:kew-gardens-palm-house-glasshouse-victorian-visitors-lawn-summer}
At Richmond, there is the hill, with its famous view, over the bend of the river, and the meadows, and the woods, which painters have painted for a hundred years. And there are boats to hire, and people row on the river, the young men in their striped blazers, and boaters, and the young ladies in white, with parasols, trailing their fingers, in the cool water.

--

This is the great age of boating on the Thames. Every summer weekend, the river, above London, is crowded with rowing boats, and punts, and skiffs, and canoes, and little steam launches, and houseboats, moored along the banks, with flowers in boxes, on their roofs.

In 1889, the year after our story, a writer, Jerome K. Jerome, will publish a funny book, about three young men, and a dog, who take a boat trip up the Thames, called Three Men in a Boat. It will become one of the best-loved comic books in the English language, and it is still read, and loved, today.

{img:rowing-boats-punts-river-thames-willows-summer-afternoon-picnic}
Further up, at Hampton Court, there is the great Tudor palace of King Henry the Eighth, with its red brick, and its chimneys, and its gardens, and its famous maze, where the visitors get lost, and laugh, and try to find the way out.

--

And in the evening, as the sun sets, the steamboats carry the tired, happy families, back down the river, toward the city, through the golden light, past the willows, and the meadows, and the boathouses, and the children fall asleep, on their mothers' laps, and the lights of London begin to shine, ahead of them, in the dusk.
