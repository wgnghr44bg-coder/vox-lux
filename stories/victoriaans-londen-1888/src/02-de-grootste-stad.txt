In 1888, London is enormous.

It has grown and grown all through the century. In 1800, about a million people lived here. Now there are more than five and a half million. And every year tens of thousands more arrive. They come from the countryside and from Ireland and Scotland. They come from all over Europe and the world. They are looking for work and a new life.

--

The city spreads out for miles in every direction. Street after street of houses and shops. Churches and factories and warehouses. Railway lines and chimneys. The city swallows up the old villages and fields around it. Places that were quiet villages fifty years ago are now part of London. Long straight streets of new brick houses join them to the centre.

Every morning, hundreds of thousands of people pour into the centre to work. They come by train and by bus and on foot across the bridges. And every evening they pour out again.

{img:london-bridge-crowds-workers-walking-morning-fog-carts}
The heart of London is the City. It is the old square mile inside the ancient walls. The Romans built a town here nearly two thousand years ago. Now it is full of banks and offices. From here, people run the trade of half the world. In the daytime its streets are packed with clerks in black coats and top hats. They hurry from office to office. At night and on Sundays it is almost empty and silent.

--

To the west is the West End. This is the London of the rich and the fashionable. There are tall white houses and quiet squares. There are the great shops and the theatres and the parks.

To the east is the East End. This is the London of the docks and the workshops and the poor. It is crowded and busy and noisy. Sailors and dock workers live here. So do newcomers from far away, and workers of a thousand trades.

{img:victorian-terraced-houses-new-suburb-street-brick-lamp-posts}
And all around are the suburbs. This is the new London of clerks and shopkeepers and teachers. They live in long rows of neat brick houses. Each house has a little front garden and a bay window and iron railings.

--

London is noisy. Iron wheels and horses' hooves clatter on the stones. Street sellers shout. Carts rumble. Trains whistle and church bells ring.

And London is dirty. The air is full of smoke from a million coal fires. The streets are full of mud and horse droppings. Little boys with brooms sweep a path across the road for ladies and gentlemen. In return they hope for a penny.

--

But London is also magnificent and exciting and full of life. Every visitor who comes here, from anywhere in the world, is amazed by it.
