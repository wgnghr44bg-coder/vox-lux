Beside the Tower of London, in 1888, something new is rising from the river.

Two great piers, of granite, and concrete, have been sunk, into the bed of the Thames, and on top of them, workers are building two tall towers, of steel. It is a new bridge: Tower Bridge.

--

For many years, the people of the East End have needed a new bridge, across the river, below London Bridge. But the bridge must not stop the tall ships, from sailing up the river, to the wharves, and the Pool of London, beside the Tower.

So the engineer, Sir John Wolfe Barry, and the architect, Sir Horace Jones, designed a very special bridge: a bascule bridge, with two great leaves, or arms, in the middle, which can be lifted up, like a drawbridge, to let the tall ships pass through, and then lowered again, for the traffic.

{img:workers-riveting-steel-high-tower-bridge-river-below-boats}
The building began in 1886, and it will take eight years. Hundreds of workers are building it, riveting the steel together, high above the river, without safety nets.

--

The towers are being built of steel, for strength, but they will be covered with stone, granite, and Portland stone, so that they look like old Gothic towers, to match the Tower of London, beside them. High up, between the towers, there will be two walkways, so that people can cross, even when the bridge is raised.

And the great arms of the bridge will be lifted, by steam engines, hidden in the piers, powering hydraulic machinery, so that they rise, smoothly, and quickly, in about a minute.

--

When it is finished, in 1894, it will be opened by the Prince of Wales, and it will become the most famous bridge in the world, the symbol of London.

{img:tower-of-london-white-tower-walls-river-thames-autumn-afternoon}
Beside it, the Tower of London, which is already eight hundred years old, looks on, as it has looked on at everything, since William the Conqueror built it. The ravens hop about on the grass. The Yeoman Warders, the Beefeaters, in their dark blue uniforms, show the visitors around, and tell them the old stories. And the Crown Jewels glitter, behind their railings.

--

In 1888, many Londoners come to watch the new bridge being built. They stand on the riverbank, and on London Bridge, and look at the great steel towers, rising up, higher every month, and wonder what it will look like, when it is finished.

Today, more than a hundred and thirty years later, Tower Bridge still opens, several hundred times a year, for tall ships, and sailing boats, and cruise ships, and the traffic stops, and people stop, and watch, as the great arms rise up, slowly, into the sky.
