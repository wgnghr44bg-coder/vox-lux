Every evening at dusk, the lamplighters come into the streets of London.

There are about five hundred of them. Each one has his own round of streets, with perhaps a hundred lamps or more. They carry a long pole with a little flame at the top and a hook. They walk quickly from lamp to lamp. They reach up and open the little glass door at the top. They turn on the gas and light it. And they move on to the next one, without stopping.

--

Behind them, one by one, the lamps come on all along the street. Small soft yellow lights in the blue dusk, like a string of beads. Children run behind the lamplighters to watch. And the lamplighters wave to them.

In the morning at dawn, they come again. They put the lamps out one by one. They clean the glass. And they go home to sleep.

{img:gaslit-london-square-evening-lamps-glowing-rain-wet-pavement}
The gas comes from gasworks beside the river. There, coal is heated in big ovens to make the gas. The gas is stored in huge round iron tanks. They rise and sink as they fill and empty. From there, pipes under the streets carry the gas to every lamp and every house in London.

--

Gaslight is soft and warm and golden. It flickers. It does not light up the whole street like electric light does today. It makes little pools of light under each lamp, with shadows in between. On a misty evening, each lamp has a soft halo around it. On a rainy evening, the light shimmers on the wet pavements.

Inside the houses there is gaslight too. The lamps hiss gently. You light them with a match and turn them up or down with a little tap. In poorer homes there are oil lamps and candles.

{img:victorian-bedroom-candle-washstand-window-gaslamp-street-below}
When people go up to bed, they carry a candle up the dark stairs. The little flame makes shadows dance on the walls. From the bedroom window they can look down into the street. The gas lamp glows softly below, all night long. A policeman walks slowly past. A cab clatters home in the distance. And the church clock strikes the hours.

--

Electric light is coming. A few streets and theatres and grand houses already have it. It is bright and white and steady. But for many years yet, the soft golden gaslight will light the streets of London. And the lamplighters will walk their rounds at dusk and dawn.

Even today, in some streets of London, old gas lamps still burn every evening, just as they did in 1888.
