Over all of this, there is the old Queen.

Queen Victoria became queen when she was only eighteen. She was a small young girl with blue eyes and fair hair. One early morning she was woken up and told the news. The king, her uncle, had passed away. Now she was the queen.

--

In 1888 she is sixty-nine. She has ruled for more than fifty years. She is small and round and always dressed in black, with a little white lace cap. In her photographs she looks stern. But people who know her say she laughs and has a lovely smile.

She has worn black for more than twenty-five years. Her beloved husband, Prince Albert, passed away when he was only forty-two. She was heartbroken. For many years she hid away from the world. She stayed at her castles and houses far from London. People called her the Widow of Windsor.

{img:golden-jubilee-1887-procession-open-carriage-crowds-flags-london}
But last year she came out again. She had been queen for fifty years. And the whole country wanted to celebrate.

--

There was a great procession through the streets of London to Westminster Abbey. The Queen rode in an open carriage pulled by cream-coloured horses. Around her rode princes from all over Europe. Many of them were her own sons and grandsons. Soldiers came from all over the Empire. She would not wear a crown. She wore a bonnet with diamonds and lace instead.

Hundreds of thousands of people lined the streets and cheered. There were flags and banners and flowers everywhere. In the evening, bonfires were lit on the hills all over the country, one after another. There were fireworks and parties. Poor people were given dinners. And children were given mugs and medals to remember the day.

{img:jubilee-street-party-children-tables-bunting-mugs-1887}
The Queen wrote in her diary that she would never forget that day. The crowds had been so kind and so happy.

--

The Queen has nine children and many grandchildren. They have married into the royal families of Europe. So people call her the grandmother of Europe. Her eldest daughter is the Empress of Germany. This year has been sad for her. In June, the daughter's husband passed away, after being emperor for only ninety-nine days. Now the Queen's grandson William is the new emperor of Germany. He is only twenty-nine.

The Queen works hard every day. She reads her government papers in red boxes. She writes letters and her diary. She meets her ministers.

{img:queen-victoria-indian-secretary-garden-tent-writing-letters}
And she is learning a new language. Last year two young men from India came to work for her. One of them, Abdul Karim, became her teacher. A little every day, he teaches her to speak and write a language of India. And he tells her about India. She rules it as Empress. But she has never seen it.

--

To the people of London, the Queen is far away and a little mysterious. They rarely see her. But she is always there, like the river and the Tower. Her face is on every coin and every stamp. Her name is everywhere. Soon a whole age will be named after her. And when she passes away, years later, after nearly sixty-four years as queen, it will feel to many people as if the world itself has changed.
