The Victorians love sport.

Many of the great sports of the world got their rules in England in these years. Football and rugby. Tennis and boxing. And many more.

--

Cricket is the great summer game. In 1888 a team from Australia is visiting England. They play England in three big matches. Australia wins the first one. Everyone in England is worried. Will they lose the series? But England wins the next two.

Thousands of people come to watch. They sit in the sunshine in their straw hats. They watch the slow peaceful game all day long. There is the soft knock of bat on ball. And polite clapping drifts across the green grass.

And football is becoming the great game of working people.

--

The rules of football were written in London twenty-five years ago, at a meeting in a pub. And in 1888, the first football league begins, with twelve clubs. Most of them are from the north and the middle of England, where football is most loved. The first season starts in September.

On Saturday afternoons, many workers now have time off. They go to watch the games in their thousands, in cloth caps and scarves. They cheer and shout. And they go home happy, or a little sad.

{img:lawn-tennis-victorian-ladies-long-dresses-garden-net-summer}
Lawn tennis is very new and very fashionable. It was invented only about fifteen years ago. The first big tennis championship at Wimbledon was held soon after. Ladies and gentlemen play it in their gardens and at clubs. The ladies play in long dresses and hats.

--

And there is the bicycle.

For years there have been bicycles in London. But they were hard to ride and dangerous. The penny farthing had a huge front wheel and a tiny back wheel. The rider sat high on top. If he hit a stone, he would fly right over the handlebars. Only brave young men rode them.

{img:safety-bicycle-1888-young-woman-riding-park-road-hat}
But three years ago, a new bicycle was invented. It has two wheels of the same size and a chain that drives the back wheel. It is just like the bicycles of today. People call it the safety bicycle. And in 1888, a Scottish animal doctor called Dunlop invents a tyre filled with air. He wants his little son's tricycle to be more comfortable on the bumpy roads.

--

Together, these two inventions will start a great bicycle craze. Soon everyone will ride bicycles. Men and women, young and old. For women most of all, the bicycle will bring a new freedom. They can go where they like, alone or with friends. And they can wear lighter, simpler clothes.

On a sunny Saturday afternoon in 1888, in a London park, the first young ladies and gentlemen wobble along the paths on their new bicycles. They ring their bells and laugh. And the older people watch and shake their heads.
