Tonight we are going nearly a hundred and forty years back, to London, in the year 1888.

--

It is an autumn evening, and the light is fading over the great city. Along the river Thames, the gas lamps are being lit, one by one, and their soft yellow light shines on the wet stones of the embankment, and on the dark water, where the barges, and the little steamboats, are making their way home.

Across the river, the Houses of Parliament rise up, golden, and grey, with the great clock tower, and its bell, Big Ben, striking the hour, deep, and slow, over the rooftops.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

London, in 1888, is the greatest city in the world.

{img:victorian-london-rooftops-chimneys-smoke-st-pauls-dome-autumn}
It is the capital of the United Kingdom, and of the British Empire, the largest empire the world has ever seen, stretching across a quarter of the earth, from Canada, to India, to Australia, to Africa, so that it is said that the sun never sets on it. And it is the largest city on earth, with more than five and a half million people, more than any city has ever had before.

Ships come up the Thames, from every corner of the world, bringing tea, and sugar, and cotton, and wool, and spices, and timber, and grain. Money flows through the banks, and the offices, of the City. Railways run out of London, in every direction, to every part of the country. And people come, from everywhere, to live, and work, here.

--

It is a city of great wealth, and of great poverty. Of grand houses, in quiet squares, with gardens, and servants, and of crowded, narrow streets, where whole families live in a single room. A city of new inventions: the telephone, and electric light, and the underground railway. And of old ways: horses, and gas lamps, and coal fires, and fog.

The old Queen, Victoria, has been on the throne for more than fifty years. Last year, in 1887, she celebrated her Golden Jubilee, and the whole city was decorated with flags, and flowers, and lit with lights, and fireworks, and crowds filled the streets to see her drive past, in her open carriage, to Westminster Abbey.

{img:hansom-cab-horse-wet-cobbled-street-gaslight-evening-london}
Tonight, we are going to walk through the streets of Victorian London, in 1888.

--

We will ride on the top of a horse omnibus, and travel underground, on the steam trains of the Metropolitan Railway. We will visit a family home in Kensington, and spend a day with a young housemaid. We will walk through the markets of Covent Garden, and Billingsgate, and down to the great docks on the river. We will see the new bridge, rising beside the Tower of London. We will spend an evening at the music hall, and at the Savoy Theatre, and walk through the fog, with the most famous detective in the world, who has only just been born, in the pages of a magazine.

So let us step out into the evening, and the gaslight, and the sound of the horses' hooves, on the wet stones, and begin.
