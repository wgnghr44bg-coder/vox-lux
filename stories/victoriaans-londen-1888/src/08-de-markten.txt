Before dawn, while most of London is still asleep, the markets are already awake.

At Covent Garden, in the middle of the West End, the great market hall is full of light and noise and colour. All night long, carts and wagons have been coming in from the farms around London. They are piled high with vegetables and fruit and flowers.

--

There are cabbages and carrots and onions and potatoes. There are big heaps of apples and pears and plums. In summer there are strawberries and cherries. From warmer countries come oranges and lemons and bananas and pineapples.

The porters carry baskets on their heads. They pile up ten or twelve, one on top of another. And they walk fast through the crowds without dropping a single one.

And there are flowers. Roses and violets and lilies. Daffodils in spring. Ferns and palms in pots. The flower market is full of their scent and colour.

--

Flower girls in shawls come early to buy their flowers. Then they go out into the streets with their baskets. They sell little bunches of violets to gentlemen on their way to work, for a penny.

By the river near London Bridge is the fish market, called Billingsgate. From five in the morning, fish comes in from the boats and the trains. Cod and herring and salmon and eels. Oysters and shrimps and crabs and lobsters. They lie in big piles in baskets and boxes, wet and silver.

{img:billingsgate-fish-market-porters-leather-hats-fish-baskets-river}
The fish porters wear special flat leather hats with a wide brim. They carry the heavy boxes of fish on their heads. That way the water runs off and does not go down their necks.

--

Billingsgate is famous for its noise. And for the language of its porters. People say it is the roughest in London.

There is a great meat market in a new hall of iron and glass. And in the City there is a beautiful new market with a glass roof. Its iron arches are painted red and cream and green and gold. It sells chickens and game and cheese.

{img:costermonger-barrow-fruit-street-market-gaslight-saturday-night}
All over London, in the poorer streets, there are street markets. Sellers stand by their barrows of fruit and vegetables and fish. On Saturday nights they work by the light of flickering lamps. Each one shouts out what he sells, in his own special cry.

--

There are street sellers of everything. Hot potatoes and roasted chestnuts and pies. Pea soup and ginger beer. Coffee from little stalls on the corners, for the workers early in the morning. Matches and bootlaces and newspapers. Songs printed on sheets of paper. Toys, and birds in cages.

And so every morning, the markets of London feed five and a half million people. With food from all over the country and all over the world.
