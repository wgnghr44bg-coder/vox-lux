Before dawn, while most of London is still asleep, the markets are already awake.

At Covent Garden, in the middle of the West End, the great market hall, with its columns, and its glass roofs, is full of light, and noise, and colour. All night long, the carts, and wagons, have been coming in, from the market gardens, and the farms, around London, piled high with vegetables, and fruit, and flowers.

--

There are cabbages, and carrots, and onions, and potatoes, and turnips, and celery, and lettuces, and great heaps of apples, and pears, and plums, and in the season, strawberries, and cherries, and baskets of watercress. And from further away, by train, and ship, oranges, and lemons, and bananas, and pineapples, from warm countries, far away.

The porters carry the baskets on their heads, piled up, ten, or twelve, one on top of another, balanced, and walk, quickly, through the crowds, without dropping them.

And there are flowers: roses, and violets, and lilies, and carnations, and daffodils, in spring, and chrysanthemums, in autumn, and ferns, and palms, in pots, filling the flower market with their scent, and colour.

--

The flower girls, and women, in their shawls, and their old hats, come early, to buy their flowers, and then go out, into the streets, with their baskets, to sell little bunches of violets, and buttonholes, to the gentlemen, on their way to work, for a penny.

At Billingsgate, beside the river, near London Bridge, there is the fish market. Here, from five o'clock in the morning, the fish comes in, from the boats, and the trains: cod, and haddock, and herrings, and plaice, and sole, and salmon, and eels, and oysters, and shrimps, and crabs, and lobsters, in great piles, on the stone floor, in baskets, and boxes, glittering, and wet, and silver.

{img:billingsgate-fish-market-porters-leather-hats-fish-baskets-river}
The fish porters wear special hats, of leather, flat on the top, with a broad brim, on which they carry the heavy boxes of fish, on their heads, so that the water, and the fish scales, run off, and do not go down their necks.

--

Billingsgate is famous for its noise, and for the language of its porters, which is said to be the roughest in London.

At Smithfield, in the north of the City, there is the great meat market, in a new hall, of iron, and glass. And at Leadenhall, in the heart of the City, there is a beautiful new market, built only a few years ago, in 1881, with a glass roof, and iron arches, painted in red, and cream, and green, and gold, selling poultry, and game, and meat, and cheese.

{img:costermonger-barrow-fruit-street-market-gaslight-saturday-night}
And all over London, in the streets of the poorer districts, there are street markets, where the costermongers sell fruit, and vegetables, and fish, from their barrows, and stalls, by the light of flaring naphtha lamps, on Saturday nights, crying their wares, in a hundred different cries.

--

There are street sellers of everything: of hot potatoes, and roasted chestnuts, and pies, and sheep's trotters, and pea soup, and whelks, and ginger beer, and coffee, from stalls, on the street corners, for the workers, early in the morning. And of matches, and bootlaces, and newspapers, and songs, printed on sheets of paper, and toys, and birds, in cages.

And so, every morning, the markets of London feed five and a half million people, from all over the country, and all over the world.
