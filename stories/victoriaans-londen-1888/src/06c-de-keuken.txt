Let us go down the stairs to the kitchen.

It is a big room under the street, with a stone floor and white walls. A window sits high up near the ceiling. It looks out at the pavement. So from the kitchen you can see the feet of people walking past outside.

--

In the middle stands a long wooden table. It has been scrubbed so often that it has turned white. Here the cook prepares the food. Along one wall stands a huge black iron stove. It has a fire in the middle and ovens on each side. There is a tank for hot water. On top, pans bubble all day long. The stove burns coal from morning to night. It keeps the kitchen warm. In summer it makes it very hot.

On the walls hang rows of copper pans and moulds for jellies. They are polished till they shine. There are shelves of plates and dishes. And there is the row of bells for the servants.

{img:victorian-cook-making-pastry-flour-rolling-pin-kitchen-table}
The cook is a big kind woman of about fifty. She comes from Devon, in the south west. She has worked in kitchens since she was a girl. She is the most important of the servants. She rules the kitchen. And she knows how to make everything.

--

Her day starts early and ends late. In the morning she makes breakfast and bakes bread and makes soup. The delivery men come to the little door under the street. The milkman comes with his cans. The baker brings bread. The butcher's boy brings meat. The cook checks everything and argues about the prices.

In the afternoon she makes cakes and scones and sandwiches for tea. Then she starts on dinner. Soup and fish and a roast and vegetables. And a pudding steamed in a bowl wrapped in cloth. Or a jelly from a copper mould. It wobbles and shines on its plate. Will it come out of the mould in one piece? The cook holds her breath every time. And every time, it does.

{img:victorian-larder-shelves-jars-preserves-cheese-hams-cool-room}
Next to the kitchen there is a room with a big stone sink. The dishes and pans are washed there. And there is a cool store room with stone shelves. The window is covered with fine wire, against the flies. Meat and milk and butter and cheese are kept there. There are no fridges yet. Some houses have an ice box, filled with blocks of ice brought by the ice man.

--

The cook has a very famous book. It is called Mrs Beeton's Book of Household Management. It has more than a thousand pages. It tells you everything. How to make soup. How to run a house. What to do when the children are ill.

And in the evening, when dinner is served and cleared and the dishes are washed, the cook sits down at last. She sits in her armchair by the kitchen fire with a cup of tea. She puts her feet up and reads the newspaper. The kettle sings. And the kitchen cat sleeps on the warm rug in front of the stove.
