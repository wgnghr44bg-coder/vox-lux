Let us visit a family home in Kensington, in the west of London.

The house stands on a quiet square. In the middle of the square is a private garden with tall plane trees and lawns and flowers. Iron railings surround it. Only the families who live on the square have a key. The houses around it are tall and narrow and white. They have pillars by the front doors and black iron railings. Steps lead down to a lower floor under the street.

--

The family who live here are comfortable, but not very rich. The father is a lawyer in the City. There is the mother, four children and a grandmother. And like most families of their kind in London, they have servants. There is a cook and a housemaid. There is a nursemaid for the little children. And a boy comes in every day to clean the boots and carry the coal.

The house has five floors. Down below the street are the kitchen and the cool store rooms. There is a coal cellar under the pavement. And there is a room where the servants eat.

{img:victorian-parlour-drawing-room-fireplace-piano-ferns-gaslight}
On the ground floor are the dining room and the father's study. On the first floor is the drawing room. It is the best room in the house. Here the mother welcomes her visitors in the afternoon. And here the family sits together in the evening.

--

The drawing room is full of things. Victorians love things. There are heavy velvet curtains with lace behind them. The wallpaper has patterns and so do the carpets. There is a big marble fireplace with a mirror above it. On the shelf stands a clock under a glass dome, with china figures and photos in silver frames. There are armchairs and a sofa and little tables. There is a piano. There are ferns and palms in pots. And a canary sings in its cage.

In the evening, the room is lit by gas lamps on the walls. They give a soft warm golden light, with a gentle hiss. And a coal fire burns in the grate.

{img:victorian-nursery-children-rocking-horse-toys-nanny-fireside}
Above are the bedrooms. And near the top of the house is the nursery. The children live there. They play and have lessons and eat their meals there, with the nursemaid. At the very top, under the roof, are little attic rooms where the servants sleep. They are cold in winter and hot in summer.

--

The children do not see much of their parents during the day. After tea, they come down to the drawing room for an hour. They are washed and brushed and in their best clothes. They play quietly and talk. Perhaps their mother reads to them. Then they go back up to the nursery for supper and bed.

In the nursery there is a rocking horse and a doll's house. There are tin soldiers and a wooden Noah's ark with pairs of animals. There are picture books. And there is a big guard in front of the fire, where the children's night clothes hang to warm before bed.

{img:victorian-family-dinner-table-silver-candles-father-carving}
In the evening, the parents have dinner in the dining room, at seven or eight. There is soup and fish, and then roast beef carved by the father at the table. There are vegetables and potatoes. Then there is a pudding and fruit and cheese. The housemaid serves it in her black dress and white apron.

--

After dinner they go up to the drawing room. The mother plays the piano and the eldest daughter sings. The father reads the newspaper. The grandmother sews by the fire. The gas lamps hiss softly. The clock ticks under its glass dome. And outside, in the square, the lamps are lit, and the rain falls gently on the trees.
