Under the streets of London there is something no other city in the world has yet. A railway under the ground.

The first underground railway in the world opened in London twenty-five years ago. It ran from Paddington to Farringdon, about six kilometres, under a big road.

--

It was built in a simple way. The builders dug a big ditch along the middle of the street. They built a brick tunnel inside it. Then they covered it over and put the street back on top. For years the streets were a mess. But when it was finished, it was a great success. On the first day, nearly forty thousand people rode on it.

By 1888, the lines have grown and joined together. They make a big loop around the centre of London. And there are branches running out to the suburbs.

{img:victorian-underground-train-carriages-first-class-gaslit-passengers}
The trains are pulled by steam engines. They run through the tunnels, puffing out smoke and steam. The engines are built to make as little smoke as they can. But still the tunnels and stations are often full of smoke. They smell of sulphur, like rotten eggs. And passengers sometimes arrive with black smudges on their faces.

--

Between the tunnels there are open parts where the smoke can escape. The stations have glass roofs to let the smoke out and the daylight in. Some people say the air in the tunnels is good for your lungs. Most people do not agree.

The carriages have first, second and third class. They are lit by gas lamps. Every morning and evening they are full of clerks and workers. Many ride on cheap early tickets for workmen.

{img:tunnel-digging-workers-shield-clay-candles-1880s-deep-tube}
And now, in 1888, something new is being built, deep under the ground.

--

Under the river Thames and under the City, engineers are digging a new kind of railway. It is not just under the street. It is deep down, in round iron tunnels. The workers push a big iron shield forward through the clay, inch by inch. They work by candlelight, far below the busy streets. Above them, the river flows. Will the tunnel hold? Day after day, they push on.

It will be called the City and South London Railway. Its trains will not be pulled by steam. They will run on electricity. It will open in two years' time. It will be the first deep electric underground railway in the world. And because of its round tunnels, Londoners will soon give it a name. The tube.

--

From these beginnings will grow the London Underground. Today it is a great web of lines under the whole city. Millions of people use it every day. Some of the old stations, like Baker Street, can still be seen and used today, with their brick walls and arches.
