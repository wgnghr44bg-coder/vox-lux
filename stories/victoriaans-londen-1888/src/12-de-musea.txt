In South Kensington, in the west of London, there is a whole area of great museums.

One museum is full of art and beautiful things. Furniture and silver and glass. Pots and tapestries and statues from all over the world. Later it will be called the Victoria and Albert Museum.

--

And there is the new Natural History Museum. It opened only seven years ago. It is a wonderful building, like a great church of nature. It is covered with tiles of pale blue and honey colour. Carved animals and plants climb up the pillars and peer down from the walls.

Inside is a great hall with a high roof. The glass cases are full of stuffed animals and birds and fish. There are fossils and skeletons and shining stones. And there are dinosaurs.

The idea of dinosaurs is still quite new. The word dinosaur was made up only about forty-five years ago. The scientist who made it up also started this museum. In a park in south London, there are big models of dinosaurs beside a lake. They were made more than thirty years ago. Children love to visit them. They are not quite right, as we know now. But they are still there today.

--

In the middle of London is the British Museum, with its great columns. It holds treasures from the ancient world. The famous Rosetta Stone, which helped people read old Egyptian writing. Egyptian mummies. Statues from Greece. Great stone bulls with wings, from the old lands of Iraq.

And in the middle of the museum is the Reading Room. It is a great round room under a huge dome, painted blue and gold. Long desks are arranged like the spokes of a wheel. Books line the walls all around. Scholars and writers from all over the world come here to read and study and write. Many great books of this age were written under this dome.

{img:british-museum-reading-room-dome-readers-desks-lamps-quiet}
It is very quiet in the Reading Room. There is only the sound of pages turning and pens scratching, and now and then a soft cough.

--

The museums are free and open to everyone. On Sundays and holidays, crowds of working families come. They look at the treasures and the animals and the machines. The Victorians believe that museums and libraries and parks will make people's lives better and happier and wiser.

New free libraries are opening all over London too. Anyone can borrow books there. Anyone can read the newspapers in warm bright reading rooms.

{img:science-museum-steam-engines-machines-victorian-families-looking}
There are wonderful machines to see as well. Steam engines and trains and model ships. Clocks and telescopes. Among them is the Rocket, one of the very first steam trains, built nearly sixty years ago.

--

And so on a rainy afternoon, Londoners can spend hours wandering through the warm halls. They walk among the dinosaurs and the mummies and the steam engines. It costs nothing. Then they step out into the gaslight and the rain, with their heads full of wonders.
