The Vikings counted the year in winters, not in years.

[pause]

In the old Norse world, there were really only two seasons. A long summer of light, and a long winter of darkness. When someone spoke of an old man, they might say he had lived through seventy winters. Winter was not just a time of year. It was the measure of a life. Every winter survived was something to be counted, and remembered, and quietly celebrated.

[long-pause]

A Viking longhouse had no chimney at all.

[pause]

In the middle of the house burned a long open hearth, and the smoke simply drifted up into the dark roof beams. It slipped out slowly through a small hole, or through the thatch itself. The air inside was warm and hazy, and it smelled of wood smoke, wool, and dried fish. The smoke even helped. It kept the roof dry, and it preserved the meat that hung above the fire through the long winter months.
