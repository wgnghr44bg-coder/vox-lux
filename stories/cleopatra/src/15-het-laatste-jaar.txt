The last year of Cleopatra's life, from the autumn of 31 to the summer of 30 before Christ, is a strange, sad and beautiful time.

She does not give up. She makes plans. She has some of her ships dragged over land to the Red Sea. Perhaps she hopes to escape with her children and her treasure to India or Arabia. But desert people burn the ships.

--

She sends messages to Octavian, asking him to let her children rule Egypt. He does not answer, or answers coldly.

Meanwhile, in Alexandria, Antony and Cleopatra close their old club of friends and start a new one. They call it the Partners in Death, the friends who will die together. All winter long they hold feasts and parties, with music and dancing every night, as if there were no tomorrow.

{img:evening-feast-palace-hall-friends-lamps-music-quiet-sadness}
Caesarion, now sixteen, is welcomed as a grown young man in a great ceremony. The people of Alexandria feast for days. Cleopatra wants them to see that Egypt has a young king who can rule after her.

--

She also builds a tomb for herself. It is a great stone building next to a temple of Isis, by the sea, near the palace. She has all her greatest treasures carried into it. Gold and silver, pearls and emeralds, ebony and ivory. And piles of wood and kindling. If Octavian tries to take her treasure, she can burn it all.

In the summer of 30 before Christ, Octavian arrives at Alexandria with his army.

On the last night before he enters the city, an old writer tells a strange story. In the middle of the night, when Alexandria was quiet and full of fear, people suddenly heard music. Instruments of every kind, voices singing, and a joyful crowd dancing and shouting. The sound passed right through the city, to the gate facing the enemy. Then it went out through the gate, and faded away. The people believed it was the god of wine, whom Antony loved, leaving him.

--

The next day, the first of August, Antony's last soldiers and his fleet went over to Octavian. Antony believed all was lost. He heard a false report that Cleopatra was dead, and ended his own life. We will not describe it. At the end he was brought to Cleopatra in her tomb, and died in her arms.

Some days later, Cleopatra chose to end her own life too. She would not be taken to Rome and led in chains through the streets in Octavian's victory parade.

{img:empty-royal-chamber-golden-couch-diadem-figs-basket-soft-light}
The story says a farmer brought her a basket of figs. Hidden among the figs was a small snake, an Egyptian cobra. The cobra was the royal snake of Egypt, the sign of the pharaohs. Octavian's messengers found her lying on a golden couch in her royal robes, with her two faithful maids beside her.

One of the Romans asked a maid: was this well done? And she answered: it was very well done, and fitting for a queen descended from so many kings.

--

Cleopatra was thirty-nine. She had ruled Egypt for twenty-two years.

Octavian, despite his anger, gave orders that she should be buried beside Antony, as she had wished, with royal honours, in her tomb by the sea. That tomb has never been found.

{img:sea-at-dusk-alexandria-lighthouse-glow-quiet-calm-ending}
With her death, three hundred years of her family's rule came to an end. And so did three thousand years of the pharaohs. Egypt became a province of Rome.
