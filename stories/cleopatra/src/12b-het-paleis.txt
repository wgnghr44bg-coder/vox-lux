What was life like in the palace of the Ptolemies, in Alexandria, in Cleopatra's time?

The palace was a world of its own: a great complex of buildings, by the sea, full of people. There were the royal family, and their children, and their nurses, and tutors. The courtiers, called the friends of the king, and the kinsmen of the king, with their titles, and their purple robes, and their gold. The ministers, and the generals, and the officials, and the scribes. The guards, Macedonian and Greek and Gallic. The doctors, and the astronomers, and the scholars. The cooks, and the servants, and the musicians, and the dancers.

--

The rooms were splendid, in a mixture of Greek and Egyptian styles: floors of marble, and mosaic, walls of coloured stone, and painted plaster, columns of granite, and ebony, and ivory, and gold. There were gardens, with fountains, and shady colonnades, and pools, and rare plants, and birds, and animals, from Africa, and India.

The Roman poet Lucan, describing a banquet that Cleopatra gave for Julius Caesar, says that the roof was coated with gold, and the walls with marble, and onyx, and ebony, and the doors with tortoiseshell, and emeralds, and that the couches were covered with purple, and gold, and that the guests were served by slaves of every colour, from every land.

{img:queen-reading-reports-scribes-ministers-morning-palace-office}
But Cleopatra was not idle. Her days were full of work. Every morning, she met her ministers, and read the reports from all over Egypt: about the harvests, and the flood, and the taxes, and the grain stores, and the temples, and the trade. She received ambassadors, from Rome, and Judaea, and Arabia, and Parthia, and spoke to them in their own languages. She judged disputes, and signed orders, and wrote letters. She managed one of the richest countries in the world.

--

And she was a mother, of four children, who lived with her in the palace, with their tutors, and their nurses. Caesarion had a famous tutor, a Greek scholar, named Rhodon. And the twins, and little Ptolemy, were taught by a scholar named Nicolaus of Damascus, who later wrote a history of the world.

In the evenings, there were banquets, and music, and plays, and poetry, and conversation, with scholars, and philosophers, and visitors, and friends.

{img:palace-terrace-evening-sea-view-children-mother-lamps-calm}
And sometimes, perhaps, Cleopatra walked alone, in the evening, on the terrace of her palace, on the little island of Antirhodos, by the sea, and looked out over the harbour, at the lighthouse, shining on the dark water, and at the ships, coming and going, and thought about the future of her children, and her country.
