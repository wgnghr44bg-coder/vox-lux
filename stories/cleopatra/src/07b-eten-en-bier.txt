What do the people of Egypt eat and drink in the time of Cleopatra?

The most important food is bread. The Egyptians bake many kinds. Flat round loaves, tall pointed loaves, loaves shaped like animals, and sweet breads with honey and dates. The women grind the grain on stones. A little sand from the stones often gets into the bread, and over the years it wears down people's teeth.

--

The second most important food is beer. Egyptians drink it every day, men, women and children. It is thick, cloudy and filling, made from barley bread soaked in water and left to bubble. It is not very strong. Workers are often paid partly in bread and beer.

The Greeks of Alexandria prefer wine. Some comes from vineyards around the lake behind the city, and its white wine is famous. Some comes from Greece.

{img:market-stall-onions-leeks-garlic-cucumbers-melons-figs-baskets}
The Egyptians eat lots of vegetables. Onions, leeks and garlic, which the Greeks say Egyptians love more than anything. Lettuce, cucumbers, beans and lentils. And fruit, like dates, figs, grapes, pomegranates and melons.

--

They eat fish from the Nile, the lakes and the sea, fresh or dried in the sun. They catch ducks, geese and quails in the marshes with nets. Meat is more expensive. It is eaten mostly by the rich, and at festivals.

They cook with oil, olive oil for the rich and sesame oil for ordinary people. They sweeten food with honey and dates, and season it with salt and herbs like cumin and mint.

{img:greek-banquet-alexandria-couches-fish-dishes-wine-garlands}
The rich Greeks of Alexandria hold great feasts in the Greek style, lying on couches. There are many dishes. Fish and oysters, prawns and roast meat, pastries and cakes, fruit and wine. There are garlands of flowers, perfumes, music and dancers. And at the palace, the feasts are the grandest of all.

--

And everyone, rich and poor, loves the sweets of Egypt. Honey cakes, dates stuffed with nuts, and fig cakes.

In the evenings, little food stalls line the streets of Alexandria. They sell hot bread, roasted chickpeas, fried fish, lentil soup and beer to people walking home. The smell of baking bread and roasting fish drifts through the warm evening air, along with voices, music and laughter.
