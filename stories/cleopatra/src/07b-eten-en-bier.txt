What do the people of Egypt eat and drink, in the time of Cleopatra?

The most important food is bread. The Egyptians bake many kinds: flat round loaves, and tall conical loaves, and loaves shaped like animals, and sweet breads, with honey, and dates, and figs. The bread is made from wheat, or barley, ground on stone grinders, by the women, and often contains a little sand, from the grinding stones, which wears down people's teeth.

--

The second most important food is beer. The Egyptians drink beer every day, men and women and children: a thick, cloudy, nourishing beer, made from barley bread, soaked in water, and left to ferment, and strained, sometimes flavoured with dates. It is not very strong. Workers are often paid partly in bread and beer.

The Greeks of Alexandria prefer wine, from the vineyards of the Delta, and around the lake Mareotis, whose white wine is famous, and from Greece, and the islands.

{img:market-stall-onions-leeks-garlic-cucumbers-melons-figs-baskets}
The Egyptians eat a great many vegetables: onions, and leeks, and garlic, which the Greeks say the Egyptians love more than anything, and lettuce, and cucumbers, and radishes, and beans, and lentils, and chickpeas. And fruit: dates, and figs, and grapes, and pomegranates, and melons, and the fruit of the sycamore fig, and the doum palm.

--

They eat fish, from the Nile, and the lakes, and the sea: grey mullet, and perch, and catfish, and tilapia, fresh, or salted, or dried in the sun. And birds: ducks, and geese, and pigeons, and quails, caught in the marshes with nets. Meat, beef, and mutton, and goat, is more expensive, and eaten mostly by the rich, and at festivals.

They cook with oil: olive oil, from Greece, for the rich, and sesame oil, and linseed oil, and castor oil, for ordinary people. They sweeten their food with honey, and dates. And they season it with salt, and herbs, cumin, and coriander, and dill, and mint.

{img:greek-banquet-alexandria-couches-fish-dishes-wine-garlands}
The rich Greeks of Alexandria give great banquets, in the Greek style, reclining on couches, with many dishes: fish, and seafood, oysters, and prawns, and eels, and roast meats, and game, and pastries, and cakes, and fruits, and wine, and garlands of flowers, and perfumes, and music, and dancers.

--

And everyone, rich and poor, Greek and Egyptian, loves the sweet things of Egypt: honey cakes, and dates stuffed with nuts, and fig cakes, and the sweet sticky juice of the carob pod.

In the evenings, in the streets of Alexandria, there are little food stalls, selling hot bread, and roasted chickpeas, and fried fish, and lentil soup, and beer, to the people walking home. The smell of baking bread, and roasting fish, drifts through the warm evening air, with the sound of voices, and music, and laughter.
