Today, Alexandria is a big modern city, the second city of Egypt, with more than five million people. It stretches for many kilometres along the shore.

Much of the ancient city lies buried under the modern streets. Or under the sea. Over the centuries, earthquakes and the slow sinking of the land have pulled part of the old coast, and the palace quarter, beneath the water of the harbour.

--

Along the seafront runs a long, curving promenade. In the evening, people walk there, sit in cafés, fish, and watch the sun go down over the sea.

At one end of the bay, on the spot where the lighthouse once stood, there is a sturdy pale fort, built five hundred years ago with some of the lighthouse's stones. You can walk around it and look out to sea, where the great light once shone.

And under the water of the harbour, divers have found the remains of the ancient city. Great stone blocks from the lighthouse. Columns and statues of Cleopatra's family dressed as pharaohs. Sphinxes. And the foundations of the palaces, including the little island where Cleopatra had her private palace. Some statues have been lifted out of the sea, and can be seen in museums.

--

Near the place where the old Library may have stood, there is a great new library, opened in 2002. It is a huge round building, tilted toward the sea like the rising sun. Its granite walls are carved with letters from every alphabet in the world. Inside is a vast reading room with light pouring in from above.

On a hill in the old Egyptian quarter stands one tall column of red granite, all that is left of the great temple of Serapis. Nearby there are underground tombs carved in the rock, decorated in a strange mix of Greek, Roman and Egyptian styles.

{img:bibliotheca-alexandrina-tilted-disc-building-sea-sunlight}
And along the Nile, the great temples built by Cleopatra's family still stand. Dendera, with Cleopatra and her son on its back wall. Edfu and Philae. In the 1970s, the temple of Philae was moved stone by stone to a new island, to save it from the water of a great dam.

--

The Nile still flows from the south, through the desert, to the sea. Its flood no longer comes every summer, because a great dam now holds back its water in a huge lake. But the green fields are still there along its banks, and the palm trees and the villages. And boats with white sails still glide on the water in the evening light.

So we leave Alexandria for tonight, with the evening wind coming in from the sea, and the light of the old lighthouse long gone, but the waves still breaking on the stones where it stood.
