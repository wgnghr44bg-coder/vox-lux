Tonight we are going more than two thousand years back, to the city of Alexandria in Egypt, in the year 31 before Christ.

--

On the shore of the Mediterranean Sea, at the edge of the great green Nile delta, stands a city of white marble and golden light. It has a lighthouse taller than any building in the world. It has a library holding the knowledge of the ancient world. And it has a palace by the sea, where a queen lives.

Her name is Cleopatra. She is the last pharaoh of Egypt. Tonight we will spend a little time in her world. In her city, along her river and among her people, in the last years when Egypt was still free.

If you enjoy quiet journeys into the distant past, you are welcome to subscribe to the channel, so that the next one is easy to find.

--

{img:nile-delta-green-fields-palms-canals-river-branches-haze}
Let us picture Egypt in the time of Cleopatra.

It is one of the oldest countries in the world. For three thousand years, kings called pharaohs have ruled here. They built temples, pyramids and tombs along the banks of the Nile. When Cleopatra is born, the great pyramids are already two and a half thousand years old. They are as old to her as she is to us.

--

Egypt is a strange and wonderful land. It is a long, narrow ribbon of green along a river, right through the middle of a huge desert.

The Nile flows north from the mountains of Africa, for more than a thousand kilometres, to the sea. On both sides of it, for a few kilometres, there are fields, palm trees and villages. And then, suddenly, the desert begins. Endless sand and rock under the burning sun.

In the north, near the sea, the river splits into many branches. It spreads out into a great triangle of green land, full of fields, marshes and canals. The Greeks call it the Delta, because it looks like their letter delta, a triangle.

And at the western edge of the Delta, on a strip of land between the sea and a great lake, lies the city of Alexandria.

--

Alexandria is not an old Egyptian city. It is a Greek city in Egypt.

It was founded three hundred years earlier by Alexander the Great, the young king who conquered half the world. The story goes that he marked out the plan of the city on the ground with barley flour, because there was no chalk. Then birds came and ate the flour. The fortune tellers said it was a good sign. The city would feed people from many lands.

{img:young-alexander-marking-city-plan-barley-flour-birds-coast}
They were right. In Cleopatra's time, Alexandria is the greatest city in the world after Rome. It is perhaps the most beautiful and the most learned. Half a million people may live there. Greeks and Egyptians, Jews and Syrians, Arabs and Romans, and people from every land around the sea.

--

It is a city of trade, learning, beauty and pleasure.

Its harbours are full of ships carrying Egyptian grain to Rome, and goods from Africa, Arabia and India. Its streets are wide and straight, lined with columns, temples, palaces and gardens. Its scholars are the most famous in the world. Its people are lively and witty and love music, horse races and festivals.

{img:canopic-way-colonnades-crowds-chariots-white-marble-sunlight}
And at its heart, by the sea, stands the palace of the Greek kings and queens of Egypt. Cleopatra lives there with her children and her court. It is the year 31 before Christ. And everything is about to change.
