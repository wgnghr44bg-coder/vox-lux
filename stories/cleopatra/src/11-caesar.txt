In the autumn of 48 before Christ, Julius Caesar, the greatest general in Rome, sailed into Alexandria with a small army. He was chasing his rival Pompey, whom he had beaten in a Roman civil war.

But Pompey was already dead. When he landed on the coast of Egypt a few days before, asking for help, the advisers of the young king had taken his life on the beach. They thought it would please Caesar. When Caesar heard what they had done, it is said, he turned away and wept.

--

Caesar decided to stay in the palace and settle the quarrel between Cleopatra and her brother, which was tearing Egypt apart. He sent for both of them.

But Cleopatra was outside the city with her army. Her brother's soldiers guarded the roads and the palace gates. How could she reach Caesar without being caught?

An old writer tells the story. At dusk, she sailed into the harbour in a small boat with just one friend. Since there was no other way to get in unseen, she lay down inside a big sack for bedding. Her friend tied it up and carried it on his back, right through the gates and into Caesar's room. There he set it down and untied it. And out stepped the queen.

Later stories said it was a carpet. But the old writer says it was a bedding sack.

--

Caesar was fifty-two. Cleopatra was twenty-one. He was charmed by her courage, her cleverness and her wit. And they fell in love.

Caesar decided that Cleopatra and her brother should rule together, as their father had wished. But the brother's advisers refused. The people of Alexandria rose up. For months, through the winter, the Egyptian army surrounded Caesar and Cleopatra in the palace.

{img:palace-under-siege-quiet-night-walls-torches-harbour-distant}
In the end, Roman help arrived. The young king drowned in the Nile while fleeing after a battle. Cleopatra became queen again. This time she ruled with another, even younger brother, who was about twelve.

--

And then, in the spring of 47, Caesar and Cleopatra are said to have sailed up the Nile together on the royal barge. It was a floating palace, with dining halls, bedrooms and gardens, rowed by hundreds of oarsmen, with many ships following behind.

They sailed south past the pyramids and the temples, the green fields and the palm groves. Some historians doubt the journey happened, or think it was much shorter. But it is one of the great love stories of history.

{img:royal-nile-barge-floating-palace-sunset-temples-banks-fleet}
That summer, after Caesar had left to fight more wars, Cleopatra gave birth to a son. The people of Alexandria called him Caesarion, which means little Caesar.

--

Two years later, Cleopatra travelled to Rome with her little son, to visit Caesar. She stayed in his villa across the river. The Romans were fascinated by her, but also suspicious, and they gossiped about her endlessly.

She was still in Rome on the Ides of March, in 44 before Christ, when Caesar was killed in the Senate. Suddenly her great protector was gone. Soon afterwards she went home to Alexandria with her son, who was three. A few months later her young brother died. She made little Caesarion her fellow ruler. Now she had to protect Egypt on her own.
