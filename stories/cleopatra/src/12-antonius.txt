After the death of Caesar, there was civil war in Rome again. And when it was over, the Roman world was divided between two men: Octavian, Caesar's young adopted son, in the west, and Mark Antony, Caesar's old friend and general, in the east.

In the summer of 41 before Christ, Antony was at Tarsus, a city in the south of what is now Turkey, and he summoned Cleopatra to come to him, to explain why she had not helped him in the war.

--

She came, but in her own way, and in her own time.

Plutarch describes her arrival, in one of the most famous passages in ancient literature. She sailed up the river Cydnus, to Tarsus, in a barge with a gilded stern, and purple sails, and oars of silver, which kept time to the music of flutes, and pipes, and lyres. She lay under a canopy of cloth of gold, dressed as Venus, the goddess of love, in a painting. Beautiful young boys, dressed as Cupids, stood on either side of her, fanning her. Her maids, dressed as sea nymphs, and Graces, steered the boat, and handled the ropes.

{img:queen-under-gold-canopy-barge-cupids-fanning-maids-ropes}
And wonderful perfumes, from the burning incense, drifted from the ship to the banks of the river, where great crowds gathered, and the word went round that Venus had come to feast with Bacchus, for the good of Asia.

Shakespeare, sixteen hundred years later, put this passage almost word for word into his play, Antony and Cleopatra: the barge she sat in, like a burnished throne, burned on the water.

--

Antony invited her to dinner. She invited him instead to dine with her, on her ship. And he came, and was dazzled: by the thousands of lamps, hung in patterns, and the gold plates, and the jewelled cups, and the food, and the queen.

Mark Antony was about forty-two. He was a big, strong, handsome, cheerful man, with a thick neck, and curly hair, and a beard, who liked to think that he looked like Hercules, from whom his family claimed descent. He was a brave soldier, much loved by his men, and generous, and fond of feasts, and wine, and laughter, and jokes.

He fell in love with Cleopatra. And that winter, he went with her to Alexandria, and stayed there, with her, instead of going back to Rome.

--

In Alexandria, Antony and Cleopatra lived a life of pleasure, and splendour, and fun.

They formed a club, which they called the Inimitable Livers, whose members feasted together every day, with enormous, extravagant banquets. Plutarch's grandfather heard about it from a doctor, who had been a student in Alexandria at the time, who told him that he once visited the royal kitchens, and saw eight wild boars roasting at once. He thought there must be a huge party. But the cook laughed, and said there were only about twelve guests. But since nobody knew exactly when Antony would want to eat, they roasted eight boars, one after another, at different times, so that one would always be perfect, whenever he asked.

{img:royal-kitchen-alexandria-many-spits-cooks-busy-firelight}
They wandered the streets of Alexandria at night, disguised as servants, and knocked on the doors of houses, and played jokes on the people. They went fishing together. Once, the story goes, Antony was not catching anything, and secretly told some fishermen to dive under the water, and fasten fish, already caught, onto his hook. Cleopatra saw through the trick. And the next day, she invited friends to watch, and when Antony let down his line, she had a diver fasten a salted fish, from the Black Sea, onto his hook. And when he pulled it up, everyone laughed. And Cleopatra said: leave the fishing rod to us, the rulers of Pharos and Canopus. Your game is cities, and kingdoms, and continents.

--

In the spring of 40, Antony went back to Rome, and, to make peace with Octavian, he married Octavian's sister, Octavia, a good and gentle woman. And for three years, he did not see Cleopatra.

Meanwhile, in Alexandria, Cleopatra gave birth to twins: a boy and a girl, Alexander Helios, the sun, and Cleopatra Selene, the moon.

{img:queen-with-twin-babies-nursery-palace-alexandria-sea-light}
But in 37, Antony came back to the East, and sent for Cleopatra again. And from then on, they stayed together. And in 36, they had another son, Ptolemy Philadelphus.
