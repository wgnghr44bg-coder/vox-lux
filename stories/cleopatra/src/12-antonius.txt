After Caesar's death, there was civil war in Rome again. When it ended, the Roman world was divided between two men. Octavian, Caesar's young adopted son, ruled the west. Mark Antony, Caesar's old friend and general, ruled the east.

In the summer of 41 before Christ, Antony was in the city of Tarsus, in what is now Turkey. He ordered Cleopatra to come to him, and explain why she had not helped him in the war.

--

She came, but in her own way, and in her own time.

An old writer describes her arrival in one of the most famous passages of ancient times. She sailed up the river to Tarsus in a barge with a golden stern and purple sails. Its silver oars kept time to the music of flutes and harps. She lay under a canopy of golden cloth, dressed as Venus, the goddess of love. Young boys dressed as cupids stood beside her, fanning her. Her maids, dressed as sea nymphs, steered the boat.

{img:queen-under-gold-canopy-barge-cupids-fanning-maids-ropes}
Sweet smells of incense drifted from the ship to the riverbanks, where great crowds gathered. And word went round that the goddess of love had come to feast with the god of wine.

Sixteen hundred years later, Shakespeare put this scene almost word for word into his play about them. The barge she sat in, he wrote, like a polished throne, burned on the water.

--

Antony invited her to dinner. She invited him to dine on her ship instead. He came, and he was dazzled. By thousands of lamps hung in patterns, by golden plates and jewelled cups, and by the queen.

Mark Antony was about forty-two. He was big, strong, handsome and cheerful, with curly hair and a beard. He liked to think he looked like the hero Hercules. He was a brave soldier, loved by his men, generous, and fond of feasts and laughter.

He fell in love with Cleopatra. That winter he went with her to Alexandria, and stayed there with her, instead of going back to Rome. In Rome, people noticed. And Octavian noticed most of all.

--

In Alexandria, Antony and Cleopatra lived a life of pleasure and fun.

They started a club of friends who feasted together every day. A doctor who was studying in Alexandria at the time later told a story about it. He once visited the royal kitchens and saw eight wild boars roasting at once. He thought there must be a huge party. But the cook laughed. There were only about twelve guests, he said. But nobody knew exactly when Antony would want to eat. So they roasted the boars one after another, so that one would always be perfect, whenever he asked.

{img:royal-kitchen-alexandria-many-spits-cooks-busy-firelight}
They wandered the streets at night dressed as servants, knocking on doors and playing tricks. They went fishing together. Once, the story goes, Antony caught nothing. So he secretly had a diver fasten fish onto his hook. Cleopatra saw through the trick. The next day, she had a diver fasten an old salted fish onto his hook. When he pulled it up, everyone laughed. And Cleopatra said: leave fishing to us. Your catch is cities and kingdoms.

--

In the spring of 40, Antony went back to Rome. To make peace with Octavian, he married Octavian's sister, a good and gentle woman. For three years, he did not see Cleopatra.

Meanwhile, in Alexandria, Cleopatra gave birth to twins, a boy and a girl. She named them after the sun and the moon.

{img:queen-with-twin-babies-nursery-palace-alexandria-sea-light}
But in 37, Antony came back to the East and sent for Cleopatra again. From then on, they stayed together. In 36 they had another son. And in Rome, Octavian waited, and watched.
