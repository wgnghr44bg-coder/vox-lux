The most famous building in Alexandria, and one of the Seven Wonders of the World, is the great lighthouse. It is called the Pharos.

It stands on a little island at the entrance to the great harbour. A long stone road, almost a kilometre and a half long, joins the island to the city.

--

The lighthouse was built about two hundred and fifty years before Cleopatra, by the first two kings of her family.

It is enormous, perhaps a hundred metres high or more. After the great pyramids, it is the tallest building in the world. It is made of great blocks of white stone, in three parts. At the bottom is a tall square tower with many windows and rooms inside. Above that is an eight-sided tower. And on top is a round tower with columns, where the fire burns.

At the very top stands a great statue of a god. And at the corners of the first part, there are statues of sea gods blowing on big shells.

--

At night, a great fire burns at the top, fed with wood and oil. The fuel is carried up inside the tower along a winding ramp, perhaps on donkeys. During the day, it is said, a big polished bronze mirror reflects the sunlight out to sea.

The old writers say the light can be seen from fifty kilometres out at sea. The coast near Alexandria is flat and low and dangerous, full of rocks and sandbanks. Without the lighthouse, many ships would be lost. With it, they find their way safely into the harbour, by day and by night.

{img:ship-at-night-sea-sailors-seeing-distant-lighthouse-glow}
Sailors coming from Rome, Greece or Syria watch the dark horizon for the light. When they see it, they breathe a sigh of relief. They know they are almost there.

--

The lighthouse was so famous that its name, Pharos, became the word for lighthouse in many languages. In French it is phare, and in Italian and Spanish it is faro.

There is a story about the builder. He wanted his own name to be remembered, but the king wanted his name on the lighthouse. So the builder carved his own name into the stone. Then he covered it with plaster, and painted the king's name on the plaster. After many years, the plaster fell away. And the builder's name was there for everyone to see.

{img:carved-greek-inscription-stone-plaster-falling-away-sunlight}
The lighthouse stood for more than a thousand years. Then great earthquakes damaged it, and it fell. Later, a sultan of Egypt built a fort on the same spot, using some of its stones. That fort still stands there today.
