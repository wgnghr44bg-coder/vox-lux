What became of Cleopatra's children?

Caesarion, the son of Julius Caesar, was seventeen. Cleopatra had sent him away south with a teacher and some treasure, hoping he could escape to India. But his teacher persuaded him to come back, saying Octavian would let him rule Egypt. Octavian's advisers said two Caesars were one too many. And the young man was lost. We know little more.

--

But the three younger children were taken to Rome. The twins were ten, and little Ptolemy was six. In Rome they had to walk in Octavian's great victory parade through the streets, behind a statue of their mother.

And then something kind happened. They were given to Octavian's sister, Antony's former wife, the woman Antony had left for Cleopatra. She was a good and generous woman. She took them into her own household. She raised them with her own children, in her house in Rome, as part of her family.

We do not know what happened to the two boys. They disappear from history. Perhaps they died young. Perhaps they lived quietly, under other names.

--

But we do know what happened to the girl, Cleopatra Selene.

When she was about fifteen, Octavian, now the emperor Augustus, arranged for her to marry a young prince called Juba. He was the son of a North African king, and had also been raised in Rome as a captive. Juba was a scholar who loved books and art. Augustus made Juba and Selene king and queen of a great kingdom in North Africa, in what is now Algeria and Morocco.

{img:caesarea-mauretania-city-harbour-palace-north-africa-coast}
They ruled there together for many years. They made their capital on the coast into a beautiful city, like a little Alexandria, with a library, a theatre, temples and statues.

--

Cleopatra Selene put her own name on the coins of the kingdom, in Greek: Cleopatra the Queen. On some of them she put a crocodile, the sign of Egypt, her mother's land.

She and Juba had a son, and named him Ptolemy, after her family. He became king after them.

{img:silver-coin-crocodile-greek-letters-queen-name-hand-light}
And so, in a little kingdom in North Africa, Cleopatra's daughter kept her mother's memory alive. She kept the family name, and something of the spirit of Alexandria, for one more generation.
