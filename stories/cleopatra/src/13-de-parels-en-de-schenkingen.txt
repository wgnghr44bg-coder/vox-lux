There is a famous story, told by a Roman writer, about a bet between Antony and Cleopatra.

Cleopatra bet Antony that she could spend an enormous fortune on a single dinner. Antony did not believe her. So she gave a feast. It was splendid, but not much more splendid than usual. Antony laughed and asked how much it had cost.

--

Then Cleopatra asked for the second course. The servants set down in front of her one single cup, filled with vinegar.

In her ears she wore two pearls, the largest in the world. She took one from her ear and dropped it into the vinegar. When it had dissolved, she drank it. She was about to do the same with the other one, when the judge of the bet stopped her, and said she had won.

Would vinegar really dissolve a pearl? Scientists have tried it. Strong vinegar can slowly dissolve a pearl, especially if it is crushed first. Perhaps Cleopatra knew that. Or perhaps it is only a story. The writer says the second pearl was later cut in two and made into earrings for a statue of Venus in Rome.

--

In 34 before Christ, Antony held a great ceremony in Alexandria.

In the great sports hall of the city, in front of a huge crowd, there was a silver platform with two golden thrones for Antony and Cleopatra, and smaller thrones for the children. Cleopatra was dressed as the goddess Isis.

{img:silver-platform-golden-thrones-royal-family-gymnasium-crowd}
Antony declared Cleopatra the Queen of Kings. He called Caesarion, now thirteen, the King of Kings and the true son of Julius Caesar. And he gave kingdoms to the children. To little Alexander, lands in the east, some of which had not even been conquered yet. To his twin sister, lands in North Africa. And to little Ptolemy, only two, Syria and lands along the coast.

The little boys were dressed in the costumes of the lands they would rule. One wore a tall eastern hat. The other wore boots and a cloak like the old Macedonian kings.

--

In Rome, this caused a scandal. Octavian used it to turn the Roman people against Antony. Antony has been bewitched by the Egyptian queen, he said. He is giving away Roman lands to her and her children. He wants to make Alexandria the capital of the world, instead of Rome.

Octavian found Antony's will, which was kept by priestesses in Rome. He read it aloud to the Senate. In it, Antony asked to be buried in Alexandria beside Cleopatra, even if he died in Rome. The Romans were furious.

{img:roman-senate-reading-document-senators-murmuring-candles}
In 32 before Christ, Rome declared war. Not on Antony, but on Cleopatra. And everyone knew that a great war was about to begin. One that would decide who ruled the whole Roman world.

--

In Alexandria, life went on much as before. People went to the markets, the baths, the theatre and the races. Ships came and went. Scholars read in the Library. Along the Nile, the farmers watched the river rise and fall, paid their taxes, and hoped for a good harvest. They paid little attention to the quarrels of the great.

But in the palace by the sea, Cleopatra and Antony began to prepare their fleet, their army and their treasure for the war that was coming.
