Cleopatra the Seventh was born in Alexandria, in the winter of 69 before Christ.

In the year 31, she is thirty-eight years old. She has been queen of Egypt for twenty-one years, since she was eighteen. She is one of the most famous women in all of history. And yet, we know surprisingly little about her for certain, because almost everything that was written about her was written by her enemies, the Romans, after her death.

--

What did she look like?

Her coins show a woman with a strong face, a large, curved nose, a firm chin, and her hair pulled back, in a bun, in the Greek style, called the melon hairstyle, with braids, like the segments of a melon, and a royal diadem, a white band, tied around her head. In the Egyptian temples, she is shown in the old Egyptian style, as a goddess, with a tall crown, of horns, and a sun disc, and feathers.

The Greek writer Plutarch, who lived about a hundred and fifty years later, but who talked to people whose grandfathers had known her court, says that her beauty was not, in itself, so remarkable that no one could compare with her. But that her presence was captivating, and her conversation irresistible, and her voice sweet, like an instrument of many strings, which she could turn easily to any language she liked.

--

She was very clever, and very well educated. She spoke many languages: Greek, of course, her own language, and Egyptian, the first of all her family to bother to learn it, and also the languages of the Ethiopians, and the Troglodytes, and the Hebrews, and the Arabs, and the Syrians, and the Medes, and the Parthians, Plutarch says, so that she could speak to their ambassadors without an interpreter.

She studied in the great Museum of Alexandria, with its scholars: mathematics, and astronomy, and philosophy, and medicine, and rhetoric, and history. Later writers said that she wrote books on medicine, and cosmetics, and weights and measures. Perhaps some of this is true.

{img:young-princess-studying-scrolls-library-alexandria-scholars}
She was also a skilful ruler. She managed the economy of Egypt carefully, and the harvests, and the grain, and the money. She made Egypt rich again, after the troubles of her father's reign. She was popular with the Egyptian people, who saw her as a living goddess, Isis on earth.

--

Her early life was difficult and dangerous.

When she was about eleven, her father was driven out of Alexandria, and her older sister, Berenice, seized the throne. When her father came back, with a Roman army, Berenice was put to death. Then, when her father passed away, in 51 before Christ, Cleopatra, at eighteen, became queen, together with her younger brother, Ptolemy the Thirteenth, who was only ten, and who, according to the custom of the family, became her husband.

{img:young-queen-and-boy-king-throne-room-alexandria-courtiers}
But her brother's advisers, a eunuch named Pothinus, and a general named Achillas, and a teacher named Theodotus, turned against her, and drove her out of Alexandria. She fled to Syria, and gathered an army, and came back, to fight for her throne.

And at that very moment, in the summer of 48 before Christ, the greatest man in Rome arrived in Alexandria: Julius Caesar.
