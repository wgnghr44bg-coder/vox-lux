Cleopatra has been remembered and imagined for two thousand years.

The Roman poets wrote about her as a dangerous enemy. One of them wrote a poem celebrating her death. Yet even he admitted that she died nobly, without fear, like a queen. A Greek writer told her story with fascination. He gave us most of the stories we know today.

--

In 1606, Shakespeare wrote his great play about Antony and Cleopatra. In it she is passionate, proud, witty and unforgettable. Age cannot wither her, he wrote, nor custom stale her endless variety.

Painters painted her again and again. At her feast with the pearl. Meeting Antony on her golden barge. In the twentieth century she was played in films by great actresses. The most famous film, from 1963, was one of the most expensive ever made.

{img:painting-style-queen-on-golden-barge-river-old-master-colours}
And there are two famous stone pillars called Cleopatra's Needles. They are tall, slim pillars of red granite, carved with hieroglyphs. Once they stood in front of a temple in Alexandria that Cleopatra began to build. But they are much older than her. They were made for a pharaoh fourteen hundred years before her time, and brought to Alexandria later.

In the nineteenth century, one of them was given to Britain. It was shipped to London in a special iron tube, which nearly sank in a storm. It was set up on the bank of the river Thames, and it still stands there. The other was given to the United States, and set up in Central Park in New York.

--

{img:obelisk-central-park-new-york-trees-autumn-walkers}
So if you walk along the Thames in London in the evening, or through Central Park in New York, you can still see a piece of Cleopatra's Alexandria. It stands tall among the trees and the traffic. Its ancient picture writing, worn by two thousand years of sun and rain, still tells the names of pharaohs that almost nobody passing by can read.
