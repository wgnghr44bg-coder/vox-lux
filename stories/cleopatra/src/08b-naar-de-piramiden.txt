Even in the time of Cleopatra, Egypt was full of tourists.

Greeks, Romans and other visitors came to see its ancient wonders, which were already thousands of years old. They sailed up the Nile from Alexandria. Local guides showed them the old cities, temples and pyramids, and told them stories. Some of the stories were true, and some were not.

--

The first great sight, a few days up the river, was Memphis, the ancient capital of Egypt. There was a famous sacred bull there, which visitors could watch and feed in its stall.

And then, at the edge of the desert, the pyramids of Giza.

{img:great-pyramid-smooth-white-casing-stones-giza-sphinx-ancient}
In Cleopatra's time, the great pyramids still have their outer skin of smooth, white, polished stone. It shines in the sun so brightly that it hurts the eyes. Their tops are still sharp. One Greek visitor said the guides read him the writing on a pyramid. It listed, they told him, how many onions and radishes the builders had eaten. The guides were probably making it up.

--

The Great Sphinx lies in front of them, half buried in sand that blows in from the desert, and must be cleared away now and then.

The visitors were amazed. The pyramids were counted among the Seven Wonders of the World. They are the only one of the seven that still stands today. Some visitors climbed them. Some carved their names on them, as tourists always have.

{img:colossi-of-memnon-two-great-statues-thebes-plain-dawn-visitors}
Further south, at Thebes, were the great temples with forests of huge columns, and the painted tombs of the kings, which visitors could already enter.

And there were two enormous seated statues of a pharaoh on the plain. An earthquake had cracked one of them. And every morning at sunrise, as the stone warmed in the sun, it made a strange, soft singing sound, like a harp string breaking. The Greeks believed it was a hero greeting his mother, the goddess of the dawn. Visitors came from everywhere to hear it. They carved poems on its legs, which can still be read today.

--

Those who travelled even further south could see the great stone quarries where obelisks were cut. And they could see the beautiful island of Philae, with its temple of Isis, among palm trees, rocks and rushing water.

So for the people of Cleopatra's time, as for us, Egypt was already an ancient land of wonders. A land of gods and kings and mysteries, which they came to see, and remembered all their lives.
