Beyond the green valley of the Nile, on both sides, there is the desert.

To the west, the great Western Desert, part of the Sahara, stretches away for more than a thousand kilometres: a vast sea of sand, and stone, and dunes, and flat plains of gravel, and strange rocks, carved by the wind into mushrooms, and towers. To the east, between the Nile and the Red Sea, the Eastern Desert, of rocky mountains, and dry valleys, where gold, and precious stones, and fine granite, and porphyry, are mined.

--

The desert is not completely empty. Here and there, in the Western Desert, there are oases: places where water comes up from deep under the ground, in springs, and wells, and palm trees grow, and villages, and fields, and orchards, in the middle of the sand.

The most famous is the oasis of Siwa, far out in the desert, near the border of Libya, with its lakes, and palm groves, and its temple of the god Ammon, whose oracle was famous all over the Greek world. Three hundred years before Cleopatra, Alexander the Great himself made the long, dangerous journey across the desert to Siwa, to ask the oracle a question. The priest greeted him as the son of the god. And nobody knows what else the oracle told him. He never told anyone.

{img:siwa-oasis-temple-ruins-palm-groves-salt-lake-sunset}
In the desert, there are animals too: gazelles, and ibexes, and foxes, with huge ears, called fennecs, and jackals, and hyenas, and ostriches, and lizards, and scorpions. And the people of the desert, the nomads, with their camels, and their goats, and their tents, who travel from oasis to oasis, and know the desert's secret wells, and paths.

--

{img:desert-night-stars-dunes-cold-clear-silence-milky-way}
The Egyptians fear the desert, a little. It is the red land, the land of Set, the god of chaos and storms, and of the dead, whose tombs are built at its edge. But they also love its silence, and its beauty: the golden dunes at sunset, the cold, clear nights, with more stars than you can count, the strange shapes of the rocks, the pure, dry air.

And many of the first Christian monks, a few hundred years after Cleopatra, will go out into the Egyptian desert, to live alone, as hermits, in caves, and huts, in the silence, praying, and found the first monasteries in the world.
