Beyond the green valley of the Nile, on both sides, lies the desert.

To the west stretches the great Western Desert, part of the Sahara. It goes on for more than a thousand kilometres. There is sand and stone, dunes and flat plains of gravel, and strange rocks carved by the wind into mushrooms and towers. To the east, between the Nile and the Red Sea, there are rocky mountains and dry valleys, where gold and fine stone are mined.

--

The desert is not completely empty. Here and there, water rises from deep underground in springs and wells. Around them grow palm trees, and there are villages and fields and orchards, right in the middle of the sand. These places are called oases.

The most famous is the oasis of Siwa, far out in the desert. It has lakes, palm groves and a temple of the god Ammon, whose oracle was famous all over the Greek world. Three hundred years before Cleopatra, Alexander the Great made the long, dangerous journey across the desert to ask the oracle a question. The priest greeted him as the son of the god. Nobody knows what else the oracle told him. Alexander never told anyone.

{img:siwa-oasis-temple-ruins-palm-groves-salt-lake-sunset}
There are animals in the desert too. Gazelles and wild goats, little foxes with huge ears, jackals, ostriches, lizards and scorpions. And there are desert people with their camels and goats and tents. They travel from oasis to oasis, and know the desert's secret wells.

--

{img:desert-night-stars-dunes-cold-clear-silence-milky-way}
The Egyptians fear the desert a little. To them it is the red land, the land of the god of storms and chaos. But they also love its silence and beauty. The golden dunes at sunset. The cold, clear nights with more stars than anyone could count. The strange rocks and the pure, dry air.

A few hundred years after Cleopatra, the first Christian monks will go out into the Egyptian desert. They will live alone in caves and huts, praying in the silence. And there they will found the first monasteries in the world.
