Egypt, in Cleopatra's time, is full of temples, and full of gods.

The Egyptians have hundreds of gods: Ra, the sun god, who sails across the sky every day in his boat. Osiris, the god of the dead, and of the rebirth of the crops. Isis, his wife, the great mother goddess, the goddess of magic, and healing, and love. Horus, their son, the falcon god, the protector of the king. Hathor, the cow goddess, of joy, and music, and love. Anubis, the jackal god, who guides the dead. Thoth, the ibis god, of wisdom, and writing. And many more.

--

Many of the gods are shown as animals, or with animal heads, because the Egyptians believe that the gods can live in animals, and that certain animals are sacred to them: the cat to Bastet, the ibis to Thoth, the falcon to Horus, the crocodile to Sobek, the bull to Apis.

At Memphis, the old capital, there is a living sacred bull, the Apis bull, a black bull, with special white markings, who lives in a temple, in great comfort, and is worshipped as the living image of the god Ptah. When he passes away, he is mummified, and buried in a great underground tomb, with enormous stone coffins. And the priests search the whole of Egypt for a new bull, with the right markings.

{img:apis-bull-temple-courtyard-priests-garlands-memphis-calm}
In the year 51, at the very beginning of her reign, Cleopatra herself sailed up the Nile, to the city of Hermonthis, to take part in the installation of a new sacred bull, the Buchis bull. An inscription records that she rowed the sacred boat herself, and that all the people saw her.

--

The Ptolemies built many great temples, in the old Egyptian style, all along the Nile. Some of them are among the best preserved temples of ancient Egypt, and can still be seen today.

At Dendera, there is the great temple of Hathor, the goddess of love, and joy, and music, with tall columns, topped with the face of the goddess, and a ceiling painted with the stars, and the signs of the zodiac, in blue and gold. On the back wall of the temple, outside, there is a huge carving of Cleopatra, and her son, Caesarion, making offerings to the gods, in the old Egyptian style.

At Edfu, there is the great temple of Horus, the falcon god, almost complete, with its huge gateway, its pylons, and its courtyards, and its dark, mysterious sanctuary. At Philae, on an island in the Nile, in the far south, there is the beautiful temple of Isis, surrounded by the river, with palm trees.

--

The temples are not places where ordinary people go to worship. They are the houses of the gods, and only the priests can enter the inner parts.

Every day, in every temple, the priests wake the god, in the morning, in the dark sanctuary, by opening the doors of the shrine, where the statue of the god lives. They wash the statue, and dress it, in fine linen, and anoint it with perfumed oil, and offer it food, and drink, and incense, and flowers, and sing hymns. In the evening, they close the shrine again, and the god sleeps.

{img:priests-white-linen-shaved-heads-incense-sanctuary-candles}
The priests wear white linen, and shave their heads, and their whole bodies, to be pure, and wash several times a day, and do not eat certain foods, like fish, and beans.

--

But on festival days, the god comes out of the temple, in a golden boat, carried on the shoulders of the priests, in a great procession, with music, and dancing, and singing, and incense, and the people line the streets, and the river banks, to see the god pass, and ask him questions, and pray.

Once a year, at the great festival of the Beautiful Meeting, the statue of Hathor, from Dendera, sails up the Nile, in her golden boat, to visit her husband, Horus, at Edfu, a journey of many days, with crowds of pilgrims following, and feasting, and celebrating, along the way.

{img:golden-sacred-boat-procession-nile-crowds-music-festival}
Of all the gods, the most loved, in Cleopatra's time, is Isis: the faithful wife, and loving mother, who brought her husband back to life, and protected her baby son. Her worship is spreading, all over the Mediterranean world, even to Rome. And Cleopatra presents herself to the people of Egypt as Isis, the new Isis, the living goddess on earth.
