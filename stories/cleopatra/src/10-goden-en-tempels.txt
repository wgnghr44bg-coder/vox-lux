Egypt in Cleopatra's time is full of temples, and full of gods.

The Egyptians have hundreds of gods. Ra, the sun god, sails across the sky every day in his boat. Osiris is the god of the dead and of the crops that grow again each year. Isis, his wife, is the great mother goddess, of magic and healing and love. Horus, their son, is a falcon god who protects the king. Hathor is the cow goddess of joy and music. Anubis, the jackal god, guides the dead. And Thoth, the ibis god, is the god of wisdom and writing.

--

Many gods are shown as animals, or with animal heads. The Egyptians believe gods can live in animals. The cat belongs to one goddess, the ibis to Thoth, the falcon to Horus, the crocodile to another god.

At Memphis there is a living sacred bull. It is black, with special white markings. It lives in a temple in great comfort and is worshipped as the living image of a god. When it dies, it is preserved and buried in a great underground tomb. Then the priests search all of Egypt for a new bull with exactly the right markings.

{img:apis-bull-temple-courtyard-priests-garlands-memphis-calm}
At the very start of her reign, Cleopatra herself sailed up the Nile to welcome a new sacred bull in another city. An old inscription says she rowed the sacred boat herself, and all the people saw her.

--

Her family built many great temples in the old Egyptian style along the Nile. Some of them are among the best preserved temples in Egypt.

At Dendera there is the great temple of Hathor. Its tall columns are topped with the face of the goddess. Its ceiling is painted with the stars in blue and gold. And on the back wall, outside, there is a huge carving of Cleopatra and her son, making offerings to the gods.

At Edfu stands the great temple of Horus, almost complete, with its huge gateway and its dark, mysterious inner room. And on an island in the far south stands the lovely temple of Isis, surrounded by the river and palm trees.

--

Ordinary people do not go inside to worship. The temples are the houses of the gods, and only priests may enter the inner rooms.

Every morning, in every temple, the priests wake the god. They open the doors of the shrine where the statue lives. They wash it, dress it in fine linen and rub it with scented oil. They offer it food, drink, incense and flowers, and sing hymns. In the evening, they close the shrine again, and the god sleeps.

{img:priests-white-linen-shaved-heads-incense-sanctuary-candles}
The priests wear white linen and shave their heads and bodies, to be pure. They wash several times a day, and do not eat certain foods, like fish.

--

But on festival days, the god comes out. The statue rides in a golden boat, carried on the priests' shoulders, in a great procession with music and dancing and incense. The people line the streets and the riverbanks to see the god pass, and to pray.

Once a year, the statue of Hathor sails up the Nile in her golden boat to visit her husband Horus, in his temple many days away. Crowds of pilgrims follow her, feasting and celebrating all along the way.

{img:golden-sacred-boat-procession-nile-crowds-music-festival}
Of all the gods, the most loved in Cleopatra's time is Isis. She is the faithful wife who brought her husband back to life, and the loving mother who protected her baby son. Her worship is spreading all over the Mediterranean, even to Rome. And Cleopatra presents herself to her people as the new Isis, the living goddess on earth. Now she will need all the help a goddess can give.
