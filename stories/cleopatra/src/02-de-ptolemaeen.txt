When Alexander the Great died in Babylon, at only thirty-two, his generals divided his great empire between them.

One of them, a nobleman called Ptolemy, took Egypt. He had been Alexander's friend since childhood. He was a wise and careful man. He made himself king of Egypt, and started a family of kings and queens who would rule Egypt for nearly three hundred years.

--

Ptolemy even took Alexander's body while it was being carried home, and brought it to Alexandria. He buried it in a great tomb in the middle of the city, so the city would share the great conqueror's glory. In Cleopatra's time, people still come from all over the world to see it.

All the kings of the family were called Ptolemy. Most of the queens were called Cleopatra, Berenice or Arsinoe. Our Cleopatra is the seventh queen with that name.

The Ptolemies were Greeks, not Egyptians. They spoke Greek and lived like Greek kings, with Greek friends, Greek soldiers and Greek customs.

But to the Egyptian people they were pharaohs. They were crowned in the old Egyptian way, with the ancient crowns. They built Egyptian temples along the Nile to the old gods. On the temple walls, they are shown as pharaohs, with Egyptian clothes and crowns, and their names written in hieroglyphs, the old picture writing.

--

So they ruled two peoples in two ways. Greek kings to the Greeks, and pharaohs to the Egyptians.

The first Ptolemies were great rulers. They made Egypt rich and powerful, and Alexandria the most splendid city in the world. They built the great lighthouse and the great library, and invited the best scholars from all over Greece.

{img:ptolemaic-pharaoh-relief-temple-wall-offering-to-gods-hieroglyphs}
But the later Ptolemies were weaker. The family quarrelled and fought among themselves, brothers against sisters, mothers against sons, all for the throne. Following an old royal custom, they often married their own brothers and sisters. There were riots and plots in the palace.

--

And meanwhile, in the west, a new power was rising. Rome.

By the time Cleopatra was born, Rome had conquered Greece and most lands around the Mediterranean. Egypt was the last great kingdom still free. And the Romans looked at Egypt, with its endless fields of grain and its gold, with greedy eyes.

{img:roman-envoys-arriving-alexandria-harbour-galley-togas}
Cleopatra's father loved to play the flute, so people called him the Flute Player. He kept his throne only by paying huge bribes to powerful Romans. Once, the people of Alexandria drove him out. He fled to Rome, and came back only with the help of a Roman army.

That was the world Cleopatra was born into. A rich, ancient and beautiful country, ruled by a quarrelling Greek family, living in the shadow of Rome. How long could Egypt stay free?
