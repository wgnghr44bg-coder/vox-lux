When Alexander the Great passed away, in Babylon, in 323 before Christ, at the age of thirty-two, his generals divided his great empire between them.

One of them, a Macedonian nobleman named Ptolemy, took Egypt. He was a friend of Alexander since childhood, a wise and careful man. He made himself king of Egypt, as Ptolemy the First, and founded a dynasty, a family of kings and queens, who ruled Egypt for nearly three hundred years.

--

Ptolemy even took Alexander's body, as it was being carried home to Macedonia, and brought it to Egypt, and later to Alexandria, and buried it there, in a great tomb, in the middle of the city, so that Alexandria would have the protection, and the glory, of the great conqueror. Alexander's tomb is still there, in Cleopatra's time, and people come from all over the world to see it.

All the kings of the family were called Ptolemy. And the queens were mostly called Cleopatra, or Berenice, or Arsinoe. Our Cleopatra is the seventh queen of that name.

The Ptolemies were Greeks, not Egyptians. They spoke Greek, and lived like Greek kings, in their palace in Alexandria, with Greek courtiers, and Greek soldiers, and Greek scholars, and Greek customs.

But to the Egyptian people, they were pharaohs. They were crowned in the old capital, Memphis, in the Egyptian way, with the ancient crowns of Upper and Lower Egypt. They built Egyptian temples, all along the Nile, to the old Egyptian gods, and were shown on the walls of the temples, in the old style, as pharaohs, making offerings to the gods, with Egyptian clothes, and crowns, and names, written in hieroglyphs.

--

So they ruled two peoples, in two ways: as Greek kings to the Greeks, and as pharaohs to the Egyptians.

The first Ptolemies were great rulers. They made Egypt rich, and powerful, and Alexandria the most splendid city in the world. They built the great lighthouse, and the library, and the museum, and invited the greatest scholars, and poets, and scientists, from all over the Greek world.

{img:ptolemaic-pharaoh-relief-temple-wall-offering-to-gods-hieroglyphs}
But later Ptolemies were weaker. The family quarrelled, and fought, among themselves: brothers against sisters, mothers against sons, for the throne. Following an old Egyptian royal custom, they often married their own brothers and sisters, to keep the power in the family. There were rebellions, and riots, and plots, and murders, in the palace.

--

And meanwhile, in the west, a new great power was rising: Rome.

By the time Cleopatra was born, in 69 before Christ, Rome had conquered Greece, and most of the lands around the Mediterranean. Egypt was the last great independent kingdom left. And the Romans looked at Egypt, with its endless fields of grain, and its gold, and its riches, with greedy eyes.

{img:roman-envoys-arriving-alexandria-harbour-galley-togas}
Cleopatra's father, Ptolemy the Twelfth, called the Flute Player, because he loved to play the flute, kept his throne only by paying enormous bribes to powerful Romans, and borrowing money to do it. Once, the people of Alexandria drove him out, and he fled to Rome, and came back only with the help of a Roman army.

That was the world into which Cleopatra was born: a rich, ancient, beautiful country, ruled by a quarrelling Greek family, under the shadow of Rome.
