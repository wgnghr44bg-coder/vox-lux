Let us spend an ordinary day in Alexandria in the time of Cleopatra, with an ordinary family.

The father is a Greek glassmaker with a little workshop in the city. Alexandria is famous for its glass. Beautiful bottles, bowls and beads in blue, green, purple and amber are sold all over the Mediterranean.

--

The family lives in a house of mud brick, plastered and painted, two or three floors high, on a narrow street near a canal. On the ground floor are the workshop and the kitchen. Above are the rooms where the family lives and sleeps. On the flat roof there is a terrace, where they sit in the cool of the evening. On the hottest nights they sleep up there under the stars.

The day begins at dawn. The sun rises red and gold over the Delta, and the city wakes up. Birds sing in the palm trees. Donkeys bray. The water carriers come down the street with their jars of water from the canal.

{img:water-carrier-donkey-jars-narrow-street-morning-alexandria}
The mother, the daughters and the slave woman make breakfast. There is fresh bread from the clay oven in the courtyard, a little cheese, dates and figs, and some beer, or water mixed with wine.

--

The father goes down to the workshop, where the furnace is already hot.

Glass is made from sand, soda and lime, melted together in the great heat of the furnace until it glows. In these very years, a wonderful new trick is spreading. A glassmaker takes a little blob of hot glass on the end of a long hollow pipe, and blows into it. The glass swells like a bubble. He turns it and shapes it quickly, before it cools, into a bottle or a cup. It is much faster than the old ways. Soon glass will be cheap enough for ordinary people.

In the afternoon, the father goes to the baths and then to the sports ground. There he talks with his friends about business and politics, about the queen and about the Romans. Lately, the talk is mostly about the Romans.

--

The mother runs the household. She goes to the market with a slave to buy fish from the sea and the lake, and onions, lettuce, lentils and fruit. She spins and weaves fine white linen for the family's clothes.

The children go to school, or play in the street and on the roof. They have clay dolls, little wooden horses on wheels, balls, spinning tops, and pet birds and cats.

{img:children-playing-flat-roof-cats-toys-palm-trees-afternoon}
Cats are everywhere in Egypt, and they are loved and respected. Egyptians believe cats are sacred to a goddess. Harming a cat is a terrible crime. A Greek visitor in these years once saw a crowd attack a Roman who had accidentally killed a cat in the street. Nothing could save him, not even the king's officials.

--

In the evening, the family gathers on the roof for supper. The sun sets over the lake in a blaze of orange and red, and the lighthouse begins to shine out at sea.

A cool breeze comes in from the sea. The stars come out, bright and close. From the street below come the sounds of evening. People talking and laughing, music from a tavern, a dog barking.

{img:family-supper-rooftop-sunset-lake-lighthouse-lamps-calm}
Then the family lies down to sleep on the roof, on mats and cushions, under a light linen sheet. Above them shine the stars. Far away they hear the sea, and see the faint glow of the great lighthouse on the horizon.
