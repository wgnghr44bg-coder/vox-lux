By the end of November, the galleys are late. They should have been home by now.

At first, nobody is very worried. Galleys are often late. The winds are not always good, and the ships must sometimes wait in harbours along the way. But as the days go by, people begin to worry more. The weather has been bad this autumn. There have been storms on the sea. And there has been no news.

--

In the family's house on Murano, nobody talks about it very much. But everyone is thinking about it. Lucia counts the days on her fingers, again and again. Nonna Bettina lights a candle every evening in front of a little picture of the Madonna. Matteo works long hours in the workshop, as if work could make the time go faster. Nicolò is quieter than usual.

{img:grandmother-lighting-candle-madonna-icon-evening-quiet-prayer}
Chiara goes up to the altana every day. She looks out across the lagoon toward the open sea. She watches every sail that appears on the horizon. Is that them? No, it is only a fishing boat. Is that them? No, it is a ship from the mainland. Every evening, she comes down slowly, and does not say anything.

--

One day, a merchant at the Rialto brings news. He has heard from a ship that came in from the south. The galleys were seen in a harbour on the coast of Greece, two weeks ago. They were sheltering from a storm. They were all safe, at that time. Lucia hurries home across the lagoon to tell the family. Everyone feels a little better. But two weeks is a long time. And there might have been more storms since then.

{img:woman-hurrying-home-boat-news-grey-lagoon-afternoon-hope}
In December, the doge orders special prayers in all the churches of Venice for the galleys. The bells ring every evening at the same time. People stop what they are doing and say a prayer. In the Basilica of San Marco, many candles are lit. Families of the sailors come to kneel and pray together.

--

The family from Murano rows across to San Marco one evening for the prayers. The great church is full of people, and full of candles. The golden mosaics glow and glitter in the soft light. The priests sing the old prayers in Latin. Chiara kneels between her mother and her grandmother. She looks up at the mosaics of the saints and the angels. She asks them, very quietly, to bring her uncle home safely.

{img:candlelit-basilica-families-kneeling-praying-golden-mosaics-december}
On the way home, the boat crosses the dark lagoon. There is no moon, and the stars are hidden by clouds. A cold wind blows from the sea. Matteo rows slowly, without speaking. Nicolò holds the lantern at the front of the boat. Chiara sits close to her grandmother, wrapped in a shawl. Nonna Bettina holds her hand. He will come home, the old woman says quietly. The sea has always given him back before. And it will give him back again.

The other families of sailors on Murano visit each other often during these weeks. They share news and rumours, and they comfort each other. The women bring little gifts of food to each other's houses. The children play together, a little more quietly than usual. Everyone is waiting for the same thing. And somehow, waiting together makes it a little easier.

Nicolò makes something special during these weeks. He works on it secretly in the evenings, when the workshop is quiet. It is a small glass bowl, blue with white swirls, the best thing he has ever made. He wants to give it to his uncle when he comes home. Every evening, he works on it a little more. And every evening, he says to himself: he will come home, and I will give him this bowl.
