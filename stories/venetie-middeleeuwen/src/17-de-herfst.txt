Summer turns into autumn. The days get shorter, and the evenings get cooler. On the islands of the lagoon, it is time for the grape harvest.

Many families in the lagoon have small vineyards on the islands. In autumn, everyone helps to pick the grapes. They carry them in big baskets to the wine presses. The juice runs out into barrels, purple and sweet. The children are allowed to eat as many grapes as they like. They come home with purple fingers and purple faces.

--

On Murano, the glass furnaces burn on as always. In autumn, the merchants come to buy glass for the winter trade. Lucia is very busy. She sells cups and bottles, beads and bowls. She writes everything down carefully in her account book. It has been a good year for the workshop. Matteo has made some of his most beautiful glass ever. There is a set of cups in pale blue glass, with thin white threads swirling through them.

{img:pale-blue-venetian-glass-cups-white-threads-workshop-table-light}
Nicolò has made progress too. He can now make good glass beads, round and smooth, in many colours. He has started to blow small bottles. Some of them are still a bit lopsided. But some of them are good enough to sell. When the first one is sold, he is so proud that he cannot stop smiling all day.

--

In the evenings, the family sits by the fire, because the nights are getting cold. Nonna Bettina knits and tells stories. Chiara practises her letters on the wax tablet. Nicolò carves a little boat out of wood. Lucia does her accounts. And Matteo sits and stares into the fire, thinking. He is thinking about his brother Zorzi. The galleys should be home soon.

{img:family-evening-fireside-grandmother-knitting-girl-wax-tablet-boy-carving}
In October, news arrives from the east. A fast boat has come into Venice with letters. The convoy of galleys has left Alexandria, the letters say. They are on their way home. Everyone in the family is very happy. Chiara jumps up and down. Nicolò grins. Lucia says a little prayer of thanks. And Matteo smiles, for the first time in days.

--

So they wait. October goes by, and November begins. The leaves fall from the few trees on Murano. The rain comes, and the wind blows cold from the north. Every day, Chiara runs to the altana to look out to sea. Every evening, she comes down and shakes her head. Not yet, she says.

{img:girl-on-rooftop-terrace-looking-out-to-sea-grey-november-sky-wind}
Other families are waiting too. All over Venice, there are families of sailors, waiting for their men to come home. The women gather at the wells and talk about it. The men talk about it at the Rialto. When will the galleys come? Will they be home before Christmas? Nobody knows. And the November days grow shorter and darker, one after another.

In autumn, the first chestnuts arrive at the markets from the hills of the mainland. Men roast them over little fires in the campi and sell them in paper cones. The smell of roasting chestnuts drifts through the streets. The children love them. They burn their fingers peeling them, and then they blow on them and eat them hot. It is one of the best things about autumn.

The evenings are getting darker now, and the family spends more time indoors. Lucia lights the oil lamps earlier. The fire in the fireplace is lit every evening. The family gathers around it after supper, wrapped in warm wool. Rosso the cat curls up on Nonna Bettina's lap. And the sound of the rain on the roof tiles makes everyone feel snug and safe.

On the first of November, the family goes to the little church of Murano for the feast of All Saints. The next day is All Souls, when families remember those who have passed away. They light candles for Matteo's father and for Lucia's parents. They leave flowers at their resting places. Nonna Bettina tells the children a little story about each one. It is a quiet and gentle day, full of memories.
