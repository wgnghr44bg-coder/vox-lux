Every year, in spring, Venice holds its most famous festival. It is called the Sensa, and it falls on Ascension Day, forty days after Easter. On this day, the doge of Venice marries the sea.

It sounds very strange, but it is a beautiful ceremony. Venice owes everything to the sea. The sea protects the city, and it brings ships and trade and wealth. So once a year, the Venetians thank the sea, and promise to look after it. And the doge does this by marrying it, with a golden ring.

--

On the morning of the festival, the doge goes on board a magnificent ship. It is called the Bucintoro. It is a great galley, painted red and gold, with a golden lion at the front. There is a covered deck with cushions and curtains of red velvet. The workers of the Arsenal row it, with great golden oars. Behind it come hundreds and hundreds of other boats, decorated with flowers and flags.

{img:hundreds-decorated-boats-following-golden-ship-lagoon-spring-flags}
The whole city comes out to watch. People crowd along the waterfront and on every bridge and roof. They wave flags and cheer. Musicians play on the boats. Church bells ring all over the city. It is the happiest day of the year in Venice.

--

The Bucintoro rows slowly out across the lagoon, to the place where the lagoon meets the open sea. There, everything becomes quiet. The bishop blesses the water with holy water. Then the doge stands up at the side of the ship. He takes a golden ring from his finger. He holds it up for everyone to see. Then he says the old words: We marry you, O sea, as a sign of our true and lasting rule. And he throws the ring into the water.

{img:doge-throwing-gold-ring-into-sea-bucintoro-side-quiet-moment}
The ring sinks down into the blue water and disappears. A great cheer goes up from all the boats. Trumpets sound, and drums beat. The bells of Venice ring out across the lagoon. The sea has been married once again.

--

This year, the whole family from Murano goes out in their boat to watch. Nicolò rows, and Matteo helps. Lucia and Nonna Bettina sit in the middle, in their best clothes. Chiara sits at the front, so she can see everything. They follow the great golden ship across the lagoon, among hundreds of other boats.

{img:family-small-boat-watching-festival-crowds-boats-lagoon-sunshine}
When the doge throws the ring, Chiara stands up to see better. The boat wobbles, and Lucia grabs her dress. Sit down, she says, or you will marry the sea too! Everyone laughs. Afterwards, there is a great fair in the Piazza San Marco. It lasts for many days. There are stalls selling everything you can imagine. Matteo even sells some of his glass there, and does very good business. And in the evening, the family rows home across the lagoon, tired and happy, as the sun goes down.

During the fair, there are many stalls selling things from all over the world. There are carpets from Persia and silks from China. There are spices from India and furs from Russia. There are fine cloths from Flanders and swords from Germany. People come from far away to buy and sell. The square is so crowded that you can hardly move. And the noise of people talking in many languages fills the air.

Chiara is allowed to spend one small coin at the fair. She walks from stall to stall for a long time, thinking carefully. In the end, she buys a little ribbon of blue silk, for her hair. She ties it on at once and looks at herself in a piece of polished glass. Isabetta says it looks lovely. Chiara decides that it is the best coin she has ever spent.
