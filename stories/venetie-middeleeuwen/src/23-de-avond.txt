So we leave Venice tonight, in the year 1300, as the evening falls.

The sun is setting behind the mountains of the mainland, far to the west. The lagoon turns gold, then pink, then a deep soft blue. The boats are coming home across the still water, one by one. A fisherman pulls his boat up to a landing and ties it to a striped post. The seagulls settle on the rooftops for the night. And from Murano, the glass furnaces glow orange in the dusk.

--

In the family's house, the lamps are being lit. Lucia is closing her account book and putting it away in the chest. Matteo is coming in from the workshop, wiping the soot from his hands. Nicolò is sitting by the window, holding up a little blue bead to the last of the light. Nonna Bettina is folding her knitting and putting it in her basket.

{img:venetian-family-evening-room-lamps-glass-window-grandmother-children}
Uncle Zorzi is home, sitting by the fire with his boots off. He is telling Nicolò about the lighthouse of Alexandria, for the third time tonight. Nicolò does not mind. He could listen to his uncle's stories forever.

--

Up on the altana, Chiara is sitting with her friend Isabetta. They are wrapped in shawls against the evening chill. Chiara is holding her little glass bird from Egypt. She holds it up to the sky, so the last light shines through it. Green and gold. Isabetta says it is the most beautiful thing she has ever seen. Chiara agrees. They sit together quietly and watch the stars come out over the lagoon.

{img:two-girls-rooftop-terrace-dusk-glass-bird-held-to-sky-lagoon-stars}
Across the water, the towers and domes of Venice are dark against the evening sky. The bells of San Marco ring for evening prayer. Then the bells of all the churches answer, near and far, across the lagoon. The sound drifts softly over the water. And then it fades, and everything is quiet.

--

Down in the courtyard, Rosso the cat is sitting by the well, washing his face with his paw. A late swallow swoops low over the roof and disappears. In the workshop, the night worker is feeding the furnace with wood. Its warm orange glow spills out through the open door onto the canal. And the water shines softly in the dark, gold and blue.

The tide is coming in, gently, filling the canals. The water laps softly against the old brick walls and the wooden posts. A boatman somewhere is singing an old song, slowly, as he rows home in the dark. The lamps in the windows are reflected in the still water. And the whole city on the water settles down to sleep, under the quiet stars.
