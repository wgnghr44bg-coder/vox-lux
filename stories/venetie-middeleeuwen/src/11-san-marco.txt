At the heart of Venice lies a great open square, called the Piazza San Marco. And at one end of the square stands the most famous church in Venice. It is the Basilica of San Marco, the church of Saint Mark.

The church is very different from the churches in other parts of Italy. It has five round domes on its roof, like a church in Constantinople. Its front is decorated with columns of coloured marble, brought from all over the Mediterranean. Above the doors, there are arches filled with pictures made of tiny pieces of glass and gold. These pictures are called mosaics. They glitter in the sunshine.

--

Inside, the church is dim and quiet and golden. The walls and the domes are covered with mosaics. There are pictures of saints and angels, of stories from the Bible. There is the story of Noah and his ark, and the story of the creation of the world. The background of every picture is made of tiny squares of gold glass. When candles are lit, the whole church shimmers and glows, like the inside of a jewel box.

{img:interior-san-marco-golden-mosaics-domes-candles-worshippers-dim}
Many of the tiny pieces of gold glass were made on Murano. Matteo's father helped to make some of them, long ago. He made the gold glass by putting a thin layer of real gold leaf between two layers of clear glass. Then the glass was cut into tiny squares, smaller than a fingernail. Artists pressed them into wet plaster, one by one, to make the pictures. It took many years.

--

Above the main door of the church stand four great horses of bronze. They are covered in gold, and they shine in the sun. They are very old, more than a thousand years old even then. The Venetians brought them back from Constantinople about a hundred years before our story. Nobody is quite sure who made them. They look as if they are about to step forward, with their heads held high.

{img:four-bronze-horses-san-marco-gilded-sunlight-pigeons-loggia}
On Sundays and holy days, the family from Murano sometimes comes to San Marco for mass. They row across the lagoon in their best clothes. Nonna Bettina wears her best linen cap. Lucia wears a dress of blue wool, and a little necklace of glass beads. The children are scrubbed clean and told to behave.

--

Inside the church, Chiara looks up at the golden domes with her mouth open. She tries to count the angels, but there are too many. She finds the little picture of Noah's ark, with all the animals going inside two by two. She looks for the pieces of gold glass her grandfather made. She cannot tell which ones they are. But Matteo tells her they are up there, somewhere, shining.

{img:family-standing-piazza-san-marco-after-mass-pigeons-bell-tower-sun}
After mass, the family walks out into the great square. It is full of people in their Sunday clothes. There are pigeons everywhere, pecking at crumbs. Children run and play between the columns. The great bell tower stands tall above everything. Its bells are ringing, so loudly that the whole square seems to shake. Matteo buys the children a little sweet cake each, from a man with a tray. And they sit on the steps in the sunshine and eat them slowly.

The Piazza San Marco is not only a place for church. It is also a place for markets and fairs. On certain days, there are stalls selling food and cloth and many other things. There are jugglers and acrobats, and men with performing animals. There are storytellers, who tell old tales to anyone who will listen. And there are always people walking up and down, just to see and be seen.

Near the water, there are two tall columns of stone. On top of one of them stands the winged lion of Saint Mark. On top of the other stands Saint Theodore, the older protector of Venice, standing on a crocodile. Visitors arriving by sea step ashore between these two columns. It is the grand entrance to the city. Chiara likes the crocodile best. She has never seen a real one, but Uncle Zorzi says they live in the river Nile.
