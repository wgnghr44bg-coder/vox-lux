How did a great city come to be built on the water? The story goes back about eight hundred years before our story, to the time when the Roman Empire was falling apart.

In those days, armies from the north came into Italy. People in the towns on the mainland were frightened. So some of them fled to the islands in the lagoon. The shallow water and the mud protected them. Armies on horseback could not follow them there. And big ships could not sail into the shallow lagoon without knowing the hidden channels.

--

At first, the people lived simply, in houses of wood and reeds. They fished and made salt, and they traded along the rivers and the coast. Slowly, over many years, more people came. The little villages on the islands grew bigger. People began to build with brick and stone. They built churches and houses and little bridges between the islands. And slowly, the villages joined together into one city.

{img:workers-driving-wooden-piles-into-mud-building-foundations-venice}
Building on the soft mud of the lagoon is not easy. So the Venetians found a clever way to do it. They took long wooden poles, made from the trunks of oak and larch trees. They drove these poles deep into the mud, side by side, thousands of them. On top of the poles, they laid flat planks of wood. Then they laid stones on top of the planks. And on top of the stones, they built their houses and churches.

--

The wooden poles are under the water and the mud, where there is no air. So they do not rot. Instead, over the years, they become as hard as stone. Many of the poles that hold up Venice today were put there hundreds of years ago. The whole city stands on a hidden forest of wooden poles, upside down under the water. The wood for these poles came from the forests in the mountains, floated down the rivers on rafts.

{img:timber-rafts-floating-down-river-from-mountain-forests-to-lagoon}
The early Venetians chose a leader, whom they called the doge. The word doge comes from the Latin word for leader. The doge was chosen by the people, not born into the job like a king. The first doges lived simply, among the people. Later, the doges became grander. But they were always chosen, never simply born to rule.

--

There is a famous old story about Saint Mark, who wrote one of the books of the Bible. His body lay in a tomb in the city of Alexandria, in Egypt. About four hundred and seventy years before our story, two Venetian merchants brought his body back to Venice. They hid it in a basket under some pork, so the guards would not look inside. From then on, Saint Mark was the protector of Venice. His symbol, a lion with wings, appears everywhere in the city.

{img:winged-lion-saint-mark-stone-carving-column-sunlight-venice}
The Venetians are proud of their unusual city. They say it is a gift from God, built where nobody else would build. They call it the Most Serene Republic. And they look after it with great care. Every Venetian knows that the city lives with the water, and must always respect it. The sea brings them their wealth. But the sea could also take everything away.

The early Venetians were very good at trade. They had little land for farming, so they had to buy most of their food. They sold salt and fish, and they carried goods for other people along the rivers and coasts. Little by little, their boats went further and further. They sailed down the coast of the Adriatic Sea, and then out into the wider Mediterranean.

Every old family in Venice has its own story about how its ancestors came to the lagoon. Nonna Bettina tells the children that their family came from a town on the mainland, long ago. They came in a little boat, she says, with a goat, a cooking pot and a holy picture. The children do not know if it is true. But they love the story, and they ask for it again and again.

Over the years, the Venetians also learned to make their islands bigger. They filled in shallow places with earth and stones and rubbish. They built walls of wood and stone along the edges of the canals. Slowly, the little islands joined together, with bridges and narrow streets between them. Today, the city stands on more than a hundred small islands. But from above, it looks like one great island, shaped a little like a fish.
