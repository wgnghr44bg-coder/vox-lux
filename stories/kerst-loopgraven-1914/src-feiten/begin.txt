On a frozen field in Belgium, a young soldier lifts his head above the edge of a muddy trench. It's Christmas morning, 1914. The air is so cold it stings. Across a strip of white, frosty grass, less than a hundred metres away, a man in a grey coat is climbing out of the enemy trench. He isn't carrying a rifle. He's carrying a small Christmas tree, and he's smiling.

How could such a thing happen, in the middle of a war? Who were these men, on both sides of the wire, and what was daily life like for them through that first cold winter?
