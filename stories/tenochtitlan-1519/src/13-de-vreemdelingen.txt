In April 1519, eleven Spanish ships anchor off the coast of the Gulf of Mexico, near the place that is now the city of Veracruz.

On board, there are about five hundred Spanish soldiers, and a hundred sailors, and sixteen horses, and some cannons, and a few dogs. Their leader is a man named Hernán Cortés.

--

Cortés is thirty-four years old, from a small town in Extremadura, in the dry west of Spain. He studied law for a while, at the University of Salamanca, and then went to the Caribbean, as a young man, looking for his fortune. He has been living in Cuba, as a landowner, and an official.

He is clever, and charming, and persuasive, and very ambitious, and very bold. The governor of Cuba sent him to explore the coast, and to trade. But Cortés has decided to do much more: to find the great kingdom that he has heard about, in the mountains, and to conquer it.

On the coast, Moctezuma's officials come to meet the strangers, with gifts: beautiful things of gold and feathers and cloth. They ask the strangers to go away. But the gifts only make the Spanish more eager to go on.

--

Cortés has a remarkable helper.

On the way along the coast, in the land of the Maya, in the Tabasco region, the local people gave the Spanish twenty young women, as a gift. One of them was a young Nahua woman, named Malintzin, who had been born in a noble family, in the lands of the Mexica, but had been sold, or given away, as a child, to the Maya, and had grown up among them.

{img:young-indigenous-woman-interpreter-standing-between-groups}
So Malintzin speaks both Nahuatl, the language of the Mexica, and Maya. And among the Spanish, there is a man named Gerónimo de Aguilar, who was shipwrecked on the coast of Yucatán, years before, and lived among the Maya, and learned their language. So, at first, Malintzin translates from Nahuatl to Maya, and Aguilar from Maya to Spanish. Soon, Malintzin learns Spanish too.

--

The Spanish call her Doña Marina. She becomes Cortés's interpreter, and his adviser, and his companion. She is clever, and brave, and calm, and she understands the ways of the Mexica, and the other peoples of Mexico, and helps Cortés to understand them.

In Mexico today, people have very mixed feelings about her. For a long time, she was seen as a traitor, who helped the Spanish conquer her own people. But today, many people see her differently: as a young woman, who had been sold into slavery as a child, and who did what she had to do, to survive, in a terrible time. And as the mother of one of the first children of mixed Spanish and Native blood, the beginning of the Mexican people.

{img:interpreter-woman-speaking-calmly-council-chiefs-listening}
The Mexica call Cortés Malinche, after her, because he is always with her.

--

Cortés founds a little town on the coast, which he calls Villa Rica de la Vera Cruz, the rich town of the true cross. And then, so that none of his men can turn back, he has his ships run aground, and stripped, and sunk.

Then, in August 1519, the Spanish march inland, toward the mountains, and Tenochtitlan.

On the way, they meet the Totonac people, of the coast, who are tired of paying tribute to the Mexica, and become their allies. And then, in the mountains, they come to Tlaxcala, the independent republic, the old enemy of the Mexica.

--

At first, the Tlaxcalans fight the Spanish. There are several battles. But then they make peace, and become their allies, against the Mexica. Thousands of Tlaxcalan warriors join the Spanish.

This is the secret of the conquest. It is not only a few hundred Spanish soldiers who will defeat the Mexica. It is the Spanish, together with tens of thousands of Native warriors, from the peoples who hated Mexica rule: Tlaxcalans, and Totonacs, and others, who see the strangers as useful allies, in their own long struggle against Tenochtitlan.

{img:tlaxcalan-warriors-banners-meeting-spanish-camp-hills}
At the beginning of November 1519, the Spanish and their allies climb up into the high pass between the two great volcanoes, Popocatepetl and Iztaccihuatl, through pine forests, and snow, and cold winds.

And from the top of the pass, they look down, for the first time, on the Valley of Mexico, with its lakes, and its cities, shining in the sun.
