Among all the gifts that the people of Mexico gave to the world, perhaps the most beloved is chocolate.

The cacao tree grows in the hot, wet lowlands of southern Mexico and Central America, far from the cool Valley of Mexico. It is a small tree, which grows in the shade of taller trees, with big, shiny leaves. Its fruits, the cacao pods, grow straight out of the trunk and the branches, large and oval, yellow and orange and red and purple.

--

Inside each pod, there are thirty or forty seeds, the cacao beans, wrapped in a sweet white pulp. The beans are taken out, and left to ferment for a few days, and then dried in the sun, and roasted, and ground on a metate, into a thick, dark paste.

The people of Mexico and Central America have been making chocolate from cacao for thousands of years. The Olmecs, and the Maya, and then the Mexica.

{img:woman-grinding-roasted-cacao-metate-paste-dark}
The Mexica cannot grow cacao in the high valley. So they get it as tribute, and by trade, from the hot lands of the south. It is so valuable that cacao beans are used as money. Moctezuma's storehouses, the Spanish said, contained more than forty thousand loads of cacao beans.

--

The Mexica do not eat chocolate as we do. They drink it, cold, or at room temperature, as a foaming drink, called xocolatl.

To make it, they mix the cacao paste with water, and flavour it with chili, or vanilla, or flowers, or honey, or maize. Then they pour it from one cup to another, from a height, again and again, to make a thick, rich foam on the top. The foam is the best part.

It is a drink for the rich, and for warriors, and for feasts. Moctezuma drinks it, it is said, from golden cups, many times a day. Ordinary people drink it only on special occasions.

--

After the conquest, the Spanish learned to drink chocolate too. At first, they did not like its bitter taste. But they added sugar, and cinnamon, and drank it hot, and soon, it became very popular in Spain, and then all over Europe, as a fashionable drink, in the chocolate houses of London and Paris and Amsterdam.

The word chocolate comes from the Nahuatl language, the language of the Mexica.

{img:european-chocolate-house-seventeenth-century-cups-pot}
And so do many other words that we still use today: tomato, from tomatl. Avocado, from ahuacatl. Chili, from chilli. Chia, and coyote, and ocelot, and axolotl, and guacamole, from ahuacamolli, avocado sauce.

--

Many of the foods that the whole world eats today first came from Mexico and the Americas.

Maize, which is now grown all over the world. Beans, many kinds. Squash, and pumpkins. Tomatoes, which the Italians would later make into their sauces. Chili peppers, which would travel to India, and Thailand, and China, and Hungary, and change the cooking of half the world. Avocados. Vanilla. Turkeys. Peanuts. Sweet potatoes. And chocolate.

{img:market-basket-tomatoes-chilis-avocados-maize-beans-squash}
It is hard to imagine the food of the world without them. Before 1519, nobody in Europe or Asia or Africa had ever tasted any of them.
