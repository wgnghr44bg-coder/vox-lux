In the sacred precinct of Tenochtitlan, there is a ball court. And all over Mexico and Central America, in almost every city, there are ball courts too, for one of the oldest games in the world.

The Mexica call it ullamaliztli. It has been played for more than three thousand years, by the Olmecs, and the Maya, and the Toltecs, and many other peoples, long before the Mexica.

--

The court is shaped like a capital letter I: a long, narrow alley, between two sloping or straight walls of stone, with wider areas at each end. High up, in the middle of each side wall, there is a stone ring, set vertically, like a hoop turned on its side.

The ball is made of solid rubber, heavy and bouncy, about the size of a large grapefruit, or a little bigger. The Mexica get the rubber from the rubber trees of the hot lands, on the Gulf coast, as tribute. In fact, the Spanish had never seen rubber before they came to Mexico. They were amazed by the way the ball bounced, as if it were alive.

{img:solid-rubber-ball-close-player-hip-padding-leather}
The players may not touch the ball with their hands, or their feet. They must hit it with their hips, and their knees, and their elbows, and their buttocks, and keep it in the air, and send it to the other side. They wear thick pads of leather on their hips, and their knees, to protect themselves, because the ball is heavy, and hard.

--

The rules are not entirely known to us. But it seems that the teams score points when the other team lets the ball touch the ground in the wrong place, or fails to send it back. And if a player manages to send the ball through one of the stone rings, high on the wall, which is very difficult, and very rare, his team wins the game at once. And by custom, the lucky player may claim the cloaks and jewellery of all the spectators. So, when this happens, the spectators run away, laughing, as fast as they can.

{img:stone-ring-high-on-wall-ball-court-sunlight-shadow}
The game is very fast, and very exciting, and very difficult. The best players are famous, and admired. The nobles love to watch, and bet on the games: gold, and jewels, and cloaks, and feathers, and even houses and land.

--

The ball game also has a sacred meaning.

The Mexica believe that the ball court is like the sky, and the ball is like the sun, or the moon, moving across it. The game is a picture of the struggle between light and darkness, day and night. On certain festivals, the game is played as a ceremony, in honour of the gods.

{img:spanish-court-1528-indigenous-players-demonstrating-game}
In 1528, Cortés took some Mexica ball players with him to Spain, to show the game to the Emperor Charles the Fifth. A German artist, Christoph Weiditz, who was at the Spanish court, drew pictures of them playing, with their leather hip pads, which still exist today.

--

And the game is still played today, in a few villages in the northwest of Mexico, in the state of Sinaloa, where it is called ulama. The players still hit the heavy rubber ball with their hips, on a long, narrow court of earth, just as their ancestors did, thousands of years ago.

It is one of the oldest team sports in the world. And every time a child kicks a rubber ball, anywhere in the world, they are using something that the people of ancient Mexico gave us.
