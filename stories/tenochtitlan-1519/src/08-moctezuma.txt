The ruler of Tenochtitlan, in 1519, is Moctezuma Xocoyotzin, Moctezuma the younger.

His title is huey tlatoani, which means great speaker, because the ruler is the one who speaks for his people. He is about fifty years old. He has ruled since 1502, for seventeen years.

--

Moctezuma is a slim man, of medium height, with a thin, dark beard, and long hair. He is serious, and dignified, and religious, and very proud. Before he became ruler, he was a priest, and a great warrior. When the nobles came to tell him that he had been chosen as ruler, it is said, they found him sweeping the floor of a temple.

He is very clean, Bernal Díaz says, and bathes every afternoon. He changes his clothes four times a day, and never wears the same clothes twice. He wears a cloak of fine white cotton, and sandals with golden soles, and a crown of turquoise.

When he goes out, he is carried in a litter, covered with feathers and gold, and nobles walk before him, sweeping the ground, and laying cloths for him to walk on. Nobody may look him in the face. Everyone must bow, with their eyes cast down, when he passes.

--

Moctezuma lives in a magnificent palace, near the Great Temple.

It has hundreds of rooms, and halls, and courtyards, with walls of stone, carved and painted, and floors of polished plaster, and ceilings of fragrant cedar wood. There are gardens, full of flowers, and medicinal plants, and fountains, and pools. There are rooms for his wives, and his children, and his servants, and his guests, and his councillors.

{img:palace-hall-cedar-ceiling-painted-walls-feather-hangings}
Every day, three hundred cooks prepare his meals. More than three hundred dishes are brought to him, on little warming dishes, with fires underneath, so that he can choose what he wants. He eats alone, behind a gilded wooden screen, so that nobody can see him eat, while musicians play, and jesters and dwarfs and acrobats entertain him.

And after the meal, he drinks chocolate, foaming, in golden cups, and smokes tobacco, in a painted tube.

--

And Moctezuma has a zoo.

It is the first great zoo in the Americas, perhaps in the world. There are hundreds of birds, from every part of the empire: eagles, and hawks, and parrots, and quetzals, and hummingbirds, and herons, and flamingos, and ducks, in cages, and in great pools, of fresh water and salt water. Three hundred people work just to look after the birds. When the birds moult, their feathers are collected, and used by the featherworkers.

{img:aviary-courtyard-colourful-birds-parrots-quetzal-pools}
There are animals too: jaguars, and pumas, and wolves, and foxes, and snakes, in a special house. Bernal Díaz says that the noise of the animals, the roaring and the howling and the hissing, was frightening to hear.

--

Moctezuma has gardens, too, outside the city, in the hills, at places like Chapultepec, and Oaxtepec, with beautiful flowers, and trees, and medicinal plants, brought from every part of the empire, and pools, and pavilions, where he goes to rest, and enjoy the beauty of nature.

He has a library of painted books, and scribes, and astronomers, and wise men, who advise him.

And he is a powerful ruler. He has made the empire stronger, and larger. He has made the nobles more important, and separated them more from the common people. He has fought many wars, and conquered many cities. But he has also made enemies.

--

To the east of the valley, beyond the mountains, there is a small, independent republic, called Tlaxcala, which the Mexica have never been able to conquer.

For many years, the Mexica and the Tlaxcalans have fought each other, in a strange kind of war, called the flower war, arranged by both sides, not to conquer land, but to capture warriors. The Tlaxcalans hate the Mexica, more than anything.

{img:mountain-ridge-tlaxcala-town-walls-distant-valley-haze}
And many of the peoples who pay tribute to the Mexica resent them, and would be glad to see them overthrown.

In the spring of 1519, Moctezuma does not know that this resentment will soon be used by strangers from across the sea.
