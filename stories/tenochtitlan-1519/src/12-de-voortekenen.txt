In the years before 1519, the old chronicles say, there were strange signs in Tenochtitlan.

These stories were written down after the conquest, by Mexica scholars and Spanish friars, and some of them may have been told, or changed, afterward, to explain what happened. But they are part of the story that the Mexica themselves told about the end of their world.

--

They say that, ten years before the Spanish came, a great fiery light appeared in the night sky, in the east, like a flame, or a great ear of fire, which shone every night, for a year, from midnight to dawn. The people were afraid, and wept.

They say that the temple of Huitzilopochtli caught fire, by itself, and burned, and that the more water the people threw on it, the more it burned.

{img:lake-waves-rising-calm-day-strange-wind-city-shore}
They say that the temple of Xiuhtecuhtli, the god of fire, was struck by lightning, without any thunder, on a day of light rain. That a comet fell, in daylight, in three parts, from west to east, with long tails of sparks. That the lake boiled up, and rose in great waves, on a day without wind, and flooded the houses.

--

And they say that, at night, a woman was heard weeping, in the streets, crying out: oh my children, we must go far away. Oh my children, where shall I take you?

And that some fishermen caught a strange bird, like a crane, grey, with a mirror on its head. And they took it to Moctezuma. And when he looked into the mirror, he saw the night sky, and the stars. And when he looked again, he saw a great crowd of men, coming toward him, riding on animals like deer, armed for war. And when he called his wise men to look, the bird vanished.

These are the omens that the Mexica remembered.

--

Moctezuma, the stories say, was deeply troubled. He called his wise men, and his priests, and asked them what the signs meant. And when they could not tell him, he was angry.

There is a story, too, that Moctezuma remembered the old legend of Quetzalcoatl, the feathered serpent god, who had gone away across the eastern sea, long ago, and promised to return, in a year called One Reed. And 1519 was a year One Reed.

{img:eastern-sea-coast-dawn-horizon-waves-calm-waiting}
Some historians today doubt this story. They think that it was invented after the conquest, by the Spanish, or by the Mexica themselves, to explain why Moctezuma did not fight the Spanish at once. We do not really know what Moctezuma believed.

--

But we do know that, in the years before 1519, news began to come to Tenochtitlan, from the coast, of strange things.

The Spanish had arrived in the Caribbean in 1492, with Christopher Columbus. They had settled on the islands, on Hispaniola and Cuba. And from there, they had begun to explore the coasts of the mainland. In 1517 and 1518, two Spanish expeditions sailed along the coast of the Gulf of Mexico, and the Yucatán, and met the Maya, and other peoples.

{img:large-sailing-ships-white-sails-gulf-coast-distant-watchers}
And messengers came to Moctezuma, with paintings, made by his scribes on the coast, showing great houses floating on the sea, with white sails, like clouds, and strange men, with white faces, and long beards, and clothes of metal, and strange weapons.

Moctezuma ordered his people on the coast to watch, and to report everything.
