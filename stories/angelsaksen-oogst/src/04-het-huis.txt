The house of Wulfric's family is a long, low building of wood. It is about ten metres long and five metres wide. Its walls are made of wooden posts, set in the ground, with the spaces filled with woven branches covered in clay. The clay is mixed with straw and dung, and when it dries, it is hard and keeps out the wind. The roof is very steep, made of thick bundles of straw. It reaches down almost to the ground.

--

There is one door, in the middle of one of the long walls. There are no glass windows. Glass is far too expensive for farmers. Instead, there are a few small openings in the walls, which can be closed with wooden shutters. So inside, even in the daytime, it is quite dark. The light comes mostly from the open door, and from the fire.

The house has one big room. In the middle of the floor, there is the hearth, a low square of flat stones, where the fire burns day and night. There is no chimney. The smoke rises up into the roof, and finds its way out through the straw. This keeps the roof dry and free of insects. But it also means that the inside of the house always smells a little of smoke, and the roof beams are black.

{img:inside-anglo-saxon-house-central-hearth-smoke-rising-beams-benches}
Along the walls, there are wide wooden benches, covered with straw and sheepskins. During the day, the family sits on them. At night, they sleep on them, wrapped in woollen blankets. There are wooden chests, where the family keeps its clothes and its few precious things. There is a loom leaning against the wall. And hanging from the beams, there are herbs, onions, and bags of seeds, drying in the smoke.

Near the fire, there are the cooking things. A big iron cauldron, hanging from a chain over the fire. Clay pots for storing food. Wooden bowls and spoons. A quern, which is two round flat stones, one on top of the other, for grinding grain into flour by hand. And a wooden bucket for water, which Hild fills at the well every morning.

--

{img:sunken-floored-workshop-hut-anglo-saxon-loom-weights-small-building}
Next to the main house, there are two smaller buildings. They are strange little huts, with their floors dug down into the ground, about half a metre deep. Archaeologists call them sunken huts. One of them is Eadgyth's workshop, where she weaves cloth on a big upright loom. The other is a storehouse, for tools, firewood and food. In winter, the sunken floors keep the huts warmer.

Around the buildings, there is a yard, with a fence of woven branches. Here, the chickens scratch in the dust and the geese wander about. There is a pen for the pigs, and a shelter for the oxen and the cows. And behind the house, there is the vegetable garden. Eadgyth grows cabbages, onions, leeks, beans, peas and garlic. There are also herbs. Parsley, mint, sage and dill. And in one corner, under an apple tree, there are three straw beehives.

{img:vegetable-garden-anglo-saxon-cabbages-beans-straw-beehives-apple-tree}
The house is simple, but it is warm and cosy. The thick roof keeps out the rain. The fire keeps away the cold. In summer, the door stands open all day, and the light and the warm air come in. Birds fly in and out. The cat sleeps in the sun on the doorstep. And the smell of bread and woodsmoke drifts out into the yard.

--

Wulfric built this house himself, with the help of his neighbours, when he married Eadgyth. Before that, he lived in his father's house, at the other end of the village. Building a house takes about a month, if everyone helps. The wood comes from the forest. The straw comes from the fields. The clay comes from the riverbank. Everything that is needed is found close by. And when a house becomes too old, after twenty or thirty years, the family simply builds a new one next to it.

At night, the house is very dark. The fire is covered with ashes, so that it glows softly until morning, without going out. The family sleeps close together on the benches, warm under their blankets. The cat curls up on Modor's feet. The dog lies by the door. And sometimes, in the roof above them, a mouse rustles in the straw, or a bird settles down to sleep.
