When the very last field is cut, and the last cart of sheaves is brought home, the village celebrates. In later centuries, the English called this harvest home. It is one of the happiest moments of the year. The hard work is done. The grain is safe. And everyone can rest, at least for a little while.

--

The last cart comes home in a procession. It is decorated with flowers, green branches and ribbons. On top sits the corn dolly, the figure made from the last sheaf. The oxen have flowers around their horns. The children ride on top of the sheaves, laughing and waving. And behind the cart, the whole village walks, singing an old harvest song, all the way from the field to the barn.

When the cart arrives in the village, the old people come out of their houses to greet it. They clap and cheer. Modor stands at the door, smiling. Wynflaed, the old widow, waves her stick. And the cart rolls slowly into the barn, where the last sheaves are unloaded and stacked. Wulfric hangs the corn dolly above the barn door, where it will stay until spring.

{img:village-green-long-tables-harvest-supper-lanterns-families-together}
Then the village has its own harvest supper. It is not as grand as the feast in the lord's hall. But it is more cosy. Long tables are set up in the open space in the middle of the village, on wooden trestles. Every family brings food. Bread from the new harvest. Stews and soups. Cheese and butter. Apples, plums and blackberries. A roasted pig, turning on a spit over a fire. And barrels of ale.

Everyone eats together. Old and young, rich and poor, neighbours and friends. Even the priest comes, and says a prayer of thanks before the meal. He thanks God for the good harvest, for the sun and the rain, and for the strong arms of the reapers. Everyone says amen. And then they eat and drink and talk, until the sun goes down.

--

{img:villagers-dancing-in-circle-firelight-music-pipes-drum-evening}
After the meal, there is music. One of the young men plays a pipe, made from a bird bone. Another beats a small drum. And the people dance. Simple dances, in long lines and circles, holding hands, stepping and turning. The children dance too, getting in everyone's way. Wulfric dances with Eadgyth. Hild dances with the other girls. And Cuthwine dances by himself, round and round, until he falls over.

There are also games. Wrestling matches between the young men, in the grass. Races for the children. A game where people try to catch a greased pig, which always escapes. Everyone laughs until they cry. And when it gets dark, a big bonfire is lit. The sparks fly up into the night sky, among the stars.

{img:girl-receiving-glass-beads-gift-father-bonfire-light-smiling}
By the light of the bonfire, Wulfric takes out the little pot of glass beads he bought in Hamwic. He gives them to Hild. Honey-coloured beads, shining like little drops of sunshine in the firelight. Hild is speechless. She threads them onto a string at once, and wears them around her neck. Then she runs off to show her friends. Eadgyth smiles at her husband. Cuthwine gives Modor her new antler comb. Modor holds it for a long time, and says nothing at all, but her eyes are shining.

--

Late at night, the bonfire burns down to glowing embers. People slowly go home to their beds. The stars are very bright. The air is cool, with the first smell of autumn in it. The harvest is home. The barns are full. And for tonight, nobody in the village has a single worry in the world.

The next morning, the village is very quiet. Everyone sleeps a little longer than usual. Even the roosters seem to crow more softly. When people finally get up, they smile at each other across the yards, a little sleepy, a little stiff from the dancing. And then, slowly, the ordinary work of the day begins again. But everyone feels lighter. The harvest is home.
