On the first of August, there is a special day. In later centuries, the Anglo-Saxons called it hlafmæsse. It means loaf mass. Over time, the name became Lammas, and it is still called that in some parts of England today. It is the festival of the first bread of the new harvest.

--

A few days before, Wulfric cut a small amount of the new wheat, from the best corner of his best strip. Just enough to fill a basket. He brought it home, and the family beat the grains out of the ears with sticks, on a clean cloth on the floor. Then Hild and her mother ground the grain into flour, with the quern stones. They turned the top stone round and round, and fine white flour came out at the edges.

Early on the morning of the first of August, Eadgyth bakes a loaf from this flour. It is the first loaf of the new harvest. She mixes the flour with water, a little salt and some yeast from the ale. She kneads the dough on a wooden board, and shapes it into a round loaf. Then she bakes it on a flat stone in the hot ashes of the fire, covered with an upturned clay pot, so it bakes evenly.

{img:round-loaf-baking-under-clay-pot-hot-ashes-hearth-glowing}
The smell of the new bread fills the whole house. Cuthwine wakes up and sniffs the air. Is it ready? Can I have some? His mother laughs and says no. This loaf is not for eating. Not yet. First, it must go to the church, to be blessed. Cuthwine sighs a big sigh. But he knows the custom. Every family in the village does the same.

When the loaf is ready, Eadgyth wraps it in a clean white cloth. The whole family puts on their best clothes. Wulfric wears his good woollen tunic, with a leather belt. Eadgyth wears her blue dress, with a brooch at her shoulder. Hild has a new ribbon in her hair. Even Cuthwine has had his face washed, although his hair still sticks up at the back.

--

{img:villagers-walking-to-small-church-carrying-loaves-white-cloths-morning}
Then they walk together to the little church. It is about half an hour's walk away, in the next village. Along the way, they meet other families, all carrying their loaves wrapped in white cloths. The children run ahead. The old people walk slowly. Everyone is talking and smiling. It is a happy day. The harvest is ready, and the first bread is baked.

The church is small and made of wood, with a little bell hanging in a frame outside. Inside, it is simple and dim, with a wooden altar and a cross. The priest is waiting at the door. He is a kind, middle-aged man, who looks after the people of several villages. He greets everyone by name. Then the people go inside, and lay their loaves on the altar, one by one.

{img:priest-blessing-loaves-on-altar-small-wooden-church-candles}
The priest says a prayer over the bread. He thanks God for the sun and the rain, for the soil and the seed, and for the harvest that is ready in the fields. He asks God to protect the harvest, and to keep away the storms, until all the grain is safely home. The people say amen. Then the priest blesses the bread, and gives each family back its loaf.

--

Some people break their blessed loaf into four pieces, and put one piece in each corner of their barn. They believe it will protect the grain from mice and damp. It is an old custom, much older than the church. Modor does this every year. Then the rest of the bread is eaten by the family, at home, with fresh butter and honey. Cuthwine finally gets his piece. He says it is the best bread he has ever tasted. He says that every year.
