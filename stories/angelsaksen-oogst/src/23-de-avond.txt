So we leave our Anglo-Saxon village tonight, in the year 700, as the evening falls. The sun is going down behind the low hills. Its last golden light is shining across the stubble fields, where the stooks stand in long rows, casting long shadows. The swallows are swooping low over the river. And a soft mist is beginning to rise from the meadows.

--

In the village, the smoke is rising from the thatched roofs, straight up into the still evening air. The cows have been milked. The geese have been shut in for the night. The oxen are lying down in their shelter, chewing slowly. And in the doorway of the house, the cat is washing its face, in the last warm patch of sunlight.

{img:inside-house-night-fire-glowing-family-asleep-on-benches-blankets}
Inside the house, the fire is burning low. Its red glow lights up the dark roof beams, and the bunches of herbs hanging from them. Modor is sitting by the fire, her spindle finally still in her lap. Eadgyth is putting away her weaving. Wulfric is sharpening his sickle, for tomorrow, with slow, steady strokes of the whetstone.

--

Little Cuthwine is already asleep on his bench, wrapped in his woollen blanket, with one arm hanging down. Hild is lying beside him, not quite asleep. She is holding her honey-coloured beads in her hand, and listening to the sounds of the evening. The crackle of the fire. The soft voices of her parents. An owl calling from the woods. The river, flowing slowly past in the dark.

{img:moonlit-field-stooks-silver-light-owl-flying-quiet-countryside}
Outside, the moon is rising over the fields. It shines on the stooks of wheat, and turns them silver. An owl glides silently over the stubble, looking for mice. A fox trots along the edge of the field, and stops to sniff the air. The bees are quiet in their straw hives. The whole countryside is still, and peaceful, and full of the smell of the harvest.

--

The grain is ripe. The weather is fair. Tomorrow, the reapers will go out to the fields again, with their sickles over their shoulders, singing. But for now, the village sleeps. The fire glows. The stars come out, one by one, over the quiet hills of England. And somewhere, very softly, the river goes on flowing, as it has for thousands of years.
