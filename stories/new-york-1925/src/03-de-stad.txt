New York is made of five parts, called boroughs. The most famous is Manhattan, a long, narrow island between two rivers. The others are Brooklyn, Queens, the Bronx and Staten Island. Great bridges and ferries connect them all.

Manhattan is the heart of the city. At its southern tip, there are the old streets of downtown, with the banks of Wall Street and the busy harbour. Further north, there are the shops and theatres of Midtown. In the middle of the island, there is a great park, called Central Park, with lakes and meadows and woods. And at the northern end, there is Harlem, a lively neighbourhood full of music.

--

The streets of Manhattan are laid out in a grid, like a chessboard. The avenues run from north to south, and the streets run from east to west. Most of them have numbers instead of names. So it is easy to find your way. If you are on Fifth Avenue and Forty-Second Street, you know exactly where you are.

{img:elevated-train-tracks-steel-above-street-1925-trains-shadows-shops-below}
Getting around New York is easy, but noisy. There are trolleys, running on rails in the streets. There are buses and taxis, cars and trucks. Above some of the avenues, there are elevated railways, called the El. Trains rattle along on tall iron tracks, high above the street, past the second-floor windows of the buildings. And under the ground, there is the subway, the underground railway, carrying millions of people every day.

--

The subway is one of the wonders of New York. It opened about twenty years before, in 1904. A ride costs just five cents, a nickel, and you can go almost anywhere in the city. The trains are crowded and hot in summer. But they are fast. People read their newspapers, chat with friends, or doze as the train rushes through the dark tunnels.

{img:subway-car-1925-passengers-straps-newspapers-hats-electric-lights}
Every neighbourhood of New York is different. In Little Italy, people speak Italian, and shops sell pasta and olive oil. In Chinatown, next door, there are Chinese restaurants and shops selling tea and silk. On the Lower East Side, many Jewish families live, and the streets are full of pushcarts selling everything. In Harlem, there are jazz clubs and churches and poets. In each neighbourhood, people keep their own language and customs, but they are all New Yorkers.

--

The most famous place in the city is Times Square. It is where Broadway, the street of the theatres, crosses Seventh Avenue. At night, it is lit up with thousands of electric lights. There are huge signs advertising chewing gum and cigarettes, cars and shows. People call Broadway the Great White Way, because of all the lights. Crowds walk there every evening, just to look.

{img:times-square-1925-night-electric-signs-crowds-theatres-cars-glow}
For the people of New York, the city is noisy, crowded and sometimes tiring. But it is also exciting and full of life. There is always something to see, something to hear, something new to discover. People say that New York never sleeps. And in 1925, it seems to be true.

New York also has many bridges. The most famous is the Brooklyn Bridge, finished in 1883. It is a great suspension bridge, hanging from thick steel cables, between two tall towers of stone. People walk across it on a wooden path above the traffic, enjoying the view of the river and the city. On summer evenings, couples stroll across it, hand in hand, as the sun sets.

In the evenings, the streets of New York are busy in a different way. People come out of their offices and factories and head home. The subway and the elevated trains are packed. The cinemas and theatres open their doors. Restaurants and cafés fill up. And in the neighbourhoods, people sit on their stoops and fire escapes, talking with their neighbours in the warm evening air.

In the middle of Manhattan, Central Park is a green island in the city of stone. It has lakes with rowing boats, meadows with sheep, and woods with winding paths. There is a zoo, and a carousel for children. On Sundays, families from all over the city come to the park, to walk and picnic and breathe the fresh air. It feels like the countryside, right in the middle of the busiest city in America.
