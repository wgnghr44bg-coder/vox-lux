In 1925, the most famous man in New York is not a president or a businessman. He is a baseball player. His name is Babe Ruth.

Babe Ruth plays for the New York Yankees. He is big and round, with a wide grin and a big appetite. He is the greatest hitter in the history of baseball. He hits the ball further and higher than anyone has ever done before. When he hits a home run, the ball flies right out of the stadium, and the crowd goes wild.

--

Babe Ruth grew up in Baltimore, in a home for boys who needed looking after. There, a kind teacher taught him to play baseball. He became a famous player very quickly. In 1920, the Yankees bought him from another team. And in 1923, they built a huge new stadium in the Bronx, called Yankee Stadium. People called it the House that Ruth Built, because so many people came to see him play.

{img:babe-ruth-style-big-baseball-player-swinging-bat-stadium-crowd-cheering}
Babe Ruth is loved by everyone, but especially by children. He visits hospitals and orphanages, and signs autographs for every child who asks. He jokes and laughs and eats enormous meals. In 1925, he was ill for a while at the beginning of the season, and missed many games. The whole city worried about him. But he came back. And the children of New York cheered louder than ever.

--

Tony has never seen Babe Ruth play. Tickets cost money, and Yankee Stadium is far away, in the Bronx. But one Saturday in August, Sal says he has a surprise. A passenger in his taxi, a kind old businessman, gave him two tickets for the Yankees game. Tony cannot believe it. He jumps up and down around the kitchen, shouting. Nonna tells him to be quiet. But she is smiling too.

{img:father-son-subway-to-bronx-baseball-caps-excited-crowd-fans-1925}
On Saturday, Sal and Tony take the subway to the Bronx. The train is full of baseball fans, wearing their caps and talking about the game. When they come out of the station, there is the stadium: huge and white, with flags flying from the top. They walk in with the crowd, and climb up to their seats, high in the stands. From up there, they can see the whole green field below them, bright in the sunshine.

--

The game is exciting. The Yankees are playing against Boston. Tony watches every pitch, every hit, every run. He eats a hot dog and drinks a bottle of soda. And then, in the seventh inning, Babe Ruth comes up to bat. The whole stadium goes quiet. The pitcher throws. Babe Ruth swings. There is a loud crack. And the ball flies up, up, up into the blue sky, over the fence, and into the crowd. A home run!

{img:boy-cheering-wildly-stands-home-run-crowd-standing-hats-flying-stadium}
The crowd jumps up and roars. Hats fly into the air. Tony jumps up and down and screams until his voice is hoarse. Sal laughs and hugs him. Babe Ruth trots slowly around the bases, waving his cap to the crowd. On the way home, Tony cannot stop talking about it. That night, he puts his Babe Ruth card under his pillow. And he dreams of baseballs, flying up into the sky, forever.

Baseball is called America's pastime. Almost every American boy, and many girls, play it. In New York, there are three big teams: the Yankees, the Giants and the Brooklyn Robins, later called the Dodgers. Each team has its own fans, who argue about which team is best. On summer afternoons, you can hear baseball games on radios from open windows all over the city.

Tony keeps the ticket from the game in a little box under his bed, with his Babe Ruth card. He takes it out sometimes and looks at it, remembering the crack of the bat and the ball flying into the sky. He tells everyone about it: his friends, his teacher, the customers on his corner. Sammy, his friend, is very jealous. But he admits that it must have been wonderful.
