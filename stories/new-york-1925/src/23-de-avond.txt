So we leave New York tonight, in the year 1925, as the evening falls.

The sun is setting behind the tall buildings of Manhattan. The sky turns orange, then pink, then a deep soft blue. One by one, the lights of the city come on. Windows glow yellow in the skyscrapers. The electric signs of Times Square blaze into life. And down in the harbour, the Statue of Liberty stands quietly against the darkening sky, with her torch held high.

--

In Little Italy, the street is busy with the evening. The pushcart sellers are packing up their carts. Mothers are calling their children in for dinner. Old men are sitting on the stoops, smoking their pipes and talking. Somewhere, someone is playing an accordion, an old song from Naples. And from the open windows, the smells of cooking drift out into the street: garlic and tomatoes, fresh bread and frying onions.

{img:tenement-kitchen-evening-family-dinner-lamp-radio-glowing-1925-cozy}
In the family's little kitchen on the fourth floor, the lamp is lit. Nonna has made her special soup, with beans and pasta. Sal has just come home, and he is telling a funny story about a passenger who forgot his hat. Rosa is laughing. Tony is setting the table, with his Babe Ruth card sticking out of his shirt pocket. And Lucy is turning the dial of the radio, looking for music.

--

After dinner, the radio plays softly. A dance band is playing a slow, sweet song. Lucy sings along, quietly. Sal puts his arm around Rosa, and they sway a little to the music, by the stove. Nonna sits in her chair by the window, with her rosary in her hands, looking out at the lights of the city. Tony is lying on the floor, half asleep, with his head on a cushion.

{img:grandmother-window-rosary-city-lights-night-girl-singing-radio-softly}
Later, Rosa sends the children to bed. Lucy lies in the bed she shares with Nonna, looking at the ceiling. Through the thin walls, she can hear the radio still playing in the kitchen, and her parents talking softly. She can hear the elevated train rattling past, and a car honking far away. She can hear a neighbour singing a lullaby to a baby. And she thinks about all the music in the world, and all the songs she will sing one day.

--

Nonna comes to bed, slowly, and says her prayers in Italian, very softly. She says a prayer for each of her family, one by one. Then she lies down beside Lucy, and pats her hand. Lucy is almost asleep. Outside, the great city hums and glows, all night long. The lights shine in the skyscrapers. The trains rattle through the tunnels. The ships sound their horns in the harbour.

And high above it all, above the towers and the bridges and the busy streets, the stars come out over New York. And the city that never sleeps settles down, at last, a little bit, for the night.
