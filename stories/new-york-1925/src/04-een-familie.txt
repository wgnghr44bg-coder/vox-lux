Let us meet the family who lives in Little Italy, on the Lower East Side of Manhattan.

The father is called Salvatore, but everyone calls him Sal. He is about forty years old, with dark curly hair and a big moustache. He was born in a village near Naples, in Italy. He came to America when he was a young man, with his mother. He worked many jobs: in a bakery, on the docks, in a factory. Now he drives a yellow taxi. He knows every street in New York. He is cheerful and talkative, and he loves his family more than anything.

The mother is called Rosa. She was born in New York, to Italian parents. She is about thirty-five, small and quick, with dark eyes and a warm smile. She works as a seamstress in a big dressmaking workshop, in the garment district. She sews dresses for the shops on Fifth Avenue. In the evenings, she cooks and cleans and looks after everyone. She is tired a lot of the time. But she never complains.

--

They have two children. The daughter is called Lucia, but at school she is called Lucy. She is twelve years old. She loves music more than anything. She listens to the radio whenever she can, and she knows all the new songs by heart. She sings while she does her homework, and while she helps her mother with the dishes. She dreams of being a singer on the radio one day.

{img:girl-twelve-1925-singing-by-radio-kitchen-apartment-bobbed-hair-happy}
The son is called Antonio, but everyone calls him Tony. He is nine years old, and he is never still. He sells newspapers on the street corner, before and after school. He knows everyone in the neighbourhood. He is crazy about baseball, and his great hero is Babe Ruth, the famous player of the New York Yankees. He has a baseball card of Babe Ruth, which he keeps in his pocket at all times.

--

The grandmother lives with them too. She is Sal's mother, and her name is Filomena. Everybody calls her Nonna. She is nearly seventy. She came to America with Sal, twenty years ago, through Ellis Island. She speaks only a few words of English. She spends her days cooking, praying and sitting by the window. She makes the best pasta in the whole street. She keeps a little shrine to the Virgin Mary on a shelf, with candles and paper flowers.

{img:italian-grandmother-1925-black-dress-making-pasta-kitchen-table-flour}
Nonna does not really understand the new world of radios and cars and flappers. She shakes her head at the short dresses of the young women. She does not trust the telephone. But she loves her family, and she loves New York in her own way. She likes to sit on the stoop of the building in the evening, the steps in front of the door, and talk with the other old Italian women of the street.

--

The family is not rich. They live in a small apartment with three rooms, on the fourth floor of an old building. Sal and Rosa both work long hours. Every cent is counted. But they have enough to eat, and a little to spare. Last year, they saved up and bought a radio. It stands in the corner of the kitchen, in a wooden cabinet, and it is the pride of the family.

{img:family-dinner-1925-small-kitchen-pasta-bread-wine-radio-corner-evening}
In the evenings, the family eats dinner together at the kitchen table. Nonna has made pasta with tomato sauce, and there is bread and a little cheese. Sal tells them about the funny passengers in his taxi today. Rosa tells them about the beautiful dresses she is sewing. Tony tells them the headlines from the newspapers. Lucy wants to turn on the radio. And Nonna says a prayer before they eat, in Italian, while everyone waits.

On Sundays, the family goes to mass at the old church of Saint Patrick, on Mulberry Street. Nonna wears her best black dress and her lace veil. The children are scrubbed clean and wear their best clothes. After mass, they walk along the street, greeting their neighbours. Then they go home for a big Sunday lunch, which Nonna has been cooking since early morning.

The family has a little canary, in a cage by the kitchen window. Its name is Caruso, after the famous Italian singer. It sings every morning, as soon as the sun comes up. Nonna talks to it while she cooks, in Italian. She says it is the only one in the family that really listens to her. Everyone laughs. But it is true that the canary sings back whenever she speaks.
