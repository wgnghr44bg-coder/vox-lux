Why are the 1920s called the Roaring Twenties? Because it is a time of noise, excitement and change.

In 1918, the Great War came to an end. It had been a long and terrible war. Afterwards, many people wanted to forget the hard years and enjoy life again. In America, the factories were busy, and there was work for many people. Many families had more money than ever before. They bought new things: cars, radios, refrigerators, vacuum cleaners and electric irons. Life was becoming more comfortable.

--

The car changed everything. A few years before, cars were only for the rich. But now, a car called the Ford Model T is cheap enough for many families to buy. It is black and simple and very strong. People call it the Tin Lizzie. By 1925, millions of Americans have a car. On Sundays, families go for drives in the countryside. The streets of New York are full of cars, honking their horns.

{img:ford-model-t-black-car-family-sunday-drive-countryside-road-1925}
Radio is new too. Only a few years ago, there were no radio stations at all. Now, millions of families have a radio in their living room. They listen to music and news, comedy shows and sports. For the first time, people all over the country can hear the same thing at the same time. It feels like magic. Families gather around the radio in the evenings, just as they will later gather around the television.

--

There are new things for young people too. Young women, called flappers, cut their hair in short bobs and wear short dresses that swing when they dance. They dance the Charleston, a fast and happy dance with kicking legs. Many women have jobs now, in offices and shops and factories. And since 1920, American women can vote in elections. It feels like a new world.

{img:young-women-1925-bobbed-hair-dancing-charleston-dance-hall-band}
There is one strange thing about the 1920s in America. Since 1920, it has been against the law to make or sell alcoholic drinks, like beer and wine and whisky. This is called Prohibition. Many people did not like this law, and did not obey it. In New York, there were thousands of secret bars, called speakeasies, hidden behind ordinary doors. It was a strange time. The law was finally changed in 1933.

--

Not everyone in the 1920s is rich, of course. Many families in New York are still poor, working long hours for low wages. Farmers in the countryside are having a hard time. But even poor families can feel the excitement of the time. They can listen to the radio, go to the cinema, and dream of a better life. And in New York, everyone seems to be dreaming.

{img:new-york-1925-crowd-street-corner-newsboy-hats-coats-energy-morning}
In 1925, a young writer called F. Scott Fitzgerald published a novel called The Great Gatsby. It is about rich people on Long Island, near New York, with their parties and cars and dreams. It is still one of the most famous American books. It shows the glitter of the Roaring Twenties, and also a little of its sadness. But the family in our story does not go to parties like that. Their New York is the city of ordinary people, working, hoping and making a life.

The 1920s are also a time of sport. Millions of people follow baseball, boxing, football and tennis. Sports stars become as famous as film stars. People listen to the games on the radio and read about them in the newspapers. Children collect cards with pictures of their heroes. And on Saturdays, the parks and fields are full of people playing ball games.

There are new kinds of shops too. Department stores sell everything under one roof, from clothes to furniture to toys. There are chain stores, with the same shops in many towns. And people begin to buy things on credit, paying a little bit every month. Advertising is everywhere: in newspapers and magazines, on the radio, and on huge signs in the streets. Everyone, it seems, wants something new.
