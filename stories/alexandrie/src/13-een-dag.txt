Let us spend an ordinary day with Merit and her family, in the summer.

The day begins at sunrise. Merit gets up and lights the clay oven in the courtyard. She mixes flour and water and a little old dough, and shapes flat round loaves. She bakes them in the oven, and soon the smell of fresh bread fills the house. Grandmother Tiye sits beside her, grinding grain on a flat stone. The two cats wait hopefully nearby, in case anything falls.

--

Breakfast is warm bread, with a little honey or cheese, and some dates. Demetrios eats quickly and goes off to the library. Theon goes off to school, a little school run by a Greek teacher in the next street. Kallisto has finished school now, so she helps her mother. Together, they take the papyrus to the stall in the market, in a basket on Merit's head.

{img:market-alexandria-stalls-fish-fruit-papyrus-mother-daughter-morning-busy}
The market near the harbour is busy and noisy. There are stalls selling fish and fruit, vegetables and bread. There are stalls selling cloth and pots, spices and perfumes. There are sellers of little lucky charms and statues of the gods. There are storytellers and musicians, jugglers and snake charmers. People shout and bargain in many languages. Merit sets up her stall and starts to sell.

--

Kallisto helps her mother all morning. She counts the sheets of papyrus and takes the coins. She talks to the customers in Greek and in Egyptian. One of them is an old scholar from the library, who buys papyrus every week. He has a long white beard and a kind face. He asks Kallisto what she is reading these days. She tells him about Homer, and he smiles and nods. He tells her to keep reading, always.

{img:old-scholar-buying-papyrus-talking-with-girl-stall-kind-smile}
At midday, it is very hot. Merit closes the stall, and they go home. The family eats lunch in the shade of the courtyard: bread, lentils, onions and cucumbers. Then everyone rests during the hottest part of the day. Grandmother Tiye sleeps on her bed. Kallisto reads in the shade of the fig tree. The cats sleep in the coolest corner. The whole city is quiet in the afternoon heat.

--

In the late afternoon, when it is cooler, Kallisto and Theon go down to the harbour. Theon wants to see the ships, of course. Kallisto wants to sit on the harbour wall and read. They watch the big grain ships being loaded with wheat, and the fishing boats coming in. They watch the sun go down behind the lighthouse. The water turns gold and red. The sea breeze is cool and fresh.

{img:brother-sister-harbour-wall-sunset-pharos-silhouette-ships-golden}
As the sun sets, they see the keepers lighting the great fire at the top of the Pharos. First a small light, then bigger and brighter, until it shines out over the darkening sea. Theon and Kallisto watch it for a while. Then they walk home through the evening streets. The lamps are being lit in the houses. The smell of cooking drifts from the windows. And at home, their mother is waiting with supper, under the fig tree.

In the market, there are also little stalls selling food that you can eat as you walk. There are grilled fish on sticks and little meat pies. There are sweet cakes made with honey and sesame seeds. There are fresh dates and figs and slices of melon. Kallisto and Theon always beg their mother for a honey cake, and sometimes she says yes.

Before bed, Grandmother Tiye tells the children a story. Tonight, it is the story of the cat goddess Bastet, who protects homes and families. She tells them that cats are sacred because Bastet loves them. And that is why, she says, you must always be kind to Honey and Smoke. The cats, curled up on the end of the bed, seem to agree. And the children fall asleep, listening to the cats purring.
