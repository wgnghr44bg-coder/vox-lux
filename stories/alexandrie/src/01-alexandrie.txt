Tonight we travel more than two thousand two hundred years back in time. We go to the north coast of Egypt, where the land meets the Mediterranean Sea. The year is around 240 before Christ. And here, beside the sea, there is a great white city. It is called Alexandria.

--

It is early morning, and the sea is calm and blue. The sky is turning pale gold in the east. On a little island at the edge of the harbour stands an enormous white tower. It is taller than anything else in the world, except the great pyramids. At its top, a fire is still burning from the night, a small orange glow against the brightening sky. This is the lighthouse of Alexandria, called the Pharos. It is one of the wonders of the world.

In the harbour, ships are rocking gently at anchor. Fishing boats are coming in with the night's catch. Sailors are calling to each other in Greek and Egyptian, Hebrew and Syrian. On the shore, the city is waking up. Its white buildings shine in the first light of the sun. And the smell of fresh bread and salt and the sea drifts over the water.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

{img:alexandria-canopic-way-wide-street-colonnades-crowds-morning-greek-egyptian}
Alexandria is one of the greatest cities of the ancient world. It is young, only about ninety years old. But it is already rich and beautiful and famous. Perhaps half a million people live here. They come from everywhere: Greeks and Egyptians, Jews and Syrians, Persians and Africans, and many more. They trade and work and study side by side.

And Alexandria has something that no other city in the world has. It has a great library. The kings of Alexandria want to collect every book in the world, all in one place. Scholars come here from everywhere, to read and to study, to think and to write. Here, people measure the size of the Earth and map the stars. Here, they study medicine and mathematics, poetry and history.

{img:great-library-alexandria-scrolls-shelves-scholars-reading-columns-light}
Tonight we will spend some time in this city of books and the sea. We will meet a family who lives near the harbour. The father is a copyist at the great library, who copies old books by hand. The mother is Egyptian, and she sells papyrus in the market. Their daughter loves to read, and their son loves the ships. And their grandmother tells stories of the old gods of Egypt.

--

We will visit the great library and the halls where the scholars work. We will learn how papyrus is made from the reeds of the Nile. We will climb, in our imagination, to the top of the great lighthouse. We will meet a clever scholar who measured the whole Earth with a stick and a shadow. And on a stormy night, we will see how the light of the Pharos guides the ships safely home.

{img:girl-sitting-harbour-wall-reading-scroll-pharos-behind-morning-light}
So let us begin beside the harbour of Alexandria, on a calm summer morning. The great lighthouse stands white against the blue sky. A girl of twelve is sitting on the harbour wall, with a little scroll in her lap. She is reading, with her lips moving softly. Beside her, a younger boy is watching the ships come in. And the morning sun shines warm on the white city by the sea.

Along the shore, the fishermen are pulling their small boats up onto the sand. They carry baskets of silver fish up to the market. A woman is spreading out wet nets to dry on the warm stones. Little children are playing at the edge of the water, chasing the waves. And an old man sits on an upturned boat, mending a net with a wooden needle, humming an old Egyptian song.

Alexandria is also a city of gardens. Behind the white walls of the houses, there are courtyards with fig trees and palms, roses and jasmine. In the royal quarter, there are great parks with fountains and shady walks. In the evenings, when the heat of the day is over, the scent of the flowers drifts through the streets. People sit in their gardens and talk until late.
