The harbour of Alexandria is one of the busiest in the whole world. Ships come and go every day, from every port of the Mediterranean Sea.

The most important ships are the grain ships. They are big and wide, with one tall mast and a great square sail. They carry wheat from Egypt to other countries: to Greece, to Rhodes, to the islands and cities all around the sea. Egypt grows more wheat than any other land. The grain ships carry it away, and bring back gold and silver, wine and oil, wood and metals.

--

On the quays, there are always crowds of workers. Porters carry heavy sacks of grain on their backs, up the gangplanks onto the ships. Others roll barrels and carry big clay jars, called amphorae, full of wine and oil. Scribes sit at little tables, writing down every sack and every jar. Officials of the king check everything and collect the taxes. It is noisy and dusty and busy, from dawn until dusk.

{img:amphorae-stacked-quay-wine-oil-merchants-scribes-tables-harbour}
Many other goods come into Alexandria too. There is wood from Lebanon, for building ships and houses, because Egypt has very few trees. There is silver and tin from Spain. There is marble from the Greek islands. There are horses from Libya. And from the south and the east, by the Red Sea, come incense and spices, ivory and ebony, gold and precious stones from Africa and India.

--

Alexandria is also a city of shipbuilders. In the shipyards near the harbour, carpenters build big ships and small boats. The kings of Alexandria build enormous warships, bigger than any others. A few years after our story, a king called Ptolemy the Fourth will build one with forty rows of oars, and thousands of rowers. It will be so big that it can hardly move. It will be more for show than for use. But people will come from everywhere to look at it.

{img:shipyard-alexandria-wooden-hull-ribs-carpenters-working-timber-cedar}
Theon spends as much time at the harbour as he can. He knows many of the sailors and the porters. He runs errands for them, and they give him little coins or pieces of fruit. He watches the ships coming in and going out. He can tell where a ship comes from by the shape of its sails and the colour of its hull. He dreams of the day when he will sail on one of them.

--

Uncle Nebamun's ship is called the Isis. It is a big grain ship, with a painted eye on each side of the front, to help it see its way across the sea. Its sail is decorated with a picture of the goddess Isis, the protector of sailors. When the Isis is in the harbour, Theon goes on board to see his uncle. The sailors let him climb a little way up the mast. They teach him to tie knots and to coil ropes.

{img:grain-ship-isis-painted-eye-bow-sailors-boy-climbing-mast-harbour}
Before each voyage, the sailors of the Isis make an offering to the goddess. They pour a little wine into the sea, and burn some incense on a little altar on the ship. They pray for good winds and calm seas. Then they raise the anchor and the great sail. And the ship sails slowly out of the harbour, past the white lighthouse, into the open sea. Theon waves from the harbour wall until the ship is only a little speck on the horizon.

In the evenings, the sailors gather in the taverns near the harbour. They eat fish stew and bread, and drink wine. They tell stories of their voyages, of storms and pirates, faraway ports and strange customs. They sing songs in many languages. Theon is not allowed in the taverns, because he is too young. But sometimes he sits outside the door and listens to the stories.
