Alexandria was founded by one of the most famous men in history. His name was Alexander, and people called him Alexander the Great.

Alexander was a young king from Macedonia, in the north of Greece. When he was only twenty years old, he became king. Then he set off with his army to the east. In just a few years, he travelled further than anyone could have imagined. He went through Turkey and Syria, Egypt and Persia, all the way to the edge of India. He created one of the biggest empires the world had ever seen.

--

In the year 331 before Christ, Alexander came to Egypt. He was only twenty-five years old. The Egyptians welcomed him. They were tired of being ruled by the Persians. They made Alexander their pharaoh, their king. And Alexander decided to build a new city in Egypt, on the coast of the Mediterranean Sea.

{img:alexander-marking-city-plan-ground-barley-flour-birds-eating-legend}
He chose a place on a narrow strip of land, between the sea and a big lake. There was a little Egyptian fishing village there, and an island just off the coast. Alexander walked over the ground and showed his builders where the streets and the walls should go. There is a famous old story about this. The builders had no chalk to mark the lines on the ground. So they used barley flour instead. Then flocks of birds came down and ate the flour. Some people thought it was a bad sign. But the wise men said it meant that the city would feed many people from all over the world.

--

Alexander never saw his city finished. He left Egypt soon afterwards, and continued his journey east. He passed away in Babylon, eight years later, when he was only thirty-two. But his city grew and grew. And when he was gone, his body was brought back to Alexandria. He was laid to rest in a beautiful tomb in the middle of the city. People came from everywhere to visit it.

{img:tomb-of-alexander-alexandria-temple-like-building-visitors-golden-light}
The city was planned very carefully, by an architect called Deinocrates. The streets were laid out in a grid, straight and at right angles, like a chessboard. The main streets were very wide, wider than in any other city at that time. The city was built so that the cool north wind from the sea could blow through the streets in summer. It was a modern city, for its time.

--

Alexander's empire did not last long after him. His generals divided it among themselves. One of them was a general called Ptolemy. He had been Alexander's friend since childhood. He took Egypt as his share. He made himself king, and Alexandria became his capital. His family, the Ptolemies, will rule Egypt for almost three hundred years. The last of them will be a famous queen called Cleopatra.

{img:ptolemy-i-king-older-general-crown-palace-alexandria-founding-dynasty}
In our story, around 240 before Christ, the king of Egypt is Ptolemy the Third. He is the grandson of the first Ptolemy. He is a strong king, and a lover of books, like his father and his grandfather. He wants Alexandria to be the most learned city in the world. And he is willing to pay almost anything to get the books he wants.

Before he left Egypt, Alexander made a long journey into the desert, to a famous oracle. It was at an oasis called Siwa, far to the west. There was a temple there of the god Amun. People came from far away to ask the god questions. The old stories say that the priests greeted Alexander as the son of the god. Nobody knows what he asked, or what he was told. He never said.

The new city grew very quickly. People came from all over the Greek world to live there. They came because there was work, and land, and a new beginning. Egyptians came from the villages along the Nile. Jewish families came from Judea, and traders came from Syria and beyond. Within a few years, the little fishing village had become a busy town. And within a few more, it was a great city.
