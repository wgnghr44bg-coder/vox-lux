The family's house is in a quiet street, a few minutes' walk from the harbour. It is a simple house of whitewashed mud brick, with a flat roof.

From the street, you see only a plain white wall with a wooden door. There are no windows on the street side, only a few small openings high up for air. That keeps the house cool in the hot summer, and private. When you open the door, you step into a little entrance, and then into the courtyard. The courtyard is the heart of the house.

--

In the middle of the courtyard, there is a fig tree, which gives shade in summer and sweet figs in autumn. There is a stone basin with water, where the cats like to drink. There are pots of herbs and flowers: mint and basil, roses and lilies. Around the courtyard, there are the rooms of the house. There is a kitchen, a storeroom, a room for eating and talking, and bedrooms.

{img:simple-kitchen-ancient-alexandria-clay-oven-pots-jars-onions-hanging}
The kitchen has a clay oven for baking bread, and a little hearth for cooking. There are clay pots and jars on shelves, full of oil and wine, lentils and flour, dates and salted fish. Onions and garlic hang from the ceiling. There is a big stone for grinding grain. Merit and Grandmother Tiye cook here every day. The smell of fresh bread fills the house every morning.

--

The rooms have floors of beaten earth or tiles. There is very little furniture. There are a few wooden stools and a low table, some chests for clothes, and beds made of wood and woven cord. The beds have thin mattresses of linen stuffed with wool. In hot weather, the family often sleeps on the flat roof, under the stars, where it is cooler.

{img:flat-roof-alexandria-night-family-sleeping-mats-stars-pharos-light-distance}
The roof is one of the best places in the house. You climb up to it on a wooden ladder from the courtyard. From the roof, you can see over the other houses to the harbour. You can see the masts of the ships and the great white lighthouse. At night, you can see the fire burning at the top of the Pharos, like a star that has come down to Earth. Theon loves to lie on the roof and watch it, until he falls asleep.

--

In one corner of the main room, there is a little shrine. It is a small wooden box, open at the front, painted with flowers. Inside, there are little statues of the household gods. There is Bes, the funny dwarf god who protects the family and frightens away bad dreams. There is Isis, the mother goddess, holding her baby son Horus. And there is a little bronze statue of Hermes, the Greek god of travellers and messengers, for Demetrios. The family lights a little lamp in front of them every evening.

{img:household-shrine-little-statues-bes-isis-hermes-oil-lamp-flowers}
Demetrios also has a little shelf of his own scrolls. They are his greatest treasure. There are poems by Homer, which he copied himself as a young man. There is a book of fables, and a book about the stars. He keeps them carefully in a wooden box, to protect them from the damp and the mice. In the evenings, he takes one out and reads aloud to the family. And Kallisto sits beside him, following the words with her finger.

In summer, the family spends most of its time in the courtyard. It is the coolest place in the house, in the shade of the fig tree. Merit grinds grain and cooks there. Grandmother Tiye spins linen thread there. Demetrios reads there in the evenings. The children play there. Even the cats prefer the courtyard, lying in the cool shadow of the stone basin.

Once a month, Merit sweeps and washes the whole house, from the roof to the courtyard. She shakes out the mats and the linen. She sweeps away the sand that blows in from the street. She washes the clay pots and the floor tiles. Kallisto and Theon help her. They complain a little, but when it is finished, the house smells fresh and clean, and everyone feels good.
