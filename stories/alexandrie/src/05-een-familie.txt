Let us meet the family who lives in a house near the harbour, between the Greek quarter and the Egyptian quarter.

The father is called Demetrios. He is Greek, about forty years old. His grandfather came to Alexandria from an island in the Aegean Sea, when the city was young. Demetrios is a copyist at the great library. His job is to copy books by hand, carefully, word by word. He has beautiful handwriting and very good eyes. He is quiet and gentle, and he loves books more than anything, except his family.

The mother is called Merit. She is Egyptian. Her family has lived in the land of Egypt for as long as anyone can remember. She sells papyrus in the market. Papyrus is the paper of the ancient world, made from the reeds of the Nile. Her brother makes it, in a village on the edge of the lake. Merit is lively and practical, with dark eyes and a quick laugh. She speaks both Greek and Egyptian.

--

They have two children. The daughter is called Kallisto, and she is twelve years old. Her name is Greek, and it means the most beautiful. She has her mother's dark eyes and her father's love of books. She can read and write in Greek, and a little in Egyptian too. She reads everything she can find. She dreams of becoming a scholar at the library, though there are very few women scholars.

{img:girl-twelve-reading-scroll-courtyard-fig-tree-sunlight-alexandria}
The son is called Theon, and he is nine. His name is Greek too. He is cheerful and energetic, and he loves ships more than anything. He spends every free moment at the harbour, watching the ships come and go. He knows the names of many of the ships and their captains. He wants to be a sailor when he grows up, like his uncle Nebamun.

--

The grandmother lives with them. She is Merit's mother, and her name is Tiye. She is about seventy, small and wrinkled, with bright black eyes. She grew up in a village on the Nile, far to the south. She came to Alexandria when she married, long ago. She speaks only a little Greek. She tells the children stories of the old gods of Egypt, of Isis and Osiris, of Horus the falcon and Thoth the wise ibis. She knows all the old prayers and spells for good luck.

{img:old-egyptian-grandmother-linen-dress-telling-stories-children-lamp-evening}
Merit's brother, Uncle Nebamun, is a sailor on a big grain ship. He sails from Alexandria to Rhodes and Athens, carrying wheat from Egypt. He is away for weeks at a time. When he comes home, he brings presents and stories from faraway places. Theon adores him. He wants to sail with him one day. Nebamun laughs and says: when you are older, little one.

--

The family is not rich, but they live comfortably. Demetrios earns a good wage at the library. Merit earns a little from selling papyrus. They have a small house with a courtyard, and enough to eat. They have two cats, as many Egyptian families do. Cats are sacred in Egypt, and they are good at keeping mice away from the papyrus. One cat is grey and called Smoke. The other is golden and called Honey.

{img:family-courtyard-evening-supper-greek-egyptian-two-cats-lamp-fig-tree}
In the evenings, the family eats together in the courtyard, under a fig tree. There is bread and lentils, onions and cheese, fish from the harbour, and dates and figs. They speak both Greek and Egyptian at the table, mixing the two languages together. Demetrios tells them about the books he is copying. Merit tells them about the market. Theon tells them about the ships. Kallisto asks questions. And Grandmother Tiye listens, and smiles, and feeds little scraps to the cats under the table.

On their days off, the family likes to walk along the shore together. They walk out along the long causeway toward the island of Pharos. They watch the ships and the fishermen. They buy roasted nuts from a seller on the causeway. Demetrios tells the children the names of the ships and where they come from. And Grandmother Tiye walks slowly behind, holding Merit's arm, smiling at everything.

Demetrios and Merit met at the market, many years ago, when Demetrios came to buy papyrus. He came back the next day, and the day after that, to buy more papyrus than he needed. Merit soon understood why. They married a year later. Their wedding was half Greek and half Egyptian, with music and dancing from both. Grandmother Tiye still says it was the best wedding she ever saw.
