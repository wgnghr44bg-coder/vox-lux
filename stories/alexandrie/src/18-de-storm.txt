In the autumn, the sailing season is coming to an end. Soon the winter storms will come, and the ships will stay in the harbour until spring. Uncle Nebamun's ship, the Isis, is on its last voyage of the year. It has gone to Rhodes with a cargo of wheat. It should be home any day now.

The family waits for it. Every day, Theon goes to the harbour and watches the sea. Every evening, he comes home and shakes his head. Not yet. Merit says a prayer to Isis every morning and evening, at the little shrine. Grandmother Tiye says that Nebamun has always come home safely, and he will this time too.

--

Then one afternoon, the weather changes. Dark clouds pile up in the north, over the sea. The wind rises, and turns cold. The sea turns from blue to grey. Great waves begin to roll in toward the coast, crashing against the rocks and the harbour walls. The fishing boats hurry back into the harbour. The sailors tie up their ships tightly, with extra ropes. A storm is coming.

{img:waves-crashing-harbour-wall-storm-spray-dark-clouds-pharos-standing}
Theon runs home from the harbour, wet with spray. He says there is no sign of the Isis yet. The family looks at each other. Nobody says anything. Merit lights an extra lamp at the shrine. Grandmother Tiye sits down beside it and begins to pray softly in Egyptian. Demetrios puts his arm around Kallisto.

--

As night falls, the storm grows stronger. The wind howls through the streets of the city. Rain beats against the walls and the shutters, which is very rare in Alexandria. The sea roars against the rocks. The family climbs up to the roof, wrapped in cloaks, to look out at the sea. It is very dark. But out at the edge of the harbour, the great fire of the Pharos is burning, bright and steady, in the wind and the rain.

{img:pharos-fire-blazing-storm-night-rain-ship-lights-approaching-waves}
The keepers of the lighthouse are working hard tonight. They feed the fire with wood and oil, again and again. They keep it burning as brightly as they can. Its light shines out across the stormy sea, far into the darkness. Any ship out there, struggling in the storm, will see it. And it will know where the harbour is, and where the dangerous rocks are.

--

The family stays on the roof for a long time, watching the light. Then, late in the night, Theon shouts and points. Far out in the darkness, there is a tiny light, moving slowly toward the harbour. It is a ship's lantern. A ship is coming in, guided by the Pharos. Slowly, very slowly, it comes closer. It passes the lighthouse, and enters the harbour. And in the light of the great fire, they can see its sail. It has a picture of the goddess Isis on it.

{img:family-running-harbour-night-storm-easing-ship-isis-tied-up-sailors-lanterns}
The whole family runs down to the harbour, through the wet streets. The storm is easing a little now. The ship is tied up at the quay, battered but safe. The sailors are coming ashore, tired and soaked. And there is Uncle Nebamun, with water dripping from his beard, smiling widely. Merit runs to him and hugs him. Theon hugs his legs. Grandmother Tiye cries and laughs at the same time. Nebamun looks up at the great white tower, with its fire still burning. Without the Pharos, he says quietly, we would never have found our way. And he bows his head toward the light.

The next day, the sun comes out again, and the sea is calm. The family has a feast to welcome Nebamun home. Merit cooks a big fish stew, with onions and herbs. Grandmother Tiye makes honey cakes. Nebamun tells them all about the storm, and about Rhodes and its great harbour. He has brought presents: a little bronze bracelet for Kallisto, and a toy ship carved from wood for Theon.
