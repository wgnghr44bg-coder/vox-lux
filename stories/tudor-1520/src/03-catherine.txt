The queen of England, in 1520, is Catherine of Aragon.

She is thirty-four years old, six years older than Henry. She is the youngest daughter of the most famous royal couple in Europe: Ferdinand and Isabella, the king and queen of Spain, who, twenty-eight years before, sent Christopher Columbus across the Atlantic, and found the New World.

--

She came to England as a girl of fifteen, in 1501, to marry Henry's elder brother, Arthur. But Arthur passed away, five months after the wedding. And Catherine was left in England, a young widow, poor, and lonely, and uncertain, for seven years, while the two kings, her father, and Henry's father, argued about her dowry.

Then, in 1509, when Henry became king, at seventeen, one of the first things he did was to marry her. He said that he loved her. They were crowned together, in Westminster Abbey, in a great ceremony, with the people cheering in the streets.

{img:young-royal-couple-coronation-westminster-abbey-1509-crowds}
Catherine is small, and pretty, with long red-gold hair, and blue eyes, and fair skin. She is intelligent, and well educated, in Latin, and theology, and music, and dancing, and needlework. She is very religious, and kind, and dignified, and brave. Once, when Henry was away at war, in France, in 1513, she ruled England as regent, and, when the Scots invaded, she organized the army, and even rode north, in armour, it is said, to speak to the troops. And the English won a great victory, at Flodden.

--

The English people love her. She gives generously to the poor, and visits the sick, and goes on pilgrimages, on foot.

But her life has been full of sorrow. She has had six pregnancies, in eleven years. But most of her babies were lost, before or soon after birth. A son, Henry, was born in 1511, and the whole country celebrated, with bonfires, and bells, and a great tournament. But he lived only fifty-two days.

{img:queen-catherine-with-little-princess-mary-garden-greenwich-spring}
Only one child has lived: a daughter, Mary, born in 1516, who is four in 1520: a pretty, clever little girl, with red-gold hair, who plays the virginals, already, and dances, and whom her father adores, and carries around in his arms, and shows off to the ambassadors.

--

In 1520, Henry and Catherine are still a loving couple, in public. He wears her colours, at the tournaments, and they dance together, and hunt together, and pray together. But Henry wants a son, an heir, to secure the Tudor dynasty. And Catherine is thirty-four, and the doctors are beginning to doubt that she will have any more children.

The Princess Mary will one day be queen herself, as Mary the First. And her half sister, Elizabeth, not yet born, will be the greatest queen in English history. But in 1520, nobody can imagine that.
