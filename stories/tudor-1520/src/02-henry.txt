In 1520, King Henry the Eighth is twenty-eight years old. He has been king for eleven years.

He is tall, about one metre eighty-eight. That is very tall for his time. He is strong and broad and handsome. He has red-gold hair, a short beard and small bright eyes. A visitor from Venice wrote that he was the most handsome prince he had ever seen.

--

He was never meant to be king. He was the second son of King Henry the Seventh. His father won the crown in a battle in 1485, at the end of a long civil war. Henry's older brother Arthur was supposed to be king. But Arthur passed away at fifteen. So Henry, at eleven, was suddenly next in line. When his father passed away, Henry became king at seventeen. The whole country was happy.

The young king was everything his father was not. His father had been gloomy and careful. Henry was cheerful and generous and full of energy.

{img:young-king-hunting-horseback-hounds-forest-greenwich-park}
He loves sport. He can hunt all day and wear out eight or ten horses. He loves jousting, where two riders charge at each other with long poles. He plays tennis and wrestles and dances. He is one of the best archers in England.

--

But he is also clever and well taught. He speaks French and Latin, and a bit of Spanish and Italian. He reads books about God and even writes them. The year after our story, he will write a book to defend the Church. The Pope will thank him with a title. Defender of the Faith. British coins still carry the letters F D for it today.

Most of all, he loves music. He plays the lute, which is like a round guitar. He plays the organ and the flute, and he sings well. He even writes his own songs. The best known is called Pastime with Good Company. It is about the joys of being young, with friends and hunting and dancing.

{img:king-playing-lute-chamber-musicians-courtiers-listening-evening}
He owns more than seventy flutes and dozens of lutes. And he hires the best musicians in Europe to play at his court.

--

In 1520 Henry is still young and golden and loved. He has been married for eleven years to Catherine of Aragon. He has a daughter, Mary, who is four. But he does not yet have a son. A king without a son worries about the future. Who will rule after him? Will the old wars come back? That worry is small for now. In the years to come, it will grow and grow. And it will change everything.

But not yet.
