Of all his palaces, King Henry likes Greenwich best.

It stands on the south bank of the Thames, a little way down the river from London. It has a long front of red brick and stone, facing the water. There are towers and big windows, and a landing place for the royal boats. Behind it lies a large park that climbs up a hill. There are deer and woods. And on top of the hill stands a small tower with a wonderful view of the river and of London far away.

--

Henry was born at Greenwich. He married Catherine there. His daughter Mary was born there too. He loves it. It is close to London, but it feels like the country. There is the river, the park, the hunting and the fresh air.

At Greenwich, Henry has built a long field for jousting, with towers for people to watch from. He has his own armour workshop, with armour makers brought from Germany. There are tennis courts and bowling lanes. There are stables, and dogs for hunting, and a house for the hawks.

{img:greenwich-tiltyard-towers-spectators-jousting-sunny-afternoon}
From the palace windows, the king can watch the ships go up and down the Thames. Trading ships come from all over Europe. And there are ships of his own navy. He loves his ships. He often visits the new shipyards nearby, to see them being built.

--

The palace is gone now. It was pulled down about a hundred and fifty years later. In its place stand big white buildings with domes, by the river.

But the park is still there, with its old trees and its deer and its hill. There is an old dead oak tree, held up by props. People call it Queen Elizabeth's Oak. The story says that Henry once danced under it. And that his daughter Elizabeth played there as a child.

--

{img:greenwich-park-hill-view-river-thames-deer-old-trees-evening}
On summer evenings, the king and queen sometimes walk up the hill with their friends and the little princess. They sit under the great trees. Below them lie the palace and the shining river. Boats are going home to London. Far away, the spires of the city rise out of the haze. And in the woods, the deer are calling as the sun goes down.
