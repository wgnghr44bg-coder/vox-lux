Of all his palaces, King Henry's favourite is Greenwich.

It stands on the south bank of the Thames, about eight kilometres downstream from London, where the river makes a great loop, with a long front of red brick, and stone, facing the water, with towers, and bay windows, and a landing stage, where the royal barges come and go. Behind it, there is a great park, rising up the hill, with deer, and woods, and a little castle, a tower, on the top of the hill, with a wonderful view, over the river, and London, in the distance.

--

Henry was born at Greenwich, in 1491. He married Catherine there, in 1509. His daughter Mary was born there, in 1516, and later, his daughter Elizabeth, in 1533. He loves it. It is close to London, but in the country, with the river, and the park, and the hunting, and the fresh air.

At Greenwich, Henry has built a tiltyard, for jousting, with towers, for the spectators, and an armoury, where his own armourers, brought from Germany, make his armour. There are tennis courts, and bowling alleys, and a cockpit, and stables, and kennels, and mews, for the hawks.

{img:greenwich-tiltyard-towers-spectators-jousting-sunny-afternoon}
And from the windows of the palace, the king can watch the ships, sailing up and down the Thames, to and from London, and the sea: merchant ships, from all over Europe, and the ships of his own navy, which he loves, and visits, and inspects, at the new royal dockyards, nearby, at Deptford, and Woolwich.

--

The palace is gone now. It was pulled down in the seventeenth century, and replaced by the great buildings of the Royal Hospital for Seamen, designed by Christopher Wren, which are now the Old Royal Naval College, with its domes, and colonnades, by the river.

But the park is still there, with its old trees, and its deer, and the hill, with its view. And in the park, there is an old oak tree, now dead, and propped up, called Queen Elizabeth's Oak, under which, the story says, Henry and Anne Boleyn danced, and their daughter, Elizabeth, played as a child.

--

{img:greenwich-park-hill-view-river-thames-deer-old-trees-evening}
In the evenings, at Greenwich, in summer, the king and queen would sometimes walk up the hill in the park, with their friends, and the little princess, and sit under the great trees, and look down at the palace, and the river, shining in the evening light, with the boats going home to London, and the towers, and the spires, of the city, far away, in the haze, and listen to the deer calling, in the woods, as the sun went down.
