Today you can still visit many places of Tudor England. You can walk where Henry and Catherine and Wolsey and More walked, five hundred years ago.

Hampton Court still stands by the Thames. It still has its red brick and its great gates and its twisted chimneys. It has the Great Hall with its beautiful carved wooden roof. And it has the huge Tudor kitchens. On some days, cooks in Tudor clothes still roast meat there on big spits, in front of the giant fireplaces.

--

In the palace hangs the big painting of the Field of the Cloth of Gold. It is full of tiny people and golden tents and the wine fountain, and the dragon in the sky. There is also the old tennis court, still used today. It is one of the oldest tennis courts in the world.

There are the gardens, with their patterns of low hedges. And there is a famous maze of tall hedges, the oldest in Britain. Families still get happily lost in it on summer afternoons. And there is a great grape vine, more than two hundred and fifty years old, that still grows grapes.

In London there is the Tower, with Henry's tall armour in the White Tower. There are the crown jewels and the ravens. And there are the Beefeaters in their red and gold uniforms, as they have worn for five hundred years.

--

At Greenwich, Henry's palace is gone. But there are beautiful old buildings by the river. And the park where he hunted still climbs up the hill, with a wonderful view of London.

In Portsmouth you can see the Mary Rose in her museum. In Westminster Abbey you can see the graves of Henry's parents, in a chapel with a stone ceiling like lace. And the graves of his daughters Mary and Elizabeth. In the cathedral of Peterborough is the grave of Catherine of Aragon. Every year people leave pomegranates there, her symbol, and flowers. A banner hangs above with her name. Katharine, Queen of England.

{img:catherine-of-aragon-grave-peterborough-pomegranates-flowers-banner}
And all over England there are still Tudor houses and inns. They have black wooden beams and white walls, crooked roofs and tall chimneys. People still live in them today.

--

So we leave Tudor England tonight, on a summer evening in 1520. The golden tents are shining in the valley in France. The young king is laughing with his friends. And lute music drifts across the water at Greenwich.

The apprentice in Cheapside is closing his shutters. The villagers are dancing round the maypole. The cardinal is in his red brick palace. And the little princess is asleep, with all of her future still before her.
