Today, you can still visit many of the places of Tudor England, and walk where Henry, and Catherine, and Wolsey, and More, walked, five hundred years ago.

Hampton Court Palace still stands, by the river Thames, with its red brick, and its great gatehouses, and its twisted chimneys, and its courtyards, and its Great Hall, with its magnificent carved wooden roof, built by Henry, and its tapestries, and its huge Tudor kitchens, where, on some days, cooks in Tudor costume still roast meat, on great spits, in front of the enormous fireplaces, and bake pies, and make pottage, the Tudor way.

--

In the palace, there is the great painting of the Field of the Cloth of Gold, full of tiny figures, and golden tents, and the wine fountain, and the dragon in the sky. And there is the real tennis court, built on the site of the court where Henry played, and still used today, one of the oldest tennis courts in the world.

There are the gardens, with their knot gardens, and their famous maze, of tall hedges, planted about a hundred and seventy years after Henry, the oldest hedge maze in Britain, where families still get lost, happily, on summer afternoons. And the great vine, planted in 1768, still producing grapes, the largest, and one of the oldest, grape vines in the world.

In London, there is the Tower of London, with Henry's armour, so tall, and strong, in the White Tower, and the crown jewels, and the ravens, and the Yeomen Warders, the Beefeaters, in their Tudor uniforms, red and gold, and dark blue, as they have worn for five hundred years.

--

At Greenwich, where Henry was born, and loved to live, his palace is gone, but on the site, there are the beautiful buildings of the Old Royal Naval College, by the river, and the park, where he hunted, rising up the hill, to the Royal Observatory, and the line of zero longitude, with a wonderful view over the river, and London.

In Portsmouth, there is the Mary Rose, in her museum, with all the belongings of her crew. And in Westminster Abbey, there are the tombs of Henry's parents, Henry the Seventh, and Elizabeth of York, in the beautiful chapel that Henry the Seventh built, with its stone ceiling like lace. And the tombs of Mary, and Elizabeth. And in Peterborough Cathedral, there is the grave of Catherine of Aragon, where, every year, people leave pomegranates, her symbol, and flowers, and there is a banner, with her name, and the words: Katharine, Queen of England.

{img:catherine-of-aragon-grave-peterborough-pomegranates-flowers-banner}
And all over England, there are Tudor houses, and inns, and villages, with their black timber, and white plaster, and their crooked roofs, and their tall chimneys, and their little diamond-paned windows, still lived in, today.

--

So we leave Tudor England tonight, on a summer evening in 1520, with the golden tents shining in the valley in France, and the young king laughing among his friends, and the music of the lutes drifting across the water at Greenwich.

The apprentice in Cheapside closing his shutters, the villagers dancing round the maypole, the cardinal in his red brick palace, and the little princess asleep, with all of the future still before her.
