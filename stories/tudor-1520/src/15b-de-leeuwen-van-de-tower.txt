At the east end of London, by the river, stands the Tower of London.

It is an old royal castle. It was built by William the Conqueror more than four hundred and fifty years before. In the middle stands a big square tower of white stone, called the White Tower. Walls and towers and a moat surround it. In 1520, the Tower is many things at once. It is a royal palace and a store for weapons. It keeps the king's treasure. It is where England's coins are made. It is a prison for important prisoners. And it is also a zoo.

--

For nearly three hundred years, the kings of England have kept wild animals at the Tower. They live in a building near the main gate called the Lion Tower. There are lions and leopards and other animals. They were gifts to the kings from foreign rulers.

Once there was a polar bear. It was allowed to swim and fish in the Thames on a long chain. Once there was an elephant, a gift from the king of France. It had its own house. And there were many lions. People believed that when a king passed away, his lion would die too.

{img:lion-tower-menagerie-lions-cages-visitors-looking-tudor}
Londoners can visit the animals for a small fee. Or they can bring a cat or a dog to feed to the lions. It is one of the great sights of London. Every visitor wants to see it.

--

The animals stayed at the Tower for more than six hundred years. Then about two hundred years ago, the last ones were moved to the new London Zoo.

Today at the Tower there are wire sculptures of the animals that once lived there. A lion, a polar bear, an elephant and some monkeys. And there are the ravens. There are always at least six of them. An old legend says that if the ravens ever leave the Tower, the kingdom will fall. So a special guard looks after them. He is called the Ravenmaster. Their wings are trimmed a little, so they cannot fly far.

{img:ravens-tower-green-yeoman-warder-ravenmaster-feeding-morning}
The guards of the Tower, called Beefeaters, still live there with their families, inside the walls. Every night at ten o'clock they lock the gates in a small old ceremony. They have done this every single night, without fail, for more than seven hundred years.
