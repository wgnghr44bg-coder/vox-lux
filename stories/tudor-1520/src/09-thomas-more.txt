One of the most remarkable men in England in 1520 is Thomas More.

He is forty-two years old. He is a lawyer and a judge, a scholar and a writer. He sits on the king's council. And he is the king's friend. Henry likes to visit him at home. They walk together in the garden, with the king's arm around his neck. They talk about the stars and about God and about poetry.

--

More is famous all over Europe for a little book. It is called Utopia. It is about a made-up island in the New World. The name means no place. On this island, everyone lives in peace. Nobody owns things for himself alone. Everyone works six hours a day and spends the rest of the time learning. The people choose their own rulers. And gold is worth so little there that they use it to make chains for prisoners.

It is a clever and funny book. But it is also serious. It makes people think about the problems of their own country. The poor are poor, and the rich are greedy. Farmers are pushed off the land to make room for sheep. In Utopia, More writes that the sheep have become so hungry they eat up men.

{img:scholar-writing-latin-book-study-books-candle-window-tudor}
Ever since, people have used the word utopia for a perfect made-up world.

--

More is warm and funny and kind. He is also very religious. His good friend Erasmus describes him in a letter. His hair is dark brown, Erasmus writes, and his eyes are grey-blue. He is always smiling and joking.

More loves his family and his home. In a few years he will build a new house at Chelsea, a village by the Thames. It will have a big garden running down to the river. He will live there with his wife Alice and his children and grandchildren. And with many pets. A monkey, dogs, rabbits, birds, a fox and a weasel.

{img:holbein-style-family-portrait-room-tudor-household-reading-calm}
He believes girls should learn as much as boys. That is unusual in his time. He teaches his daughters Latin and Greek and the stars. His eldest daughter Margaret becomes one of the most learned women in England. She even writes letters to Erasmus, in Latin.

--

A few years later, a German painter called Hans Holbein will come to stay with More. He will draw the whole More family together at Chelsea. It is the first big family portrait in English art. The painting is lost. But the drawing survives. It shows them reading and talking. There is a little dog. And the monkey is climbing up Lady Alice's dress.

More's story will later turn sad. He will have to choose between his king and what he believes is right. We will come back to that. But in 1520 he is happy and respected. And he is still the king's friend.
