Tonight we go about five hundred years back. We go to England, in the spring and summer of the year 1520.

--

A young king sits on the throne. He is tall and golden-haired. He is strong and clever and full of music and laughter. His court shines with silk and gold. His palaces stand along the river Thames. His chief minister is building the finest house in England. And this summer, the king will sail to France. There he will meet the king of France, in the grandest meeting of kings Europe has ever seen.

The king is Henry the Eighth. He is twenty-eight years old. Tonight we will spend a little time in his England. It is long before the troubles that come later. These are the years when everything still seems golden. But even now, there is one worry in the palace. The king has no son.

If you like these slow journeys into the past, you are welcome to subscribe to the channel, so that the next one is easy to find.

--

England in 1520 is a small country. Perhaps two and a half million people live there. That is fewer than in one big city today.

Most of them live in the countryside. They live in small villages of wooden houses with straw roofs. Each village has a church, a green and a manor house. Around it lie fields and meadows and woods. The people are farmers and shepherds and craftsmen. They grow wheat and barley, oats and peas and beans. And above all, they keep sheep.

--

England is the land of sheep. English wool is the finest in Europe. It is sold all over the continent, as wool and as cloth. It makes England rich. Rich wool traders build big churches in their towns, as thanks to God. In Parliament, the most important judge sits on a big cushion stuffed with wool. It is called the Woolsack. It reminds everyone where England's money comes from.

{img:sheep-grazing-cotswold-hills-wool-church-tower-golden-stone}
There are a few towns, mostly small. Each has a weekly market and a few streets of shops and inns. There are a few cities, like Norwich and Bristol and York. Each has maybe ten thousand people. And there is London.

--

London has perhaps fifty or sixty thousand people, and it is growing fast. It is busy and noisy and dirty and exciting. It lies along the north bank of the Thames, inside old Roman walls. It has a hundred churches. It has the great church of Saint Paul's, with the tallest spire in England. It has the Tower of London, the old royal castle. And it has London Bridge, covered with houses and shops.

{img:old-st-pauls-cathedral-tall-spire-london-roofs-thames-1520}
West of the city, along the river, stand the palaces. They belong to the king and the bishops and the great lords. Their gardens run down to the water. The king and his court travel up and down the Thames in big painted boats. The rowers wear the king's colours, green and white.
