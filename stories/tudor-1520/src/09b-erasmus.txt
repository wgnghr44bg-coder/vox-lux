One of the most famous visitors to Tudor England is the Dutch scholar Erasmus of Rotterdam.

Erasmus is the most famous writer in Europe, in 1520. He was born in Rotterdam, around 1466, the son of a priest, and became a monk, and then a scholar, and travelled all over Europe, writing books, in Latin, and making friends with the scholars, and the princes, of every country. He is witty, and wise, and gentle, and sharp, and believes that people should study the Bible, and the great writers of ancient Greece and Rome, and live simple, good lives, and that the Church should be reformed, peacefully, with learning, and kindness.

--

He has visited England several times, and loves it. He is a close friend of Thomas More, and stayed in his house, in London, where he wrote his most famous book, In Praise of Folly, a funny, clever satire, about the follies of the world, the Church, and the scholars, and the princes. He dedicated it to More, with a joke: the Latin word for folly, moria, sounds like More's name.

He taught Greek at Cambridge University, from 1511 to 1514, at Queens' College, where there is still a tower, called Erasmus's Tower. He complained, in his letters, about the cold, and the bad beer, and the bad wine, and the plague, and the expense. But he also wrote that the English girls were very pretty, and that they had a charming custom, of kissing visitors, when they arrived, and when they left.

{img:queens-college-cambridge-old-court-tower-river-cam-spring}
In 1516, he published the first printed edition of the New Testament in Greek, with a new Latin translation, which scholars all over Europe used, and which helped William Tyndale, and Martin Luther, to translate the Bible into English, and German.

--

Erasmus wanted peace: between the kings of Europe, and within the Church. He was delighted by the Field of the Cloth of Gold, and the hope of peace between England and France. But in the following years, Europe will be torn apart, by the quarrels of the Reformation, and wars. And Erasmus, who wanted everyone to be reasonable, will be attacked by both sides.

He passed away, in 1536, in Basel, in Switzerland. And today, the great European student exchange programme, which lets millions of young people study in other countries, is named after him: Erasmus.

--

{img:erasmus-letter-writing-candle-many-letters-friends-europe-desk}
Erasmus wrote thousands of letters, to his friends, all over Europe, in Latin, witty, and warm, and full of news, and gossip, and ideas, and many of them still survive. In one of them, he describes the house of Thomas More, in London, as a school, and a university, of Christian life, where everyone, the children, and the servants, studied, and read, and nobody was ever idle, or quarrelled, and everyone was cheerful.
