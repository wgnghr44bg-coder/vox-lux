The Tudors love gardens.

The great houses have pleasure gardens in the new style from France and Italy. They are called knot gardens. Low clipped hedges make patterns like knots. The spaces between them are filled with flowers and herbs or coloured sand. They look best from the windows above. There are paths of gravel and grass. There are fountains and sundials. And there are shady seats under roses and honeysuckle.

--

Henry likes tall wooden posts in his gardens. They are painted green and white, the Tudor colours. On top sit carved golden animals, like lions and dragons and greyhounds. Some gardens also have a small man-made hill. A path winds up to a little summer house on top. From there you can see the gardens and the river.

The flowers are old English flowers, and some new ones. There are roses, red and white, the signs of the Tudor family. There are pinks that smell of cloves. There are violets and primroses and lilies and lavender.

{img:tudor-orchard-apple-pear-cherry-trees-blossom-bees-skeps-spring}
Every house has a garden, even the smallest cottage. People grow cabbages and onions, peas and beans and lettuce. They grow herbs for cooking and medicine. They also spread herbs on the floors, to make the house smell sweet. And they grow fruit trees, apples and pears and plums and cherries. Many keep bees in round baskets of straw.

--

Henry loves fruit. He has new kinds planted from France. His fruit grower plants great orchards in Kent. That is the start of Kent's fame as the garden of England.

On summer evenings, the ladies and gentlemen of the court walk in the gardens. They pick flowers and herbs. They sit in the shade and play music and talk. They smell the roses and the lavender. And swallows swoop low over the river.

--

{img:still-room-woman-distilling-rosewater-herbs-bottles-tudor-house}
In the great houses, the lady has a special room called a still room. Here she and her maids make medicines and perfumes from the garden. They make rose water and lavender water. They make syrups and creams, sugared flowers and fruit jam for the winter. They write their recipes down in household books. Mothers hand these books to their daughters, for many generations.
