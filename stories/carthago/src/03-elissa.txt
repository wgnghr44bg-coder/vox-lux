The people of Carthage tell a wonderful story about how their city began. It is the story of a queen called Elissa.

Long ago, the story says, Elissa was a princess of Tyre. Her brother became king of Tyre. He was greedy and unkind. He wanted Elissa's wealth for himself. So Elissa decided to leave Tyre. One night, she secretly loaded her treasure onto ships. With a group of loyal friends and followers, she sailed away, west across the sea.

--

After a long voyage, with a stop on the island of Cyprus, Elissa and her people came to the coast of Africa. They found a beautiful place, with a sheltered bay and a green hill rising above it. It was a perfect place for a harbour and a city. But the land belonged to a local king, called Iarbas. Elissa went to him and asked if she could buy some land to settle on.

{img:local-african-king-meeting-elissa-tent-oxhide-on-ground-bargain}
The king did not want to give her much land. So he said, with a smile, that she could have as much land as could be covered by the hide of one ox. An oxhide is not very big. It would cover only a small patch of ground, about the size of a room. The king thought that was a good joke. But Elissa was clever. She agreed at once.

--

Then she took the oxhide and cut it into very thin strips, thinner than a thread. She cut and cut, until she had one enormous long strip of leather. Then she laid the strip on the ground, in a great circle, around the whole hill above the bay. The king was amazed. But he had made a promise, and he kept it. The whole hill belonged to Elissa.

{img:people-laying-thin-leather-strip-circle-around-hill-legend-sunset}
That hill was called Byrsa, which some people say means oxhide in Greek. On it, Elissa and her people built their new city. They called it Qart-hadasht, which means New City in Phoenician. The Greeks and Romans later called it Carthage. And the hill of Byrsa became the heart of the city, with its temples and its strong walls.

--

The Romans later told the story differently. They called the queen Dido. In a famous Roman poem, Dido falls in love with a Trojan hero called Aeneas, who comes to Carthage after a long journey. But Aeneas must leave her, to found a city in Italy. It is a sad and beautiful story, and it was very famous for many centuries. But the people of Carthage told their own story, about their wise and brave queen, Elissa.

{img:byrsa-hill-carthage-temples-walls-top-view-sea-bay-afternoon}
The old writers say that Carthage was founded in the year 814 before Christ. That is more than six hundred years before our story. Archaeologists think it was a little later, perhaps fifty or a hundred years. Either way, by the time of our story, Carthage is very old and very rich. Its people are proud of their long history. And every child of Carthage knows the story of Elissa and the oxhide.

The story also says that when the first people of Carthage dug the foundations of the city, they found the head of an ox. That was a sign, the priests said, that the city would be rich, but would have to work hard. So they dug in another place, and found the head of a horse. That was a sign that the city would be strong and brave. And the horse became one of the symbols of Carthage, found on many of its coins.

Elissa, the girl in our story, is very proud of her name. She knows that she is named after the great queen. When she plays with her friends, she always wants to be Queen Elissa, cutting the oxhide into strips. She uses a piece of string instead. And she lays it out in a circle around the little garden on the roof, and says: this is my city now.

The old stories say that Queen Elissa ruled Carthage wisely for many years. She built walls and temples and harbours. She made laws, and settled quarrels between her people. The people loved her. After she was gone, they honoured her as a goddess. And for hundreds of years, they told her story to their children, as Grandmother Batbaal tells it now.
