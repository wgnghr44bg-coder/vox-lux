Tonight we travel about two thousand two hundred years back in time. We go to the north coast of Africa, where the land reaches out into the blue Mediterranean Sea. The year is around 180 before Christ. And here, on a green hill above the sea, stands one of the richest cities in the world. It is called Carthage.

--

It is early morning, and the sea is calm and silver. The sky over the mountains to the east is turning pink and gold. Fishing boats are coming in to the shore, with their sails folded. Seagulls cry and circle above the water. On the hill above the harbour, the white walls of houses and temples catch the first light of the sun. And somewhere in the city, a cock is crowing, and a door is opening, and a new day is beginning.

The air smells of salt and fresh bread, of olive oil and wood smoke. In the narrow streets, the first people are coming out of their houses. A baker is pulling flat loaves from a clay oven. A woman is carrying a jar of water on her head. A boy is leading a donkey loaded with baskets of figs. And down at the harbour, the sailors are already at work.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

{img:carthage-circular-military-harbour-cothon-ship-sheds-island-tower}
Carthage is a city of the sea. Its people are famous sailors and traders. Their ships sail all over the Mediterranean, and even beyond it, into the great ocean. They bring back silver and tin, gold and ivory, wine and oil. They sell purple cloth and fine pottery, glass beads and carved ivory. Carthage has two great harbours, one for trade and one for warships. The warship harbour is round, with an island in the middle. There is nothing like it anywhere else in the world.

Most people today know Carthage only because of its wars with Rome, and its famous general Hannibal, who crossed the mountains with elephants. But tonight, we will not follow the wars. They were long and sad. Tonight we will visit Carthage in a time of peace, between the wars, when the city was rich and busy and full of life again.

{img:carthaginian-family-house-courtyard-mother-children-morning-light-180bc}
We will meet a family who lives near the harbour. The father builds ships in the great round harbour. The mother weaves cloth and sells it in the market. Their son wants to be a sailor, and their daughter loves to listen to old stories. And their grandmother remembers the city of Tyre, far away in the east, where their people first came from.

--

We will learn about the old Phoenicians, who invented the alphabet. We will hear the story of Queen Elissa, who founded the city with a clever trick and an oxhide. We will see how the people of Carthage made the most precious colour in the world, from little sea snails. We will hear about a sailor who travelled far down the coast of Africa. And we will wait with the family, for a ship that has sailed beyond the edge of the known world.

{img:boy-sitting-harbour-wall-watching-ships-sails-carthage-morning-sun}
So let us begin beside the harbour of Carthage, on a summer morning long ago. A boy of twelve is sitting on the stone wall of the quay, swinging his legs. He is watching the ships come in, one after another, with their painted eyes and their square sails. He knows the name of every ship. And one day, he is sure, he will sail on one of them, all the way to the end of the world.

Along the shore, the fishermen are unloading their catch. There are silver sardines and red mullet, tuna and octopus. Women come down with baskets to buy fish for the day. Children run between the boats, looking for crabs among the rocks. And an old fisherman sits on an upturned boat, mending his net with a wooden needle, humming an old song about the sea.

On the hill of Byrsa, the priests of the temple of Eshmun are climbing the long stairs in the early light. They carry little bowls of incense and jars of oil. At the top, they open the great doors of the temple and light the lamps inside. Sweet smoke rises into the morning air. From up there, they can see the whole city waking below them, and the sea stretching away to the horizon.
