In the spring, Uncle Bomilcar sets off on a long voyage. His ship will sail to Gades, beyond the Pillars of Heracles. Then it will sail north, along the ocean coast, to buy tin. It will be away for many months.

Before he leaves, he comes to say goodbye to the family. He brings presents: a little carved ship for Hanno, and a shell necklace for Elissa. He tells them he will be back before the autumn storms. He hugs his sister and his mother. Then he walks down to the harbour, with his bag on his shoulder. The family goes with him, to wave as the ship sails away.

--

The ship is big and round, with a tall mast and a great square sail. Its name is the Star of Tanit. It has a crew of about twenty sailors, and a captain who has made this voyage many times. The ship is loaded with goods to sell: purple cloth, pottery, glass beads and wine. It will come back with tin and silver, if all goes well.

{img:family-waving-from-quay-merchant-ship-leaving-harbour-spring-morning}
The family watches the ship sail slowly out of the harbour, with its sail filling in the wind. It grows smaller and smaller, until it is just a little white speck on the blue sea. Then it is gone. Hanno stands on the harbour wall for a long time, still looking. His mother puts her hand on his shoulder. He will come back, she says. He always does.

--

Spring turns into summer. Life goes on in Carthage. Himilco works in the harbour. Arishat weaves and sells her cloth. The children go to school and play in the streets. But every day, Hanno goes down to the harbour to watch for the Star of Tanit. And every evening, Grandmother Batbaal lights the lamp for Tanit, and prays for her son on the sea.

{img:grandmother-praying-lamp-niche-evening-worried-hopeful-courtyard}
In late summer, news arrives from Gades, with another ship. The Star of Tanit reached Gades safely, the news says. It sailed north, toward the tin islands, at the beginning of the summer. Everyone is relieved. But they know that the ocean beyond Gades is wild and dangerous, with great waves, strong tides and thick fogs. And the autumn storms will come soon.

--

Autumn comes. The days get shorter. The wind changes, and the sea becomes grey and rough. One by one, the trading ships come home to Carthage, before the winter. But the Star of Tanit does not come. Every day, Hanno watches. Every day, the family waits. Arishat is quiet. Grandmother Batbaal prays longer in the evenings.

{img:boy-watching-grey-sea-harbour-wall-autumn-wind-waiting-alone}
Then, one stormy evening, a ship comes into the harbour. It is not the Star of Tanit. But it brings news. Its captain met the Star of Tanit at Gades, two weeks ago, he says. The Star of Tanit was damaged by a great storm in the ocean, and had to stop for repairs. But all the crew were safe. It would set off for Carthage as soon as it could. The family hugs each other with relief. And they wait, a little more patiently now.

On the ship, the sailors have a hard but simple life. They sleep on the deck, wrapped in blankets. They eat bread and dried fish, olives and cheese. They drink water from big jars, and a little wine. They work the sails and the steering oars, and pump out the water that leaks in. And in the evenings, they sing old sailors' songs and tell stories under the stars.

While her brother is away, Arishat weaves a special cloth for him. It is a thick cloak of wool, dyed blue like the sea, with a border of little ships. She works on it every evening, after her other work is done. Elissa helps her wind the wool. It takes the whole summer. She wants it to be ready when he comes home, to keep him warm on his next voyage.
