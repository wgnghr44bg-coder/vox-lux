Ten days later, early in the morning, Hanno is at the harbour, as always. The sky is clear after the storms, and the sea is calm and blue. And there, coming slowly into the trade harbour, is a big round ship, with a patched sail and a painted eye on its side. It is the Star of Tanit.

Hanno runs all the way home, faster than he has ever run in his life. He bursts into the courtyard, shouting. The ship is here! Uncle Bomilcar is home! The whole family drops what they are doing and runs down to the harbour. Even Grandmother Batbaal comes, as fast as her old legs can carry her, holding Elissa's hand.

--

On the quay, the sailors are tying up the ship. And there is Uncle Bomilcar, with a big smile on his face. He is thin and brown from the sun and the wind, and his beard has grown long. He jumps down onto the quay and hugs everyone at once. Grandmother Batbaal cries with happiness. Elissa hugs his leg and will not let go. Hanno just grins and grins.

{img:sailor-uncle-reunion-quay-family-hugging-grandmother-crying-happiness}
That evening, there is a feast in the courtyard of the family's house. Arishat cooks a big pot of fish stew, with onions and herbs. There is fresh bread, olives, cheese and figs. Himilco brings a jar of good wine. Neighbours come to welcome Bomilcar home. The courtyard is full of people, laughing and talking. Someone plays a flute, and the children dance.

--

After supper, Uncle Bomilcar tells the story of his voyage. He tells them about Gades, the old city on an island at the edge of the ocean. He tells them about the great tides, and the waves as high as houses. He tells them about the fog, so thick that you could not see the front of the ship. He tells them about the storm that tore the sail and broke the mast. And he tells them about the green misty islands in the north, where the people live in round houses and dig tin from the earth.

{img:courtyard-feast-night-lamps-uncle-telling-voyage-children-listening-neighbours}
Then he opens his bag and takes out his presents. For his mother, a soft warm cloak of wool from the north. For Arishat, a little silver bracelet from Spain. For Himilco, a fine knife with a handle of bone. For Elissa, a little piece of amber, golden and glowing, with a tiny insect trapped inside. And for Hanno, a real sailor's knife, in a leather case. Hanno holds it as if it were made of gold.

--

{img:boy-and-uncle-flat-roof-night-stars-pointing-north-star-harbour-below}
Late that night, when the neighbours have gone home, Hanno sits with his uncle on the roof of the house. They look up at the stars. Uncle Bomilcar shows him the stars that sailors use to find their way at night. That one points to the north, he says. Follow it, and you will never be lost. Hanno looks up at the star for a long time. Then he asks if he can sail with his uncle, one day. Uncle Bomilcar smiles. When you are older, he says. And when you can swim properly. Hanno laughs. And this time, he does not blush at all.

The next day, the whole family goes to the temple of Melqart to give thanks for Bomilcar's safe return. They bring a little gift of incense and a cake of honey. Bomilcar kneels before the altar and says a prayer of thanks. Grandmother Batbaal says a prayer too, with tears in her eyes. Then they walk home together through the sunny streets, all of them smiling.

Arishat gives her brother the blue cloak with the little ships. Bomilcar puts it on at once and turns around, to show everyone. It is the most beautiful cloak he has ever had, he says. He will wear it on every voyage, and think of his sister and her family every time. Arishat blushes with pleasure. And Elissa says proudly that she helped to wind the wool.
