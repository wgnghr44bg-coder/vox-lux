The people of Carthage worship many gods. Their gods came with them from Tyre, long ago, and some new ones were added in Africa.

The most important god is Baal Hammon. He is an old, wise god, with a long beard, who sits on a throne. He is the lord of the sky and the harvest. The most important goddess is Tanit. She is the great mother goddess of Carthage, the protector of the city and its people. Her sign, the triangle with the circle, is seen everywhere in Carthage, on houses and stones and coins.

--

There is also Melqart, the god of the city of Tyre. He is a god of the sea and of sailors, and of new beginnings. The Greeks thought he was like their hero Heracles. There is Eshmun, the god of healing and good health. His great temple stands on top of the hill of Byrsa. People go there when they are ill, to pray for help. And there are many other gods and goddesses, of the sea and the fields, of the home and the family.

{img:sailors-praying-small-shrine-harbour-offering-wine-melqart-before-voyage}
The people of Carthage pray to their gods every day. They have little shrines in their homes, with figures of the gods. They pour a little wine or oil as an offering. They burn incense, which fills the air with sweet smoke. Before a long voyage, sailors go to the temple of Melqart and pray for a safe journey. When they come home safely, they give thanks.

--

The priests of Carthage are important people. They wear long linen robes, and they shave their heads. They look after the temples and lead the ceremonies. They know the old prayers and the old songs. They play music on flutes and drums and lyres at the festivals. Some of them also study the stars, and know the calendar of the seasons.

{img:priests-linen-robes-shaved-heads-procession-temple-steps-flutes-drums}
The Greeks and Romans later told many unkind stories about the religion of Carthage. Some of these stories may have had some truth in them, and some were probably exaggerated by enemies. Historians still discuss them today. For our story, we will remember the ordinary people of Carthage, praying in their homes and temples, for good health, safe journeys and happy families, just as people do everywhere.

--

Every evening, in the family's house, Grandmother Batbaal lights a little lamp in front of the figure of Tanit, in the niche of the courtyard. She says a short prayer, very softly. She asks the goddess to look after the family: Himilco in the harbour, Arishat and the children, and Uncle Bomilcar, far away on the sea. Elissa often stands beside her, and says the prayer too. She likes the little flame, and the quiet words, and the feeling of being safe.

{img:grandmother-girl-lighting-lamp-tanit-niche-courtyard-evening-prayer}
On feast days, the whole family goes to the temples. They wear their best clothes. They bring little gifts: cakes and flowers, wine and incense. They join the processions through the streets, with music and singing. They see the priests in their white robes, and the great statues of the gods. And afterwards, they have a special meal at home, with meat and sweets and wine. It is a time of joy and thanks, for the whole city.

One of the most important festivals in Carthage is in spring, when the year begins again. People clean their houses and put on new clothes. They decorate the temples with flowers and green branches. There is music in the streets, and dancing, and feasting. And people pray for a good year, with plenty of rain and a rich harvest.

The people of Carthage also believe that the spirits of their ancestors are still near them. They visit the graves of their families on special days. They bring little gifts of food and flowers, and oil for the lamps. They talk to their ancestors, and tell them the news of the family. In this way, the people of Carthage feel that their families are never really separated, even by death.
