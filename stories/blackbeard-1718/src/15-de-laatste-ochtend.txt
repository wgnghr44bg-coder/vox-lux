In November 1718, Governor Spotswood of Virginia sends two small sloops, the Jane and the Ranger, south along the coast, to Ocracoke.

They are not warships. They are small, private sloops, hired by the governor, because the Navy's warships are too big to go into the shallow waters of the sounds. They carry no cannons. But on board, there are about sixty sailors from two Navy warships, which are anchored in Virginia.

--

They are led by a young Navy officer, Lieutenant Robert Maynard, of the warship Pearl. He is about thirty-five, an experienced and steady officer.

At the same time, Spotswood sends soldiers overland, to the town of Bath, to look for Blackbeard there, and to seize any goods he has hidden.

Spotswood also announces a reward: one hundred pounds for capturing Blackbeard, and smaller sums for each of his men. This is a great deal of money, more than most sailors would earn in many years.

--

On the evening of the twenty-first of November, 1718, Maynard's two sloops arrive at Ocracoke Inlet, and see Blackbeard's sloop, the Adventure, at anchor, in the channel inside the island, at Teach's Hole.

Blackbeard has only about twenty men with him. The rest are in Bath, or elsewhere. He does not know that anyone is coming. Johnson says that he spent the night drinking, and talking, with his men, and with the master of a trading sloop that was anchored nearby.

{img:evening-sound-anchored-sloops-lanterns-quiet-island-trees}
Maynard does not attack in the dark, because the channels are narrow and shallow, and full of sandbars, and he does not know them. So the two sides wait, through the night, a little way apart, on the quiet water, among the islands, each knowing that the other is there.

--

At dawn, on the twenty-second of November, Maynard's sloops raise their anchors, and move toward Blackbeard's sloop.

What happened next, we will tell gently, and from a distance, as if we were standing far away, on the beach of Ocracoke, in the early morning light, watching the three little ships on the water.

There is a short, fierce fight, on the water of the sound, and then on the deck of Maynard's sloop, the Jane, as the two crews meet. It lasts only a few minutes.

And when it is over, the morning is quiet again. And Blackbeard has fallen, on the deck of the Jane, in the cold grey light of a November morning, at the age of about thirty-eight.

--

Maynard writes afterward, in a letter, that Blackbeard was a brave man, and fought hard to the end. Several of Maynard's men, and about half of Blackbeard's, have also been lost. The rest of Blackbeard's men surrender.

The story of the fight has been told, and retold, and made more and more dramatic, over the centuries. Johnson says that Blackbeard received many wounds before he fell. But we do not need to know all that.

{img:ocracoke-sound-calm-morning-after-gulls-low-light}
It is enough to know that the most famous pirate in history ended his days, not in a great sea battle, but in a short, confused fight, in a quiet sound, behind a sandy island, on the coast of North Carolina.

--

Maynard sails back to Virginia, with his prisoners, and with proof that Blackbeard is gone.

The prisoners are taken to Williamsburg, the capital of Virginia, and put on trial in March 1719. Most of them are hanged. But one, Samuel Odell, who was a trader who had only happened to be on board that night, is found not guilty. And another, Israel Hands, Blackbeard's sailing master, who was in Bath at the time of the fight, is pardoned, in return for giving evidence against others. Johnson says that he ended his days as a beggar in London.

More than a hundred and sixty years later, the writer Robert Louis Stevenson would borrow his name, Israel Hands, for one of the pirates in his famous novel, Treasure Island.

--

Governor Eden of North Carolina, and his secretary, Tobias Knight, are accused of helping Blackbeard. Some letters, and sugar from the French ships, are found in Knight's barn. But they are never punished. Knight passes away the next year, and Eden a few years later.

And Spotswood, the governor of Virginia, who sent the expedition, is criticized by many people, for sending his men into another colony without permission. And there are long quarrels, for years, about the reward money.

{img:quiet-colonial-church-graveyard-oaks-bath-autumn}
But Blackbeard's story is over. He was a pirate for only about two years. And yet, three hundred years later, he is still the most famous pirate who ever lived.
