One of the strangest pirates of all sails with Blackbeard, in the spring of 1718. His name is Stede Bonnet.

Stede Bonnet is not a sailor at all. He is a rich gentleman, from the island of Barbados, the owner of a large sugar plantation, with a fine house, and a wife, and children. He is educated, and owns a library of books. He is about thirty years old. He is a major in the island's militia. He has everything that a man of his time could want.

--

And then, one day, in 1717, he decides to become a pirate.

Nobody knows why. Captain Johnson says that it was because his wife was unkind to him, and that he was suffering from a disorder of the mind. Some historians think that he was unhappy, and in debt, and bored. Whatever the reason, he does something that no other pirate ever did. He buys his own pirate ship.

{img:sloop-being-fitted-barbados-harbour-cannons-loaded}
Most pirates steal their ships. Bonnet buys a sloop, and fits it out with ten guns, and hires a crew of seventy men, and pays them wages, as if it were an ordinary ship. He names it the Revenge. And one night, he sails away from Barbados, without telling his family, and becomes a pirate.

--

He sails up the coast of America, and captures a few ships, off Virginia, and the Carolinas, and New York. But he does not know how to sail a ship, or how to command men. His crew does not respect him. They call him the gentleman pirate.

In the autumn of 1717, he arrives in Nassau, and meets Blackbeard. And Blackbeard, seeing that Bonnet is not a seaman, persuades him to come aboard his own ship, as his guest, and to let one of Blackbeard's men command the Revenge.

Johnson says that Bonnet spent the next months on Blackbeard's ship, walking up and down the deck in his morning gown, reading his books, and feeling rather unhappy, and wishing he could go home. He told his friends that he was ashamed of what he had done, and would be glad to leave the life, if only he could live in some country where he was not known.

--

Bonnet sails with Blackbeard through the winter and the spring of 1718. And in the end, Blackbeard leaves him behind, at Beaufort, in North Carolina, as we shall see.

Then Bonnet takes back his sloop, and goes on as a pirate for a few more months, under a false name, Captain Thomas. But in the autumn of 1718, he is caught by a ship from Charleston, at the mouth of the Cape Fear River, in North Carolina, after a long fight on the sandbanks.

{img:charleston-1718-town-harbour-wooden-houses-church-spire}
He is taken to Charleston, and put on trial. He escapes, once, disguised as a woman, but is caught again. And in December 1718, he is hanged in Charleston, after writing a long and sorrowful letter to the governor, asking for mercy. It is a sad story, of a man who wanted adventure, and did not know what he was doing.

--

Bonnet's sloop, the Revenge, was later used by the Royal Navy. And the place where he and his men were hanged, at the edge of the marsh, near Charleston harbour, was called White Point. Today, it is a quiet, beautiful park, with old oak trees, and benches, and a bandstand, where people walk their dogs, and look out over the harbour.

There is a small stone there, which remembers the pirates.

{img:white-point-garden-oaks-harbour-benches-quiet-afternoon}
Stede Bonnet's strange story has been told many times since. In recent years, it was even made into a television comedy, which imagined him as a gentle, kind man, who becomes a pirate, and becomes friends with Blackbeard.
