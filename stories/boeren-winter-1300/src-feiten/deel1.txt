HEAD: The village of Wharram Percy belongs to its lord, and the people of the village have duties to him.
BODY: Most of the families, like John's, are what were called villeins, or serfs. If you lived here, you'd owe the lord a share of your work. They aren't slaves. They have their own houses and their own land, and they can keep most of what they grow. But they aren't free, either. They are bound to the manor. They can't leave the village without the lord's permission. And in return for their land, they must work for the lord, on his land, a certain number of days every week. The lord's land, which is called the demesne, is scattered among the strips of the open fields, just like the villagers' own land. So, two or three days a week, John and his son must go and plough and sow and harvest the lord's strips, before they can work on their own. At busy times of the year, like the harvest, they must work extra days, which are called boon days, when the whole village works together for the lord. On those days, the lord gives them a meal, of bread and cheese and ale. And sometimes meat, and everyone eats together in the fields. The villagers also pay rents to the lord, in money, or in kind: a few hens at Christmas, a basket of eggs at Easter. They must grind their grain at the lord's mill, and pay the miller a share of the flour. And when they marry, or when someone dies, they must pay a fee to the lord. The lord's business in the village is managed by a reeve. The reeve is one of the villagers, chosen by the others, every year, to organize the work on the lord's land. And to keep the accounts. He is a busy and important man, who must be fair to his neighbours. You'd hardly ever see the lord himself. And honest with the lord, which isn't always easy.
