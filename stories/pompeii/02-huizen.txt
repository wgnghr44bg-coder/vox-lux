Beneath the opening, set into the floor, was a shallow pool, to catch the rainwater.

Around the atrium were smaller rooms.

[pause]

Bedrooms, often small and simple, with just enough space for a bed.

[pause]

A room where the master of the house received visitors.

[pause]

A little wine, some grains of incense, perhaps a flower.

[pause]

What would strike you most, walking through a house like this, is the colour.

The walls were painted from floor to ceiling.

[pause]

A warm, deep colour, like the inside of a pomegranate.

{img:peristyle-garden}
At the back of many larger houses, there was a garden.

[pause]

It was surrounded on all sides by a covered walkway, supported by columns, so that you could walk in the shade even on the hottest days.

[long-pause]

In the garden grew roses, and violets, and lilies.

[pause]

The largest house in Pompeii is known today as the House of the Faun.

[pause]

It takes its name from a small bronze statue, found in its atrium, of a dancing faun, a woodland spirit with pointed ears, arms raised in joy.

[long-pause]

The house was enormous.

[pause]

It took up an entire block, with two atriums, and two gardens, and dozens of rooms.

[pause]

And on the floor of one room, there was a mosaic made of more than a million tiny stones.

[long-pause]

It shows a great battle from centuries before, between Alexander the Great and the king of Persia.

[pause]

Horses rearing, spears raised, the faces of the soldiers full of expression.

[pause]

Someone, long ago, spent months placing every tiny stone by hand.

[pause]

And the family who lived there walked across it every day.

[long-pause]

Not every house was so grand, of course.

[pause]

Most people in Pompeii lived in much smaller homes.

[pause]

Some families lived in just one or two rooms above their shop, or at the back of their workshop.

[pause]

They cooked on a small stove, slept on simple beds, and spent most of their day out in the street, working, and talking, and trading.

[long-pause]

Many of the people who lived in the great houses were not members of the family at all.

[pause]

Fish from the bay, perhaps, or roast pork, or chicken, often flavoured with honey, and herbs, and a salty fish sauce called garum, which Romans loved to add to almost everything.

[pause]

And at the end, there was fruit.

[pause]

Figs, and grapes, and apples, and pomegranates.

[long-pause]

Wine was always served, mixed with water, in painted cups.

[pause]

Sometimes there was music, or someone reading poetry aloud.

[pause]

And the conversation went on long into the night, by the soft light of the oil lamps.

[long-pause]

In some summer dining rooms, the couches stood outside in the garden, beneath a pergola covered in vines.

Guests could lie back and look up through the leaves at the stars, while a small stream of water flowed along a channel beside them.
