When Pliny arrived at Stabiae, he found his friend Pomponianus very worried.

[pause]

Pomponianus had already loaded his belongings onto a ship, ready to leave as soon as the wind changed.

[pause]

But the same wind that had brought Pliny there so quickly, was blowing straight onto the shore, and no ship could sail out against it.

[long-pause]

Pliny embraced his friend.

[pause]

He comforted him, and encouraged him, and tried to calm his fears.

[pause]

And to show that there was nothing to be afraid of, he did something rather remarkable.

[pause]

He asked for a bath.

[long-pause]

After his bath, he lay down to dinner, cheerful, or at least, as his nephew wrote, pretending to be cheerful, which was just as brave.

[pause]

They ate together, as the darkness deepened outside.

[pause]

And all the while, the sky over the mountain was lit up by broad sheets of flame and tall bright fires, glowing through the night.

[long-pause]

To reassure the others, Pliny told them that these were only fires in the villages, left burning by country people who had fled in a hurry.

[pause]

Perhaps he believed it.

[pause]

Perhaps he simply wanted his friends to feel a little less afraid.

[long-pause]

{img:pliny-sleeping-room}
And then, Pliny went to bed.

[pause]

And he fell asleep.

[long-pause]

He was a big, heavy man, and he breathed loudly in his sleep.

[pause]

The people waiting outside his door could hear him snoring, deeply and peacefully, through the night.

[long-pause]

It is one of the most human moments in the whole story.

[pause]

The great scholar, the admiral of the fleet, the man who had written about everything in the world, sleeping soundly, while the sky burned and the ash fell.

[pause]

Perhaps he was exhausted after the long voyage.

[pause]

Perhaps he truly wasn't afraid.

[pause]

Or perhaps he simply knew that there was nothing more to be done until morning.

[long-pause]

{img:courtyard-filling-ash}
But while he slept, the courtyard outside his room was slowly filling up.

[pause]

Ash and pumice drifted down, and piled up against the door, higher and higher.

[pause]

If he stayed there much longer, it would become impossible to get out.

[long-pause]

So they woke him.

[pause]

He came out, and joined Pomponianus and the others, who had not slept at all.

[pause]

Together, they discussed what to do.

[long-pause]

Should they stay inside the house?

[pause]

The walls were trembling with frequent earthquakes, and seemed to sway back and forth, as if they might come loose from their foundations.

[pause]

Or should they go outside, into the open air, where the pumice stones were falling?

[pause]

They were light, and full of holes, but still, there were so many of them.

[long-pause]

They chose the open air.

[pause]

It was, as Pliny's nephew wrote, a choice between two fears.

[long-pause]

{img:pillows-torches-night}
They tied pillows on top of their heads with strips of cloth, to protect themselves from the falling stones.

[pause]

It is a strange and touching image.

[pause]

A group of friends, in the middle of the night, pillows tied to their heads, stepping out together into the dark.

[long-pause]

By now it should have been morning.

[pause]

But the darkness was deeper than any night.

[pause]

They lit torches and lamps, and made their way slowly down to the shore, to see if the sea would let them escape.

[pause]

But the waves were still wild and high, and the wind was still against them.

[long-pause]

There, on the beach, Pliny lay down on a sheet spread out on the sand.

[pause]

He asked for cool water, and drank.

[pause]

And there, we will leave him for now, resting by the sea, with his friends around him.

Meanwhile, back in Misenum, the young Pliny and his mother had spent an anxious evening.

[pause]

After his uncle left, he had gone back to his studies, just as he had said he would.

[pause]

Then he had taken a bath, eaten dinner, and gone to bed.

[pause]

But he didn't sleep much.

[long-pause]

That night, the earthquakes grew stronger.

[pause]

For days, there had been small tremors, and nobody had paid them much attention.

[pause]

But now, the ground was shaking so hard, that it seemed as if everything might not just move, but turn over.

[long-pause]

His mother came rushing into his room.

[pause]

He was already getting up, intending to wake her.

[pause]

They went out together into the small courtyard of the house, which lay between the building and the sea.

{img:young-pliny-reading-livy}
And there, in the middle of that frightening night, the young Pliny did something that he himself later found a little embarrassing.

[pause]

He called for a book.

[pause]

It was a volume of history, by the great Roman historian Livy.

[pause]

And he sat down, and began to read, and even to make notes, just as though nothing were happening at all.

[long-pause]

He wasn't sure, when he wrote about it years later, whether it had been courage or foolishness.

[pause]

I was only seventeen, he said.

[long-pause]

A friend of his uncle's arrived, who had come from Spain to visit.

[pause]

When he saw the young man sitting calmly reading, and his mother sitting beside him, he scolded them both.

[pause]

The mother for putting up with it.

[pause]

And the son for being so careless.

[pause]

But the young Pliny went on reading.

It was a long night, all around the bay.

[pause]

In the villas of Stabiae, people sat awake, listening to the falling stones.

[pause]

On the roads leading away from Pompeii, the refugees kept walking, their torches small and flickering in the dark.

[pause]

In the harbour at Misenum, the warships that had stayed behind rocked at their moorings.

[pause]

And in countless homes, families held each other close, and waited for the dawn.

[long-pause]

Many of them were already safe.

[pause]

Far enough away, now, that only a fine grey dust drifted down on them.

[pause]

In Naples, and Nuceria, and the villages in the hills, people opened their doors to strangers, and made room for them by the fire.

[pause]

Shared their bread, and their blankets, and their water.

[long-pause]

That, too, is part of this story.

[pause]

Not only the mountain, and the ash, and the darkness.

[pause]

But the kindness of the people who took the refugees in.

[pause]

Who made space, in their small houses, for those who had lost everything.

[long-pause]

The night went on, and on.

[pause]

Longer, it seemed, than any night before.

[pause]

And slowly, somewhere behind the great grey cloud, the sun was rising on the second day.
