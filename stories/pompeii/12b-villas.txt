Pompeii and Herculaneum were not the only places the ash preserved.

[pause]

All around the mountain, other buildings lay hidden beneath the ground, and some of them have been found too.

[long-pause]

A few kilometres west of Pompeii, in the modern town of Torre Annunziata, lies a great villa at a place the Romans called Oplontis.

[pause]

Some believe it may have belonged to the family of Poppaea, the wife of the emperor Nero.

[long-pause]

It is enormous.

[pause]

There are long painted halls, and gardens, and a swimming pool more than sixty metres long, lined with statues and shaded by trees.

[pause]

The walls are covered in paintings of great beauty.

[pause]

Painted columns and painted doorways, opening onto painted gardens, with birds, and baskets of fruit, and a peacock perched on a ledge.

[long-pause]

At the time of the eruption, the villa seems to have been partly empty, perhaps being repaired after the earthquake.

[pause]

Its owners were probably far away.

To the north of Pompeii, at a place called Boscoreale, archaeologists found a working farm.

[pause]

It had rooms for pressing grapes, and threshing wheat, and storing olive oil.

[pause]

There were rows of huge clay jars, set into the ground in a courtyard, which could hold thousands of litres of wine.

[long-pause]

And in one of the villas there, hidden in a dry cistern, they found a remarkable treasure.

[pause]

More than a hundred pieces of silver.

[pause]

Cups, and plates, and bowls, beautifully decorated with scenes of plants and animals and gods.

[pause]

And a bag of gold coins.

[long-pause]

Someone had hidden them there, during the eruption, hoping perhaps to come back for them when it was all over.

[pause]

They never did.

[pause]

And the silver waited in the dark, for almost two thousand years.

And at Stabiae, where Pliny spent his last night, the great villas along the cliff have been partly uncovered too.

[pause]

[pause]

Delicate figures float across the walls, against backgrounds of soft blue and white.

[pause]

[pause]

She has become one of the most famous images from the whole Roman world.

[pause]

People call her simply, Spring.

[long-pause]

Walking through these villas today, it is easy to imagine the lives of the people who once lived there.

[pause]

Reading in the shade of the colonnades.

[pause]

Watching the sunset over the islands.

[pause]
