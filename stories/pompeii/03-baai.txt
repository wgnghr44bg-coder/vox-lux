Pompeii was not alone beside the bay.

[pause]

Along the curving shoreline, there were other towns, and villages, and great country estates.

[long-pause]

On the other side of Vesuvius, right at the edge of the sea, lay a smaller town called Herculaneum.

[pause]

According to legend, it had been founded by the hero Hercules himself.

[long-pause]

Herculaneum was quieter than Pompeii, and in some ways more elegant.

[pause]

Its houses had terraces looking out over the water.

[pause]

Wealthy families from Rome came there to escape the heat and noise of the capital.

[pause]

Fishermen pulled their boats up onto the beach below the town, and mended their nets in the shade.

[long-pause]

Just outside Herculaneum stood one of the grandest villas on the whole bay.

[pause]

It belonged, many believe, to a powerful Roman family, related by marriage to Julius Caesar.

[pause]

The villa had long gardens with pools, and dozens of bronze and marble statues.

[pause]

And it had something even more precious.

[pause]

A library.

[long-pause]

On its shelves were hundreds of scrolls, rolls of papyrus covered in handwriting.

[pause]

Books of philosophy, mostly, written in Greek.

[pause]

The owner and his friends would sit in the shade of the garden, reading, and discussing big questions.

[pause]

How to live well.

[pause]

How to be happy.

[pause]

How not to be afraid.

[long-pause]

Today, the house is known as the Villa of the Papyri, after those scrolls.

[pause]

And we will come back to them, at the very end of our story.

[long-pause]

{img:stabiae-villas}
To the south of Pompeii, across a small river, lay Stabiae.

[pause]

It was less a town than a string of luxurious villas, built along a cliff above the sea.

[pause]

From their terraces, you could look out across the whole bay.

[pause]

To the north, the green cone of Vesuvius.

[pause]

To the west, the islands, floating on the water.

[pause]

And far across the bay, the bright white buildings of the other towns.

[long-pause]

The villas of Stabiae had painted rooms, and heated baths, and long covered walkways where their owners could stroll in any weather.

[pause]

Some had their own fish ponds, cut into the rocks by the sea.

[pause]

It was a place for resting, and reading, and doing very little at all.

[long-pause]

{img:puteoli-harbour}
On the far side of the bay, to the north west, lay the busy port of Puteoli.

[pause]

Ships arrived there from every corner of the Mediterranean.

[pause]

Grain from Egypt, to feed the city of Rome.

[pause]

Glass and spices from the east.

[pause]

Marble, and wine, and pottery, and cloth.

[long-pause]

The harbour was crowded with ships, and the docks were full of noise.

[pause]

Sailors called out in many languages.

[pause]

Porters carried heavy jars up and down the gangplanks.

[pause]

Merchants counted coins, and wrote careful notes on little wax tablets.

[long-pause]

Close by was Baiae, the most fashionable resort in the whole Roman world.

[pause]

It was famous for its natural hot springs, bubbling up from deep underground.

[pause]

The rich and powerful came there to bathe, and to relax, and, some said, to misbehave.

[long-pause]

Nobody thought it strange that hot water came up from the ground there.

[pause]

Or that in some places, the earth steamed gently, and smelled faintly of sulphur.

[pause]

It was simply part of the landscape.

[pause]

A gift, like the rich soil, and the warm sun.

[long-pause]

{img:misenum-fleet}
And at the very tip of the bay, on a rocky headland, lay Misenum.

[pause]

Misenum was the home of the Roman navy's largest fleet.

[pause]

In its harbour, dozens of warships rested at anchor, long and narrow, with rows of oars along their sides.

[pause]

Thousands of sailors and rowers lived there, in barracks beside the water.

[long-pause]

In the summer of our story, the commander of that fleet was a man named Gaius Plinius Secundus.

[pause]

We know him today as Pliny the Elder.

[long-pause]

{img:pliny-study}
Pliny was in his mid fifties.

[pause]

He had been a soldier, and a lawyer, and a trusted official of the emperor.

[pause]

But above all, he was a man who loved to learn.

[long-pause]

He had written a vast book, called the Natural History.

[pause]

It was an attempt to describe everything in the known world.

[pause]

The stars and the planets.

[pause]

The lands and the seas.

[pause]

Animals, and plants, and minerals, and medicines, and works of art.

[pause]

It filled thirty seven volumes.

[long-pause]

Pliny was famous for never wasting a moment.

[pause]

He woke long before dawn, and began working by lamplight.

[pause]

While he ate, someone read to him.

[pause]

While he bathed, someone read to him.

[pause]

Even when he travelled, he took a secretary with him, with a book and writing tablets, so that not a single hour would be lost.

[long-pause]

He once scolded his nephew for going on a walk.

[pause]

You could have spent those hours reading, he said.

[long-pause]

That nephew was also staying at Misenum that summer.

[pause]

His name was Gaius Plinius Caecilius Secundus.

[pause]

We call him Pliny the Younger.

[long-pause]

{img:pliny-younger-reading}
The young Pliny was seventeen years old.

[pause]

His father had died when he was a child, and his uncle had taken him under his wing.

[pause]

He lived in his uncle's house with his mother, Plinia, who was the elder Pliny's sister.

[long-pause]

Like his uncle, the young Pliny loved books.

[pause]

He was studying hard, and hoped one day to become a lawyer, and an orator, speaking before the courts in Rome.

[pause]

And he would one day become a famous writer himself, known especially for his letters.

[long-pause]

Many years later, when he was a grown man, a friend of his, the historian Tacitus, would ask him to write down what he remembered of that summer.

[pause]

And he did.

[pause]

His two letters, describing what he saw and heard, have survived for almost two thousand years.

[pause]

They are the only eyewitness account we have of what was about to happen.

[pause]

And much of what we know, we know because a seventeen year old boy was paying attention.

[long-pause]

A late fire in a kitchen.

[pause]

Deep beneath it, far below the vineyards and the forests, something has been gathering for a very long time.

[pause]

But on the surface, nothing has changed.

The night is warm, and still.

[pause]

The sea breathes softly against the shore.

[pause]

Soon, the sky in the east will begin to grow pale.
