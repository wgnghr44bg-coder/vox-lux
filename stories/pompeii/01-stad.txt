{img:bay-of-naples-vesuvius}
The town was called Pompeii.

[pause]

It stood in the south of Italy, in a region called Campania, not far from the city we now call Naples.

[pause]

To the west lay the Bay of Naples, wide and blue, dotted with fishing boats and trading ships.

[pause]

And to the north, rising gently above the fields and vineyards, stood a mountain.

[long-pause]

The Romans called it Vesuvius.

[long-pause]

To the people who lived beneath it, Vesuvius did not look dangerous at all.

[pause]

Its slopes were green, and covered with vines, and dotted with farms and villas.

[pause]

Wild boar lived in its forests.

[pause]

Shepherds grazed their flocks on its lower hills.

[pause]

And the soil around it was some of the richest in all of Italy.

[long-pause]

A painting found in Pompeii shows the wine god Bacchus, dressed in grapes, standing beside a single green mountain.

[pause]

Many believe it is Vesuvius, as the people of the time saw it.

[pause]

Not a threat, but a gift.

[pause]

A generous mountain, that gave them wine.

[long-pause]

{img:vesuvius-vineyards}
Nobody living there knew that Vesuvius was a volcano.

[pause]

It had been quiet for many centuries, longer than anyone could remember.

[pause]

There were no stories of fire, no old warnings passed down from grandparents.

[pause]

One Greek writer, a geographer named Strabo, once noticed that the rocks near its summit looked burnt, as if they had been touched by fire long ago.

[pause]

But even he thought of it as something from the distant past.

[long-pause]

About a hundred and fifty years before our story, a famous rebel named Spartacus, who had escaped from a school for gladiators, hid with his followers on the slopes of Vesuvius.

[pause]

The story goes that when Roman soldiers blocked the only path down, the rebels made ladders from wild vines, and climbed down the cliffs in the night.

[pause]

To them, the mountain was a hiding place.

[pause]

Nothing more.

[long-pause]

Pompeii itself was already very old.

[pause]

It had been founded perhaps seven centuries earlier, by a people called the Oscans, long before Rome became the centre of an empire.

[pause]

Over the years, it had been home to Greeks, and Etruscans, and a people from the mountains called the Samnites.

[pause]

Each of them left something behind, a temple here, a style of building there, a few words carved into stone.

[long-pause]

About a hundred and sixty years before our story, Pompeii became a Roman colony.

[pause]

Roman veterans settled there, and Latin slowly became the everyday language of its streets.

[pause]

By the time of our story, Pompeii was a busy, prosperous little town, with perhaps eleven or twelve thousand people living inside its walls, and many more on the farms and estates around it.

[long-pause]

It was not a great city, like Rome.

[pause]

It was a place where ordinary people lived ordinary lives.

[pause]

And that, in the end, is exactly what makes it so extraordinary.

[long-pause]

{img:town-gate-street}
Let's walk in through one of its gates.

[pause]

The town wall is made of large, pale stones, and the gate is a tall, shadowed arch.

[pause]

Beyond it, a street runs straight ahead, paved with big, uneven blocks of dark volcanic stone.

[long-pause]

Look down, and you'll see deep grooves worn into the paving.

[pause]

These are the tracks of cart wheels, pressed into the stone by years and years of traffic.

[pause]

Carts carrying wine, and grain, and building stone, and firewood, rolling in and out of town every day.

[long-pause]

Every so often, large stepping stones cross the street from one side to the other.

[pause]

The streets could be muddy, and sometimes they carried rainwater, and worse.

[pause]

So pedestrians would step from stone to stone, keeping their sandals dry, while cart wheels passed neatly through the gaps.

[long-pause]

On almost every corner, there is a fountain.

[pause]

A stone basin, with water flowing steadily from a carved face, perhaps a god, or a lion, or a cockerel.

[pause]

The water came from the mountains, carried by a great aqueduct, and then through a web of lead pipes beneath the streets.

[pause]

Most families didn't have running water at home.

[pause]

So they came to the fountain with their jars, and filled them, and stopped to talk with their neighbours.

[long-pause]

Over the years, so many hands rested on the edges of these fountains, that the stone became smooth, and worn into gentle hollows.

[pause]

You can still see those hollows today.

[pause]

The traces of thousands of people, leaning down to drink.

[long-pause]

{img:wall-graffiti}
The walls along the streets are covered in writing.

[pause]

Some of it is painted neatly in red and black letters.

[pause]

Some of it is scratched quickly into the plaster.

[long-pause]

There are election notices, asking people to vote for one candidate or another.

[pause]

There are advertisements for rooms to rent, and for gladiator shows at the amphitheatre.

[pause]

And there are little personal messages, left by passers by.

[pause]

Greetings to friends.

[pause]

Complaints about neighbours.

[pause]

Declarations of love.

[long-pause]

One message, scratched on a wall, simply says that someone was here, and says hello.

[pause]

Another praises a woman for her kindness.

[pause]

Another, rather sadly, reports that a certain man owes someone money.

[long-pause]

Reading them today, it feels as if the people of Pompeii have only just stepped out of the room.

[long-pause]

{img:forum-jupiter}
At the heart of the town is the forum.

[pause]

A long, open square, surrounded by columns, with temples and public buildings on every side.

[pause]

At the far end stands the Temple of Jupiter, the king of the gods.

[pause]

And behind it, framed perfectly between the columns, you can see the green shape of Vesuvius, rising in the distance.

[long-pause]

The forum was the place where everything happened.

[pause]

Merchants sold their goods.

[pause]

Officials met to discuss the business of the town.

[pause]

Lawyers argued cases in the great hall called the basilica.

[pause]

And people simply came to walk, and talk, and see and be seen.

[long-pause]

Nearby stood the Temple of Apollo, with a bronze statue of the god drawing his bow.

[pause]

There was a market hall for meat and fish, and another building where wool merchants gathered.

[pause]

And all around, the sound of voices, and footsteps, and the occasional braying of a donkey.

[long-pause]

{img:earthquake-repairs}
But not everything in Pompeii was perfect.

[pause]

Seventeen years before our story, on a winter's day, the ground had shaken violently.

[pause]

It was a powerful earthquake.

[long-pause]

Walls cracked.

[pause]

Columns fell.

[pause]

Statues toppled from their pedestals, and water pipes burst beneath the streets.

[pause]

In some parts of the town, whole houses had to be rebuilt.

[long-pause]

A relief carved in stone, found in the house of a banker, seems to show that day.

[pause]

The Temple of Jupiter is tilting, and the horses beside an altar look as if they are about to run.

[long-pause]

The people of Pompeii did what people have always done after a disaster.

[pause]

They cleaned up, and they began to rebuild.

[pause]

Seventeen years later, many of those repairs were still going on.

[pause]

Scaffolding stood against walls.

[pause]

Piles of building stone waited in the streets.

[pause]

Painters were busy freshening up rooms, with pots of colour laid out on the floor.

[long-pause]

Nobody knew that the earthquake had been a kind of message.

[pause]

That deep beneath the mountain, something was slowly waking.

[long-pause]

In the years that followed, small tremors came and went.

[pause]

In Campania, that was nothing unusual.

[pause]

The ground here had always moved a little, now and then.

[pause]

People would feel a shiver beneath their feet, pause for a moment, and then carry on with their day.

[long-pause]

After all, the mountain looked exactly as it always had.

[pause]

Green, and quiet, and generous.

[long-pause]

In the evenings, the streets of Pompeii grew calm.

[pause]

Shopkeepers closed their wooden shutters, sliding each board into its groove, one after another.

[pause]

Families gathered for their evening meal.

[pause]

Oil lamps were lit, small clay lamps with a single flame, casting a warm, flickering light on the painted walls.

[long-pause]

From the gardens came the scent of roses, and myrtle, and the sound of water trickling in a fountain.

[pause]

Somewhere, someone was playing a flute.

[pause]

A dog barked once, far away, and then was quiet.

[long-pause]

Above the town, the sky slowly filled with stars.

[pause]

And beyond the rooftops, the dark shape of Vesuvius rested against the night, as calm and still as it had been for centuries.
