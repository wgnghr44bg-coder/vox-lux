Most people in Tuscany lived in the countryside and worked the land. Many were sharecroppers. They lived on a farm owned by a rich family in the city. At the end of each year, they gave half of everything they had grown to the owner. Half the wheat, half the wine, half the olive oil. Even half the eggs, the cheese and the chickens. The other half they kept for themselves. The farmhouse had stables and storerooms below. You'd see them come back to the hills at dusk with an empty cart. Upstairs was a big kitchen with an open hearth, where the family lived and ate.

HEAD: Nobody at the time knew what caused the sickness, and many thought it came from bad air or the stars.
BODY: In 1346, a sickness came to Caffa. You might think they were foolish. They simply didn't have the knowledge we have. It had travelled from the East, along the caravan roads, from the lands of the Mongol empire. Nobody knew exactly where it had begun. Somewhere far away, people said. Today, we know what it was. It was caused by tiny living things, bacteria, far too small to see without a microscope. They lived in the fleas on rats and other small animals. When those fleas bit people, the people fell ill. Sometimes, the sickness could also pass from person to person. But in the fourteenth century, nobody knew anything about bacteria or fleas. Nobody knew why people fell ill, or how illness spread. Some thought it came from bad air rising out of the earth. Some thought the stars and planets had lined up in an unlucky way. Many believed it was sent by God. Imagine facing something like that, with no way of understanding it. People did the best they could, with what they knew.

BODY: Among the crowds of Avignon was a man who would become one of the most famous poets in history. You'd hear his poems read aloud for centuries afterwards. His name was Francesco Petrarca. He first saw her, he wrote, on Good Friday in 1327, in a church in Avignon. He fell in love at once. But she was married to someone else. So for twenty years, he loved her only from a distance. He wrote hundreds of short poems about her, called sonnets. You might know a line or two yourself. Poets all over Europe imitated them for centuries.

BODY: That spring, Petrarch was away from Avignon, travelling in Italy. In May, a letter reached him from a friend. It told him that Laura had died, on the sixth of April 1348.
