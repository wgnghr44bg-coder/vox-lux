Outside the walls of Florence, the countryside rolls away in every direction, in soft green and golden hills.

The Florentines call it the contado, the country that belongs to the city. It is a beautiful, gentle landscape: hills covered with olive groves, their leaves silver-green in the wind; vineyards climbing up the slopes in neat rows; fields of wheat and barley in the valleys; dark, slender cypress trees standing like sentinels along the roads and around the farmhouses; and little stone villages and churches perched on the hilltops.

--

Most of the people of Tuscany live in the countryside, and work on the land.

Many of them are sharecroppers. They live on a farm owned by a rich family in the city, and work the land, and at the end of each year, they give half of everything they have grown to the owner: half the wheat, half the wine, half the olive oil, half the eggs and the cheese and the chickens. The other half, they keep for themselves.

The farmhouse is built of stone, with a red-tiled roof, two storeys high. On the ground floor, there are the stables for the oxen and the donkey, and storerooms for the tools and the grain. Upstairs, there is a big kitchen with an open hearth, where the family cooks and eats and spends the evenings, and a few small bedrooms.

--

The year on the farm follows the seasons.

In autumn, the farmers plough their fields with oxen, and sow the wheat. In winter, they prune the vines and the olive trees, mend their tools, and cut wood for the fire. In spring, they hoe the fields and plant the vegetables, and the sheep have their lambs. In early summer, they cut the hay. In midsummer, under the hot sun, they harvest the wheat with sickles, and thresh it on the stone floors in front of their houses.

{img:wheat-harvest-sickles-tuscan-summer-field}
In late September, there is the grape harvest, the vendemmia, the happiest time of the year, when everyone, young and old, goes out into the vineyards to pick the grapes, and then the grapes are trodden in great wooden tubs to make the wine. And in November, the olives are picked, and taken to the press, and the new green oil flows out, and everyone eats bread soaked in it, warm from the fire.

--

The rich families of Florence love the countryside too.

Many of them own villas in the hills around the city: comfortable country houses, with gardens and orchards, fountains and fishponds, and views over the valley of the Arno. In the summer, when the city is hot and dusty and smelly, they leave Florence and go to their villas, for weeks or months at a time.

{img:villa-garden-fountain-view-florence-hills}
There, they walk in their gardens, and ride in the hills, and hunt with hawks and dogs, and sit in the shade of the trees in the afternoons, reading and talking and playing music. In the evenings, they eat in the open air, under a pergola covered with vines, with the lights of Florence twinkling far away in the valley below.

--

Above Florence, on a hill to the north, there is a little town called Fiesole.

It is much older than Florence. It was built by the ancient Etruscans, long before the Romans came, and there are still the ruins of a Roman theatre there, and ancient walls. From the hill of Fiesole, there is a wonderful view of the whole of Florence, spread out in the valley below: the river, the walls, the towers, the roofs, the half-built cathedral.

{img:fiesole-hill-view-florence-valley-evening}
On the slopes between Fiesole and the city, among the olive groves and the cypresses, there are many villas and gardens and monasteries, and little paths and lanes winding between stone walls covered with ivy and wild roses.

--

Boccaccio knows these hills well.

His family has a small property in the countryside, at Certaldo, a little hilltop town to the southwest of Florence, built of red brick. He has spent many summers there. And he loves to walk in the hills around Florence, among the villas and the gardens.

One day, he will set his most famous book in these hills, in a beautiful villa with a garden full of flowers and fountains, where a group of young people will take refuge, and tell each other stories, to pass the time.

--

On a summer evening in the countryside, the air is full of the scent of hay and wild thyme and the warm earth.

The farmers come home from the fields, tired and sunburnt. The women cook supper over the fire. The children bring in the goats. The oxen are led into the stable. Then the family sits outside on the stone bench by the door, in the cooling air, and watches the sun go down behind the hills.

{img:farm-family-stone-bench-sunset-fireflies}
The fireflies come out among the olive trees, and the crickets begin to sing, and the bells of the village church ring for the evening prayer. Far away, in the valley, the lights of the great city glow faintly in the dusk.

It is a peaceful world, which has been much the same for hundreds of years.
