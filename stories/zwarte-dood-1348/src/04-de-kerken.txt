In the heart of Florence, the people are building a cathedral.

It was begun about fifty years ago, to replace an old, small church that stood on the same spot. The Florentines wanted it to be the largest and most beautiful church in all of Christendom, so that everyone who came to their city would be amazed.

In 1347, it is still far from finished. And it will not be finished for nearly a hundred years more.

--

The walls are slowly rising, of brown stone, covered with patterns of white and green and pink marble, in squares and stripes and stars. Inside, the great nave is open to the sky in places, and the floor is littered with blocks of stone and piles of sand and lime.

All around it, the stonemasons are at work. In their sheds and yards, they cut and carve the blocks of marble, with their chisels and mallets, in a constant ringing and tapping. The carpenters build the wooden scaffolding and the great wooden cranes, worked by men walking inside huge wooden wheels, which lift the heavy stones up to the top of the walls.

--

Beside the cathedral, a bell tower is rising.

It was designed by the most famous painter in Italy, Giotto, who began it about thirteen years ago, when he was already an old man. He died only three years later, when only the first storey was finished. Now, another master, Andrea Pisano, is continuing the work.

{img:bell-tower-rising-scaffold-marble-panels}
On the walls of the lowest storey, there are little carved panels of marble, showing the work of human beings through the ages: a man ploughing a field, a weaver at his loom, a shepherd with his sheep, a sailor in his boat, a builder, a doctor, a horse rider, an astronomer looking at the stars. People stop in the street to look at them, and point out their own trades.

--

Across the square from the cathedral, there is a very old building, shaped like an octagon, covered with white and green marble.

It is the Baptistery of Saint John, the patron saint of Florence. Every child born in the city is baptized here, in the great stone font in the middle. Inside, the domed ceiling is covered with mosaics of gold and colour, showing the story of the world from the Creation to the Last Judgment, glittering in the light of the candles.

On its southern side, there is a pair of new bronze doors, made by Andrea Pisano just a few years ago, showing the life of Saint John, in little golden pictures.

--

On the twenty-fourth of June, the feast of Saint John, the whole city celebrates.

For days beforehand, the streets are decorated with banners and tapestries hanging from the windows. The shops display their finest goods. On the eve of the feast, there is a great procession, with the clergy and the guilds and the government, carrying candles and banners and relics, through the streets to the Baptistery.

{img:feast-procession-banners-candles-street}
On the day itself, there are races, and games, and music and dancing in the squares. There is a horse race, called the palio, through the streets of the city, with the riders galloping wildly over the stones, and the crowds cheering. The prize is a length of costly red cloth.

--

Florence has many other churches, too.

In the west of the city, there is the great church of Santa Maria Novella, belonging to the Dominican friars, who wear black and white robes. In the east, there is the church of Santa Croce, belonging to the Franciscan friars, who wear plain brown robes tied with a rope.

The friars preach in the squares in front of their churches, to great crowds of people. They visit the sick and the poor, and give food to the hungry. Inside their churches, the walls are covered with paintings by Giotto and his pupils, telling the stories of Saint Francis and the saints, in bright colours.

--

There are also many small churches, chapels and monasteries all over the city, and the bells of each of them ring at the hours of prayer, from before dawn until late at night.

The bells tell the time of day for everyone in the city: when to get up, when to start work, when to eat, when to stop work, when to go to bed. Most people do not have clocks. They listen to the bells.

{img:bell-ringers-tower-ropes-evening-light}
In the evening, at sunset, all the bells of Florence ring together, for the evening prayer, the Ave Maria. And all over the city, people stop for a moment, wherever they are, in the street or in their shops or in their homes, and say a short prayer, before going on with their evening.
