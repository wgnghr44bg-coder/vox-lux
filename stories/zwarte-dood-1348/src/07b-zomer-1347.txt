The summer of 1347 is a good summer in Tuscany.

After the hunger of the year before, the harvest this year is rich. The wheat in the fields around Florence grows tall and golden. The farmers go out with their sickles in June and July, and bring in the sheaves, and thresh them on the stone floors in front of their houses, and the grain pours into the sacks.

--

In the city, there is relief everywhere.

The price of bread falls. The bakers' shelves are full again. The market is crowded with fresh fruit and vegetables: cherries and apricots, then peaches and figs and melons, beans and cucumbers and lettuces. People who went hungry last year can eat well again, and they are grateful.

The friars hold services of thanksgiving in the churches. The guilds hold feasts in their halls. The government is pleased, and the people of Florence feel that perhaps the hard times are over.

--

In the summer heat, the city slows down.

By midday, the sun beats down on the stone streets and squares, and the air is hot and still. The shopkeepers pull down their awnings. People keep to the shade of the narrow streets and the loggias. In the afternoon, after the midday meal, many people rest, in the coolest rooms of their houses, behind closed shutters, while the city grows quiet, and only the cicadas sing in the gardens.

{img:shuttered-street-afternoon-heat-cicadas}
In the evening, when the heat breaks, everyone comes out again.

--

On summer evenings, the young people of Florence go down to the river.

Below the city walls, the banks of the Arno are shady with poplars and willows, and the water is shallow and slow in summer. The boys swim and splash in the river, and lie on the sandy banks in the evening sun. Fishermen stand in the shallows with their nets. Washerwomen kneel on flat stones at the edge of the water, beating the clothes clean, and laughing and calling to one another.

{img:arno-riverbank-summer-evening-swimmers-poplars}
Further along, there are mills on the river, with great wooden waterwheels turning slowly in the current, grinding the new wheat into flour.

--

In the squares, there is music and dancing.

Young men and women dance in rings, holding hands, to the music of pipes and drums and lutes, under the evening sky. Storytellers sit on the steps of the churches and tell old stories of knights and ladies, of Charlemagne and King Arthur, to little crowds of listeners. Children chase each other through the crowds, and dogs bark, and old men sit on stone benches by the walls of the palaces, talking about the old days.

In the houses of the rich, there are dinners in the gardens and on the roof terraces, with lanterns hanging from the vines, and music, and long conversations late into the night.

--

In the countryside, the grapes are ripening on the vines, and the olives are swelling on the trees.

In the villas in the hills, the rich families are spending the summer away from the heat of the city. They walk in their gardens in the evening, and sit by their fountains, and watch the sun go down behind the hills, and the lights of Florence come on, far below in the valley.

{img:villa-terrace-sunset-view-florence-lights}
In September, there is the grape harvest, and everyone, rich and poor, goes out into the vineyards, and the wine presses creak, and the new wine flows into the barrels. In November, the olives are picked, and pressed, and the new oil is poured over hot bread, and everyone says it is the best for years.

--

Nobody knows that it is the last ordinary summer that many of them will see.

The rumours from the East are still very far away. The ships from the Black Sea are still at sea. And in Florence, on a warm summer night in 1347, the people sleep peacefully, with their windows open to the night air, and the stars shining over the hills, and the river flowing softly under the bridges, and the bells of the churches ringing the hours, one after another, through the quiet dark.
