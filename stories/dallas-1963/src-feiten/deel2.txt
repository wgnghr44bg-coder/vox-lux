BODY: The next morning, Friday, the twenty-second of November, the President woke early. You'd feel the drizzle on your face as you waited. Outside the hotel window, it was grey and drizzling. In the parking lot across the street, a crowd of several thousand people had been gathering since before dawn. They stood patiently in the light rain. At a quarter to nine, the President came out of the hotel without a hat or a raincoat and crossed the street to speak to them from the back of a truck. The crowd cheered. Someone called out, asking where Mrs. Kennedy was. Mrs. Kennedy is organising herself, the President answered, with a grin. It takes her a little longer, but of course, she looks better than we do when she does it. The crowd laughed. It was a warm moment in a cool, damp morning. People later remembered the cheerfulness more than the rain.

HEAD: At breakfast, the whole room stood up and applauded when Mrs. Kennedy finally arrived.
BODY: Then he went back into the hotel, to a big breakfast in the ballroom, given by the Fort Worth Chamber of Commerce. You'd hear the chairs scrape back as everyone stood up. About two thousand people were there, the businessmen and community leaders of the city, sitting at long tables. Halfway through the meal, Mrs. Kennedy arrived at last, in her pink suit and pink hat, and the whole room rose to its feet and applauded. At the end, the people of Fort Worth gave the President a gift, a fine Texas cowboy hat. Everyone called for him to put it on. He smiled and held it up. He said he'd wear it later, and then they could all come and see. The crowd laughed again. It was a happy morning, full of jokes and warm handshakes. The President looked rested and cheerful, and Jackie looked radiant. Few people in the ballroom could have guessed how much they'd treasure these small, bright moments.
