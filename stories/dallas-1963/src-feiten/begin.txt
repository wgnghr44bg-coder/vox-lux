High above the streets of Dallas, on the roof of an old office tower, a red horse with wings turned slowly in the dark. It was made of glass tubes filled with glowing gas. Its wings flickered as it turned, and drivers saw it from miles away across the flat Texas land. For almost thirty years, it had told them the same thing. You're nearly home.

In the autumn of 1963, that horse looked down on one of the fastest growing cities in America. Wide new streets. Brand new houses. Shopping carts, television sets and football on Friday nights. Yet most of us only know this city from a single hour of a single day. The people who lived there knew it as breakfast tables, school buses and busy shop windows.
