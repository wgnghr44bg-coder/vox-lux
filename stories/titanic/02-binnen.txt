Then came the four great funnels, painted buff yellow with black tops, the colours of the White Star Line.

[pause]

But the designers kept it, because four funnels looked grand, and strong, and fast.

[pause]

In those days, people believed that the more funnels a ship had, the better she must be.

Deep inside her, the ship was divided into sixteen compartments, with strong steel walls between them.

[pause]

If one or two of them filled with water, the doors could be closed, and the ship would still float.

It was a careful and clever design, and people who read about it in the newspapers began to call her practically unsinkable.

Inside, hundreds of craftsmen were at work.

[pause]

Electricians pulled hundreds of miles of wire through the ship, for the lamps, and the heaters, and the lifts.

There were even three electric lifts in the first class, and one in the second, which was very modern in those days.

Carpets were rolled out along the corridors, and curtains were hung at the windows.

[pause]

[pause]

For quieter moments, there was a reading and writing room, painted white, with tall windows and soft armchairs.

The second class was simpler, but still very comfortable.
