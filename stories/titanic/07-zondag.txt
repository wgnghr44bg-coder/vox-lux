It was the fifth day of the voyage.

[pause]

The morning was bright and sunny, and quite cold.

At half past ten, a church service was held in the first-class dining saloon.

[pause]

Captain Smith led it himself, reading from the White Star Line's own prayer book.

[pause]

The passengers sang hymns together, while the ship's musicians played.

[pause]

In the second and third classes, there were services too, in English, and some in other languages.

[pause]

And afterwards, everyone went back to their usual Sunday pleasures.

[pause]

Long walks on deck, long lunches, and long, lazy afternoons.

[long-pause]

Lawrence Beesley spent part of that Sunday afternoon in the library, writing letters.

[pause]

Outside the windows, the sunshine sparkled on the water.

[pause]

In the lounge, people read newspapers that had been printed before they left, and talked about when they would arrive.

[pause]

If all went well, they would see the coast of America on Wednesday morning.

[pause]

Children ran along the decks, and some of the young people in third class played games on the open deck at the back.

[pause]

It was, by every account, a quiet and happy day.

[long-pause]

There had been a plan for a lifeboat drill that morning, so that the crew could practise.

[pause]

But for reasons that nobody is quite sure of, it did not take place.

Up in the radio room, messages were coming in from other ships.

[pause]

Some of them were warnings.

[long-pause]

In those days, sailors knew that in spring, large pieces of ice would break away from the glaciers of Greenland, and drift south into the Atlantic Ocean.

[pause]

That year, there seemed to be more ice than usual.

[pause]

Ships that were crossing the ocean further west had seen icebergs, and large fields of flat ice, drifting in the sea.

[long-pause]

A ship called the Caronia sent a warning that morning.

[pause]

Then a ship called the Baltic.

[pause]

Then the Amerika, and later a ship called the Mesaba.

[pause]

Some of these messages reached the bridge.

[pause]

Captain Smith showed one of them to Bruce Ismay, who put it in his pocket for a few hours.

[pause]

Some of the messages were never delivered to the bridge at all, because the radio operators were so busy sending passengers' messages to land.

[long-pause]

The officers on the bridge knew there was ice ahead.

[pause]

They changed course a little, further to the south.

[pause]

But the ship kept moving at her full speed, which was normal in those days.

[pause]

Almost every captain did the same.

[pause]

Sailors believed that in clear weather, they would see an iceberg in plenty of time.

In the afternoon, the temperature began to fall.

[pause]

Passengers who went for a walk on the deck came back inside very quickly, rubbing their hands.

[pause]

The sun set in a clear sky, turning the sea from blue to gold, and then to a deep, dark grey.

[pause]

[pause]

[pause]

The last hymn they sang was a sailors' hymn.

[pause]

It was a prayer for those in peril on the sea.

[pause]

Then Reverend Carter thanked everyone, and there was coffee and biscuits, and people said goodnight.

[pause]

All over the ship, lights were going out in the cabins.

[pause]

The corridors grew quiet.

[pause]

[pause]

And the great ship moved on through the night, steady and calm, with only the soft hum of her engines far below.

It was a very beautiful night.

[pause]

There was no moon.

[pause]

But the sky was so clear, and the stars were so bright, that they seemed to go all the way down to the horizon.

[pause]

[long-pause]

And the sea was completely calm.

[pause]

There was no wind at all, and no waves.

[pause]

[long-pause]

Normally, this would have been a perfect night.

[pause]

[pause]

[pause]

On that night, there were no waves at all.

[pause]

And without the moon, there was very little light.
