On board, there were more than two thousand people.

Around thirteen hundred passengers, and almost nine hundred crew.

[pause]

They came from dozens of different countries, and they spoke many different languages.

For a few days, they were all living together in this great floating town, far from any land.

The mornings in first class started slowly.

[pause]

Stewards knocked softly on the doors, bringing tea, or coffee, or hot water for shaving.

[pause]

Some passengers had breakfast in their cabins.

[pause]

Others walked down the grand staircase to the dining saloon, where there were baked apples and fresh fruit, porridge and grilled fish, eggs, ham, and warm rolls with butter and marmalade.

[long-pause]

After breakfast, many passengers went out for a walk along the promenade deck.

[pause]

They wrapped themselves in warm coats and scarves, because the wind at sea was cold, even in spring.

[pause]

They walked slowly up and down, sometimes arm in arm, talking quietly, or simply looking out at the sea.

[pause]

Some sat in deck chairs, with thick woollen blankets over their knees, reading books, and drinking cups of hot beef tea brought round by the stewards.

{img:astor-couple-deck}
Among the first-class passengers were some very famous names.

[long-pause]

The richest man on board was John Jacob Astor.

[pause]

He was an American businessman who owned many buildings in New York, and he had even written a novel about the future.

[pause]

He was travelling with his young wife Madeleine, who was expecting their first child.

[pause]

They had been on a long honeymoon in Egypt and in Europe, and now they were going home.

[pause]

They had brought their dog with them, an Airedale terrier called Kitty.

[long-pause]

In fact, there were around a dozen dogs on board.

[pause]

Most of them lived in kennels on one of the lower decks, and a crew member took them out for walks every day.

[pause]

There were small Pomeranians, a Pekingese, a French bulldog, and a large Newfoundland.

[pause]

The owners would go and visit them, and sometimes a little dog show was even planned for later in the voyage.

[long-pause]

{img:straus-couple-reading}
Another well-known couple were Isidor and Ida Straus.

[pause]

Isidor was one of the owners of Macy's, a very large department store in New York.

[pause]

He was sixty-seven years old, and Ida was sixty-three.

[pause]

They had been married for more than forty years, and they were almost never apart.

[pause]

When they were, they wrote letters to each other every single day.

[pause]

People on board often saw them walking slowly together along the deck, or sitting side by side in their deck chairs, holding hands.

[long-pause]

And then there was Margaret Brown.

[pause]

She had grown up in a small town in the American Midwest, in a family that had very little money.

[pause]

Her husband had found gold in the mountains of Colorado, and suddenly they had become rich.

[pause]

But Margaret never forgot where she came from.

[pause]

She was warm and lively, and she loved to talk, and she spoke several languages.

[pause]

She had been travelling in Europe, and she was going home because her grandson was ill.

[pause]

She would later become one of the best-known people of the whole voyage.

There were many others, too.

[pause]

Writers and artists, actors and bankers.

[pause]

A young film actress named Dorothy Gibson.

[pause]

A businessman named Benjamin Guggenheim.

[pause]

A young British countess named Noël, who was travelling to join her husband in California.

[pause]

And of course, Bruce Ismay, the head of the White Star Line, who wanted to see how his beautiful new ship performed.

[pause]

And Thomas Andrews, who spent his days walking all over the ship, from the top decks to the engine rooms, still writing down small things to improve in his notebook.

In the afternoons, some passengers swam in the pool, or went to the Turkish bath.

[pause]

Some played cards, or squash, or wrote letters.

[pause]

And some rode the mechanical horse in the gymnasium, laughing, while the instructor showed them what to do.

[long-pause]

At four o'clock, there was afternoon tea.

[pause]

In the lounge, with its tall windows and its soft green chairs, the stewards brought round silver pots of tea, and small plates of sandwiches, cakes and biscuits.

[pause]

The orchestra played quietly in the corner.

[pause]

Outside the windows, the sea rolled slowly past, grey and blue and silver.

[pause]

Ladies talked about the people they had met, and the clothes they had seen, and the plans they had for when they arrived in New York.

[pause]

Gentlemen talked about business, and about the ship, and about how fast she was going.

[pause]

Every day at noon, the distance the ship had travelled in the last day was posted up on a board, and some passengers even made small bets on it.

[long-pause]

Then, as the light began to fade, everyone went back to their cabins to dress for dinner.

[pause]

The men wore black suits and white shirts.

[pause]

The women wore long silk dresses, gloves, and jewels that glittered in the lamplight.

[pause]

Then they walked down the grand staircase, under the glass dome, to the dining saloon.

[long-pause]

Dinner in first class was long, and slow, and rich.

[pause]

On one evening, there were ten courses.

[pause]

There were oysters, and a clear soup, and poached salmon with a creamy sauce.

[pause]

There was roast beef, and lamb with mint sauce, and roast duckling with apple sauce.

[pause]

Then peaches in jelly, chocolate éclairs, and French ice cream.

[pause]

The meal could last for hours.

[long-pause]

Afterwards, some people went to the reception room, where the small orchestra played soft music.

[pause]

Waltzes, and light tunes from the theatre, and popular songs of the day.

[pause]

People sipped coffee, and talked, and laughed quietly.

[pause]

Some went out for one last walk on the deck before bed.

[pause]

The night air was cold and fresh, and smelled of salt.

[pause]

Above them, the smoke from the funnels drifted away across the stars.

[pause]

Below them, they could hear the soft rush of the sea along the sides of the ship.

[pause]

And far away, in every direction, there was nothing but the dark ocean, stretching away to the edge of the world.

[long-pause]

And one by one, they went to bed, in cabins with soft beds, electric heaters and warm lights, while the great ship moved on through the dark.
