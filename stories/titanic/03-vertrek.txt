Titanic arrived in Southampton just after midnight, a few days before Easter.

[pause]

Southampton was a busy port on the south coast of England, full of cranes and warehouses and railway lines that ran right up to the water.

[pause]

From there, the great liners of the White Star Line crossed the ocean to New York.

[long-pause]

For a week, the ship was loaded with everything she would need.

[pause]

Coal, first of all, thousands of tons of it, poured into her bunkers deep below.

[pause]

Then food.

[pause]

There were tens of thousands of eggs, and tons of meat and fish, and potatoes, and oranges, and flour.

[pause]

There were crates of tea and coffee, and barrels of beer, and thousands of bottles of wine.

[pause]

There were plates and cups and glasses, tablecloths and napkins, and around forty thousand pieces of linen for the beds and the tables.

[long-pause]

The crew came on board too.

[pause]

Most of them lived in Southampton, in small brick houses in the streets close to the docks.

[pause]

Many had been out of work for weeks, because of a long strike in the coal mines, which had kept many ships in port.

[pause]

So a job on the new Titanic was very welcome.

[long-pause]

There were almost nine hundred of them.

[pause]

Sailors and firemen, cooks and bakers, waiters and stewards, and even a few young boys who worked as bellboys and lift operators.

[pause]

They said goodbye to their families at their front doors, and walked down to the docks together in the early morning light.

[long-pause]

On the morning of departure, the crew were checked, and counted, and signed on for the voyage.

[pause]

They lined up in the big hall by the docks, gave their names, and received their pay book.

[pause]

Then they went on board to find their places.

[pause]

The sailors went to their quarters at the front of the ship.

[pause]

The firemen went down to the boiler rooms, to start raising steam.

[pause]

The stewards put on their white jackets, and went to wait at the doors, ready to welcome the passengers.

[pause]

A few men arrived a little too late, and found the gangway already closed.

[pause]

They stood on the quay, disappointed, and watched the ship leave without them.

[pause]

At the time, they must have thought that they were very unlucky.

[long-pause]

{img:boarding-passengers-gangway}
The day of departure was Wednesday, the tenth of April, in 1912.

[pause]

It was a fresh spring morning, cool and bright.

[long-pause]

The passengers began to arrive in the middle of the morning.

[pause]

Many came by special trains from London, which stopped right beside the ship.

[pause]

There were families with children, holding their parents' hands.

[pause]

There were young men with a single suitcase, and older couples with trunks that needed several porters to carry them.

[pause]

They walked up the gangways, one by one, and were shown to their cabins by the stewards.

[long-pause]

Some wandered around the decks, looking at everything with wide eyes.

[pause]

Some went straight to their cabins to rest.

[pause]

And some stood at the railings, waving to friends and family down on the quay.

[long-pause]

Exactly at noon, the great whistles on the funnels sounded.

[pause]

The deep sound rolled across the port, and could be heard for miles.

[pause]

The ropes were cast off, and small tugboats began to pull Titanic slowly away from the quay.

[long-pause]

{img:near-miss-ss-new-york}
As she moved out along the channel, something unexpected happened.

[pause]

Two other ships were tied up side by side along the quay.

[pause]

One of them was called the New York.

[pause]

As Titanic passed, the huge ship pushed so much water around her that the New York was pulled towards her, and her thick mooring ropes snapped, one after another, with a sound like pistol shots.

[long-pause]

Slowly, the smaller ship swung out towards Titanic.

[pause]

For a moment, it seemed they might touch.

[pause]

But a quick-thinking tugboat captain caught the New York with a rope and pulled her back.

[pause]

And on the bridge of Titanic, the captain stopped the engines.

[pause]

The two ships passed each other with only a few feet to spare.

[long-pause]

For about an hour, everything waited, while the New York was tied up safely again.

[pause]

Then Titanic continued on her way, slowly and carefully, out into the open water.

[pause]

She sailed down the long, calm waterway called Southampton Water, past green fields and small villages, and past the Isle of Wight, lying low and green in the sunshine.

[pause]

People came out of their houses to watch her pass.

[pause]

On board, passengers explored the decks, and found their way around the long corridors, and got lost, and laughed, and asked the stewards for directions.

[pause]

Some of the people watching thought it might be a bad sign.

[pause]

But most of them soon forgot all about it.

{img:cherbourg-tender-evening}
That afternoon, Titanic crossed the English Channel to France.

[pause]

In the early evening, she stopped outside the harbour of Cherbourg.

[pause]

The harbour there was too small for such a large ship, so the passengers came out to her in two small ferry boats, called tenders.

[pause]

One of them was called Nomadic.

[pause]

She was built in Belfast too, and she is still there today, the last White Star ship left in the world.

[long-pause]

Among the people who came aboard at Cherbourg were some of the richest travellers of the voyage.

[pause]

There were American businessmen coming home from holidays in Europe, with their wives, their servants and their luggage.

[pause]

And there were families from much further away, from Lebanon and Syria and Croatia, who had travelled for weeks across land to begin their journey to America.

[long-pause]

The tenders came slowly out across the harbour, in the golden light of the evening, carrying passengers, luggage and sacks of mail.

[pause]

Waiting passengers stood on their decks, looking up in wonder as they came closer to the great ship.

[pause]

From the water, she seemed impossibly tall, like a long black cliff with rows and rows of little round windows.

[pause]

They climbed through a door in her side, and stepped into a warm, bright hall, where the stewards were waiting to welcome them.

[long-pause]

By the time the tenders pulled away, it was dark.

[pause]

Titanic switched on all her lights.

[pause]

People on the shore saw her there, blazing in the night like a floating city, before she turned, and sailed away to the west.

[long-pause]

{img:queenstown-green-hills}
The next morning, she reached her last stop.

[pause]

It was a little town on the south coast of Ireland, called Queenstown.

[pause]

Today it is called Cobh.

[pause]

Once again, the passengers came out on small tenders, and so did the mail.

[pause]

Most of the people who came aboard there were young Irish men and women, travelling in third class, on their way to new lives in America.

[long-pause]

A few people left the ship at Queenstown.

[pause]

One of them was a young teacher named Francis Browne, who was studying to become a priest.

[pause]

His uncle had given him a ticket for the first part of the voyage, from Southampton to Ireland.

[pause]

He loved photography, and in those two days he took many pictures of life on board.

[pause]

Children playing on the deck.

[pause]

Passengers walking in the sunshine.

[pause]

The gymnasium, the dining room, his own cabin.

[pause]

Some rich passengers liked him so much that they offered to pay for him to stay on board all the way to New York.

[pause]

But he sent a telegram to his superior, and the answer came back: get off that ship.

[pause]

So he did.

[pause]

His photographs are some of the only pictures we have of life on board Titanic.

[long-pause]

In the early afternoon, the anchor was raised once more.

[pause]

As the ship moved slowly past the green hills of Ireland, a young man in third class played a sad, gentle tune on his pipes, called Erin's Lament.

[pause]

The people from Ireland stood at the railings, and watched the coast of their homeland grow smaller and smaller behind them.

[pause]

Then Ireland was gone.

[pause]

And ahead of Titanic, there was nothing but the wide, grey Atlantic Ocean.
