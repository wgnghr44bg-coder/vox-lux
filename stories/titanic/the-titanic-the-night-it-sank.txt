[pause]

It made linen, and rope, and tobacco.

[pause]

[long-pause]

On the edge of the city, where the River Lagan flows out into a long, calm bay called Belfast Lough, there was a shipyard.

[pause]

It stood on a flat piece of land known as Queen's Island, and it was one of the largest shipyards in the world.

[pause]

Every morning, long before sunrise, thousands of men walked through its gates.

[pause]

[pause]

The sound of their footsteps filled the dark streets like the sound of a river.

At the time of our story, around fifteen thousand people worked at the yard.

[pause]

The yard had its own rhythm.

[pause]

In the morning, a loud horn sounded across the rooftops, calling everyone to work.

[pause]

[pause]

And in the evening, when the horn sounded for the last time, the great river of men flowed out through the gates again, and back into the narrow streets of red brick houses, where their families were waiting for them with supper.

[pause]

On Sundays, the yard was silent.

[pause]

The ships stood still in the grey light, waiting.

[pause]

Her older sister, Olympic, was always a few months ahead.

For a while, Titanic stood alone under the great gantry, growing taller and more complete, week by week.

From the streets of Belfast, people could see her dark hull rising above the rooftops.

[pause]

Families would walk down to the water on a Sunday afternoon, just to look at her.

Everyone in the city knew someone who worked on her.

[pause]

A father, an uncle, a brother, a neighbour.

In a way, she belonged to all of them.

Carpets were rolled out along the corridors, and curtains were hung at the windows.

The most beautiful part of the ship was the grand staircase, in the first class.

There was a restaurant for those who wanted to dine later, or more privately.

For quieter moments, there was a reading and writing room, painted white, with tall windows and soft armchairs.

For the gentlemen, there was a smoking room, panelled in dark mahogany, with coloured glass windows lit from behind.

And for those who liked to keep busy, there was even more.

The second class was simpler, but still very comfortable.

But on Titanic, most of them had small cabins, with white painted walls, and bunks for two, or four, or six people.

[pause]

There was a dining room with long tables, a smoking room, and a general room with benches and a piano, where people could sit together in the evenings.

[long-pause]

It was plain, but it was clean, and warm, and bright.

For many of the passengers, it was the best place they had ever slept.

[long-pause]

Everywhere on the ship, there were small comforts.

Some of the finest rooms had their own private deck, where passengers could sit in the fresh air, hidden from everyone else.

[pause]

There were barbers, and a small shop that sold postcards and souvenirs.

[pause]

There was a hospital, with a doctor, and a small dark room where photographs could be developed.

[pause]

Sailors and firemen, cooks and bakers, waiters and stewards, and even a few young boys who worked as bellboys and lift operators.
