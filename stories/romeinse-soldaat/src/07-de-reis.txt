The journey to Britain is long. Lucius and the other new soldiers for the British legions travel together, under the command of a centurion. They march north through Gaul, along the great Roman roads. Every day, they march about twenty-five kilometres. At night, they sleep in army posts along the road, or in their tents. It takes them more than a month to cross the whole of Gaul.

--

Along the way, Lucius sees the country change. In the south, there were olive trees and vineyards, and the sun was hot. As they go north, the olive trees disappear. The land becomes greener and flatter. There are big forests of oak and beech, and wide fields of wheat. The weather becomes cooler, and it rains more often. The houses have steeper roofs. And the people speak with different accents.

They pass through big cities. Lugdunum, which is now Lyon, the great capital of Gaul, built where two rivers meet. Durocortorum, now Reims, with its wide streets and triumphal arch. And many smaller towns, with their markets and temples and baths. In each town, the soldiers rest for a night, and spend a little of their money in the taverns.

{img:roman-lighthouse-gesoriacum-coast-ships-harbour-grey-sea-cliffs}
At last, they reach the coast, at a port called Gesoriacum, which is now Boulogne, in France. Here, the Roman fleet of Britain has its base. There is a busy harbour, full of ships. And on a hill above the harbour, there is a tall lighthouse, built by the emperor Caligula. At night, a great fire burns at the top, to guide the ships across the sea. Lucius has never seen the sea so grey and wild.

On a clear day, from the cliffs, you can just see a pale line on the horizon. The white cliffs of Britain. Lucius stands on the cliff for a long time, looking at it. To the Romans of the south, Britain was a strange, mysterious island, at the very edge of the world. Beyond it, they believed, there was only the endless ocean.

--

{img:roman-transport-ship-crossing-channel-soldiers-on-deck-waves-sails}
They cross the sea in a big transport ship, with a square sail and a team of rowers. The crossing takes most of a day. The sea is rough, and the ship rolls and pitches. Many of the soldiers are seasick, leaning over the side. Lucius feels sick too, but he holds on, staring at the horizon, as the sailors told him to. Marcus, the big Spaniard, is the sickest of all. He says he would rather fight ten battles than cross the sea again.

In the evening, they arrive at Dubris, which is now Dover, under the white cliffs. There is another lighthouse there, on the cliff top, answering the one in Gaul. Lucius steps off the ship onto the stone quay. His legs are still wobbly. It is raining softly. The air smells of salt and seaweed and wood smoke. He is in Britain.

{img:soldiers-arriving-dover-quay-white-cliffs-rain-lighthouse-evening}
From Dubris, they march west, across the south of Britain, for about two weeks. They pass through Londinium, which is now London. It is already a big, busy city, with a long wooden bridge over the river Thames, a forum, a basilica and warehouses full of goods from all over the empire. Then they march on, through the green countryside, past farms and villas and small towns.

--

At last, they reach the fortress of the Second Legion Augusta, at a place called Isca. Today it is called Caerleon, in Wales. It lies on a river, surrounded by green hills. As they march through the great gate, the soldiers of the legion come out to watch the new arrivals. Some of them shout jokes. Some of them nod in welcome. And Lucius looks around him at the long streets and the stone buildings, and thinks that this will be his home.
