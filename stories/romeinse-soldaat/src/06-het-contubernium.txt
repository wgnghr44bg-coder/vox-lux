In the Roman army, the most important group is the smallest one. It is the contubernium, the eight men who share a tent on the march, and a room in the barracks. They eat together, sleep together, work together and fight together. They are like brothers. And for twenty-five years, they will be the closest thing Lucius has to a family.

--

The eight men also share a mule. The mule carries their leather tent, their cooking equipment and their millstone for grinding grain. It is a patient, grumpy animal, with long ears and a loud voice. Lucius's group has called it Cicero, after a famous Roman speaker who talked too much. Cicero the mule does not like to be hurried. But he is strong, and he never gets lost.

The leader of Lucius's group is a soldier called Titus. He is about thirty-five years old, and has served for fifteen years. He has a scar on his chin and a deep, calm voice. He has been to Germany and to Africa. He is not an officer, but everyone listens to him. He teaches the young soldiers how to survive. How to keep your feet dry. How to light a fire in the rain. How to keep out of trouble with the centurion.

{img:veteran-soldier-scar-chin-teaching-young-soldier-sharpen-sword}
There is Marcus, a big, cheerful man from Spain, who laughs at everything and is always hungry. There is Quintus, from Italy, a thin, serious young man, who can read and write very well, and wants to become a clerk. There is Felix, from North Africa, with dark skin and a beautiful singing voice. And there are two brothers from Gaul, Sextus and Gnaeus, who are always arguing and always together.

The eighth man is a quiet soldier called Aulus. He is from Syria, at the other end of the empire. He has been a soldier for ten years. He does not talk much about himself. But he knows a lot about medicine and herbs. When someone in the group is sick or hurt, Aulus looks after him. He carries a little box of herbs and ointments in his pack.

--

{img:roman-barracks-room-bunks-shields-on-wall-soldiers-relaxing}
In the barracks, the eight men share two small rooms. The front room is for their equipment. Armour, shields, weapons and packs are hung on the walls and stored on shelves. The back room is where they sleep. There are bunk beds along the walls, with straw mattresses and woollen blankets. There is a small hearth, or a brazier, for warmth and cooking. And a little window, high up, which lets in some light.

It is crowded. Eight grown men, with all their things, in two small rooms. It smells of leather, wool, smoke and sweat. There is no privacy at all. But the men get used to it. They learn to put up with each other's habits. Marcus snores. Quintus reads by lamplight late at night. The two brothers argue in the middle of the night. And Felix sings in the morning, whether anyone wants him to or not.

{img:soldiers-sharing-meal-barracks-bowls-bread-lamp-laughter}
They do everything together. They cook their meals together, taking turns. They clean their equipment together. They play dice together in the evenings. When one of them is ill, the others take over his duties. When one of them gets a letter from home, he reads it aloud to the others. And when one of them is sad, the others notice, and find a way to cheer him up.

--

Lucius is the youngest in the group. At first, the others tease him a lot. They call him puer, the boy. They give him the worst jobs, like cleaning the cooking pot and fetching water. But Lucius does not complain. He works hard, and learns quickly. And after a few months, they stop calling him the boy. Titus puts a hand on his shoulder one evening, and says that he is one of them now. Lucius feels warm all over.

In the evenings, when the work is done, the eight men often sit outside their barrack door, on a wooden bench, in the last light of the day. They watch the other soldiers come and go. They talk about the day, about home and about the future. Quintus wants to become a clerk. Marcus wants to open a tavern. Felix wants to see Rome. And Lucius, when they ask him, says that he just wants to see everything.
