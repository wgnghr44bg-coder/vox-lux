A soldier's life is not only work. There is also free time. And the Roman soldiers know very well how to enjoy it. In the evenings, on holidays and on rainy days, they find many ways to pass the time.

--

The most popular pastime is gambling, with dice. The soldiers throw the dice from a little cup, onto the table, and bet small coins on the result. They shout and laugh when they win, and groan when they lose. The officers do not really approve. But they cannot stop it. Dice have been found at almost every Roman fort. Some of them are even weighted, to cheat. Clearly, not every soldier was honest.

They also play board games. One game is like a mixture of chess and draughts. It is played on a board with squares, scratched into a stone or a piece of wood, with little round counters of glass or bone. The players try to trap each other's pieces. Quintus is the best player in the group. He beats everyone. Only Aulus, the quiet Syrian, can sometimes beat him.

{img:roman-soldiers-hunting-deer-forest-dogs-spears-misty-morning}
Another favourite is hunting. The north of Britain is full of wild animals. Deer, wild boar, hares and birds. On free days, the soldiers go out into the forests and hills with spears, nets and hunting dogs. The British hunting dogs are famous. The Romans admire them, and even send them back to Italy. The hunters bring back meat for the cooking pot, and stories for the evening.

One officer at a fort in the north set up an altar to the god Silvanus, the god of the forests, to thank him for a wonderful hunt. He wrote on it that he had caught a wild boar of extraordinary size, which many before him had failed to catch. We can still read his proud words today. Clearly, even two thousand years ago, hunters liked to tell everyone about their biggest catch.

--

{img:saturnalia-feast-soldiers-wearing-felt-caps-gifts-candles-december}
The best holiday of the year is the Saturnalia, in December. It is a festival of the god Saturn, and it lasts for several days. During the Saturnalia, the normal rules are turned upside down. Work stops. Everyone eats, drinks and celebrates. People give each other small gifts, like candles, little clay figures and sweets. Masters serve their slaves at dinner. And everyone wears a soft felt cap, as a sign of freedom.

In the fort, the soldiers decorate their barracks with green branches. They have feasts, with roast meat, wine and cakes. They play games, sing songs and tell jokes. Each group chooses a king of the Saturnalia, by throwing dice. The king can give silly orders to everyone, and they must obey. One year, Marcus is chosen as king. He orders Titus to sing a love song. Titus, red in the face, sings it. Everyone laughs so much that they cannot breathe.

{img:soldiers-gathered-around-storyteller-fire-winter-night-listening}
In the long winter evenings, the soldiers also tell stories. Stories about their homelands, about famous battles, about gods and heroes. Felix tells stories about the deserts of Africa, about lions and camels and cities of white stone. Aulus tells stories about Syria, about the great cities of the east, with their markets full of silk and spices. The two Gaulish brothers tell stories about their village, and argue about every detail.

--

And sometimes there are visitors. Travelling entertainers come to the vicus. Jugglers, acrobats, musicians and actors. Once, a troupe of actors puts on a funny play in the courtyard of the fort, about a greedy old man and a clever slave. The soldiers laugh until they cry. Another time, a man comes with a trained bear, which dances to a flute. The children of the vicus follow him all over the village. Life on the frontier is far from home. But it is never boring.

Some soldiers spend their free time making things. Little carvings of wood or bone. Rings and brooches. Leather pouches and belts. Lucius learns to make small clay lamps, like his father, using clay from the riverbank and a little kiln in the vicus. He makes one for each of his friends, with a different picture on each. Felix gets a lion. Marcus gets a pot of stew. Everyone laughs at that one.
