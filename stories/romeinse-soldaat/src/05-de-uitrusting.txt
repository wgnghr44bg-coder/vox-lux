When Lucius becomes a real soldier, he receives his equipment. Some of it is given to him by the army. But he must pay for it himself, out of his wages, a little each month. So he takes very good care of everything. A Roman soldier's equipment is heavy, well made and very practical. Let us look at it, piece by piece.

--

First, his clothes. He wears a tunic of wool, red or off-white, which comes down to his knees. In the cold north, he will wear two tunics, one on top of the other, and woollen trousers underneath, like the local people. Over his shoulders, he wears a thick woollen cloak, fastened with a brooch. The cloak is also his blanket at night. And around his waist, he wears a belt, decorated with metal plates, with a little apron of leather strips hanging down in front, which jingle as he walks.

On his feet, he wears the famous soldier's boots, called caligae. They are a kind of heavy sandal, made of leather, with many straps, and with iron nails in the soles. The nails make them last longer, and grip the ground. When a group of soldiers marches on a stone road, you can hear the crunch of their boots from far away. In winter, the soldiers stuff the boots with wool, or wear socks underneath.

{img:caligae-hobnailed-roman-boots-close-leather-straps-stone-road}
For armour, Lucius has a body armour made of strips of iron, held together with leather straps and little bronze hooks. It covers his chest, his back and his shoulders. It is flexible, so he can move and bend easily. But it is heavy, about nine kilos. It takes some practice to put on and take off. The soldiers help each other with the buckles. The armour must be kept oiled and polished, or it rusts in the wet British air.

On his head, he wears an iron helmet, with a wide guard at the back to protect his neck, and two hinged plates on the sides to protect his cheeks. There is a ridge across the front, to protect his face from blows. Inside, the helmet is lined with felt, to make it comfortable. On parade days, the soldiers fix a crest of horsehair on top.

--

{img:roman-shield-curved-red-painted-designs-gladius-pilum-display}
His shield is big and curved, like a section of a tube. It is about a metre high, made of thin layers of wood glued together, covered with leather, and painted red. On the front, there is a design, the same for the whole legion, often with lightning bolts or wings. In the middle, there is a round metal boss, which protects the hand. When marching, the shield is protected from the rain by a leather cover.

His weapons are a short sword, called the gladius, which hangs at his right side. A dagger at his left side. And two javelins, called pila. A pilum has a long, thin iron point, attached to a wooden shaft. It is designed so that the iron bends when it hits something, so the enemy cannot throw it back. Lucius keeps his sword very sharp, and very clean.

{img:soldier-pack-on-pole-furca-cooking-pot-tools-bag-marching}
And then there is the pack. On the march, a Roman soldier carries everything he needs on a pole over his shoulder, called a furca. On the pole hang a leather bag with his clothes, a cooking pot, a water bottle, a small sack of grain for three days, a cloak bag, and tools. Every soldier carries a tool for digging and building. A pickaxe, a spade, a saw or a basket for carrying earth. The whole load can weigh more than thirty kilos.

--

Because of these heavy loads, the soldiers were sometimes called Marius's mules, after a famous general who made them carry all their own equipment. The soldiers did not mind the joke very much. They were proud of their strength. And Lucius, after a few months, carries his heavy pack as if it were nothing at all. He even whistles as he marches.
