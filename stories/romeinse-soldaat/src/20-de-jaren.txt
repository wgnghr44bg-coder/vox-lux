The years go by. Lucius serves in the army for twenty-five years in all. He serves at Isca and on the wall, and in other forts in Britain. He marches thousands of kilometres. He builds walls, roads and bridges. He guards gates and towers, in sun and snow. And slowly, he grows older, and wiser, and more experienced.

--

He is promoted. First, he becomes the leader of his contubernium, like Titus was. Then he becomes an optio, the assistant of a centurion. It is an important job. He helps to train the soldiers, check the guards and keep the records. Because he can read and write well, he is very useful. And finally, after almost twenty years, he becomes a centurion himself. He wears the crest from side to side, and carries the vine stick.

As a centurion, Lucius tries to be the kind of officer he would have liked when he was young. Strict but fair. He knows the names of all his soldiers, and something about their families. He teaches them what Titus taught him. How to keep your feet dry. How to light a fire in the rain. How to keep out of trouble. And he never forgets what it was like to be eighteen, far from home, with blisters on his feet.

{img:old-friends-roman-veterans-sitting-bench-sunlight-laughing-together}
The friends of the old contubernium go different ways. Quintus becomes a clerk at the headquarters, as he always wanted. Marcus marries a woman from the vicus, and opens a little tavern after he retires, famous for his bean stew. Felix is sent to another legion, in Germany, and writes letters for many years. The two Gaulish brothers go home to Gaul together, still arguing. And Aulus becomes a medical orderly in the hospital, helping the doctors.

Titus retires after twenty-five years, when Lucius has served for ten. On his last day, the whole contubernium gives him a farewell feast. Titus gives Lucius his old dagger, as a gift. He says that Lucius will make a good soldier, and a good man. Then he walks out of the gate, with his pack on his shoulder, and settles on a small farm not far away. Lucius visits him every year, until the old man passes away peacefully, in his sleep, at a great age.

--

{img:discharge-ceremony-veteran-receiving-reward-officers-standards}
And at last, after twenty-five years, Lucius's own service comes to an end. He is forty-three years old. There is a ceremony in the courtyard of the fortress. He receives his honourable discharge, and a large sum of money, about ten years' pay. It is enough to buy a good piece of land, and to live comfortably for the rest of his life. His name and his good service are written down in the records of the legion, so that nobody can ever doubt that he served Rome well.

Now, at last, he can officially marry Brica. They have been together for more than twenty years. Their son Gaius is grown up, and has joined the army himself. They also have a daughter, called Lucia, with red hair like her mother. There is a small wedding, in the vicus, with all their friends. Marcus cooks. Quintus writes the marriage contract. And Lucius and Brica stand together, a little grey, a little wrinkled, and very happy.

{img:retired-veteran-farm-britain-green-hills-wife-grandchildren-evening}
Lucius does not go back to Gaul. He has been away too long. Britain is his home now. With his money, he buys a small farm near the wall, with fields, a few cows, some sheep and an orchard. He builds a house in the Roman style, with a tiled roof and a little bath. He plants an olive tree in a pot, by the door, to remind him of Nemausus. It never grows very well, in the British weather. But he looks after it anyway.

--

In the evenings, he sits in front of his house with Brica, looking out over the green hills. On the horizon, he can see the long white line of the wall, which he helped to build, running across the land. His grandchildren play in the orchard. The sun goes down. And Lucius thinks about the young man who left Nemausus long ago, with his grandfather's belt buckle. He has had a good life. A soldier's life. And he would not change any of it.
