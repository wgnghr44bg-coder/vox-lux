Around the year 120, the Roman Empire is at its greatest. It stretches from Britain in the north to Egypt in the south, and from Spain in the west to Mesopotamia in the east. About sixty million people live inside its borders. They speak many languages, and worship many gods. And all of them are protected by the Roman army.

--

The emperor at this time is called Hadrian. He has just come to power, in the year 117. He is a thoughtful, restless man, about forty years old, with a short beard. He was the first emperor to wear a beard, and soon all the fashionable men of Rome copied him. Hadrian loves Greek culture, architecture and poetry. And he has decided that the empire is big enough. He does not want to conquer new lands. He wants to protect the lands Rome already has.

So the army's main job, in Hadrian's time, is to guard the borders. Along the edges of the empire, there is a long line of forts, watchtowers, roads and walls. Soldiers live in these forts all their lives. They watch the frontier, patrol the roads, collect taxes, build bridges and keep the peace. It is a long, quiet, careful job.

{img:roman-legion-on-parade-standards-eagle-rows-of-soldiers-sunlight}
The Roman army has two main kinds of soldiers. First, there are the legionaries. They are the heart of the army. To become a legionary, you must be a Roman citizen. There are about thirty legions in the whole empire. Each legion has about five thousand men. They are heavy infantry, well trained, and well equipped. They are also the best builders and engineers in the world.

Second, there are the auxiliaries. They are soldiers from the conquered peoples of the empire, who are not yet Roman citizens. They come from many lands. Spain, Gaul, Germany, Syria, North Africa. Some are archers, some are horsemen, some are foot soldiers. They serve for twenty-five years. And when they retire, they receive a great reward. They become Roman citizens, and so do their children.

--

{img:centurion-crest-helmet-vine-stick-inspecting-soldiers-line}
A legion is divided into smaller groups. The smallest group is the contubernium, eight men who share a tent and a room. Ten of these groups make a century, eighty men, led by an officer called a centurion. Six centuries make a cohort. And ten cohorts make a legion. At the head of the legion, there is a commander, called a legate. And the symbol of the legion is a golden eagle on a pole, which the soldiers guard with their lives.

The centurions are the most important officers. They are tough, experienced soldiers, who have risen from the ranks. They wear a helmet with a crest from side to side, so their men can see them. And they carry a stick made of vine wood, as a sign of their rank. Some centurions are kind and fair. Some are very strict. Every soldier hopes for a good centurion.

{img:roman-soldiers-building-road-stones-levelling-engineers-countryside}
Roman soldiers do not only fight. In fact, they spend much more time building. They build forts, walls, roads, bridges, aqueducts and canals. Many of the roads they built are still used today, or still lie under modern roads. A Roman legion is like a moving town, with carpenters, blacksmiths, surveyors, doctors and clerks. Everything the army needs, it can make itself.

--

In Britain, around the year 120, there are three legions. One is at Isca, in the west, near what is now Wales. One is at Deva, which is now the city of Chester. And one is at Eboracum, which is now York. Between them and around them, there are dozens of smaller forts, with auxiliary soldiers. Altogether, about fifty thousand soldiers guard the province of Britain. It is one of the most heavily guarded parts of the whole empire.
