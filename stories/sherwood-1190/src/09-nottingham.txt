Once a month, Hugh takes his charcoal to Nottingham, to sell at the market. It is a long journey, about thirty kilometres, through the forest. He leaves before dawn, with the pony and the cart. And one day in the summer, he takes Wat and Alys with him. It is the first time Alys has ever been to a town. She is so excited that she cannot sleep the night before.

--

They walk south through the forest, along the old road. The road is just a track of earth and sand, with ruts from the cart wheels. It winds through the oak woods and across the heaths. They meet other travellers. A pedlar with a pack on his back. Two monks on mules. A farmer driving some sheep to market. A group of pilgrims, singing as they walk. Everyone greets each other, and asks for news.

In the late morning, they come out of the forest, onto a hill. And there, below them, is Nottingham. A town of about two or three thousand people, with houses of timber and thatch, crowded together, and church towers rising above them. A wall surrounds the town. And on a great rock of red sandstone, at one end of the town, high above the river Trent, there stands the castle. It is the biggest building Alys has ever seen.

{img:nottingham-market-square-1190-stalls-crowds-animals-church-tower}
Nottingham Castle is a royal castle. It belongs to the king. It was built by William the Conqueror, more than a hundred years before, and has been made bigger and stronger since. It stands on the rock, with steep cliffs on two sides. Its walls and towers are of stone. It is one of the strongest castles in England. The sheriff of Nottingham lives there, and rules the county for the king.

Under the castle, and under the whole town, there are caves. The rock is soft sandstone, easy to dig. So for hundreds of years, the people of Nottingham have dug caves into it. They use them as cellars, for storing food and ale. As workshops. Even as homes. There are hundreds of caves, under the houses and the streets. An old name for the town meant the place of caves.

--

{img:sandstone-caves-under-nottingham-cellars-barrels-lantern-light-tunnels}
The market of Nottingham is held in a big open square, in the middle of the town. It is the biggest market square in England. On market days, it is crowded with stalls and people. Farmers sell grain, cheese, butter, eggs and vegetables. Butchers sell meat. Weavers sell cloth. Potters sell pots. There are pedlars, jugglers and musicians. Animals are bought and sold. Sheep, pigs, cattle, horses and geese.

Hugh sells his charcoal to a blacksmith, who has a forge near the market. He gets a good price. With the money, he buys salt, a new iron knife, some cloth for Agnes, and a little bag of dried figs, which have come all the way from the warm countries of the south. Figs are a rare treat. Alys has never tasted one. She eats it very slowly, with her eyes closed.

{img:girl-tasting-fig-market-stall-wide-eyes-father-smiling-medieval}
They walk through the town. Alys stares at everything. The tall houses. The busy streets. The shops with their signs. The church of St Mary, on its hill, with its tall tower. The river Trent, wide and slow, with boats and barges carrying goods. And always, above everything, the great castle on its rock, with the banner of the king flying from its tower.

--

On the way home, in the evening, Alys falls asleep on the cart, among the empty charcoal sacks, holding her bag of figs. Wat walks beside his father, leading the pony. The forest is quiet and golden in the evening light. Hugh tells Wat that the sheriff of Nottingham is a powerful man, and that it is best to stay out of his way. Wat nods. He does not know yet that, in the stories, the sheriff of Nottingham will become the most famous villain in England.

Before they leave the town, Hugh takes the children to the church of St Mary, to light a candle. Inside, it is cool and dim, after the bright and noisy market. The candles flicker in front of a painted statue of the Virgin. Alys kneels, and says a prayer for her mother, and for her hedgehog. Wat says a prayer that he may one day become a good archer. And Hugh says a prayer of thanks for the good price he got for his charcoal.
