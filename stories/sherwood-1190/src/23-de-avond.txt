So we leave Sherwood Forest tonight, in the year 1190, as the evening falls. The sun is going down behind the great oaks. Its last golden light comes through the branches in long beams, and lies on the grass of the glades. The deer are coming out of the woods, to graze in the cool of the evening. And the birds are singing their last songs of the day.

--

In the village of Edwinstowe, the smoke is rising from the thatched roofs. The cows have been milked. The chickens are shut in. The pigs are back from the forest, grunting in their sty. In the little church, the priest is lighting a candle. And on the village green, a few children are still playing, until their mothers call them home.

{img:cottage-evening-firelight-family-supper-girl-hedgehog-boy-whittling-bow}
In a small cottage at the edge of the village, a family is sitting around the fire. Hugh is mending his axe. Agnes is spinning. Wat is whittling a new arrow, carefully, with his knife. And Alys is sitting on the floor, with her hedgehog in her lap, telling it a story. About the forest, and the deer, and the great old oaks, and the green man who lives in the deepest woods.

--

Out in the forest, Ralph the forester is riding slowly home, along a quiet path, his horn at his side. The trees are dark around him. An owl glides silently across the path. A fox stops, and looks at him, and trots on. The forester smiles. Everything is as it should be. The deer are safe. The forest is quiet. And soon he will be home, by his own fire.

{img:moonlight-sherwood-oaks-silver-glade-owl-flying-peaceful-night}
The moon rises over Sherwood. It shines through the bare branches and the new leaves, and makes silver patterns on the forest floor. It shines on the charcoal pit in its clearing, where a thin line of blue smoke is rising into the night. It shines on the walls of Rufford Abbey, where the monks are singing Compline. And it shines on the great old oak near Edwinstowe, which is already old, and will grow older still, for a thousand years.

--

The forest is quiet now. The wind whispers softly in the leaves. A stream murmurs over its stones. Somewhere far away, a stag calls, once, and then is silent. And under the great oaks of Sherwood, in the green and silver darkness, all the stories that will ever be told about this forest are waiting, quietly, to be born.
