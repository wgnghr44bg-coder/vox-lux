In the autumn, the forest gives a great gift to the people of Sherwood. Acorns. The great oak trees drop millions of them onto the ground. And the pigs of the villages love acorns more than anything. So every autumn, the pigs are taken into the forest, to eat them. This is called pannage. And it is Wat's favourite time of the year.

--

Every family in the village has a few pigs. They are not like the pink pigs of today. They are smaller, thinner and hairy, with long legs and long snouts, brown or black in colour. They look a little like wild boar. They are tough and clever, and they can find food almost anywhere. In the summer, they eat roots, grass and scraps. But in the autumn, in the forest, they grow fat on acorns and beechnuts.

The village pays a small fee to the king's officials for each pig that goes into the forest. Then Hugh, as the swineherd, gathers all the pigs of the village together, and drives them into the woods. Wat helps him. Every morning, they take the pigs out, and every evening, they bring them back. Hugh has a horn, made from a cow's horn. When he blows it, the pigs come running, because they know it means a little extra food.

{img:swineherd-father-knocking-acorns-down-long-pole-pigs-gathering-below}
Sometimes, Hugh uses a long pole to knock the acorns down from the branches. The pigs crowd around underneath, squealing and pushing, gobbling up the acorns as fast as they fall. It is a funny sight. Alys sometimes comes along to watch, with her hedgehog in a basket. She has given names to all the pigs. Old Grumble. Little Black. Snout. Brownie. And the biggest one, a huge old sow, she calls Queen Matilda.

The pannage season lasts for about two months, from late September to the middle of November. During this time, the pigs grow fat and heavy. In November, the month that the Anglo-Saxons once called the month of sacrifice, some of them will be turned into bacon, ham and sausages, to feed the family through the winter. Alys does not like to think about that. She always cries a little. But she knows that it is the way of things.

--

{img:autumn-forest-sherwood-golden-oaks-beech-trees-leaves-falling-path}
Autumn in Sherwood is very beautiful. The oak leaves turn golden brown. The beech leaves turn copper and red. The birch leaves turn bright yellow. The bracken turns rusty brown. And the air smells of damp earth, fallen leaves and mushrooms. In the mornings, there is mist among the trees, and dew on the spider webs, which shine like strings of pearls in the sun.

Agnes goes into the forest too, at this time of year, to gather food. Hazelnuts, blackberries, crab apples, sloes and rosehips. Mushrooms, which she knows very well, which are good to eat and which are dangerous. Beechnuts, which can be pressed to make oil. And herbs for medicine. She takes Alys with her, and teaches her everything. Alys learns quickly. She has her mother's good eye.

{img:mother-daughter-gathering-hazelnuts-blackberries-forest-baskets-autumn}
The forest is like a great pantry for the people of Sherwood. Even though they may not hunt the king's deer, they can gather nuts, berries, fruit and mushrooms, and catch fish in the streams, if they have permission. Rabbits are not protected in the same way as deer, and the boys set little traps for them. The forest gives them wood for their fires, timber for their houses, bracken for their beds, and food for their animals.

--

One golden autumn evening, Wat and Hugh are bringing the pigs home through the forest. The sun is setting, and its red light shines through the trees. The pigs are trotting along the path, grunting happily, fat and full. And suddenly, in a clearing, Wat sees a great stag, with huge antlers, standing perfectly still, looking at them. For a long moment, nobody moves. Then the stag turns, and walks slowly into the trees, and disappears. Wat says nothing. But he will remember it all his life.
