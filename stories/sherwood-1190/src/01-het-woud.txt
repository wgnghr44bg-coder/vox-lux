Tonight we travel back more than eight hundred years, to the year 1190. We go to the middle of England, to the county of Nottingham. And there, stretching for many kilometres to the north of the town, there is a great royal forest. Ancient oak trees, quiet glades, heathland and little villages. It is called Sherwood. And in the stories, it is the home of the most famous outlaw in the world. Robin Hood.

--

It is early morning in spring. A soft mist lies among the trees. The sun is rising, and its light comes through the branches in long golden beams. Birds are singing everywhere. Blackbirds, thrushes, wrens and robins. A woodpecker is tapping on a dead tree. And in a clearing, a group of deer are grazing quietly, their ears turning this way and that, listening to the sounds of the forest.

The oak trees of Sherwood are very old. Some of them are hundreds of years old already. Their trunks are huge and gnarled, and their great branches spread out wide, like the arms of giants. Under them, the ground is covered with bracken and grass, and in spring, with bluebells, a carpet of soft blue flowers stretching away under the trees. It is one of the most beautiful places in England.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

{img:medieval-village-edge-of-forest-thatched-cottages-smoke-church-spring}
But Sherwood is not an empty wilderness. People live here. In little villages, with thatched houses and small stone churches, surrounded by fields and pastures. They farm the land, keep animals, cut wood and make charcoal. They live in the forest, and from the forest. And they live under special laws, because the forest belongs to the king.

Tonight, we will spend a year in Sherwood Forest, with one of these families. We will see how they live and work, among the great oaks. We will meet the foresters, who guard the king's deer, and the monks of the abbey in the woods. We will visit the town and the castle of Nottingham. And we will learn about the real history of England in the year 1190, when King Richard the Lionheart had just gone away on crusade.

{img:robin-hood-legend-figure-green-hood-bow-oak-tree-forest-calm}
And we will follow the legend of Robin Hood. The outlaw in green, who lived in the forest with his merry men. Who took from the rich and gave to the poor. Who was the best archer in England. We will meet Little John and Friar Tuck, Maid Marian and the Sheriff of Nottingham. And we will ask the old question. Was there ever a real Robin Hood?

--

{img:bluebells-carpet-under-ancient-oaks-sherwood-spring-morning-soft-light}
There are some quarrels and some fights in the stories of Robin Hood. We will see them only from a distance, and gently, as if they were a game. Because most of all, the legend of Robin Hood is about other things. About friendship and fairness. About kindness to the poor. And about the green forest, where people could be free. So let us begin, in the misty morning, under the old oaks of Sherwood, in the year 1190.
