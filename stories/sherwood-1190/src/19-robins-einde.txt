All the old stories of Robin Hood end in the same way. Gently, and a little sadly. Alys does not tell this story very often. Only on quiet evenings, when the grandchildren are older, and ask for it. And she tells it softly, as the fire burns low.

--

Robin Hood grew old. He had lived in the greenwood for many years. He had had many adventures. But now he was tired, and often ill. His hair was grey, and his bones ached in the cold. One day, he felt very unwell. And he decided to go to a priory called Kirklees, in Yorkshire, where his cousin was the prioress, the head of the nuns. She was known as a skilled healer. He hoped she could help him.

Little John went with him, as always. They walked slowly through the forest, to the priory. Robin went inside, to be cared for by his cousin. Little John waited outside. In those days, one of the common treatments for illness was to let a little blood, which people believed could make the body well. The prioress began to treat Robin. But the old stories say that she did not treat him well. And Robin grew weaker, instead of stronger.

{img:old-outlaw-at-window-holding-bow-little-john-beside-him-evening-light}
Robin knew that he did not have long. He blew his horn, softly, three times. And Little John heard it, outside, and came running up the stairs, to his friend's room. He found Robin lying on the bed, pale and weak. Little John knelt beside him, and held his hand. He was crying. And Robin smiled at him, and told him not to be sad.

Then Robin asked for his bow and an arrow. Little John helped him to sit up, by the open window, from which he could see the green forest outside. Robin drew the bow, one last time, with the last of his strength. And he said, where this arrow falls, there you must bury me. Then he let the arrow fly, out of the window, over the trees. And it fell, far away, in a green glade.

--

{img:arrow-flying-out-of-window-over-trees-sunset-green-forest-distance}
Robin lay back, and closed his eyes. And he passed away, peacefully, with his oldest friend beside him, and the green forest outside the window. Little John stayed with him for a long time. Then he carried him out, to the place where the arrow had fallen. And there, under the trees, in the green glade, he buried him, with his bow at his side. As Robin had asked.

The old ballad ends with a few simple words. That Robin Hood lies under a stone, by the high road, near Kirklees. And that he was a good outlaw, and did poor men much good. That is all. It is a quiet ending, for a hero who had so many adventures. But it is a fitting one. He ended his life in the green forest he loved, with his friend at his side.

{img:old-grave-stone-woodland-glade-moss-ferns-quiet-afternoon-light}
Near Kirklees, in Yorkshire, there is still an old grave, in a wood, behind iron railings. It is called Robin Hood's Grave. Nobody knows who is really buried there, if anyone. The stone has an old inscription, which says that here lies Robert, Earl of Huntingdon, and that never an archer was as good as he. But the inscription is not very old, and nobody believes it is real. Still, people have visited the grave for hundreds of years.

--

Alys finishes the story. The fire has burned down to glowing embers. The grandchildren are very quiet. The littlest one has tears in her eyes. Alys hugs her, and says that it is only a story. And that in the stories, Robin Hood never really dies. As long as people tell his stories, he is still there, in the greenwood, under the great oaks, with his bow and his merry men. And the littlest one smiles, and falls asleep in her grandmother's lap.
