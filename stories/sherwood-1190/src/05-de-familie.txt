In the middle of Sherwood Forest, there is a little village called Edwinstowe. It has about twenty houses, a small church, a mill and a village green. Around it, there are fields of oats, barley and rye, and beyond the fields, the great oaks of the forest. The village is very old. The legends say that Robin Hood and Maid Marian were married in its church. But that is a story for later.

--

In one of the cottages at the edge of the village lives a family. The father is called Hugh. He is a charcoal burner, which means that he makes charcoal from wood, in the forest. He is also a swineherd, who looks after the pigs of the village in the forest. He is about thirty-five years old, with a black beard and strong arms, and he knows the forest better than almost anyone.

The mother is called Agnes. She looks after the house, the garden, the chickens and the cow. She brews ale, bakes bread, spins wool and weaves cloth. She knows all the plants of the forest, which ones are good to eat, and which ones can heal. When someone in the village is ill, they come to Agnes for help. She is calm and wise, with grey eyes and a quiet smile.

{img:charcoal-burner-father-son-forest-clearing-smoke-stacked-wood-mound}
They have two children. Wat, who is twelve, and his sister Alys, who is nine. Wat helps his father with the charcoal and the pigs. He is quick and clever, and he loves the forest. He knows every path, every stream and every big tree. He can climb like a squirrel, and move through the woods without making a sound. And he dreams, like all the boys of the village, of becoming a great archer.

Alys helps her mother in the house and the garden. She feeds the chickens, milks the cow, and fetches water from the well. She is a gentle, dreamy girl, who loves animals and flowers. She has a pet hedgehog, which she found in the garden, and which she feeds with worms and snails. She also has a very good memory. She knows all the stories and songs that her grandmother taught her, before she passed away.

--

{img:inside-medieval-cottage-hearth-family-evening-pottage-pot-straw-beds}
The family's cottage is small and simple. It has one room, with walls made of woven branches covered with clay, and a roof of thick straw thatch. In the middle of the floor, there is a hearth, where a fire burns day and night. There is no chimney. The smoke finds its way out through the roof. The family sleeps on straw mattresses, along the walls, under woollen blankets. At one end of the cottage, separated by a low wall, the cow sleeps in winter, keeping everyone warm.

The family is not rich. They have a small strip of land in the village fields, where they grow oats and barley. They have a garden, with cabbages, onions, leeks, beans and herbs. They have a cow, a few chickens and some pigs. And Hugh earns some money from his charcoal, which he sells to the blacksmiths and the townspeople of Nottingham. It is enough to live on, in a good year.

{img:family-walking-into-forest-morning-father-axe-children-basket-dog}
Like most villagers, they owe service to a lord. The land around Edwinstowe belongs partly to the king, and partly to the church. Every year, Hugh must work a few days for the lord, ploughing or harvesting. And he must pay some rent, in money and in kind. A few hens at Christmas. Some eggs at Easter. It is the way of things, in 1190.

--

In the evenings, the family sits around the fire. Hugh mends his tools. Agnes spins. Wat whittles a piece of wood with his knife, trying to make a bow. And Alys tells stories, to her hedgehog, and to anyone who will listen. Old stories, about the forest and its creatures, about brave men and clever women, and about the green man who lives in the deepest part of the woods. Outside, the wind blows through the great oaks. And the forest whispers its own stories, in the dark.

On Sundays, the family goes to the little church of St Mary, in the middle of the village. It is a small stone church, quite new, with thick walls and round arches. The priest is an old man, who has known the family all his life. After Mass, the villagers stand outside on the green, talking about the weather, the crops and the news. And the children run around the churchyard, playing tag among the gravestones.
