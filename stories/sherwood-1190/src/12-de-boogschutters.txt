In the stories, Robin Hood is the greatest archer in England. He can split an arrow in two. He can hit a target from a distance that nobody else can even reach. And all his men are fine archers too. In the Middle Ages, archery was very important in England. And in the villages, men and boys practised it every week.

--

In 1190, the English bow is a simple wooden bow, made from a single piece of wood, with a string of hemp or linen. The best bows are made from yew, a dark evergreen tree, whose wood is strong and springy. Later, in the thirteen hundreds, the English longbow will become famous all over Europe. It will be as tall as a man, very powerful, and the English archers will be the best in the world. But already in 1190, many Englishmen can shoot well.

On Sundays, after church, the men of the village go to the archery butts. These are mounds of earth, at the edge of the village green, with targets on them. The men take turns shooting at the targets, from different distances. They compete with each other, for fun, and sometimes for a small prize. The boys watch, and practise with smaller bows. It is one of the great pleasures of village life.

{img:boy-learning-to-shoot-bow-father-guiding-arms-target-straw}
Wat has been trying to make a bow for a long time. He has carved several, from hazel and ash. But they are not very good. Then Ralph, the forester, gives him a gift. A small bow of yew, which he made himself, when he was a boy. And a few arrows, with goose feathers. Wat cannot believe it. He thanks Ralph again and again. From that day on, he practises every evening, behind the cottage, shooting at a sack of straw.

Shooting a bow well is very difficult. You must stand straight, with your feet apart. You must pull the string back to your ear, with your fingers, not your hand. You must look along the arrow, at the target, and breathe out slowly. And then you must let go, smoothly, without jerking. It takes years of practice to become good. Wat's first arrows go everywhere. Into the hedge. Into the garden. Once, almost into the cow.

--

{img:arrow-fletcher-making-arrows-goose-feathers-workshop-bundle}
Arrows are made by craftsmen called fletchers. The shaft is made of straight wood, like ash or birch. At one end, there are feathers, usually from a goose, which make the arrow fly straight. At the other end, there is a point of iron, made by the blacksmith. A good arrow is a work of art. It must be perfectly straight, perfectly balanced, and the feathers must be set at exactly the right angle.

Hugh is a good archer too. Better than Wat expected. One Sunday, at the butts, he hits the centre of the target three times in a row. The other men cheer. Wat is amazed. He did not know his father could shoot like that. Hugh laughs, and says that he practised a lot when he was young. And that a charcoal burner needs good eyes, to watch the smoke.

{img:sunday-archery-contest-village-green-crowd-cheering-target-hit}
Wat practises all summer. Every evening, until it gets dark. Slowly, his arrows begin to hit the sack of straw. Then the middle of the sack. Then a small mark that he has painted on it. By the autumn, he can hit the mark almost every time. Ralph comes to watch him one evening, and nods. Good, he says. Very good. Keep practising. And never shoot at the king's deer.

--

Wat promises. He will never shoot at the king's deer. But he dreams, at night, of the great archery contests in the stories. Of shooting against the best archers in England, in front of a great crowd. And of winning the prize, a golden arrow, or a silver one, from the hand of a lady. Like Robin Hood.

Alys watches her brother practise, sitting on the garden wall with her hedgehog. Sometimes she fetches his arrows for him, from the hedge and the garden. And once, when he is not looking, she tries the bow herself. It is very hard to pull. But she gets the arrow to fly a little way, and land in the cabbages. She tells nobody. But secretly, she is very proud.
