Near the Ishtar Gate, inside the inner wall, by the river, there is the king's palace: the Southern Palace.

It is enormous, a whole city, inside the city, with five great courtyards, one after another, from east to west, each one surrounded by rooms, and halls, and offices, and storerooms, and apartments, for the king, and his family, and his officials, and his servants, and his guards.

--

In the middle of it, on the south side of the great central courtyard, there is the throne room: a huge hall, more than fifty metres long, and seventeen metres wide, with three great doorways, onto the courtyard. And on the outside wall, facing the courtyard, there is a wonderful decoration, of glazed bricks: on a deep blue background, tall columns, like stylised palm trees, with yellow, and white, capitals, and bands of flowers, and below, a row of lions, walking, in yellow, and gold.

Inside, at the back of the hall, opposite the main doorway, there is a niche, in the wall, where the king's throne stands, and there, the king sits, to receive ambassadors, and officials, and visitors, from all over his empire, and beyond.

{img:ambassadors-bowing-throne-room-babylon-gifts-tribute-scribes}
The ambassadors come, from Egypt, and from Lydia, and from the Medes, and from the cities of Phoenicia, on the coast, and from Arabia, with their gifts, and their messages, and their servants, and they are led through the great courtyards, one after another, each one grander than the last, until they come to the throne room, and see the king, sitting, in the shadows, at the end of the long hall, in his robes, and his tall crown, surrounded by his officials, and his guards.

--

The palace is also the centre of government. Here, the king's scribes write his letters, and his orders, and his decrees, and keep the accounts, of the whole empire, on clay tablets, stored, in great archives. Here are the treasuries, full of gold, and silver, and precious things, brought, as tribute, and as gifts. And here are the storerooms, full of barley, and oil, and dates, for the king's household, and the many people who depend on him.

In another palace, nearby, the kings of Babylon keep a collection of old statues, and inscriptions, and treasures, from earlier times, and other lands, because they love the past, and want to understand it. Some people call it one of the first museums in the world.

{img:palace-garden-courtyard-babylon-evening-pool-palms-lamps-quiet}
In the evenings, when the business of the day is done, the palace grows quiet. The ambassadors go to their lodgings. The scribes go home. The lamps are lit, along the corridors, and in the courtyards, and the king walks, perhaps, on the walls, above the river, in the cool breeze, and looks out over his city, and the great tower, and the temples, and the river, glowing, in the sunset, and the palm groves, beyond the walls, stretching away, to the edge of the world.
