Babylon lies in a land called Mesopotamia. In Greek, that means the land between the rivers.

The two rivers are the Tigris and the Euphrates. They start in the mountains far to the north. They flow south for more than a thousand kilometres across the plains. Near Babylon they come close together. Then they flow on to the sea. Today most of this land is in the country of Iraq.

--

It is a flat land of rich brown soil. The rivers brought it down over thousands of years. But it hardly ever rains here. The summers are long and very hot and dry. Without the rivers, nothing would grow.

But with the rivers, everything grows. For thousands of years, people here have dug canals. These are channels that carry river water out to the fields and gardens and palm groves. So along the rivers and the canals, the land is green and rich. There are fields of barley and wheat, gardens of vegetables, and great forests of date palms. But the canals must be kept clean. If they fill with mud, the fields will dry and die.

{img:ancient-sumerian-city-ruins-ziggurat-ur-reed-marsh-boats}
This is one of the places where cities began.

--

More than five thousand years ago, here in the south, a people called the Sumerians built the first cities in the world. They had temples and walls and kings. They invented writing, the first writing in the world, pressed into clay with a reed. They made the wheel and the plough and the sailboat. They wrote the first laws and the first great stories.

By the year 600 before Christ, all this is already ancient history. The Sumerians are long gone. Their cities are old, and some are ruins. But the Babylonians remember them and honour them. They copy their writings and prayers. And they still use their old language in the temples, a bit like Latin is used in some churches.

{img:reed-marshes-southern-mesopotamia-reed-houses-canoe-water-buffalo}
In the far south, near the sea, there are great marshes of reeds and water. People live there in houses made of reeds. They travel in canoes and fish and keep water buffalo, as they have for thousands of years.

--

And in the middle of this ancient land, on the river Euphrates, lies Babylon. It has been here for more than fifteen hundred years. Long ago it was the capital of a great king called Hammurabi. He wrote down one of the first big sets of laws in the world. Since then, Babylon has been taken and destroyed and rebuilt, again and again.

And now a new family of kings has made it great once more. Greater than it has ever been.
