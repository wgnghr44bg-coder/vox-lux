Of all the wonders of Babylon, the most famous is one that may never have existed. The Hanging Gardens.

Greek and Roman writers, centuries later, told a beautiful story. They said King Nebuchadnezzar had a wife who was a princess from the mountains to the north-east. Her land was green, with mountains and forests and streams.

--

When she came to Babylon to marry the king, she was homesick. The land was flat and hot and brown. She missed the green hills and the trees of home. So the king built her a mountain in the middle of his city. It was a great garden rising up in steps, one above another, like a green hill. It was planted with trees and flowers from all over the empire. Machines lifted water from the river to the top. From there it flowed down through the steps in streams and waterfalls.

From below, the trees seemed to hang in the air above the city. So people called them the Hanging Gardens of Babylon. The Greeks counted them among the Seven Wonders of the World. Together with the Pyramids of Egypt and other great works.

{img:terraced-garden-palace-babylon-cedar-trees-pomegranates-queen-walking}
It is a lovely story. But there is a mystery.

--

The Babylonians wrote down so much on their clay tablets. But they never mention the Hanging Gardens. Nebuchadnezzar wrote so proudly about all his buildings. But he never says a word about them. And archaeologists have dug in Babylon for more than a hundred years. They have never found them for certain.

So where are they? Some scholars think the gardens were real but were close to the river, and have been washed away. Some think a strange building found in the palace, with arches and wells, might have been part of them. Others think the story is really about another city further north. There, about a hundred years earlier, a king built a famous garden on a hill. It was watered by great canals and machines. Maybe later writers mixed up the two cities.

{img:assyrian-garden-relief-style-trees-aqueduct-hillside-water-channels}
And others think the gardens were never really there at all. Perhaps they were only a dream. A legend of a beautiful green impossible garden in the desert.

--

But there certainly were gardens in Babylon. The kings and the nobles and the temples had gardens and orchards. They had palms and fruit trees and flowers and pools. And perhaps somewhere in the palace by the river there was a garden on a high terrace. A homesick queen could walk there in the evening in the shade of the trees. She could smell the flowers and look out over the walls toward the north-east, where her mountains were.

So we will leave the Hanging Gardens as a beautiful mystery. Green and cool, floating above the city in the dreams of the world, as they have for more than two thousand years.
