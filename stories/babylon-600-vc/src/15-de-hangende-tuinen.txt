Of all the wonders of Babylon, the most famous is one that may never have existed: the Hanging Gardens.

The Greek, and Roman, writers, centuries later, told a beautiful story. They said that King Nebuchadnezzar had a wife, a princess, called Amytis, from the land of the Medes, in the mountains of Iran, to the north-east, a green land, of mountains, and forests, and streams.

--

When she came to Babylon, to marry the king, she was homesick, in the flat, hot, brown land, and missed the green hills, and the trees, of her homeland. And so, the king built her a mountain, in the middle of his city: a great garden, rising up, in terraces, one above another, like a green hill, planted with trees, and flowers, and plants, from all over the empire, and watered by machines, which lifted the water, from the river, up to the top, so that it could flow down, through the terraces, in streams, and waterfalls.

From below, the trees seemed to hang in the air, above the city. And so they were called the Hanging Gardens of Babylon, and the Greeks counted them, among the Seven Wonders of the World, with the Pyramids of Egypt, and the great statue of Zeus, at Olympia, and the others.

{img:terraced-garden-palace-babylon-cedar-trees-pomegranates-queen-walking}
It is a lovely story. But there is a mystery.

--

The Babylonians themselves, who wrote down so much, on their clay tablets, and in their inscriptions, never mention the Hanging Gardens. Nebuchadnezzar, who wrote so proudly about all his buildings, never says a word about them. And the archaeologists, who have dug in Babylon, for more than a hundred years, have never found them, for certain.

Some scholars think that the gardens were real, but have simply not been found, or were near the river, where everything has been washed away. Some think they were in Nebuchadnezzar's palace, where archaeologists found a strange building, with vaults, and wells, which might have been part of them. Others think that the story is about another city, Nineveh, in the north, the capital of the Assyrians, where King Sennacherib, about a hundred years before, built a famous garden, on a hill, watered by great canals, and aqueducts, and machines, and that later writers mixed up the two cities.

{img:assyrian-garden-relief-style-trees-aqueduct-hillside-water-channels}
And others think that the gardens were never really there at all, but were a dream, a legend, of a beautiful, green, impossible garden, in the desert.

--

But there were certainly gardens in Babylon. The kings, and the nobles, and the temples, had gardens, and orchards, and parks, with palms, and fruit trees, and flowers, and pools, and the Babylonians loved them. And perhaps, somewhere, in the palace, beside the river, there was a garden, on a terrace, high above the city, where a homesick queen could walk, in the evening, in the shade of the trees, and smell the flowers, and look out, over the walls, toward the north-east, where her mountains were.

So we will leave the Hanging Gardens, as a beautiful mystery, floating, green, and cool, above the city, in the imagination of the world, as they have for more than two thousand years.
