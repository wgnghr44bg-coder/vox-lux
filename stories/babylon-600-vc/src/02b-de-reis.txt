Let us imagine that we are travellers, coming to Babylon, for the first time, in the year 600 BC.

We have come from the north, down the river Euphrates, on a boat, carrying wine, and timber, from the mountains, for many days. We have slept on the deck, under the stars, and watched the land go by: the steep banks, and the villages, of mud brick, and the fields, and the palm groves, and the herds of sheep, and goats, coming down to drink, at the river's edge, and the boys, fishing, from the banks, who wave, as we pass.

--

As we come further south, the land becomes flatter, and greener, and the canals more numerous, branching off from the river, in every direction, and the villages, closer together, and larger, and the river busier, with boats, and rafts, and the round quffas, spinning slowly, in the current.

And one morning, early, the boatman points ahead, down the river. And there, on the horizon, rising out of the morning haze, above the palm trees, we see it: a great stepped tower, standing up, against the sky, its top shining, blue, in the first sunlight. The ziggurat of Babylon.

{img:approaching-babylon-river-view-walls-towers-ziggurat-haze-boats}
As we come closer, we see the walls: long, and high, and brown, stretching away, on both sides of the river, with towers, all along them, hundreds of them, like the teeth of a great comb, and the smoke rising, from the city, behind them, and the tops of the temples, and the palms, in the gardens.

--

The boat comes in, to the quay, outside the walls, where dozens of other boats are tied up, and unloading, and the porters are carrying the jars, and the bales, and the sacks, on their backs, up the steps, and the scribes are counting, and writing, on their tablets, and the officials are collecting the tolls, and everyone is shouting, in a dozen languages.

We climb up, onto the quay, with our bags, a little unsteady, after so many days on the water, and walk, with the crowds, toward the nearest gate, in the great wall. We pass the guards, at the gate, who glance at us, and wave us through. And we walk, through the long, dark, cool passage, under the gate, and out, into the city.

{img:traveller-first-view-babylon-street-crowds-temples-amazed-sunlight}
And there is Babylon, all around us: the noise, and the crowds, and the smells, of bread, and smoke, and spices, and donkeys, and the narrow streets, between the high, blank walls, of the houses, and the temples, rising, above the rooftops, and, at the end of a long street, the great tower, standing, enormous, against the blue sky, so much bigger, now that we are close, than we had ever imagined.

--

We stand still, for a moment, in the middle of the street, with the people pushing past us, and stare, like every traveller who has ever come to Babylon, and then, we go on, to find a place to stay, and to begin to explore the greatest city in the world.
