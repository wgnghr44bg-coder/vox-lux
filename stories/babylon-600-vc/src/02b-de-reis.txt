Let us imagine we are travellers coming to Babylon for the first time.

We have come from the north, down the river, on a boat carrying wine and wood from the mountains. It has taken many days. We have slept on the deck under the stars. We have watched the land go by. Steep banks and mud brick villages. Fields and palm groves. Sheep and goats coming down to drink. And boys fishing from the banks, who wave as we pass.

--

As we go further south, the land gets flatter and greener. Canals branch off from the river in every direction. The villages get closer together and bigger. The river gets busier, with boats and rafts and round baskets that spin slowly in the current.

And one morning, early, the boatman points ahead down the river. There, on the horizon, rising out of the haze above the palm trees, we see it. A great stepped tower standing against the sky. Its top shines blue in the first sunlight. The tower of Babylon.

{img:approaching-babylon-river-view-walls-towers-ziggurat-haze-boats}
As we come closer, we see the walls. They are long and high and brown. They stretch away on both sides of the river. There are towers all along them, hundreds of them, like the teeth of a great comb. Behind them, smoke rises from the city. We see the tops of the temples and the palms in the gardens.

--

Our boat comes in to the quay outside the walls. Dozens of other boats are tied up there and unloading. Porters carry jars and sacks up the steps on their backs. Writers count everything and write it down on clay tablets. Officials collect the fees. And everyone is shouting in a dozen languages.

We climb up onto the quay with our bags. We are a little unsteady after so many days on the water. We walk with the crowds toward the nearest gate in the great wall. The guards look at us. Will they let us in? They glance at our bags. Then they wave us through. We walk through the long dark cool passage under the gate. And out into the city.

{img:traveller-first-view-babylon-street-crowds-temples-amazed-sunlight}
And there is Babylon, all around us. The noise and the crowds. The smells of bread and smoke and spices and donkeys. The narrow streets between the high blank walls of the houses. Temples rise above the roofs. And at the end of a long street stands the great tower. It is enormous against the blue sky. Much bigger, now that we are close, than we had ever imagined.

--

We stand still for a moment in the middle of the street. People push past us. And we stare, like every traveller who has ever come to Babylon. Then we go on to find a place to stay. And we begin to explore the greatest city in the world.
