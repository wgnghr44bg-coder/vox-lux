In the heart of Babylon stands the great tower. It is a ziggurat, a tower built in steps.

Its name means the temple of the foundation of heaven and earth. It is built of mud brick and baked brick. It rises in levels, one on top of another. Each level is smaller than the one below. It is like a great staircase to the sky.

--

It stands in its own great courtyard beside the temple of Marduk, near the river. Its base is square, about ninety metres on each side. It may be just as tall. That is as tall as a building of thirty floors. In its time, it is the tallest building in the whole world.

On the front, a great triple stairway leads up from the courtyard. And on the very top stands a small temple for the god Marduk. It is covered with blue shining bricks. It glows in the sun high above the city. You can see it from many kilometres away across the flat plain.

The tower is very old. There has been a tower here for more than a thousand years. It has been rebuilt and repaired again and again. The king's father began to rebuild it. Now Nebuchadnezzar is finishing it. He writes that he raised its top to rival the heavens.

--

Many people believe this tower is the Tower of Babel in the Bible. In that story, people try to build a tower up to heaven. Then God mixes up their languages. Suddenly they cannot understand each other anymore. They stop building and scatter over the earth. Babel is the Hebrew name for Babylon.

And in the streets of Babylon you can indeed hear many languages. Babylonian and Aramaic and Hebrew. Egyptian and Greek and Persian. All mixed together in the markets and the streets.

Why did the Babylonians build the tower?

--

They believed the gods lived in the heavens. The tower was a bridge or a stairway between heaven and earth. The gods could come down it to visit the city and the people. The little temple on top was a resting place for the god on his way down.

Only the priests could climb to the top. There, high above the city under the stars, they made their offerings and prayers. And they watched the sky.

{img:ziggurat-ruins-babylon-today-low-mound-water-filled-base-reeds}
Today almost nothing is left of the great tower. Over the centuries its bricks were taken away to build other buildings. Only its base remains. It is a square outline in the ground near the ruins, sometimes filled with water and reeds.

--

But we know what it looked like. A Babylonian writer wrote down its measurements on a clay tablet, and it has survived. And a Greek traveller described it more than a hundred years later. He wrote about its many levels, its stairway winding around the outside, and its temple at the top.
