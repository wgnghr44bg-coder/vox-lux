In the heart of Babylon, there is the great tower: the ziggurat.

Its name is Etemenanki, which means, in the ancient Sumerian language, the temple of the foundation of heaven and earth. It is a great stepped tower, built of mud brick, and baked brick, rising up, in levels, one on top of another, each one smaller than the one below, like a great staircase, to the sky.

--

It stands in its own great courtyard, surrounded by walls, beside the temple of Marduk, near the river. Its base is square, about ninety metres on each side, and it is perhaps as tall as it is wide, about ninety metres high, the height of a building of thirty storeys, the tallest building in the whole world, in its time.

On the front, there is a great triple stairway, leading up, from the courtyard, to the first levels. And on the very top, there is a small temple, for the god Marduk, covered with blue glazed bricks, shining, in the sun, high above the city, which can be seen from far away, across the flat plain, for many kilometres.

The ziggurat is very old. There has been a tower here, for more than a thousand years, rebuilt, and repaired, again, and again. The king's father, Nabopolassar, began to rebuild it, and Nebuchadnezzar is finishing it. In his inscriptions, he says that he raised its top, to rival the heavens.

--

Many people believe that the ziggurat of Babylon is the Tower of Babel, in the Bible: the great tower, which the people of the earth began to build, in the land of Shinar, with its top in the heavens, until God confused their languages, so that they could no longer understand each other, and they stopped building, and were scattered, over the face of the earth. Babel is the Hebrew name for Babylon.

And in the streets of Babylon, in the year 600 BC, you can indeed hear many languages: Babylonian, and Aramaic, and Hebrew, and Egyptian, and Greek, and Persian, and Elamite, and the languages of Arabia, and the mountains, all mixed together, in the markets, and the streets.

Why did the Babylonians build the ziggurat?

--

They believed that the gods lived in the heavens, and that the ziggurat was a bridge, or a stairway, between heaven, and earth, where the gods could come down, to visit the city, and the people. The little temple on the top was a place for the god to rest, on his way down.

Only the priests could climb to the top. And there, high above the city, under the stars, they made their offerings, and their prayers, and watched the sky.

{img:ziggurat-ruins-babylon-today-low-mound-water-filled-base-reeds}
Today, almost nothing is left of the great ziggurat of Babylon. Its bricks were taken away, over the centuries, to build other buildings. Only its base remains, a square outline, in the ground, sometimes filled with water, and reeds, near the ruins of the city.

--

But we know what it looked like, because a Babylonian scribe wrote down its measurements, on a clay tablet, which has survived, and because a Greek writer, Herodotus, described it, more than a hundred years later, with its eight levels, and its winding stairway, around the outside, and its temple, at the top.
