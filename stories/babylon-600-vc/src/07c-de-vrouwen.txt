The women of Babylon have more rights and more freedom than women in many other places in the ancient world.

Most women spend their lives as wives and mothers. They run the house and raise the children. They spin and weave and cook and brew. But a Babylonian woman can own things of her own. Houses and fields and silver. She can buy and sell and lend and borrow. She can make contracts and be a witness. She can go to court and win her case.

--

When she marries, her family gives her a gift to take with her. Money and furniture and clothes and jewellery. Sometimes even a house or a field. It stays hers all her life. It is written down carefully on a clay tablet in the marriage contract. If her husband sends her away without a good reason, he must give it all back. And when she dies, it goes to her children.

Many marriage contracts from Babylon have been found. They are quite simple. The husband asks the bride's father for his daughter. The father agrees. The gift is listed. And sometimes there is a promise. If the husband ever takes a second wife, he must pay the first one a lot of silver. And she may go home to her family.

{img:women-weaving-workshop-temple-babylon-looms-wool-supervisor}
Some women work outside the home. They weave in the big workshops of the temples and the palace. They run taverns and brew beer. They are midwives and nurses. They sell things in the streets and markets. And some are writers and singers and musicians and priestesses in the temples.

--

There are businesswomen too. In the old records of Babylon there are women who buy and sell houses. They lend silver and grain. They rent out fields. They manage the family's property when their husbands are away or have passed away. In the papers of one rich family, the wives and mothers appear again and again. They buy and sell and sign and witness.

Women are also respected as mothers and as keepers of the house and the family memory. The Babylonians had a saying. A house without a wife is like a field without a plough.

{img:mother-daughters-babylon-roof-evening-combing-hair-talking-sunset}
In the evening, on the rooftops of Babylon, mothers and daughters and grandmothers sit together. They comb each other's hair. They talk and laugh and share the news of the neighbourhood. They plan the marriages of the young people. And they watch the sun go down over the city.
