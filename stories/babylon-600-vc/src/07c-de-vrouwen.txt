The women of Babylon have more rights, and more freedom, than women in many other places, in the ancient world.

Most women, of course, spend their lives, as wives, and mothers, running their households, and bringing up their children, and spinning, and weaving, and cooking, and brewing. But a Babylonian woman can own property, of her own: houses, and fields, and slaves, and silver. She can buy, and sell, and lend, and borrow. She can make contracts, and be a witness, and go to court, and win her case.

--

When she marries, her family gives her a dowry: money, and furniture, and clothes, and jewellery, and sometimes, a house, or a field, or a servant. And the dowry remains hers, all her life, and is written down, carefully, on a clay tablet, in the marriage contract. If her husband divorces her, without good reason, he must give it back, and sometimes pay her more besides. And when she dies, it goes to her children.

Many marriage contracts, from Babylon, in these years, have been found. They are quite simple. The husband asks the father of the bride, for his daughter, in marriage. The father agrees. The dowry is listed. And sometimes, there is a promise: that if the husband takes a second wife, he must pay the first one a large sum of silver, and let her go home, to her family.

{img:women-weaving-workshop-temple-babylon-looms-wool-supervisor}
Some women work outside their homes. They are weavers, and spinners, in the great workshops of the temples, and the palace. They run taverns, and brew beer. They are midwives, and nurses, and wet-nurses. They sell things, in the streets, and the markets. And some of them are scribes, and singers, and musicians, and priestesses, in the temples.

--

There are businesswomen too. In the archives of Babylon, there are women who buy, and sell, houses, and lend silver, and grain, at interest, and rent out fields, and manage their family's property, when their husbands are away, or have died. In the Egibi family archive, there are wives, and mothers, who appear, again and again, in the business of the family, buying, and selling, and signing, and witnessing.

The women of Babylon are also respected, as mothers, and as the keepers of the house, and the family's gods, and the ancestors' memory. The Babylonians had a saying, that a house without a wife is like a field without a plough.

{img:mother-daughters-babylon-roof-evening-combing-hair-talking-sunset}
And in the evening, on the rooftops of Babylon, the mothers, and the daughters, and the grandmothers, sit together, and comb each other's hair, and talk, and laugh, and tell each other the news, and the gossip, of the neighbourhood, and plan the marriages, of the young people, and watch the sun go down, over the city.
