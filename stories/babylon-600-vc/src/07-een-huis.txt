Let us visit a family, in their house, in Babylon.

They live in the eastern part of the old city, in a neighbourhood called Merkes, not far from the great temple, on a narrow street, which winds between the houses, unpaved, dusty in summer, and muddy, after the winter rains.

--

From the street, the house looks very plain: just a high wall, of mud brick, plastered with mud, without windows, with a single, low wooden door. But inside, it is quite different.

Through the door, there is a little entrance room, and then, the courtyard: an open space, in the middle of the house, paved with baked bricks, open to the sky, with the rooms of the house, all around it. The courtyard is the heart of the house, where the family lives, and works, and cooks, and talks, and the children play, in the shade, and the light.

{img:courtyard-family-weaving-grinding-grain-children-playing-babylon}
Around the courtyard, there are the rooms: a long reception room, on the south side, facing north, so that it stays cool, where the family receives guests, and sits, in the heat of the day. And there are bedrooms, and storerooms, for grain, and oil, and dates, and a kitchen, with a clay oven, and perhaps a little room, for washing, with a drain.

--

The walls are thick, of mud brick, to keep out the heat, and the cold. The rooms are dark, and cool, lit only by the light, from the doors, onto the courtyard. The floors are of beaten earth, or brick, covered with reed mats, and carpets, of wool.

There is not much furniture: low wooden beds, with mattresses, and cushions, and stools, and a little table, and wooden chests, for clothes, and clay jars, for water, and oil, and grain, and baskets, everywhere. At night, the rooms are lit by little clay lamps, burning sesame oil.

{img:flat-roof-babylon-house-ladder-family-sleeping-summer-night-stars}
And on top of the house, there is the flat roof, made of palm trunks, and reed mats, covered with a thick layer of mud, rolled smooth. The family climbs up to the roof, by a ladder, or a little stairway, and uses it as another room: to dry dates, and grain, and washing, in the sun, and to sit, in the cool of the evening, and talk with the neighbours, on the next roofs.

--

And in the summer, when it is very hot, the whole family sleeps on the roof, at night, on mats, under the open sky, in the cool breeze, under the stars. All over Babylon, on summer nights, the families lie on their roofs, and look up at the stars, and talk, quietly, and fall asleep, to the sound of the dogs barking, in the streets, and the frogs, in the canals, and the watchmen, calling the hours, from the walls.

Under the floor of the house, the family buries its dead, in clay coffins, or jars, so that the ancestors are still with them, in the house, and the family gives them offerings, of food, and water, every month, and remembers them.
