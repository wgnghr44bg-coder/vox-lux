Let us visit a family in their house in Babylon.

They live in the east part of the old city, not far from the great temple. Their street is narrow and winding between the houses. It is not paved. It is dusty in summer and muddy after the winter rains.

--

From the street, the house looks very plain. It is just a high wall of mud brick, with no windows. There is a single low wooden door. But inside it is quite different.

Through the door is a little entrance room. Then comes the courtyard. It is an open space in the middle of the house, paved with bricks and open to the sky. The rooms of the house stand all around it. The courtyard is the heart of the house. The family lives and works and cooks and talks there. And the children play there, in the shade and the light.

{img:courtyard-family-weaving-grinding-grain-children-playing-babylon}
Around the courtyard are the rooms. There is a long room on the south side that faces north, so it stays cool. The family welcomes guests there and sits there in the heat of the day. There are bedrooms and store rooms for grain and oil and dates. There is a kitchen with a clay oven. And perhaps a little room for washing, with a drain.

--

The walls are thick, to keep out the heat and the cold. The rooms are dark and cool. The only light comes from the doors onto the courtyard. The floors are beaten earth or brick, covered with reed mats and wool rugs.

There is not much furniture. Low wooden beds with mattresses and cushions. Stools and a little table. Wooden chests for clothes. Clay jars for water and oil and grain. And baskets everywhere. At night, little clay lamps burn with sesame oil.

{img:flat-roof-babylon-house-ladder-family-sleeping-summer-night-stars}
On top of the house is a flat roof. It is made of palm trunks and reed mats covered with thick smooth mud. The family climbs up by a ladder. They use the roof as another room. They dry dates and grain and washing in the sun. And in the cool of the evening they sit and talk with the neighbours on the next roofs.

--

In summer, when it is very hot, the whole family sleeps on the roof at night. They lie on mats under the open sky, in the cool breeze, under the stars. All over Babylon on summer nights, families lie on their roofs. They look up at the stars and talk quietly. And they fall asleep to the sound of dogs barking in the streets, frogs in the canals, and the watchmen calling the hours from the walls.

The family buries its dead under the floor of the house. That way the ancestors are still with them in the house. Every month the family gives them a little food and water. And they remember them.
