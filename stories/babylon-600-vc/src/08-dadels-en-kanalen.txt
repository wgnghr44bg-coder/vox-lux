Around Babylon, along all the rivers and canals, grow the date palms.

They grow in great groves, tall and slender. Their crowns of long green feathery leaves rustle in the wind. Big bunches of dates hang below. In late summer they ripen, orange and red and brown.

--

For the Babylonians, the date palm is the tree of life. Its fruit is sweet and filling. It can be eaten fresh or dried and kept for months. It can be pressed into a sweet syrup like honey. The leaves are woven into baskets and mats and roofs. The fibres become ropes. The trunks become beams and bridges. The stones are burned as fuel or fed to the animals. Nothing is wasted.

Under the palms, in their shade, farmers grow other plants. Pomegranates and figs and grapes. Onions and garlic and lettuce and cucumbers. The palm groves are like great gardens, cool and green and shady in the burning summer heat.

The date palms need help. There are male trees and female trees. Only the female trees give fruit. So in spring, the farmers climb up the tall trunks with a rope around themselves and the tree. It is a long way up. One slip could be dangerous. At the top, they tie a little bunch of flowers from a male tree among the flowers of the female tree. Then the dates will grow. Farmers in Iraq still do this today, in the same way.

--

And everything depends on the canals.

All around Babylon there is a great web of canals, big and small. They carry the river water out to the fields and the palm groves. The big canals are as wide as rivers, and boats sail on them. The small ones are little ditches between the fields. Farmers open and close little gates of earth and reeds. That lets the water into their fields when they need it.

{img:irrigation-canal-shaduf-farmer-lifting-water-bucket-fields-barley}
To lift water from the canal into the field, they use a clever tool. It is a long pole balanced on a post. A bucket hangs on one end. A heavy lump of clay sits on the other end. The farmer dips the bucket into the canal. The weight helps him lift it easily. Then he swings it round and empties it into the field.

--

The canals must be cleaned and repaired every year. The river carries a lot of mud. It settles in the canals and fills them up. So they must be dug out again and again. It is hard work. If a canal is left too long, the fields at the end of it get no water. And the crops dry up.

In the fields, the farmers grow barley above all. It is the main food and drink of Babylon, for bread and beer. They also grow wheat and sesame for oil. They plough with oxen and a wooden plough. On top of the plough sits a funnel. The farmer drops seed into it. The seed falls straight into the ground as the plough moves along. It is a clever invention of this land.

{img:barley-harvest-mesopotamia-sickles-sheaves-donkeys-threshing-floor}
At harvest, in April and May, the barley is cut with curved knives. Donkeys carry it to the threshing floors. There oxen walk round and round on it to shake out the grain. The wind blows away the husks. The grain is measured and put in big sacks. And it is taken to the store rooms of the temples and the families and the king.
