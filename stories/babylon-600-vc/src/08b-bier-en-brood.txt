In Babylon, everyone drinks beer.

It is made from barley, and it is quite different from the beer of today: thick, and cloudy, and sweet, and not very strong, more like a kind of liquid food, than a drink, and it is drunk, every day, by everyone, with every meal, men, and women, and children, and the king, and the gods.

--

To make it, the brewers soak the barley in water, until it sprouts, and then dry it, and grind it, and bake it, into loaves, called beer bread. Then they crumble the loaves into water, in great jars, with dates, or date syrup, for sweetness, and leave it, for a few days, to ferment, and bubble.

The beer is thick, with bits of grain, and husks, floating on the top. So people drink it through long straws, of reed, sometimes decorated with metal, or with a little filter, at the bottom, to keep out the bits. In pictures, and carvings, from Mesopotamia, we see people sitting, around a great jar, all drinking through their long straws, together, and talking.

{img:babylonian-tavern-keeper-woman-serving-beer-jars-evening-lamp}
There are taverns, in Babylon, where people go, in the evening, to drink, and talk, and listen to music. Many of them are run by women, tavern-keepers, who brew the beer themselves. The old laws of Hammurabi, more than a thousand years before, had rules about them, to make sure they sold honest measures, at honest prices.

--

And there is wine, too, from the mountains, in the north, brought down the river, on rafts, which is expensive, and drunk by the rich, and the king, and offered to the gods.

And bread is everywhere. The Babylonians have dozens of kinds of bread, and cakes: flat breads, baked on the walls of the oven, and round loaves, and sweet cakes, made with honey, and dates, and sesame, and butter, and spices, baked in moulds, of clay, in the shapes of animals, and fish, and stars.

{img:clay-baking-moulds-patterned-cakes-dates-honey-kitchen-table}
There is also a very old collection of recipes, from Mesopotamia, written on clay tablets, more than a thousand years before our story, the oldest cookbook in the world. The recipes are for stews, and broths, of lamb, and mutton, and pigeon, and duck, cooked with water, and fat, and onions, and leeks, and garlic, and herbs, and salt, and crushed grain, and some of them have been cooked, again, by scholars, and cooks, in modern times, to taste what the food of Mesopotamia was like.

--

In the evening, in the houses of Babylon, the smell of baking bread, and onions, and garlic, and stew, and wood smoke, drifts out, over the rooftops, and the streets, and the families gather, around their meals, in their courtyards, and on their roofs, and drink their beer, through their straws, and talk, about the day.
