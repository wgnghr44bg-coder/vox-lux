Nebuchadnezzar rules for forty-three years. He passes away as an old man.

After him, Babylon has several kings in a short time. The last king of Babylon is an unusual man who loves old things and the moon god. He spends ten years far away in the deserts of Arabia. He leaves his son to rule in Babylon. And far to the east, a new power is growing.

--

About sixty years after our story, Cyrus the Great, king of the Persians, comes with his army from the east. Babylon is taken quickly, almost without a fight. It is said the people welcome Cyrus with green branches. He enters the city in peace. He honours Marduk and the temples. And he lets the people live as before.

On a clay cylinder, now in a museum in London, Cyrus tells his story. He says Marduk chose him to be king of Babylon. And he says he let the peoples who had been brought to Babylon go home, with their gods.

{img:alexander-the-great-babylon-palace-323bc-quiet-evening-columns}
Babylon becomes one of the capitals of the Persian Empire. It stays rich and great for two hundred years. Then Alexander the Great comes and takes it from the Persians. He plans to make it the capital of his own great empire, from Greece to India. But he falls ill with a fever in Babylon, in the old palace of Nebuchadnezzar. And he passes away there, at only thirty-two.

--

After Alexander, his generals build new cities. Slowly people move away from Babylon. The old temples are still kept. The star watchers still watch the sky and write their diaries on clay, for another two hundred years. The last clay tablet in the old writing that we know of was written about seventy-five years after Christ. After that, the old writing was forgotten.

Slowly, over the centuries, Babylon fades. The river changes its course. The canals fill with mud. The great buildings crumble. People take the bricks away to build new houses in new villages. And the great city becomes a field of ruins and mounds and broken bricks in the desert, beside the river.

{img:ruined-mounds-babylon-desert-broken-bricks-palms-medieval-traveller}
Now and then over the centuries, travellers came and looked at the mounds. Could this really be the great Babylon of the Bible and the old stories? Local people dug in the ruins for bricks. They found the stamped bricks of Nebuchadnezzar. They used them in their houses and walls and dams. So the king's name was built into houses all around the region.

--

But Babylon was not forgotten. Its name lived on in the Bible. It lived on in the writings of the Greeks and Romans. In poems and stories and paintings and songs all over the world, for two thousand years. A name of wonder and splendour and mystery. Waiting under the earth to be found again.
