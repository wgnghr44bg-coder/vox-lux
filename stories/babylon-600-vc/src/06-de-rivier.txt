Through the middle of Babylon flows the river Euphrates.

It divides the city in two: the old city, on the east bank, with the great temples, and the palaces, and the ziggurat, and the new city, on the west bank, with its own temples, and houses, and gardens. And to join them, Nebuchadnezzar's father, and Nebuchadnezzar, have built a great bridge.

--

It is one of the first great bridges of the ancient world: about a hundred and twenty metres long, resting on piers, of baked brick, and stone, shaped like boats, pointed at the front, so that the water flows past them, smoothly, without damaging them. The road across the bridge is made of wooden beams, of cedar, and palm, laid across the piers, which can be taken away, at night, so that nobody can cross, in the dark.

Along the river, inside the city, there are quays, of baked brick, with steps, leading down, to the water, where the boats tie up, and the people come, to fetch water, and wash, and bathe.

{img:round-coracle-quffa-boats-river-euphrates-traders-cargo-reeds}
And on the river, there are boats of every kind.

--

There are long, narrow boats, of wood, with sails, and oars, carrying goods, up and down the river, from the sea, and from the north. There are rafts, made of logs, floated on inflated goatskins, carrying stone, and timber, and wine, from the mountains, down the river. And there are the round boats, called quffas: big round baskets, woven of reeds, or willow, and covered with leather, or coated with bitumen, the black, sticky tar, which seeps up from the ground, in Mesopotamia, to make them waterproof.

The Greek writer Herodotus describes them, with wonder: round, like a shield, and carrying great loads, wine, in palm-wood casks, and a live donkey, or two, which are taken along, so that, when the boat arrives, and has been unloaded, it can be taken apart, and the leather loaded on the donkeys, and carried back, up the river, by land, because the current is too strong to row against.

{img:fishermen-casting-nets-euphrates-dawn-reeds-herons-quiet}
In the river, there are fish, and the fishermen go out, at dawn, in their little boats, and cast their nets, and come back, with carp, and catfish, and barbel, for the market. And there are birds: herons, and egrets, and ducks, and pelicans, and kingfishers, in the reeds, along the banks.

--

But the river can also be dangerous. Every spring, when the snow melts, in the mountains, far to the north, the river rises, and sometimes floods, over its banks, across the land, and into the city. The Babylonians build their houses on platforms, and raise their temples on great terraces, and keep their canals, and dykes, in good repair, and pray to the gods, to keep the floods away.

And in the evening, as the sun sets, behind the palms, on the west bank, the river glows, red, and gold, and the boats come home, and the people sit on the quays, and on the roofs, in the cool breeze, from the water, and watch the light fade, over the great, slow river.
