Through the middle of Babylon flows the river Euphrates.

It divides the city in two. On the east bank is the old city, with the great temples and palaces and the tower. On the west bank is the new city, with its own temples and houses and gardens. To join them, the king has built a great bridge.

--

It is one of the first great bridges of the ancient world. It is about a hundred and twenty metres long. It rests on thick pillars of brick and stone. The pillars are shaped like boats, pointed at the front, so the water flows past them smoothly. The road across the bridge is made of wooden beams. At night the beams can be taken away. That way nobody can cross in the dark.

Along the river inside the city there are brick quays with steps down to the water. Boats tie up there. People come down to fetch water and wash and bathe.

{img:round-coracle-quffa-boats-river-euphrates-traders-cargo-reeds}
And on the river there are boats of every kind.

--

There are long narrow wooden boats with sails and oars. They carry goods up and down the river. There are rafts made of logs floating on blown-up goatskins. They bring stone and wood and wine down from the mountains. And there are round boats. They are big round baskets of woven reeds covered with leather. They are coated with a black sticky tar that seeps up from the ground here. That keeps the water out.

A Greek traveller later wrote about these round boats with wonder. They are round like a shield, he wrote. They carry big loads of wine. And they carry a live donkey or two. When the boat arrives and is unloaded, it is taken apart. The leather is loaded on the donkeys. And it is carried back up the river by land, because the current is too strong to row against.

{img:fishermen-casting-nets-euphrates-dawn-reeds-herons-quiet}
There are fish in the river. Fishermen go out at dawn in little boats. They cast their nets and come back with fish for the market. And there are birds. Herons and egrets and ducks and pelicans. Kingfishers in the reeds along the banks.

--

But the river can also be dangerous. Every spring the snow melts in the mountains far to the north. The river rises. Sometimes it floods over its banks, across the land, and into the city. People watch the water creep higher day by day. The Babylonians build their houses on platforms. They build their temples on great raised terraces. They keep their canals and dams in good repair. And they pray to the gods to keep the floods away.

In the evening, as the sun sets behind the palms on the west bank, the river glows red and gold. The boats come home. People sit on the quays and on the roofs in the cool breeze from the water. And they watch the light fade over the great slow river.
