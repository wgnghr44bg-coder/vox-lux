Tonight we are going more than two and a half thousand years back, to the great city of Babylon, around the year 600 BC.

--

It is early morning, on the plain of Mesopotamia. The sun is rising, out of the flat, brown land, in the east, and its first light falls on a great river, the Euphrates, wide, and slow, and brown, flowing between banks of reeds, and groves of date palms. And beside the river, rising out of the morning haze, there is a city.

It is the largest city in the world. Its walls are so thick, that, it is said, a chariot, with four horses, can drive along the top of them, and turn round. Behind them, there are temples, and palaces, and houses, and gardens, and above them all, a great stepped tower, a ziggurat, rising, level upon level, toward the sky, with a temple, at the very top.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

This is Babylon.

{img:ishtar-gate-blue-glazed-bricks-golden-lions-dragons-morning-light}
Its name is famous, even today, more than two and a half thousand years later. It is mentioned in the Bible, and in old stories, and songs, and poems. The Tower of Babel. The Hanging Gardens. The waters of Babylon. And yet, for a very long time, it was only a name, and a mound of earth, and broken bricks, in the desert.

In the year 600 BC, it is at the height of its glory. It is the capital of a great empire, stretching from the Persian Gulf, in the south, to the mountains of Turkey, in the north, and from the borders of Persia, in the east, to the Mediterranean Sea, and the borders of Egypt, in the west. And it is ruled by one of the most famous kings of the ancient world: Nebuchadnezzar.

--

Tonight, we are going to walk through Babylon. We will pass through the great blue gate, with its golden lions, and dragons, and bulls, and walk along the Processional Way. We will climb toward the great tower, and visit the temple of the god Marduk. We will spend a day with a family, in their house, of mud brick, and sleep, on the flat roof, under the stars. We will sit in a school, with the young scribes, learning to write, on clay. We will watch the astronomers, on their towers, at night, and join the great festival of the New Year.

{img:babylon-street-mudbrick-houses-people-donkeys-morning-market}
So let us walk, together, along the riverbank, in the cool of the early morning, among the palm trees, and the reeds, and the fishing boats, toward the great walls, and the gates, of Babylon.
