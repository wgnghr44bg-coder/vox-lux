The king of Babylon, in the year 600 BC, is Nebuchadnezzar, the second of that name.

His name, in Babylonian, is Nabu-kudurri-usur, which means: O Nabu, protect my heir. Nabu is the god of writing, and wisdom, the son of Marduk, the great god of Babylon.

--

He became king in the year 605 BC, when his father, Nabopolassar, died. His father had made Babylon free, and strong, again, after a long time under the rule of the Assyrians, from the north, whose great empire had fallen, a few years before. And Nebuchadnezzar, as a young prince, had led his father's armies, and won great victories.

He will rule for forty-three years, until 562 BC, and he will become the most famous king of Babylon. In the Bible, he appears in the books of Kings, and Jeremiah, and Daniel, as a great, and proud, and powerful king.

{img:babylonian-king-inspecting-building-works-bricks-workers-scribes}
But in his own inscriptions, which he had written, on clay cylinders, and stone tablets, and bricks, and buried in the foundations of his buildings, Nebuchadnezzar does not talk much about his wars. He talks about his buildings. He talks about the temples he has restored, for the gods, and the walls he has built, for the city, and the canals he has dug, and the palaces he has made.

--

He wants to be remembered as a builder, and a pious king, who loved the gods, and made Babylon beautiful.

And he did. During his long reign, he rebuilt almost all of Babylon. He built new walls, and new gates, and new palaces, and new temples, and a new bridge, over the Euphrates, and new streets, and canals. He finished the great ziggurat, and covered the great gate, and the Processional Way, with brilliant blue glazed bricks.

{img:mud-bricks-stamped-cuneiform-inscription-king-name-close-detail}
Millions of bricks were made for his buildings. And on many of them, the brick makers pressed a stamp, with the king's name, and titles, written in cuneiform: Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, who provides for the temples Esagila and Ezida, eldest son of Nabopolassar, king of Babylon.

--

Archaeologists have found these bricks, by the thousand, in the ruins of Babylon, and they are in museums, all over the world, so that the king's name, which he hoped would last forever, still speaks, from his bricks, more than two and a half thousand years later.

In the year 600 BC, the king is still young, perhaps in his thirties, and much of his building work is just beginning. All around the city, there is the noise, and the dust, of building: the brick kilns, smoking, and the workers, carrying baskets of earth, and bricks, and the scribes, counting, and the architects, measuring, and the great walls, and gates, and temples, rising, slowly, brick by brick, under the hot sun.
