Today Babylon lies in Iraq, about eighty-five kilometres south of Baghdad, beside the river Euphrates.

A few years ago it became a World Heritage Site. It is protected as one of the most important places in the history of the world.

--

Visitors can walk through the ruins along the old streets between the mud brick walls. Some walls are still standing. Some have been rebuilt in modern times. Visitors can see the bottom of the Ishtar Gate. The moulded bulls and dragons are still in place there. They have no glaze, because they were below the blue bricks, deep in the ground. Visitors can walk along part of the holy road. And they can see the great stone lion of Babylon. It is carved of black stone and is very old and mysterious. It has stood in the ruins for many centuries.

At the entrance there is a smaller modern copy of the Ishtar Gate, painted blue. Visitors walk through it and take photos.

Around the ruins there are palm groves and fields and canals and villages. And the river flows past slowly, as it did in the time of Nebuchadnezzar. Over the centuries it has moved a little to the west.

--

In museums around the world, Babylon lives on. In Berlin, the Ishtar Gate rises blue and gold under the museum roof. In Baghdad, and in London and Paris and New York, the lions of the holy road are shown and admired. And so are the tablets of the writers and the bricks of Nebuchadnezzar.

And Babylon lives on in our hours and minutes and degrees. In the names of the stars and the star signs. In the stories of the Bible. In our language and our imagination. A name of wonder and splendour and mystery.

{img:euphrates-palm-groves-evening-today-iraq-river-calm-reflections}
So we leave Babylon tonight, in the year 600 before Christ, as the evening falls.

--

The sun is setting behind the palm groves on the west bank of the river. The great tower glows red and gold in the last light. The blue bricks of the Ishtar Gate turn deep dark blue. The golden lions on the walls of the holy road fade into the dusk. On the roofs of the houses, families spread their mats and light their little lamps. The children lie down to sleep under the open sky. On the temple roof, an old star watcher settles down with his tablet and his reed. He watches the moon rise over the river. And the stars come out one by one, over the great city and the palm trees and the quiet flowing water.
