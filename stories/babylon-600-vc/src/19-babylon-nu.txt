Today, Babylon lies in Iraq, about eighty-five kilometres south of Baghdad, near the town of Hillah, beside the river Euphrates.

In 2019, it became a World Heritage Site, protected, as one of the most important places in the history of the world.

--

Visitors can walk through the ruins, along the old streets, between the walls, of mud brick, some of them still standing, and some rebuilt, in modern times. They can see the foundations of the Ishtar Gate, with the moulded bulls, and dragons, still in place, in the walls, unglazed, below the level where the blue bricks were, deep in the ground, where Koldewey found them. They can walk along part of the Processional Way, and see the great stone lion of Babylon, carved of black basalt, standing over a fallen man, very old, and mysterious, which has stood, in the ruins, for many centuries.

Beside the site, there is a smaller copy of the Ishtar Gate, painted blue, made in modern times, at the entrance, which visitors pass through, and photograph.

Around the ruins, there are palm groves, and fields, and canals, and villages, and the river Euphrates flows past, slowly, as it did in the time of Nebuchadnezzar, though it has moved a little, over the centuries, to the west.

--

And in the museums of the world, Babylon lives on: in Berlin, where the Ishtar Gate rises, blue, and gold, under the museum roof, and in Baghdad, in the Iraq Museum, and in London, and Paris, and New York, and Chicago, and Istanbul, where the lions of the Processional Way, and the tablets of the scribes, and the bricks of Nebuchadnezzar, are shown, and studied, and admired.

And Babylon lives on, in our hours, and minutes, and degrees, and in the names of the stars, and the zodiac, and in the stories of the Bible, and in our language, and our imagination, as a name of wonder, and splendour, and mystery.

{img:euphrates-palm-groves-evening-today-iraq-river-calm-reflections}
So we leave Babylon, tonight, in the year 600 BC, as the evening falls.

--

The sun is setting, behind the palm groves, on the west bank of the river, and the great tower glows, red, and gold, in the last light. The blue bricks of the Ishtar Gate are turning deep, dark blue, and the golden lions, on the walls of the Processional Way, are fading into the dusk. On the roofs of the houses, the families are spreading their mats, and lighting their little lamps, and the children are lying down, to sleep, under the open sky. On the roof of the temple, an old astronomer is settling down, with his tablet, and his reed, to watch the moon rise, over the river. And the stars are coming out, one by one, over the great city, and the palm trees, and the quiet, flowing water.
