Babylon is surrounded by walls.

There is an outer wall, a huge triangle many kilometres long. It encloses the whole city and its gardens and fields and palm groves. And inside there is the inner city. It is almost square. The river runs right through the middle of it. A double wall of mud brick surrounds it. In front of the wall is a moat, a deep ditch full of water from the river.

--

The inner wall is about six metres thick. The outer one is nearly four. There is a space between them. Every twenty metres or so a tower stands up above the walls, all the way round. There are hundreds of them. It is one of the strongest city walls of the ancient world. Any army that comes here will have to think twice.

Eight great gates lead through the walls. Each one is named after a god. There is a gate of the sun god and a gate of the storm god and a gate of the moon god. And the most beautiful of all is in the north. It is the Ishtar Gate.

{img:ishtar-gate-front-view-blue-towers-aurochs-dragons-processional-way}
The Ishtar Gate is named after Ishtar, the goddess of love and of the morning star. Nebuchadnezzar rebuilds it more than once, each time higher and grander. It is a double gate with tall towers on each side. The passage between them is high enough for the great statues of the gods to pass through on their carts, at festival time.

--

And the gate is blue.

It is covered all over with shining bricks in a deep brilliant blue. The blue is like a precious blue stone from the far mountains. And on the blue, in rows, there are animals. They are made of bricks shaped so they stand out from the wall. They shine in yellow and gold and white and brown. There are bulls with blue horns. And there are dragons. They have the head of a snake, the body of a lion, the feet of an eagle and the tail of a scorpion.

{img:mushhushshu-dragon-glazed-brick-relief-blue-wall-golden}
The bulls belong to the storm god. The dragons belong to Marduk, the great god of Babylon. There are hundreds of them in rows all over the gate. They seem to march toward the city.

--

Along the top of the gate there are battlements and flowers in white and yellow. Inside the passage, there is a great written message. In it, Nebuchadnezzar says he built the gate and covered it with blue bricks and bulls and dragons. So that all people might look at it in wonder.

The great doors are made of cedar wood covered with bronze. At night they are closed and barred. Guards stand watch on the towers. And the city sleeps behind its walls.

{img:ishtar-gate-detail-aurochs-bull-glazed-bricks-yellow-blue-closeup}
When the morning sun shines on the Ishtar Gate, it glows like a jewel. Blue and gold and white against the brown walls and the pale sky. People who come to Babylon for the first time stop and stare. They are amazed.

--

Today you can still walk through the front of the Ishtar Gate. It has been rebuilt from the original bricks, in a museum in Berlin. And the people who see it, more than two and a half thousand years later, still stop and stare. And they are still amazed.
