Babylon is surrounded by walls.

There is an outer wall, a great triangle of walls, many kilometres long, enclosing the whole city, and the gardens, and the fields, and the palm groves, around it, and the king's summer palace, in the north. And inside, there is the inner city, almost square, with the river Euphrates running through the middle of it, surrounded by a double wall, of mud brick, with a moat, full of water, from the river, in front of it.

--

The inner wall is about six metres thick, and the outer one, nearly four metres, with a space between them, and towers, every twenty metres, or so, all the way round, hundreds of them, standing up above the walls, with battlements, along the tops. It is one of the greatest fortifications of the ancient world.

And through the walls, there are eight great gates, each one named after a god: the Gate of Marduk, and the Gate of Shamash, the sun god, and the Gate of Enlil, and the Gate of Adad, the storm god, and the Gate of Zababa, and the Gate of Urash, and the Gate of the god Sin, of the moon. And the most beautiful of all, in the north, the Ishtar Gate.

{img:ishtar-gate-front-view-blue-towers-aurochs-dragons-processional-way}
The Ishtar Gate is named after Ishtar, the goddess of love, and war, and the morning star. Nebuchadnezzar has it rebuilt, more than once, during his long reign, each time higher, and more splendid. It is a double gate, with a smaller gate, in the outer wall, and a great one, behind it, in the inner wall, with tall towers, on each side, and a passage, between them, high enough for the great statues of the gods to pass through, on their carts, in the festival processions.

--

And the gate is blue.

It is covered, all over, with glazed bricks, shining, in a deep, brilliant blue, like lapis lazuli, the precious blue stone, from the mountains of Afghanistan. And on the blue, in rows, one above another, there are animals, made of bricks, moulded, so that they stand out from the wall, and glazed, in yellow, and gold, and white, and brown: bulls, with blue horns, and dragons, with the heads of snakes, and the bodies of lions, and the feet of eagles, and the tails of scorpions.

{img:mushhushshu-dragon-glazed-brick-relief-blue-wall-golden}
The bulls are the animals of Adad, the god of the storm. The dragons are the mushhushshu, the animals of Marduk, the great god of Babylon. There are hundreds of them, in rows, all over the gate, marching, as if they were walking, toward the city.

--

Above the gate, along the top, there are battlements, and flowers, rosettes, in white, and yellow, and on the walls, inside the passage, there is a great inscription, in cuneiform, in which Nebuchadnezzar tells how he built the gate, and covered it, with blue bricks, and wild bulls, and dragons, so that all mankind might gaze on it in wonder.

At the doors, there are great cedar wood doors, covered with bronze, and at night, they are closed, and barred, and the guards stand watch, on the towers, and the city sleeps, behind its walls.

{img:ishtar-gate-detail-aurochs-bull-glazed-bricks-yellow-blue-closeup}
When the sun shines on the Ishtar Gate, in the morning, it glows, like a jewel, blue, and gold, and white, against the brown of the mud brick walls, and the pale sky, and the people who come to Babylon, for the first time, from the villages, and the faraway countries, stop, and stare, and are amazed.

--

Today, you can still walk through the Ishtar Gate, or at least the front of it, rebuilt, from the original bricks, in a museum, in Berlin. And the people who see it, more than two and a half thousand years later, still stop, and stare, and are amazed.
