Through the Ishtar Gate and out to the north runs the holy road of Babylon. It is called the Processional Way.

It is wide and straight, about twenty metres wide. It is paved with big slabs of white and reddish stone. High walls line it on both sides.

--

And on these walls there are lions.

On a background of shining blue bricks, lions walk one after another, in a long line on each side of the road. They stand out from the wall in white and yellow and gold. Their mouths are open and their tails are raised. They stride slowly and proudly toward the gate. There are about a hundred and twenty of them, sixty on each side. The lion is the animal of Ishtar, the goddess of the gate.

{img:babylonian-lion-glazed-brick-relief-striding-blue-background-detail}
Above and below them there are bands of flowers. The walls are so high that walking along the road is like walking through a great blue shining valley. And the lions walk beside you all the way.

--

On most days the road is a busy street. People come and go through the gate with donkeys and carts and baskets. Soldiers and priests and traders and farmers bring their goods to the city.

But at the New Year, in spring, it becomes the holy road. The statues of the gods are carried along it in great splendour. They go from the temple of Marduk, through the Ishtar Gate, to a special temple outside the city. And then back again. There is music and singing and dancing. Great crowds watch from both sides.

{img:procession-priests-carrying-gold-statue-marduk-processional-way-crowds}
Even the stones of the road have writing on them. They carry Nebuchadnezzar's name and a little prayer to Marduk, asking for a long life.

--

Today the lions of the road are spread around the world. Some are still in Babylon. Many are in Berlin, where part of the road has been rebuilt beside the gate. Others are in museums in Paris and London and New York and many other cities. Lions from Babylon, striding on their blue background through museum halls all over the world. Just as they once strode toward the Ishtar Gate.

When you stand in front of one and look closely, you can see the marks of the brick makers' hands. You can see little bubbles in the glaze. And you can imagine the hot sun of Babylon shining on it, the crowds walking past, and the river flowing nearby.
