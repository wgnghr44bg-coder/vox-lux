Through the Ishtar Gate, and out, to the north, runs the Processional Way: the great sacred road of Babylon.

It is a wide, straight road, about twenty metres wide, paved with great slabs of white limestone, and red breccia, a kind of reddish stone, and lined, on each side, with high walls.

--

And on these walls, there are lions.

On a background of shining blue glazed bricks, there walk lions, one after another, in a long line, on each side of the road, moulded in relief, and glazed, in white, and yellow, and gold, with red, or golden, manes, with their mouths open, and their tails raised, striding, slowly, majestically, toward the gate. About a hundred and twenty of them, sixty on each side. The lion is the animal of Ishtar, the goddess of the gate.

{img:babylonian-lion-glazed-brick-relief-striding-blue-background-detail}
Above, and below them, there are bands of flowers, rosettes, in white, and yellow, and red. And the walls are so high, that walking along the road, between them, is like walking through a great, blue, shining canyon, with the lions, walking beside you, all the way.

--

On most days, the Processional Way is a busy street, with people coming, and going, through the gate, with their donkeys, and their carts, and their baskets, and soldiers, and priests, and merchants, and farmers, bringing their goods to the city.

But at the New Year, in the spring, it becomes the sacred road, for the great procession, when the statues of the gods are carried along it, in splendour, from the temple of Marduk, through the Ishtar Gate, and out, to a special temple, outside the city, and back again, with music, and singing, and dancing, and great crowds, watching, from both sides.

{img:procession-priests-carrying-gold-statue-marduk-processional-way-crowds}
The stones of the road are inscribed, too, with Nebuchadnezzar's name, and a little prayer, to Marduk, asking for a long life.

--

Today, the lions of the Processional Way are scattered, around the world. Some are still in Babylon, in Iraq, and many are in Berlin, in the museum, where part of the road has been rebuilt, beside the Ishtar Gate. And others are in museums in Istanbul, and Paris, and London, and New York, and Chicago, and Copenhagen, and Munich, and Detroit, and elsewhere: lions from Babylon, striding, on their blue background, through the halls of museums, all over the world, as they once strode, toward the Ishtar Gate.

And when you stand in front of one, and look at it, closely, you can see the marks of the brick makers' hands, and the little bubbles in the glaze, and imagine the hot sun of Babylon, shining on it, and the crowds, walking past, and the river, flowing, nearby.
