In the spring, at the beginning of the month of Nisan, in March, or April, when the barley is ripening, in the fields, Babylon celebrates the New Year: the festival of Akitu.

It is the greatest festival of the year, and it lasts for twelve days.

--

During the first days, the priests of Esagila perform their ceremonies, in the temple, at dawn: prayers, and hymns, and purifications, and the reciting of the great poem of creation, the Enuma Elish, telling how Marduk defeated the chaos, and made the world, and the stars, and the people.

On the fifth day, there is a strange, and solemn, ceremony. The king comes to the temple, and stands before the statue of Marduk. And the high priest takes away his crown, and his sceptre, and his royal robes, and lays them before the god. And then, the priest strikes the king, on the cheek, and pulls his ears, and the king kneels, before the god, and says that he has not sinned, and has not neglected his duties, and has not harmed Babylon, or its people.

{img:king-kneeling-before-marduk-statue-high-priest-temple-ceremony}
And then the priest gives him back his crown, and his sceptre, and strikes him again, on the cheek. And it is said that if the king has tears in his eyes, it is a good sign: Marduk is pleased, and the year will be good.

--

So every year, the most powerful king in the world must humble himself, before the god, and promise to rule well, and justly.

Then, the gods arrive. From all the cities of Babylonia, the statues of the gods are brought, by boat, along the rivers, and the canals, to Babylon, to visit Marduk, for the New Year: Nabu, his son, from the city of Borsippa, nearby, and many others, in their boats, decorated with gold, and flowers, and banners, with their priests, and their musicians.

{img:procession-through-ishtar-gate-golden-statues-chariots-music-crowds}
And on the eighth day, and the ninth, there is the great procession. The king takes the hand of the statue of Marduk, and leads him out, from his temple, and the statues of all the gods, on their carts, and their litters, carried by priests, go out, along the Processional Way, between the walls, with the blue bricks, and the golden lions, and through the Ishtar Gate, with music, of drums, and harps, and lyres, and pipes, and singing, and incense, and the whole population of Babylon, crowding along the road, and on the walls, and the roofs, to watch.

--

They go out, to the river, and by boat, to a special temple, outside the city, in a garden, the house of the Akitu festival, where the gods stay, for a few days, and there are ceremonies, and feasts.

And then, the gods come back, into the city, in procession, again, through the Ishtar Gate, and along the Processional Way, to Esagila. And the gods meet, together, to decide the fate of the land, for the coming year. And then, they go home, to their own cities, in their boats.

{img:babylon-festival-night-lamps-rooftops-music-families-celebrating}
All through the festival, the city celebrates. There are feasts, and music, and dancing, in the streets, and the people wear their best clothes, and visit their families, and give each other gifts. The temples give out food, and drink, to the people. And at night, the lamps burn, on the roofs, and the walls, and the city glows, and the music, and the singing, go on, until late into the night.

--

And when it is all over, the new year has begun, the world has been renewed, the king has been confirmed, and the gods have decided the fate of the land. And the people go back to their homes, and their fields, and their work, and the barley is harvested, and life goes on.
