In spring, when the barley is ripening in the fields, Babylon celebrates the New Year.

It is the greatest festival of the year. It lasts for twelve days.

--

In the first days, the priests hold ceremonies in the temple at dawn. There are prayers and songs. They recite the great poem of how Marduk beat the chaos and made the world and the stars and the people.

On the fifth day there is a strange and solemn ceremony. The king comes to the temple. He stands before the statue of Marduk. The high priest takes away his crown and his royal robes. He lays them before the god. Then the priest taps the king on the cheek and pulls his ears. The king kneels before the god. He says he has done no wrong. He has not neglected his duties. He has not harmed Babylon or its people.

{img:king-kneeling-before-marduk-statue-high-priest-temple-ceremony}
Then the priest gives him back his crown. Everyone watches the king's face. If the king has tears in his eyes, it is a good sign. Marduk is pleased. And the year will be good.

--

So every year, the most powerful king in the world must make himself humble before the god. And he must promise to rule well and fairly.

Then the gods arrive. From all the cities of the land, the statues of the gods are brought by boat to Babylon. They come along the rivers and canals to visit Marduk for the New Year. Their boats are decorated with gold and flowers and banners. Priests and musicians come with them.

{img:procession-through-ishtar-gate-golden-statues-chariots-music-crowds}
On the eighth and ninth days there is the great procession. The king takes the hand of Marduk's statue and leads him out of his temple. The statues of all the gods follow, on carts and carried by priests. They go along the holy road between the blue walls and the golden lions. They pass through the Ishtar Gate. There are drums and harps and pipes and singing and incense. The whole city crowds along the road and on the walls and the roofs to watch.

--

They go out to the river and by boat to a special temple in a garden outside the city. The gods stay there for a few days. There are ceremonies and feasts.

Then the gods come back into the city in another procession. Through the Ishtar Gate and along the holy road to the temple. The gods meet together to decide the fate of the land for the coming year. Then they go home to their own cities in their boats.

{img:babylon-festival-night-lamps-rooftops-music-families-celebrating}
All through the festival, the city celebrates. There are feasts and music and dancing in the streets. People wear their best clothes. They visit their families and give each other gifts. The temples give out food and drink. At night lamps burn on the roofs and the walls. The city glows. And the music and singing go on late into the night.

--

When it is all over, the new year has begun. The world has been made new. The king has been confirmed. The gods have decided the fate of the land. People go back to their homes and their fields and their work. The barley is harvested. And life goes on.
