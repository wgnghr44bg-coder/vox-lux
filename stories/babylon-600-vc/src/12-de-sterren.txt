At night, on the roofs of the temples and on top of the great tower, the star watchers watch the sky.

The night sky over Babylon is wonderful. The air is dry and clear. There are no city lights, only little lamps in the houses and fires on the walls. So the stars shine bright and sharp in their thousands, from one side of the flat land to the other.

--

The star watchers are priests and writers of the temple of Marduk. They have trained for many years in the old knowledge of the sky. And every night, for hundreds of years, they have watched and written down what they see.

They keep diaries of the sky on clay tablets. Night after night, they write down where the moon and the planets are among the stars. When they rise and set. When the moon goes dark. They also write down the weather and the level of the river. They write the prices of barley and dates and wool in the market. And the important events in the city and the world.

{img:night-sky-babylon-moon-venus-jupiter-ziggurat-silhouette-desert}
These diaries began around the year 650 before Christ. They went on for six hundred years. It is the longest science record kept by anyone in the ancient world. Hundreds of them still exist in a museum in London. Scientists still use them today, to study things that happened in the sky long ago.

--

With all these notes, the star watchers learned to predict the sky. When the new moon would appear. When the moon would go dark. Where the planets would move. They used clever maths with their numbers in sixties.

Some scholars now think the Babylonians even used a kind of geometry to work out the path of the planet Jupiter. Nobody in Europe would use that method again for more than a thousand years.

{img:zodiac-constellations-painted-sky-lion-bull-scorpion-babylonian-style}
The Babylonians also named the stars and the star pictures. Many names we use today come from them. They divided the path of the sun and moon and planets into twelve parts. Each part has its own star picture. The Bull and the Lion and the Scorpion. The Twins and the Scales. Later these became the twelve star signs.

--

For the Babylonians, the sky is a great book written by the gods. In it they can read the future. If the moon goes dark, or a planet moves strangely, it is a message. A sign of what is coming for the king and the country. So the star watchers send reports to the king. And sometimes the king waits nervously for their news.

But mostly they simply watch and write and calculate, night after night, with great patience and care and love for the sky. On a warm night in Babylon you can see them on the temple roof. They are wrapped in their cloaks. They look up at the moon rising over the palm trees and the river and the great dark plain. And they press their reed softly into the clay.
