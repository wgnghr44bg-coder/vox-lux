So we leave the Millers tonight, on their farm in Iowa, in the autumn of 1933, as the evening falls. The sun is setting over the cornfields, in the west, in a great blaze of orange, and red, and gold. The sky is enormous, and the clouds are lit up, pink and purple. The windmill turns slowly, in the soft breeze. And a flock of geese flies over, very high, honking, on their way south for the winter.

The evening chores are done. The cows are milked, and the hogs are fed, and the horses are in their stalls, eating their hay. The chickens have gone into their house, for the night. Rusty, the dog, is lying on the porch steps, with his head on his paws, watching the sunset. And the family is sitting on the porch, in the cool evening air, resting, after the long day.

--

{img:father-pipe-mother-mending-grandmother-rocking-chair-porch-dusk}
Walt sits in his chair, with his pipe, looking out at the fields. The corn harvest is almost done. It has been a good year, in the end. Better than last year. Better than they had feared. Ruth sits beside him, with her mending basket, sewing a button onto Eddie's shirt, in the last of the light. Grandma Hattie rocks slowly in her rocking chair, with a shawl around her shoulders, humming an old hymn, very softly.

June sits on the steps, with a book, reading, until it is too dark to see the words. Then she closes it, and just looks at the sky. Eddie sits beside her, with his arm around Rusty. He is tired, from helping with the corn. His hands are sore. But he is proud. His father said, today, that he did a man's work. He will remember that, all his life.

--

{img:farmhouse-night-lamp-glowing-window-stars-over-dark-fields-windmill}
Then the stars come out, one by one, over the wide Iowa sky. Thousands of them, bright and clear, with no city lights to hide them. The Milky Way stretches across the sky, from one horizon to the other. It is getting cold, now. Ruth says it is time to go in. The family goes inside, into the warm kitchen. Walt lights the lamp. Ruth puts the kettle on the stove. And they sit around the table, for a last cup of cocoa, before bed.

Then they go up to bed, one by one, with candles. The house is quiet. The stove ticks, as it cools. The clock on the kitchen wall ticks, softly. Outside, the wind rustles the dry corn, in the fields, with a soft, whispering sound, like the sea. Far away, a train whistle blows, long and lonely, in the night. And somewhere, an owl calls, from the trees by the creek.
