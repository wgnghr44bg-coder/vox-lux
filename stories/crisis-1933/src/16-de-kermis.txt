At the end of August, there is the county fair, in Harlan. It is the biggest event of the year, for the farm families. For a week, the fairground, at the edge of the town, is full of people, and animals, and tents, and music. There are livestock shows, and horse races, and a midway, with rides, and games, and food stands. Everyone comes, from all over the county.

The heart of the fair is the livestock show. The farmers bring their best animals, to be judged. Cattle, and hogs, and sheep, and horses, and chickens. The animals are washed, and brushed, and groomed, until they shine. The judges walk around, and look at them, very carefully, and feel them, and compare them. And the best ones get ribbons. Blue for first, red for second, and white for third.

--

{img:boy-with-calf-show-ring-4h-county-fair-judge-looking-proud-nervous}
This year, Eddie is showing a calf, for the first time. He is a member of the 4-H club, a club for farm children, where they learn about farming, and animals, and gardening, and cooking, and sewing. Each child has a project. Eddie's project is a young steer, a black Angus calf, which he has raised himself, since it was born, in the spring. He has named it Duke. He has fed it, and watered it, and brushed it, every day.

On the day of the show, Eddie washes Duke, and brushes him, until his black coat shines like silk. He puts on his best clean shirt, and his overalls, and his father's old hat, which is too big for him. Then he leads Duke into the show ring, with the other boys and girls, and their calves. He is very nervous. His hands are shaking. Duke is calm. He seems to think it is all a bit silly.

{img:prize-ribbon-red-pinned-on-calf-halter-boy-grinning-family-cheering}
The judge walks slowly along the line of calves, looking at each one. He stops at Duke, and looks at him for a long time. He feels his back, and his shoulders, and his legs. Then he moves on. Eddie holds his breath. At the end, the judge announces the winners. First place, a big brown calf, belonging to a girl from the other side of the county. And second place, Duke, the black Angus, shown by Eddie Miller.

Eddie gets a red ribbon. He is so happy that he cannot stop grinning. His family cheers, from the side of the ring. Walt shakes his hand, like a grown man. Ruth hugs him. June tells him he did well. And Grandma Hattie says that she always knew Duke was a fine animal. Eddie pins the ribbon on his overalls, and wears it for the rest of the day, and sleeps with it under his pillow.

--

June has entered the fair too. In the women's building, there are displays of canning, and baking, and sewing, and quilting. June has entered a jar of strawberry jam, and a little apron she made, from a flour sack, with careful embroidery. Her jam gets a white ribbon. Her apron gets nothing. But Ruth's peach pie gets a blue ribbon, for the third year in a row. And Grandma Hattie's quilt, which she made with the church women, gets a special prize.

{img:fair-midway-night-lights-carousel-cotton-candy-children-coins}
In the evening, the family goes to the midway. It is noisy, and bright, and exciting, with coloured lights, and music, and people shouting. There is a merry-go-round, with painted horses, and a Ferris wheel, high above the fairground. There are games, where you can throw a ball, or a ring, and win a prize. And there are stands selling popcorn, and cotton candy, and hot dogs, and lemonade. Walt gives each of the children a quarter, to spend as they like.

Eddie spends his on rides, and a hot dog. June spends hers on a ride on the Ferris wheel, and saves the rest. From the top of the Ferris wheel, she can see the whole fairground, lit up, and the town, and the dark fields beyond, stretching away, under the stars. She thinks about the world's fair, in Chicago. This is much smaller. But she likes it just as much.
