In the first hundred days after he became president, Roosevelt and the Congress passed more new laws than any government in American history, in such a short time. People called it the Hundred Days. The new laws were part of the New Deal, which Roosevelt had promised. They were meant to help the people who were suffering, and to get the economy working again, and to make sure that such a disaster could never happen again.

There were many new programmes, with many new names. So many that people could not keep track of them. They were known by their initials, like a kind of alphabet soup. The AAA, for the farmers. The CCC, for the young men. The FERA, to give money to the states, for the poor. The TVA, to build dams, and bring electricity, to the poor lands of the south. And many more.

--

{img:young-men-arriving-ccc-camp-tents-barracks-forest-truck-1933}
One of the most popular programmes was the Civilian Conservation Corps, the CCC. It gave work to young men, between eighteen and twenty-five, from poor families. They lived in camps, in the countryside, in the forests, and the parks, and worked on projects to protect nature. Planting trees, building roads and trails, stopping soil from washing away, fighting forest fires, and building parks. They were paid thirty dollars a month. Twenty-five of those dollars were sent home, to their families.

Ruth's nephew, Jimmy, joins the CCC in the summer of 1933. He is nineteen, and he has been out of work for two years. He goes to a camp in the forests of Minnesota, in the north. He writes letters home, which Ruth reads aloud, in the evenings. He says the work is hard, but good. He has never eaten so well in his life. Three big meals a day. He has put on fifteen pounds. And he has learned to use an axe, and a saw, and to drive a truck.

{img:ccc-boys-around-campfire-evening-forest-letters-harmonica-tired-happy}
Jimmy says that he has planted thousands of trees, on hillsides where the old forest was cut down. He says that one day, there will be a great forest there again, and he will take his children to see it. He says he is proud of his work. And his mother, Ruth's sister, is proud of him. The twenty-five dollars a month that he sends home keeps her family going.

The CCC was a great success. Over the next nine years, about three million young men worked in it. They planted about three billion trees. They built thousands of parks, and trails, and bridges, and roads, all over America. Many of them are still used today. And many of the young men said, later, that the CCC changed their lives, and gave them hope, and pride, when they needed it most.

--

For the farmers, there was the AAA, the Agricultural Adjustment Act. It tried to raise the prices of crops, by paying farmers to grow less. The idea was that if there was less corn, and less pork, the prices would go up. It was controversial. In the summer of 1933, the government paid farmers to slaughter millions of young pigs, to reduce the number of hogs. Many people thought this was shocking, when so many people were hungry.

{img:farmer-reading-government-letter-kitchen-table-wife-looking-over-shoulder}
Walt does not like the idea of being paid not to farm. It goes against everything he believes in. But he needs the money. So in the autumn, he signs up, and agrees to plant fewer acres of corn next year. And in return, he gets a cheque from the government. It is not much. But it helps him pay the mortgage, and keep the farm. Many farmers do the same.

Not all of the New Deal worked. Some of it was badly planned, and some of it was wasteful, and some of it was later stopped by the courts. People argued about it, then, and they still argue about it today. But it gave many people hope, in the darkest time. It showed that the government cared about them. And slowly, very slowly, things began to get a little better.
