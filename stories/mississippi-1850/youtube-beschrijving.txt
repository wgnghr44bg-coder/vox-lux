Drift off to the story of life on a Mississippi steamboat in 1850, seen through the eyes of a fifteen-year-old boy who is learning to become a river pilot. A calm history for sleep about the great brown river, the white floating palaces and the people who travelled on them.
Climb to the glass pilothouse and learn to read the water, walk through the grand saloon with its chandeliers, meet the immigrant families on the main deck, stop at lonely woodyards, hear the leadsman call "mark twain" in the dark, race another steamboat around a bend, wait out the morning fog, and arrive at last in the busy port of New Orleans.

A calm, slow-paced history documentary for sleep and relaxation. No loud
sounds, no jump scares, just a gentle voice, soft 432 Hz background music
with smooth, gentle rain sounds and softly changing images.
