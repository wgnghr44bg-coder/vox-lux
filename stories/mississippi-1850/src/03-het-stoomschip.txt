The Clara Belle is a sidewheel steamboat. That means that she has two huge paddle wheels, one on each side of the boat. Each wheel is taller than a house, made of wood and iron, with wide wooden paddles all around it. When the wheels turn, the paddles push against the water, and the boat moves forward. The wheels are covered by big curved wooden boxes. On each box, the name of the boat is painted in large golden letters.

--

The Clara Belle is about eighty metres long. She has three decks. At the bottom, close to the water, is the main deck. This is where the engines and boilers are, and where the cargo is stored. It is open on the sides, so that the cargo can be loaded easily. The firemen work here, feeding the furnaces, and the deck passengers sleep here, among the crates and barrels.

Above the main deck is the boiler deck. Despite its name, there are no boilers here. It is the deck for the cabin passengers, who have paid more for their tickets. In the middle of the boiler deck, there is a long, beautiful hall, called the grand saloon. On both sides of the saloon, there are rows of small cabins, called staterooms, where the passengers sleep. Each stateroom has its own name, painted on the door, after one of the states of America.

{img:grand-saloon-steamboat-chandeliers-carpet-gilded-arches-long-hall}
The grand saloon is the most splendid part of the boat. It is very long, almost the whole length of the boat. The ceiling is decorated with carved wood, painted white and gold. Crystal chandeliers hang down, sparkling in the light. There are thick carpets on the floor, mirrors on the walls, and soft chairs and sofas. At one end, there is a piano. People called these saloons floating palaces.

On top of the boiler deck, there is a smaller deck, called the hurricane deck. And on top of that, there is a row of cabins for the officers. It is called the Texas deck. Nobody is quite sure why. Some people say it is because the officers' cabins were the biggest, and Texas was the biggest state. On top of the Texas, at the very top of the boat, sits the pilothouse.

--

{img:pilothouse-glass-windows-big-wooden-wheel-view-over-river-bow}
The pilothouse is a small room with windows on all sides. From here, the pilot can see the whole river, in every direction. In the middle, there is a huge wooden steering wheel, taller than a man. Half of it disappears into a slot in the floor. The pilot stands behind the wheel and steers the boat. There is also a bench at the back, a little stove for cold nights, and ropes that ring bells down in the engine room.

Down in the engine room, there are two big steam engines. And in front of them, there is a row of boilers, long iron tubes full of water. Under the boilers, there are furnaces, where wood is burned to heat the water and make steam. The steam goes through pipes to the engines, which turn the paddle wheels. Then the used steam escapes through two thin pipes, with a loud puffing sound. Chuff. Chuff. Chuff. That is the heartbeat of the steamboat.

{img:engine-room-steamboat-pistons-brass-gauges-engineer-oil-can}
A steamboat burns an enormous amount of wood. The Clara Belle burns about twenty cords of wood every day. That is a pile of wood as big as a small house. So the boat must stop several times a day, to take on more wood from the woodyards along the river.

--

The Clara Belle is not the biggest or the fastest boat on the river. But she is a good, steady boat, well looked after. Her captain is proud of her. Her crew is good. And from the shore, as she steams past with her white decks shining and her chimneys smoking, she looks as beautiful as any boat on the whole Mississippi.

At the very front of the boat, on the main deck, there are two long wooden poles, standing up like the arms of a giant. They are called spars. If the boat gets stuck on a sandbar, the crew can push the spars down into the river bottom, and use ropes and the steam engine to lift the front of the boat, and walk her forward over the sand. The sailors call this grasshoppering, because the boat looks like a grasshopper, jumping slowly along.
