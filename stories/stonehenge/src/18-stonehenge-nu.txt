Today, Stonehenge is one of the most famous places in the world. More than a million people visit it every year.

Visitors walk along a path around the stones. On most days, they cannot go inside the circle, because the stones must be protected. Too many feet could wear away the ground. But they walk slowly around the outside, looking. And they are amazed at how big the stones are, and how very old.

--

Near Stonehenge, there is a visitor centre. Inside, you can see the bones and antler picks and pots that the builders left behind. You can see the face of a man who lived nearby, five and a half thousand years ago. Scientists rebuilt his face from his skull. Outside, there are copies of the Stone Age houses from Durrington Walls. They are square houses with chalk floors and hearths in the middle. And there is a big stone on a wooden sledge, with long ropes. Visitors can try to pull it. It is very hard to move, unless a lot of people pull together.

{img:modern-solstice-crowd-sunrise-stonehenge-people-celebrating-june}
And twice a year, something special happens. At the summer solstice and the winter solstice, the circle is opened to everyone. Anyone can come inside the ring. Thousands of people gather in the dark. There are druids and families, young people and old people, from all over the world. They wait through the night together. And when the sun rises on the longest day, they cheer. Drums beat, and people dance. They celebrate just as their ancestors may have done, four and a half thousand years ago.

--

So we leave the plain tonight, in the time of the builders, as the evening falls.

The midwinter sun has set between the great stones. The sky is turning from gold to rose to a deep, soft blue. The first stars come out over the plain, one by one. At Durrington Walls, the fires are burning and the last pigs are roasting. The smell of woodsmoke drifts on the cold air.

In a crowded house, Wren and Pip are falling asleep under their furs. Their hands are still sore from the rope, but they are smiling. The baby sleeps in her soft fur bag, making little sighing sounds. Bran and Ula sit by the hearth, talking quietly about the journey home. The grandmother lies wrapped in her warmest furs, smiling in her sleep.

Outside, on the plain, the great stones stand silent in the frost. They are grey and tall and patient. And over them, slowly, the stars turn. They turn as they always have, and as they always will.
