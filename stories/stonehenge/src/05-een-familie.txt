Now let us meet a family from the time of the builders.

They live about a day's walk north of Stonehenge, in a small settlement beside a stream. There are four houses there, standing close together. About twenty people live there altogether. There are parents and grandparents, children and cousins. They farm a few small fields of wheat and barley. They keep cattle and pigs and a few sheep. And they gather food from the woods and the stream.

--

The father of the family is called Bran. That is not his real name, because we do not know any real names from this time. But we will call him Bran. He is a herder, and he knows every one of his cattle. He can tell them apart by their colour, their horns and the way they walk. He is tall and strong, and he has helped to pull great stones before.

The mother is called Ula. She makes pots and weaves cloth, and she grows barley and wheat in the little fields. She knows every plant in the woods. She knows which ones heal and which ones are harmful. She knows which ones are good to eat in spring and which in autumn. People in the settlement come to her when they are feeling unwell. She gives them a warm drink of herbs and a kind word.

{img:mother-making-grooved-pottery-coil-clay-hut-doorway-children-watching}
There are three children in the family. There is a girl of twelve called Wren, and a boy of eight called Pip. And there is a baby girl who is always laughing and waving her little fists. There is also the grandmother. She is old for this time, perhaps fifty years old. Her back is bent, and her hands are stiff in the mornings. But she remembers everything. She knows all the stories and all the songs, and all the ways of the ancestors.

--

It is late autumn now. The harvest is in, and the leaves are falling from the trees. The days are getting shorter and colder. And soon the family will make a journey. They will walk south, to the great gathering on the plain, for the midwinter feast. Bran will bring pigs to share with the others. Ula will bring pots of food and baskets of hazelnuts. The children are very excited. They have heard so many stories about the feast.

{img:family-preparing-journey-pigs-baskets-pots-autumn-leaves-settlement}
For days, the whole settlement is busy getting ready. Ula grinds grain for flat bread and packs it in leather bags. Wren helps to tie bundles of dried meat and wrap up the best pots in grass. Pip is given the job of feeding the pigs extra acorns, so they will be fat for the feast. He takes it very seriously.

But the grandmother is a little worried. The journey is long and cold, and she is getting old. Will she be strong enough this year, she wonders? She does not want to stay behind. Every midwinter of her whole life, she has gone to the plain. She has always watched the sun go down between the great stones. She is not going to miss it now. Bran says he will carry her on his back if he has to. She laughs and says she is not a sack of barley. But she is grateful, all the same.
