In the very middle of Stonehenge, there is a big flat stone. It lies on the ground, under two fallen stones from the great trilithon. It is called the Altar Stone.

It is different from all the others. It is not a grey sarsen, and it is not a bluestone. It is a greenish sandstone, with tiny sparkles in it that catch the light. It is about five metres long and weighs about six tons. Nobody knows exactly what it was used for. The name Altar Stone was given to it much later. It may once have stood upright, like the other stones.

--

For a long time, people thought it came from Wales, like the bluestones. But in 2024, scientists made an amazing discovery. They studied tiny grains inside the stone. These grains hold a record of where and when the rock was formed. And they found that it did not come from Wales at all.

It came from the far north of Scotland. It came from the area around the Orkney Islands and the north-east tip of Scotland. That is more than seven hundred kilometres away from Stonehenge. It is more than three times as far as the bluestones travelled.

{img:northern-scotland-coast-orkney-sandstone-cliffs-sea-grey-sky-wild}
The coast of the far north is wild and beautiful. Tall cliffs of red and grey sandstone rise straight out of the sea. Seabirds nest on the ledges, and seals rest on the rocks below. The wind blows almost all the time, and the sky changes every hour.

How could a stone weighing six tons travel seven hundred kilometres, four and a half thousand years ago? Did it go over mountains, through forests and across rivers? Or perhaps it went by sea, down the long coast of Britain, in some kind of great boat? Nobody knows. It is one of the newest mysteries of Stonehenge, and one of the most amazing.

--

Perhaps the people who brought the stone stayed on the plain for a while. Perhaps they shared the midwinter feast and slept in the crowded houses at Durrington Walls. Perhaps they made new friends and told stories of their faraway islands. And when spring came, perhaps they began the long journey home.

But we do know this. The people of Stonehenge were connected to people far, far away. They were connected from Wales in the west to Orkney in the far north. They used the same kind of pottery and shared the same ideas. Perhaps they shared the same beliefs. Perhaps even the same families were related by marriage across the whole island.

{img:neolithic-boat-coast-sea-paddlers-heavy-stone-cargo-dawn-calm}
Imagine a long boat, paddled slowly along the coast on a calm morning. The paddlers dip their paddles in time, singing a low song. In the middle of the boat, carefully tied down, lies a great greenish stone. At night they pull the boat up onto a beach and make a fire. People from the nearby village come down to look at the stone and wonder.

Maybe the Altar Stone was a gift. Maybe it was sent from the people of the north to the people of the south. It was a piece of their holy land, carried all the way to the great gathering place. It was placed in the very centre of the circle. And in this way, it joined the far corners of Britain together in one sacred ring.
