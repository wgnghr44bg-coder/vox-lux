Stonehenge stands on a wide, open land of gentle hills. Today it is called Salisbury Plain.

Under the grass, the ground is made of chalk. Chalk is a soft white rock, made long ago from the tiny shells of sea creatures. The chalk makes the land dry, and the grass short and sweet. It is good for grazing cattle and sheep. When you dig into the chalk, it is bright white underneath. In the time of the builders, many monuments were dug into the chalk. When they were new, they shone white across the green land. You could see them from far away.

--

The plain is not empty, even in the time of our story. There are already many monuments here, built long before Stonehenge. There are long mounds where the ancestors rest. There is a great long ditch, almost three kilometres long, that runs across the land. There are rings of earth and ditches. And later, many round mounds will be built on the hilltops as well.

It is a sacred landscape, a place of the ancestors. People come here from far away, again and again, for hundreds of years.

{img:river-avon-wiltshire-valley-willows-water-meadow-dawn-neolithic}
To the east of Stonehenge flows the river Avon. It is a gentle river, winding slowly through a green valley. Willow trees lean over the water, and reeds grow along the banks. The river flows south, all the way to the sea. In the time of the builders, it is an important road. People and goods travel along it in small boats. And it may have been important in other ways too.

In the early mornings, the river is very beautiful. Mist rises from the water and drifts over the meadows. Herons stand still in the shallows, waiting for fish. Ducks paddle among the reeds, and a kingfisher flashes past like a little blue spark.

--

The weather on the plain can be wild. In winter, cold winds blow across the open land. Rain and snow sweep over the hills, and there are few trees to shelter behind. People pull their cloaks tight and hurry home to their fires. But in summer, the sun shines warmly on the grass. Skylarks sing high above, so high you can hardly see them. There are big skies and long views. And there is a feeling of being very close to the heavens.

{img:skylark-singing-over-grassland-summer-sky-chalk-downs-flowers}
In summer, the plain is full of flowers. There are yellow buttercups and purple orchids, and little blue harebells nodding in the wind. Butterflies drift from flower to flower. Sheep graze slowly across the hillsides, and their bells clink softly.

Shepherds bring their flocks up onto the plain in the mornings and down again in the evenings. They sit on the warm grass with their dogs and watch the clouds drift by. Sometimes they play a little tune on a flute made from a bird's bone. The sound carries a long way across the quiet hills.

Maybe that is why the people chose this place for their great monument. There is the open sky and the wide horizon. You can see the sun rise and set, without anything in the way. You can feel the seasons turning, one after another. It is a place between the earth and the sky.
