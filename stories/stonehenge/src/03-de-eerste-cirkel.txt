Stonehenge was not built all at once. It was built and changed and rebuilt over many hundreds of years. It is a little like an old church that keeps getting new parts added to it.

It began about five hundred years before our story, around the year 3000 before Christ. And at first, it had no great stones at all.

--

The first Stonehenge was a ring of earth. People dug a round ditch in the chalk, about a hundred metres across. They piled the white chalk into a bank beside it. They left two openings in the ring, like doors. One opening faced north-east, and the other faced south. Inside the ring, they dug a circle of fifty-six small holes. Some of these holes may have held wooden posts, or small stones.

How did they dig the ditch without metal tools? They used picks made from the antlers of red deer. They hacked at the hard chalk with these antler picks. They scooped up the loose chalk with the shoulder bones of cattle, using them like shovels. And they carried it away in baskets on their backs. Archaeologists have found dozens of these antler picks lying in the ditch. They were left behind by the diggers, all those years ago. Some of them still show the marks where their hands held them.

{img:antler-picks-cattle-shoulder-blade-shovels-chalk-ditch-digging}
Digging the ditch was hard and dusty work. The diggers worked in small teams, each team digging its own part. At midday, they sat on the bank and ate bread and cheese and drank water. Children carried baskets of chalk away and tipped them onto the growing bank. By evening, everyone was covered in white dust, from their hair to their feet.

Inside the ring, people laid their dead to rest. Their ashes were placed in the holes and in the ground. There were many people, over hundreds of years, both men and women. Some of them had come from far away. Perhaps they were important people, leaders or wise ones. Perhaps they were the families of those who built the ring. We remember them gently.

--

So from the very beginning, Stonehenge was a place for the ancestors. It was a place to honour those who had gone before. And the circle and its openings may already have been linked to the sun.

{img:white-chalk-bank-ring-evening-people-gathering-fire-smoke-ancient}
For five hundred years, the ring of earth stood on the plain. People came and went, and ceremonies were held there. The white chalk bank slowly turned green as the grass grew over it. And then, around the time of our story, something new began. People decided to build with stone, huge stone. And some of it would come from very far away.

--

Why did they build a circle? Perhaps because the sky looks round, like a great bowl over the earth. Perhaps because the sun and the moon are round. Perhaps because a circle has no beginning and no end, like the turning seasons. Or perhaps because, in a circle, everyone stands as equals. Everyone faces the middle, and everyone faces each other. Nobody knows for sure. But people all over the world, in many different times, have gathered in circles. They have gathered to remember, and to celebrate, and to be together.
