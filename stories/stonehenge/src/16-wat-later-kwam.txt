After the great stones were raised, Stonehenge kept changing.

The bluestones were moved around several times over the centuries. First in one shape. Then in another. A ring. A horseshoe. Inside the great sarsen circle. People kept rearranging them, as if they were still working out the best way. Stonehenge was never quite finished. It was always being worked on.

--

In the age of bronze that followed, people built hundreds of round burial mounds on the hills around Stonehenge. Like little round hills. They are still there today. You can see them on the skyline all around. Important people were buried in them. With bronze daggers and gold and amber and beads. People wanted to be buried near the great stones.

Carvings of bronze axes and daggers were even cut into some of the sarsens. They are hard to see now, worn smooth by time and weather. But on a low sunny evening, if you know where to look, you can find them. Little shapes of axe heads, carved on the old grey stones.

{img:carvings-bronze-axe-heads-on-sarsen-stone-low-evening-sun-faint}
Then slowly, people stopped coming. Around three thousand years ago, the great gatherings ended. Nobody knows exactly why. Times changed. Beliefs changed. New ways of life came. Stonehenge stood on the plain, quiet and empty. Sheep grazed among the stones.

--

Over the centuries, some stones fell over. Storms and frost pushed them. Some were taken away for building. Some were broken. In the Middle Ages, people told stories about how Stonehenge was made. One famous story said the wizard Merlin moved the stones by magic from Ireland. Others said giants built it. Nobody remembered the real builders anymore.

{img:medieval-shepherd-sheep-among-fallen-stones-stonehenge-moonlight-alone}
But the stones stayed. Most of them. Tall and grey on the empty plain. Through Roman times and Saxon times and Norman times. Through the Middle Ages. Waiting for people to come back. And try to understand.

--

For a while, Stonehenge even belonged to private owners. In 1915, it was sold at an auction. A local man bought it, as a present for his wife. She did not much like it. So a few years later, he gave it to the nation. Since then, it has belonged to everyone.
