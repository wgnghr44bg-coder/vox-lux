The family has come to help pull a stone.

It is the morning after the midwinter feast. Bran and Ula and the children walk north with many others. They walk to where a great sarsen is waiting on its sledge, halfway between West Woods and Stonehenge. It has been pulled this far already. Now it must travel the rest of the way. The grandmother stays behind at Durrington Walls, by the fire, with the baby.

--

There are hundreds of people gathered around the stone, perhaps two hundred or more. Long ropes run out from the sledge in many lines. People stand along the ropes, one behind the other. Strong men stand at the front, and women and older children behind them. And at the very end of one rope stand Pip and Wren. They hold on tightly, and their hearts are beating fast.

In front of the sledge, workers have laid a track of wooden rails. These are logs laid end to end in a long line. The sledge will slide along them. Behind the sledge, other workers pick up the rails it has already passed. They carry them round to the front and lay them down again. They do this over and over, all day long.

{img:wooden-rail-track-sledge-runners-workers-carrying-logs-forward-frost}
A man climbs up and stands on top of the stone. He is the leader of the pull. He raises his arms high, and everyone grips the ropes. He calls out a word, and everyone pulls together. The ropes creak and stretch, but the sledge does not move. He calls again, and they pull again, harder this time. Pip's feet slip on the frosty grass. And then, with a long low groan, the sledge slides forward. It moves only a little, but a great cheer goes up.

--

Then they find their rhythm. The leader calls, and everyone pulls, and then they rest. Call, pull, rest. Someone starts to sing a pulling song, with a strong steady beat. Everyone joins in, and the song carries across the hills. The great grey stone moves slowly forward, a little with every pull.

{img:people-singing-while-pulling-ropes-leader-on-stone-arms-raised}
On flat ground, everything goes well. But then they come to a slope. Going uphill is very hard work. Everyone leans forward and pulls with all their strength. The leader shouts encouragement. A few people slip in the mud, and they get up again, laughing. Going downhill needs even more care. The stone wants to slide ahead too fast. So some people hold the ropes from behind to slow it down. Wooden blocks are pushed under the runners. Everything is done slowly and carefully.

At midday, they stop to rest and eat. Ula has brought bread and cold pork wrapped in leaves. The children sit on the frosty grass, with their cheeks pink from the cold. Their hands are red and sore, but they do not complain.

--

By the end of the day, they have moved the stone perhaps a kilometre, or maybe a little more. Everyone is very tired. Their hands are sore from the ropes, and their legs are shaking. But they are proud of what they have done. They sit by the fires and eat and laugh and tell stories about the day.

{img:exhausted-pullers-resting-fires-evening-stone-sledge-behind-stars}
Pip falls asleep with his head in his mother's lap, still holding a little piece of rope. Wren sits close to her father by the fire. Her hands hurt, but she cannot stop smiling. I helped, she says softly. I helped to move a stone. Bran puts his arm around her shoulders. Yes, he says, you did. And one day you will tell your grandchildren about it.
