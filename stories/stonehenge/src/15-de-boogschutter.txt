About two hundred years after the great stones were raised, a man came to Stonehenge from very far away. We call him the Amesbury Archer.

He was found in 2002, when builders were getting ready to build new houses near Stonehenge. Archaeologists came to look at the ground first. And they found a grave. A man was lying on his side, with his knees bent, as if he were asleep. Around him there were more things than had ever been found in a Stone Age grave in Britain.

--

There were many arrowheads made of flint, and that is why he is called the Archer. There were two stone wrist guards. Archers wear these to protect their arm from the bowstring. There were copper knives, some of the first metal objects ever found in Britain. There were five pots of a new kind, shaped like a bell or a beaker. And there were two little hair ornaments made of gold. They are the oldest gold ever found in Britain.

Who was this man? Scientists studied his teeth very carefully. And they found that he had grown up far away, in the Alps, in the middle of Europe. He probably came from what is now Switzerland or Germany. He had travelled all the way to Britain, over mountains and rivers and across the sea.

{img:traveller-archer-crossing-sea-small-boat-white-cliffs-ahead-bronze-age}
He also had a bad knee. An old injury had left him limping, so walking was difficult for him for many years. And yet he made that very long journey.

We can imagine him crossing the sea in a small boat, on a grey morning. He sits in the boat with his bow across his knees, looking ahead. And then the white cliffs of Britain appear out of the mist. He has never seen this land before. He wonders what he will find there.

--

Why did he come all this way? Perhaps it was because of Stonehenge. Maybe its fame had spread across Europe, and he came to see it. Some people think the bluestones were believed to have healing powers. Maybe he came hoping to make his leg better.

Or perhaps he came because he knew how to do something new. He knew how to work metal, copper and gold. People in Britain did not know how to do this yet. He may have been one of the first to bring this knowledge. Perhaps he was a metalworker, a man with a new and precious skill.

{img:early-metalworker-crucible-fire-copper-pouring-mould-curious-onlookers}
Imagine him showing the people of the plain how it was done. He builds a small, very hot fire and puts lumps of green stone in a clay pot. The people gather round to watch, curious. Slowly, the stone melts into shining liquid metal. He pours it into a mould of stone. And when it cools, there is a bright new knife. The people gasp and murmur in wonder.

He was a sign of changing times. After him, more people came from Europe, with their bell-shaped pots and their metal. The Stone Age was coming to an end, and the age of metal was beginning. Bronze would come soon, and life on the plain would change. But Stonehenge would stay. People would keep coming to it, and keep changing it, for another thousand years.
