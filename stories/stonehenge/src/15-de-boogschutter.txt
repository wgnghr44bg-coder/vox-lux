About two hundred years after the great stones were raised, a man came to Stonehenge from very far away. We call him the Amesbury Archer.

He was found in 2002, when builders were digging for new houses near Stonehenge. Archaeologists came to look first. And they found a grave. A man lying on his side, his knees bent, as if asleep. And around him, more things than had ever been found in a Stone Age grave in Britain.

--

There were arrowheads made of flint. Many of them. That is why he is called the Archer. There were two stone wrist guards. Archers wear them to protect their arm from the bowstring. There were copper knives. Some of the first metal objects in Britain. There were five pots of a new kind, shaped like a bell or a beaker. And there were two little hair ornaments made of gold. The oldest gold ever found in Britain.

Who was he? Scientists studied his teeth. And they found he had grown up far away. In the Alps, in the middle of Europe. Probably in what is now Switzerland or Germany. He had travelled all the way to Britain. Over mountains and rivers and the sea.

{img:traveller-archer-crossing-sea-small-boat-white-cliffs-ahead-bronze-age}
He also had a bad knee. An old injury had left him limping. So he walked with difficulty for many years. And yet he made that long journey.

--

Why did he come? Perhaps because of Stonehenge. Maybe its fame had spread across Europe. Maybe he came to see it. Or to be healed there. Some people think the bluestones were believed to have healing powers. Maybe he came hoping to cure his leg.

Or perhaps he came because he knew something new. How to work metal. Copper and gold. People in Britain did not know how yet. He may have been one of the first to bring this knowledge. A metalworker. A man with a new and precious skill.

{img:early-metalworker-crucible-fire-copper-pouring-mould-curious-onlookers}
He was a sign of changing times. After him, more people came from Europe. With their bell-shaped pots and their metal. The Stone Age was ending. The age of metal was beginning. Bronze would come soon. And life on the plain would change. But Stonehenge would stay. People would keep coming to it. And keep changing it. For another thousand years.
