Tonight we travel about four and a half thousand years back in time. We go to a wide green plain in the south of England. The year is around 2500 before Christ. And on that plain, people are raising great stones toward the sky.

--

It is early morning, and the air is cold and still. Mist lies low over the rolling grassland, soft and white. In the east, the sky is slowly turning pink. And in the middle of the plain, there is a ring of huge grey stones, standing upright. Some of them are taller than three people standing on each other's shoulders. Some have great flat stones lying across their tops, like the top of a doorway. Some are not finished yet. Wooden frames lean against them, and thick ropes lie coiled on the grass. Smoke rises from a fire nearby, where people are waking up and stretching.

This is Stonehenge, one of the most famous places in the world. And it is also one of the most mysterious.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

{img:neolithic-people-hauling-sarsen-sledge-ropes-many-people-chalk-plain}
Who built Stonehenge, and how did they do it, and why? For hundreds of years, people have wondered about these questions. Some said it was built by giants long ago. Some said a wizard flew the stones there by magic. Some said it was built by the Romans, or by druids, or by people from far away. None of that was true.

It was built by the people who lived here, on and around the plain. They were farmers and herders, ordinary people with families. They had no metal tools, no wheels and no writing. They had only stone and wood, antler and rope. And they had their own strength, their own cleverness, and a great dream they all shared.

{img:midsummer-sunrise-through-stonehenge-trilithons-golden-light-ancient}
In the last fifty years, archaeologists have learned an amazing amount about Stonehenge. They have found out where the stones came from. Some came from not very far away. Some came from the hills of Wales, about two hundred kilometres away. One great stone even came from the far north of Scotland. They have found the great village where the builders gathered for their feasts. They have found the bones of the pigs they roasted and ate. And they have learned that Stonehenge is carefully lined up with the sun.

--

{img:neolithic-family-walking-across-plain-toward-stones-winter-morning-cattle}
Tonight we will walk on this plain in the time of the builders. We will meet a family who comes here every year for the great midwinter feast. There is a father and a mother, two children, a baby and an old grandmother. We will follow the long journey of the bluestones from the hills of Wales. We will help to pull a great grey stone across the hills on a wooden sledge. We will watch the stones being raised, and the heavy lintels lifted into place. And at the end, we will stand in the circle and watch the sun go down on the shortest day of the year.

So let us begin, on a cold morning, long ago. A family is walking slowly across the plain toward the stones. Their cattle walk beside them, breathing little clouds into the frosty air. And the great grey ring is waiting for them on the hill.
