Tonight we go about four and a half thousand years back. We go to a wide green plain in the south of England. The year is around 2500 before Christ. And on that plain, people are raising great stones toward the sky.

--

It is early morning. Mist lies over the rolling grassland. The sky in the east is turning pink. And in the middle of the plain, there is a ring. A ring of huge grey stones, standing upright. Some are taller than three people. Some have great flat stones lying across their tops, like the lintel of a door. Some are not finished yet. Wooden frames lean against them. Ropes lie coiled on the grass. Smoke rises from a fire nearby, where people are waking up.

This is Stonehenge. One of the most famous places in the world. And one of the most mysterious.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

{img:neolithic-people-hauling-sarsen-sledge-ropes-many-people-chalk-plain}
Who built it? How? Why? For hundreds of years, people have wondered. Some said it was built by giants. Some said a wizard flew the stones there by magic. Some said it was built by Romans, or by druids, or by people from far away. None of that was true.

It was built by the people who lived here. Farmers and herders. People with no metal tools, no wheels, no writing. Just stone and wood and antler and rope. And their own strength. And their own cleverness. And a great shared dream.

{img:midsummer-sunrise-through-stonehenge-trilithons-golden-light-ancient}
In the last fifty years, archaeologists have learned an amazing amount. They have found where the stones came from. Some from not far away. Some from the mountains of Wales, two hundred kilometres away. One even from the far north of Scotland. They have found the great village where the builders gathered for their feasts. They have found the bones of the pigs they ate. And they have learned that Stonehenge is lined up with the sun.

--

{img:neolithic-family-walking-across-plain-toward-stones-winter-morning-cattle}
Tonight we will walk on this plain in the time of the builders. We will meet a family who comes here for the midwinter feast. We will follow the long journey of the bluestones from Wales. We will help pull a great sarsen stone across the hills. We will watch the stones being raised and the lintels lifted. And at the end, we will stand inside the ring and watch the sun rise on the longest day of the year.
