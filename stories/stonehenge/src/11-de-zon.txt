Why was Stonehenge built? We may never know everything. But one thing is clear. It is lined up with the sun.

If you stand in the middle of Stonehenge on the shortest day of the year, in winter, and look south-west, you see the sun go down. Exactly between the stones of the tallest trilithon. As if the stones were made to frame it.

--

And if you stand in the middle on the longest day of the year, in summer, and look the other way, north-east, you see the sun come up. Right along the path that leads into the circle. Over a large stone that stands outside the ring. The Heel Stone.

The midwinter sunset and the midsummer sunrise. They are on the same line. The builders lined the whole monument up along this line. On purpose. With great care.

{img:midsummer-sunrise-heel-stone-avenue-golden-rays-mist-dawn-ancient}
For farming people, the year was everything. The seasons told them when to plant and when to harvest. When the animals would have their young. When the cold would come. And the turning points of the year were the solstices. The longest day and the shortest day.

--

Midwinter must have been a time of worry. The days get shorter and shorter. The sun gets lower and lower. It is cold and dark. Food is running low. What if the sun kept going away? What if it never came back? At midwinter, the sun seems to stop. And then it turns. The days start to get longer again. The light comes back.

Many experts think midwinter was the most important time at Stonehenge. The great feast at Durrington Walls was at midwinter. The pig bones tell us that. Perhaps people gathered at Stonehenge to watch the last sunset of the dying year. To say goodbye to the old sun. And to welcome the new one.

{img:people-watching-solstice-silhouettes-stones-sky-orange-quiet-awe}
Standing in the circle, with thousands of others. Watching the sun sink between the great stones. Feeling the cold. Feeling the dark coming. And knowing that tomorrow, the light would begin to return. It must have been a powerful feeling. Even today, people who stand there at the solstice say they feel it too.
