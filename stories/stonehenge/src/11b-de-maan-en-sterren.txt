The sun was not the only thing in the sky the builders watched. They watched the moon too. And the stars.

The moon moves in a more complicated way than the sun. It rises and sets in different places over the months. And over a long cycle of about eighteen and a half years, its path swings from far north to far south and back. Some people think the builders knew about this. And that some of the stones may point to where the moon rises at its furthest points.

--

That is still being argued about. But it is certain that the builders watched the sky carefully. Night after night. Year after year. For generations. They had no writing to record what they saw. So they remembered. In stories. In songs. In the positions of stones and posts.

Without city lights, the night sky in the Stone Age was amazing. Thousands and thousands of stars. The Milky Way stretching across the sky like a river of light. The planets moving slowly among the stars. Shooting stars. The moon, bright enough to cast shadows.

{img:milky-way-over-neolithic-plain-wooden-posts-silhouette-people-gazing}
For people who lived close to nature, the sky was a great clock and a great calendar. The stars told you the time of night. The moon told you the time of the month. The sun told you the time of year. When certain stars rose at dawn, it was time to plant. When others appeared, it was time to bring the animals in.

--

The grandmother of our family knows all this. On the walk home from the feast, she points to the stars and teaches the children. That bright one is the herder's star, she says. When it rises early, the calves are born. Those seven little ones close together are the sisters. When they go away in spring, it is time to plant. The children look up. And they try to remember.

{img:grandmother-pointing-stars-children-night-walk-frost-breath}
Pip asks why the stars do not fall down. The grandmother laughs. Because the sky holds them, she says. Like the earth holds the stones. Pip thinks about that for a long time as they walk. His breath makes little clouds in the cold air. And above them, the stars shine on.
