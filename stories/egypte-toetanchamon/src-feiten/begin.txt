In November 1922, an Englishman stood in a narrow rock-cut passage in Egypt and held a candle up to a small hole in an ancient wall. Warm air flowed out from the darkness and made the flame flicker. Then, slowly, shapes began to appear. Strange animals, statues, and everywhere, the glint of gold.

The tomb belonged to a young king called Tutankhamun. Today, his golden mask is one of the most famous faces in the world. Yet the king himself was almost forgotten for three thousand years. And the people of his time, the farmers, the scribes, the tomb-makers and the children, are hardly remembered at all. So what was life really like in Egypt, when Tutankhamun was king?
