# Outline: How Vikings Survived the Long Dark Winter (Sleepy Facts, 72 facts, 6 delen x 12)
Deel 1 (autumn, preparing): 1 Vikings counted years in winters / only two seasons; 2 last hay harvest for animals; 3 autumn slaughter month and preserving meat (gentle, no gore); 4 drying stockfish on racks; 5 storing food in whey/skyr/salt/barrels; 6 stacking firewood and peat/turf; 7 repairing the turf roof and walls; 8 ships hauled ashore and covered for winter; 9 last trading ships leave, the market closes; 10 gathering wool, flax and last berries/nuts/herbs; 11 how a longhouse was built (timber, turf, wattle); 12 the long open hearth in the middle, no chimney.
Deel 2 (inside the house): 13 benches and sleeping platforms along the walls; 14 box beds / bedding of wool and fur; 15 keeping the fire alive overnight; 16 lamps (fish oil, tallow, soapstone) and rushlight; 17 the high-seat pillars and the seat of honour; 18 smoke hole and the first windows (wind-eye); 19 animals in the byre sharing warmth; 20 dogs, cats and horses; 21 two meals a day: dagverðr and náttverðr; 22 porridge, bread and flatbread on the griddle; 23 ale and mead, brewing in winter; 24 skyr, butter, cheese and the cauldron soup.
Deel 3 (clothes and work): 25 wool tunics, layers, the cloak and brooch; 26 spinning wool by the fire; 27 the warp-weighted loom; 28 furs, leather and felt hats; 29 shoes, socks and nålebinding mittens; 30 bathing: the Saturday wash (laugardagr), combs and hair care; 31 carving wood and bone, antler combs; 32 the winter smithy and iron; 33 mending ships and making rope/sailcloth indoors; 34 skis, snowshoes and bone ice skates; 35 winter hunting (elk, hare, seals) and fishing through ice; 36 sledges and horses with ice-studded shoes.
Deel 4 (people and time): 37 who lived in a longhouse (household, kin, servants); 38 the mistress of the farm and her keys; 39 thralls and farmhands in winter (calm, factual); 40 children in winter: chores, toys, learning; 41 the board game hnefatafl; 42 storytelling by the fire and the sagas (oral); 43 skaldic poetry and kennings; 44 riddles and wordplay; 45 runes carved on sticks and bone; 46 the calendar stick (primstav) and counting winter; 47 winter months with old names (Gormánuðr, Ýlir, Mörsugur, Þorri, Góa); 48 hospitality: strangers at the door in winter.
Deel 5 (belief, Yule, the dark sky): 49 Winter Nights (Vetrnætr) festival; 50 Álfablót and the elves; 51 Yule/Jól at midwinter; 52 the Yule feast, the boar and toasts; 53 Odin and the Wild Hunt in the winter sky; 54 house spirits and ancestors in the grave mounds; 55 the myth of Fimbulwinter; 56 Sól and Máni, the sun and moon chased by wolves (myth); 57 the Northern Lights in Norse imagination; 58 stars and navigation in long nights; 59 Thor, storms and the thunder that sleeps in winter; 60 Freyr and Freyja, hopes for a fertile spring.
Deel 6 (late winter, far places, spring): 61 Iceland's winter on the farm; 62 Greenland: winter at the edge; 63 the hungry weeks of late winter (Þorri/Góa) and rationing; 64 weather lore and reading the sky; 65 illness, herbs and healing women (gentle); 66 birth and midwives in winter; 67 old people and storytelling grandparents; 68 winter assemblies and law (Icelandic things), travel over frozen ground; 69 the frozen fjords and rivers as roads; 70 the days lengthen, first signs of spring; 71 first lambs, ploughing and sowing; 72 the ships go back into the water; what archaeology tells us today (Oseberg, Jorvik, L'Anse aux Meadows) as the closing fact.
