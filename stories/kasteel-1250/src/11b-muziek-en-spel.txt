In the long evenings, the castle is full of music, stories and games.

A great household has its own minstrels. Minstrels are musicians who play at dinner, in the evenings and at feasts. Travelling minstrels come too. They walk from one great house to the next, with their instruments on their backs. They hope for a meal, a bed and a little money.

--

There are many instruments in 1250.

There is the small harp, held on the knee. There is a kind of fiddle, played with a bow. There is the lute, newly arrived from Arab lands through Spain. There are pipes and flutes, and small drums. One man can play a pipe with one hand and a drum with the other.

{img:medieval-instruments-vielle-psaltery-harp-table-chamber}
The minstrels play dances for the lords and ladies. Everyone holds hands in a circle or a long line, and sings as they dance. The minstrels also sing love songs and ballads, mostly in French, sometimes in English.

--

And they tell stories. These are long stories, sung or chanted to the sound of the harp.

There are the stories of King Arthur and his knights. Lancelot, and Gawain, and the search for the Holy Grail. There are stories of the great king Charlemagne, and of the old war at Troy. Some of these stories take many evenings to tell. Each night the minstrel stops at an exciting moment. Then everyone has to wait until tomorrow to hear what happens next.

One famous song of this time is in English, and people still sing it today. It begins: Sumer is icumen in, lhude sing cuccu. That means: summer is coming in, loudly sing, cuckoo. It is one of the oldest English songs we can still sing.

--

There are games too.

Chess is the great game of the nobles. The pieces are carved from ivory, bone or wood. Lords and ladies play together for hours, by the fire. Playing chess well shows that you were raised well.

{img:lord-and-lady-playing-chess-ivory-pieces-fireside-chamber}
There is a game like backgammon, played with dice. And there is a simpler game with lines and counters. The guards scratch its board into stone benches and window sills, so they can play while they wait.

--

People play dice too, and bet on them, though the Church does not like it. Playing cards will not come to Europe for another hundred years. But there are riddles and word games and blind man's buff.

On summer evenings, people play outside. They roll bowls on the grass and hold archery contests in the meadow.

{img:summer-evening-meadow-bowls-archery-household-relaxing-castle}
And sometimes visitors bring their own fun. There are jugglers and acrobats, and men with dancing bears. There are jesters who tell jokes, and make everyone laugh. Even the serious earl smiles at them now and then.
