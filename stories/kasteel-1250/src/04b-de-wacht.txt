Even in times of peace, a castle is always watched.

There is a small group of soldiers at Kenilworth. They are led by the constable, a knight the lord trusts. When the lord is away, the constable is in charge of the castle. Under him serve watchmen, archers and the porter, who keeps the gate.

--

The porter lives in a little room in the gatehouse. He sees everyone who comes in and goes out. He knows every face in the household, and he asks strangers their business. At night he closes the great gates. He bars them with heavy wooden beams. Then he lowers the portcullis, and keeps the keys.

On the walls, the watchmen walk all night with their lanterns. They go from tower to tower. They look out over the dark water and the sleeping fields. And they listen.

{img:watchman-blowing-horn-tower-top-dawn-mist-calm}
At certain times they blow a horn, to show they are awake and all is well. If they see or hear anything strange, they must raise the alarm. Most nights, nothing happens at all. But a watchman can never be sure which night will be different.

--

A great castle must always be ready for a siege. A siege is when an enemy surrounds a castle and waits, to starve the people inside.

So there are big stores in the keep and the cellars. There is grain and salted meat, dried fish and barrels of ale. There are arrows by the bundle, and stones to throw down from the walls. And inside the castle there is a deep well, which will not run dry.

{img:keep-cellar-stores-barrels-grain-sacks-arrows-bundles}
The gatehouse has its own tricks. In the ceiling of the gate passage there are holes, so that things can be dropped on attackers. But the best defence of Kenilworth is the water of the mere. It keeps any enemy far from the walls.

--

In 1250, though, the guards have a quiet life.

They stand at the gate in the sunshine and chat with the carters and the villagers. They practise with their bows at targets in the yard. They polish their helmets. And when the constable is not looking, they play little board games, scratched into the stone of the window sills.

{img:guardroom-soldiers-playing-merels-dice-stone-bench-lamp}
At night they walk the walls under the stars. They look out over the quiet water, the dark woods and the few lights of the village. They think of their families, and wait for the dawn. None of them knows that, some years from now, these same walls will be tested as never before.
