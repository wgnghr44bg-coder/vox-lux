Religion is part of every hour of life in the castle, in 1250.

Every morning, the lord and lady hear Mass, in the chapel, with their household. They pray at night, and in the morning, and before and after meals. They give alms to the poor, every day. They keep the fast days, and the feast days. They go on pilgrimages, to the shrines of the saints.

--

The year is shaped by the great feasts of the Church. And the greatest of all, in the castle, is Christmas.

Christmas, in 1250, is not one day, but twelve days, from Christmas Day, on the twenty-fifth of December, to Twelfth Night, on the sixth of January. Before it, there are the four weeks of Advent, a time of fasting, and preparation, with no meat, and quiet.

In the days before Christmas, the castle is busy. The servants bring in great loads of holly, and ivy, and bay, and green branches, from the woods, to decorate the hall, and the chapel. A great log, the Yule log, is dragged in, to burn in the hall fire, for the twelve days. The cooks prepare enormous quantities of food. Guests arrive, with their servants, and their horses, from all over the country.

--

On Christmas Eve, everyone goes to the chapel, for midnight Mass, by candlelight.

And then, on Christmas Day, the feast begins. The hall is full of people, and light, and noise, and the smell of roasting meat. There is a great dinner: roast boar, with its head carried in on a great platter, decorated with rosemary and bay, with an apple in its mouth, while everyone sings. And roast geese, and swans, and capons, and venison, and pies, and frumenty, and wine, and ale, and sweet spiced cakes.

{img:boars-head-carried-in-platter-feast-hall-singing-candles}
The lord gives gifts to his household: new clothes, the livery for the year, and money, and presents. And the poor of the village are invited to the castle, and given food, and drink, and alms.

--

During the twelve days, there is no work, except what is necessary. There is feasting, and music, and dancing, and singing of carols, round dances, with everyone holding hands, in a ring, singing. There are games, and riddles, and plays, and mummers, people in masks and costumes, who come to the hall and perform, and dance, and act out stories, without speaking.

On Twelfth Night, there is the last great feast. A bean is baked into a cake, and whoever finds the bean in his slice becomes the King of the Bean, and rules over the feast, and everyone must obey his silly commands.

{img:mummers-masks-costumes-dancing-great-hall-firelight}
Then Christmas is over, and the guests go home, and the hall is cleaned, and the green branches are taken down and burned, and the castle goes back to its ordinary life, in the cold, dark days of January.

--

There are other great feasts, through the year.

Candlemas, at the beginning of February, when candles are blessed in the chapel. Easter, the greatest feast of the Church, after the long fast of Lent, with eggs, which were forbidden in Lent, and new clothes, and feasting. Whitsun, in early summer, when knights are often made, and there are tournaments. Midsummer, with bonfires, on the night of Saint John. Lammas, at the beginning of August, the feast of the first bread from the new harvest. Michaelmas, at the end of September, when rents are paid, and accounts are made up, and geese are eaten.

And the feast days of the saints, many of them, each with its own customs, and its own special foods, and prayers.

--

Simon de Montfort is a very religious man. He goes to Mass often, and prays at night. He is a friend of the friars, the Franciscans and the Dominicans, who are new in England in these years, and who preach to the people, and live in poverty. He gives money to churches, and hospitals, and to the poor.

And he has a great devotion to the pilgrimage. Before he married, he went on crusade, to the Holy Land, to Jerusalem. And all his life, he will remember it.

{img:knight-praying-alone-chapel-night-candle-hair-shirt}
At Kenilworth, in the evening, after supper, when the household has gone to bed, he sometimes goes to the chapel alone, and kneels there, for a long time, in the candlelight, praying, while the castle sleeps around him.
