Who are the lord and lady of Kenilworth?

Simon de Montfort is about forty-two years old in 1250. He was born in France, around the year 1208, the youngest son of a famous and fierce French nobleman, also called Simon de Montfort, who led a cruel crusade in the south of France, and was killed when Simon was still a boy.

--

The family had a claim, through Simon's grandmother, to the great English earldom of Leicester. Simon's older brother, Amaury, was the heir. But Amaury gave his claim to Simon. And so, in 1230, at the age of about twenty-two, Simon came to England, with almost nothing, a young French knight, speaking French and Latin, but hardly a word of English, to win back his inheritance.

He was tall, and handsome, and clever, and charming, and very proud, and very religious, and very ambitious. The young King Henry liked him at once, and gave him the earldom of Leicester, and made him one of his closest friends.

Simon is a serious man. He reads a great deal, and talks with learned churchmen, and is a close friend of Robert Grosseteste, the great Bishop of Lincoln, one of the cleverest men in England, and of the Franciscan friar Adam Marsh, who writes him long letters of advice, telling him to control his temper, and to be gentle with his wife.

--

He prays a great deal. He often gets up in the night to pray. He wears a hair shirt, under his fine clothes, a rough garment of goat's hair, which scratches the skin, as a penance. He eats and drinks little. And he has a fierce temper, and does not forgive easily.

He is also a great soldier, perhaps the best in England. He has fought in France, and in the Holy Land, on crusade. And in 1250, he is far away, much of the time, in Gascony, in the south west of France, which belongs to the King of England, governing it for King Henry, and fighting the rebellious lords there.

{img:countess-eleanor-noblewoman-fine-gown-veil-castle-chamber}
His wife is Eleanor, the youngest sister of King Henry. She is about thirty-five, in 1250.

--

Eleanor was born in 1215, the youngest child of King John, and his queen, Isabella of Angoulême. When she was only nine, she was married to a great English lord, William Marshal, the Earl of Pembroke, the son of the most famous knight in England. But William died, when she was sixteen.

Heartbroken, the young widow swore a solemn vow, before the Archbishop of Canterbury, that she would never marry again, and would live a chaste and holy life, for the rest of her days.

{img:young-widow-kneeling-before-archbishop-vow-cathedral-candles}
But then she met Simon de Montfort. And they fell in love. And in January 1238, they were married, quietly, secretly almost, in the King's own little chapel, at the palace of Westminster, with the King himself giving his sister away.

There was a great scandal. Because of her vow, many people said the marriage was wrong. The King's brother, Richard, was furious, because he had not been asked. Simon had to go to Rome, to ask the Pope to approve the marriage. But in the end, it was accepted.

--

Simon and Eleanor are a devoted couple. They are both proud, and strong-willed, and quick-tempered, and they quarrel sometimes. But they love each other deeply, and they stand together, through everything.

Eleanor is a great lady. She lives in splendour, as a king's daughter should. She loves fine clothes, and jewels, and furs, and silk, and spends a great deal of money, and is often in debt. She runs her great household with energy, and authority. She manages the estates, and the money, when Simon is away. She is clever, and determined, and brave.

And they have a family. By 1250, they have four or five sons: Henry, who is about twelve, and Simon, about ten, and Amaury, and Guy, and perhaps already little Richard, the youngest. And two years later, in 1252, a daughter will be born, Eleanor, called after her mother.

--

The King, Henry the Third, is their brother and their friend. But he is a difficult king.

He is gentle, and kind, and very religious, and he loves beautiful things: churches, and paintings, and gold, and jewels. He is rebuilding Westminster Abbey, in London, in the beautiful new Gothic style, as a tribute to Saint Edward the Confessor, whom he loves. But he is not a good ruler. He changes his mind often, and spends too much money, and listens to foreign favourites, and quarrels with his barons.

{img:king-henry-third-gentle-crowned-man-westminster-abbey-building}
And he quarrels, sometimes, with Simon, his proud and difficult brother-in-law. They will be friends, and then enemies, and then friends again. And in the end, their quarrel will change the history of England.

But that is still in the future. In 1250, Kenilworth is at peace, and its life goes on, day after day, in the rhythm of the seasons.
