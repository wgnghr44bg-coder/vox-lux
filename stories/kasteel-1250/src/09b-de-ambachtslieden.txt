A great castle is like a little town. It has its own craftsmen, who keep everything working.

The most important one is the smith. His forge stands in the outer yard. A boy works the bellows to make the fire roar. All day long, the ring of the hammer sounds across the yard.

--

The smith makes and mends everything of iron. Horseshoes and nails for all the horses. Hinges, locks and keys for the doors. Tools for the gardeners, and pots and chains for the kitchen.

Sometimes he mends a knight's armour. But the best armour is made by special craftsmen in the towns. One mail shirt is made of perhaps thirty thousand little rings. Each ring is closed by hand. It takes months to make one.

{img:armourer-linking-mail-rings-workbench-careful-light}
There are other craftsmen too. One man shoes the horses and treats them when they are sick. Another makes saddles. One makes bows, and another makes arrows with goose feathers. In peaceful times, they make only a few arrows each week. If war ever comes, they will have to make thousands.

--

The carpenters make and mend everything of wood. Roofs and floors, doors and shutters, tables and beds, carts and barrels.

The masons look after the stone walls. A castle is never finished. There is always a wall to repair, or a new tower to build.

{img:masons-repairing-castle-wall-scaffold-ropes-pulley-summer}
There is even a plumber. His name comes from the Latin word for lead. He makes lead sheets for the roofs and lead pipes for water.

--

There is the glazier, who makes windows. But glass is still rare and costly in 1250. It is greenish and full of little bubbles. Most windows in the castle have no glass at all. They only have wooden shutters, or thin sheets of horn that let in a little light.

There are tailors and fur workers, who make the clothes of the household. And there are the washerwomen. They wash the sheets and shirts in big tubs. Instead of soap they often use water mixed with wood ash. Then they spread the linen on the grass and the hedges to dry in the sun.

{img:laundresses-washing-linen-tubs-drying-on-hedges-sunny}
Without these people and their hard work, the castle could not live. They are the quiet, busy heart of Kenilworth.
