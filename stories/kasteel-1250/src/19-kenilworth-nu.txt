Today, Kenilworth Castle still stands on its low hill in the green Warwickshire countryside. It is a great ruin of red stone, cared for by English Heritage.

The little town of Kenilworth has grown up around it, with houses, shops and pubs. But the castle still rises above everything, as it did in 1250.

--

You walk in through Robert Dudley's gatehouse, which still stands, with its little turrets.

Then you are in the outer yard, on the green grass. In front of you rise the great red walls. There is the keep, with one side blown away, standing like a broken tooth against the sky. There are the tall, empty windows of John of Gaunt's great hall, with the sky shining through them.

You can walk along the walls and climb some of the towers on new wooden stairs. From there you look out over the fields where the great lake used to be. You can imagine the water shining all around, and the swans, and the boats.

--

In the keep, the oldest part, you can still see the thick walls and the little windows. You can see the marks where the floors once were.

In John of Gaunt's great hall, you stand on the grass where the floor used to be. You look up at the tall windows and the fireplaces high in the walls. And you can imagine the long tables, the music and the firelight.

{img:great-hall-ruin-tall-tracery-windows-sky-grass-floor}
In Dudley's tower, new stairs take you up to where the Queen's rooms once were. You can stand where she stood, and look out through her windows.

--

The Elizabethan garden has been made again, just as it was in 1575. That was possible because a man who saw it wrote a careful description at the time.

It is a neat and lovely garden. There are sandy paths, low hedges and flowers from that time. There is a white marble fountain, and a big painted cage full of singing birds.

In the old stables there is a café, and an exhibition that tells the castle's whole story.

--

The land where the lake once lay is now green meadow, with cows and sheep and paths for walking.

In winter, after heavy rain, the meadows sometimes flood. For a few days the water spreads around the castle again. And once more, the red walls are mirrored in it, as in the days of Simon and Eleanor.

{img:flooded-meadows-castle-reflection-winter-sunset-still}
Along King John's old dam, people walk their dogs. They look across at the castle, where eight hundred years ago the King's army camped through that long siege.

--

In summer there are knights in armour at the castle, and shows with falcons swooping over the grass. Children dressed as knights and princesses run across the lawns with wooden swords.

And in the quiet evenings, when the visitors have gone, the red stone glows deep and warm, as it did in 1250. The swallows dip over the meadows where the lake used to be.

It is very peaceful.

--

So we leave the castle of Kenilworth for tonight, with the red walls glowing in the evening light, and the meadows quiet where the great lake once lay.

We leave the lord and lady at their high table, the cook with his big wooden spoon, the squires at the quintain, and the children asleep behind the curtains of their bed, nearly eight hundred years ago.
