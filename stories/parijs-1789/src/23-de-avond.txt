So we leave Paris tonight, in the summer of 1789, as the evening falls.

The sun is setting over the rooftops of the city. The sky turns gold, then pink, then a deep soft blue. On the river, the boats are tied up for the night. The washerwomen have gone home. The booksellers on the quays are closing their boxes. And all over the city, candles are being lit in the windows, one by one.

--

In the Faubourg Saint-Antoine, the workshops are closed for the day. The street is quiet. A few neighbours are sitting on their doorsteps, enjoying the cool evening air. Children are playing a last game before bedtime. A cat walks slowly along the top of a wall. And at the end of the street, where the dark towers of the Bastille once stood, there is now open sky, with the first stars coming out.

{img:courtyard-neighbours-evening-candle-wine-jug-singing-quiet-1789}
In the courtyard of the old house, the neighbours are sitting together. The water carrier is singing a song from his mountains, softly. The lace maker is nodding along. Étienne is reading a newspaper by the light of a candle. Antoine sits beside him, smoking a little pipe, listening. And Marguerite comes down with a jug of wine and some cups, and sits down with them.

--

Upstairs, Mémé is sitting in her chair by the window, knitting slowly. She looks out at the evening sky. She has seen many summers. But she thinks that this one will be remembered for a long, long time. She thinks of her own mother, and of the long hard winters long ago. And she says a little prayer for her grandchildren, that their lives will be a little easier than hers.

{img:children-asleep-bed-small-room-bastille-stone-shelf-candle-1789}
In the little bedroom, Paul is already asleep, with his paper cockade still pinned to his shirt. Louise is lying awake for a moment, looking at the ceiling. In her hand, she holds the little paper with her name printed on it. She thinks about all the things she has seen this summer. The crowds and the cafés, the leaves and the cockades, the king at the window and the words of the declaration. She thinks about the future, and wonders what it will bring.

--

Then she closes her eyes. Outside, the sounds of the city grow softer. A last cart rumbles over the cobblestones. A dog barks once, far away. The bells of a church ring out the hour, slow and deep. And over the rooftops of Paris, the summer stars shine down on the sleeping city, and on the quiet river, and on all its hopes and dreams.
