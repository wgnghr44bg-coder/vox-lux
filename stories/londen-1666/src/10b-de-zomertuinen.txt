On fine summer afternoons, the people of London love to get out of the crowded, noisy, smoky city, into the fresh air of the countryside.

The countryside is very close. A short walk north, out through the city gates, and you are in the fields: meadows full of buttercups and cows, with hedges and trees, and little villages, Islington, Hoxton, Hackney, with their inns and their gardens.

--

On the other side of the river, at Vauxhall, there is a famous pleasure garden, called the New Spring Garden.

To get there, you take a boat across the river from the stairs at Westminster or the Temple. It is a large, beautiful garden, with long, shady walks between hedges and trees, and arbours and bowers, where people can sit and eat and drink, and listen to the nightingales singing in the trees.

There is no fee to get in. People bring their own food, or buy cakes and cheesecakes and syllabubs and wine, and walk, and talk, and flirt, and listen to the musicians, who play in the bowers. It is very fashionable, and very pleasant, on a summer evening.

--

Pepys loves the Spring Garden. He goes there often, with Elizabeth, and with his friends.

He writes that it is very pleasant and cheap going there, for a man may spend what he will, or nothing, all as one. But to hear the nightingale and other birds, and here fiddles and there a harp, and here a Jew's trump, and here laughing, and there fine people walking, is mighty divertising.

{img:musicians-harp-fiddle-garden-arbour-people-listening}
He walks there, and listens to the birds, and eats a cheesecake, and drinks a glass of wine, and goes home by boat in the moonlight, singing.

--

Closer to the city, there are other places to walk.

There are Moorfields, just outside the city wall to the north, a great open space of grass and trees and gravel walks, where the people of the City walk in the evenings, and the women spread their washing on the grass to dry, and the young men play games and practise archery.

There is Hyde Park, to the west, where the fashionable people drive round and round in their coaches, in a great circle called the Ring, to see and be seen, and the ladies show off their fine dresses and their coaches. Pepys sometimes hires a coach, so that he and Elizabeth can drive round the Ring with the fashionable people.

--

And there is Saint James's Park, beside the palace of Whitehall, where the King has made a long, straight canal, lined with trees, and keeps ducks and geese and birds of every kind, and the pelicans from Russia.

The people of London walk there, and watch the birds, and watch the King, who walks very fast, with his spaniels and his courtiers, and plays a game called pall mall, a kind of croquet, along a long, straight alley, which later gives its name to a famous street.

{img:st-james-park-canal-ducks-pelicans-walkers-summer}
In the winter, when the canal freezes, the courtiers skate on it, in a new Dutch way, gliding gracefully along on iron skates, which the King has brought back from Holland. Pepys and Evelyn both write about it, amazed at how fast and how gracefully the skaters move.

--

There are other pleasures too.

There are bowling greens, behind the taverns, where the gentlemen play bowls on summer evenings. There are archery grounds. There are boat trips on the river, up to Barn Elms or Richmond, among the green meadows and the willows, with a picnic. There are horse races, and cockfights, and bear gardens on the south bank, which Pepys goes to see, and does not like very much.

And there are the simple pleasures of walking in the fields, picking flowers, sitting under a tree, and looking back at the city, with its church towers and its great old cathedral, on its hill, golden in the evening sun.

--

In the summer of 1666, Pepys and Elizabeth go out to the fields and the gardens many times.

They walk in the Spring Garden, and drive in Hyde Park, and take boats up the river. They sing in the boat, on the way home, as the sun goes down, and the river turns gold, and the city appears ahead of them, with its hundred church towers, and the lights coming on in the windows.

{img:couple-boat-singing-sunset-river-city-towers-golden}
It is a lovely summer, warm and dry and golden. And nobody, as they sit in their boats on the river in the summer evenings, listening to the nightingales, imagines what will happen at the beginning of September.
