So we leave Versailles tonight, in the summer of 1685, as the evening falls. The sun is setting at the far end of the Grand Canal, exactly where the king wanted it to set. Its last golden light shines all the way down the long water, over the gardens, and onto the windows of the palace, which glow like gold. The whole palace seems to be on fire with light.

--

In the gardens, the fountains are silent now. The courtiers have gone inside, for supper and cards and music. The gravel paths are empty. Only the statues remain, standing quietly among the clipped trees, white and still in the fading light. The swallows are swooping over the water. And from somewhere in the groves, a nightingale is beginning to sing.

{img:palace-windows-glowing-candles-dusk-terrace-orange-trees-scent}
In the palace, the candles are being lit, one by one, in the chandeliers and the silver candlesticks. Through the tall windows, you can see the glow of the candlelight, and the shapes of people moving inside. The soft sound of violins drifts out through an open window. Somewhere, people are laughing. And on the terrace below, the orange trees are in flower, and their sweet scent hangs in the warm evening air.

--

Down in the town, in a small house on a quiet street, a gardener's family is having supper. Soup and bread and a few ripe figs from the king's garden. Jacques is telling Marguerite about a pear tree that is doing well. Pierre is drawing a fountain on a scrap of paper. And Jeanne is sitting by the open window, looking up at the palace on its hill, glowing in the last light, like something from a dream.

{img:moonlight-over-versailles-gardens-statues-silver-water-quiet-night}
Later, when everyone in the town is asleep, the moon rises over Versailles. It shines on the long avenues, on the still pools, and on the marble statues of the gods. It shines on the Grand Canal, where the gondolas are tied up at the bank, rocking gently on the water. It shines on the kitchen garden, where the pears are slowly ripening on the warm stone walls. And it shines on the roof of the palace, where the Sun King is finally sleeping too.

--

The gardens are silent. The water is still. A gentle breeze moves through the trees, and the leaves whisper softly. Somewhere, a fountain drips, slowly, drop by drop, into its stone basin. Tomorrow, the sun will rise again over the palace, and the whole show will begin once more. But for now, everything at Versailles is resting, in the soft summer night.
