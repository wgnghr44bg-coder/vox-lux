Before the cathedral was built in the valley, there was an older cathedral, on a hill, about three kilometres to the north. The place was called Sarum. Today, it is called Old Sarum. It is a big round hill, surrounded by enormous earth banks and ditches, made by the people of the Iron Age, long before the Romans. Later, the Romans, the Saxons and the Normans all used it. And on top of the hill, the Normans built a castle, and a cathedral, side by side.

--

The old cathedral at Sarum was built about a hundred and fifty years before our story. But it was not a happy place. The hill was high and bare, and the wind blew constantly, so loudly that the priests in the church could hardly hear themselves sing. There was very little water. The priests had to buy it from the soldiers of the castle, at a high price. In winter, it was bitterly cold. And the cathedral was crowded inside the walls, with no room to grow.

Worst of all, the priests and the soldiers of the castle did not get along. They quarrelled constantly. There is a famous story, that one day, the priests went out of the castle for a procession, and when they came back, the soldiers had locked the gates, and would not let them in. The priests had to spend the whole night outside, in the cold and the wind.

{img:bishop-richard-poore-medieval-robes-looking-at-valley-plans-hopeful}
So the bishop, a man called Richard Poore, decided to do something bold. He would move the whole cathedral, and the whole town, down from the windy hill, into the green valley below. Where there was plenty of water, from the five rivers. Where there was room to build. And where there were no soldiers. He asked the Pope for permission, and the Pope agreed.

There is a lovely old legend about how the new place was chosen. The story says that the bishop asked an archer to shoot an arrow from the walls of Old Sarum, and promised to build the new cathedral wherever the arrow landed. The arrow flew far, far down into the valley. It hit a white deer, which ran on, and fell down in a meadow by the river. And there, the bishop built his cathedral. Of course, the meadow is more than three kilometres from the hill, much too far for any arrow. But it is a good story.

--

{img:foundation-stones-laid-ceremony-1220-bishop-crowds-meadow-banners}
On the twenty-eighth of April, in the year 1220, the new cathedral was founded. There was a great ceremony, in the meadow. The bishop laid the first three foundation stones. One for the Pope. One for the archbishop of Canterbury. And one for himself. Then the great nobles of the county came forward, and each laid a stone. And a crowd of people watched, and sang, and prayed. The work had begun.

The first part to be built was the far eastern end, the Lady Chapel, dedicated to the Virgin Mary. It took five years. In 1225, it was finished, and the first services were held in it. The bishop brought the bones of three earlier bishops from Old Sarum, and buried them in the new church. Then the builders moved on to the next part, and the next, working slowly westward, year after year.

{img:people-leaving-old-sarum-carts-belongings-walking-down-to-valley}
Slowly, the people of Old Sarum moved down into the valley too. The priests, the merchants, the craftsmen and their families. They left the windy hill, and built new houses in the new town, near the new cathedral. Later, the old cathedral on the hill was taken apart, and its stones were reused. Some of them were brought down into the valley, and built into the walls of the new close around the cathedral.

--

By 1250, Old Sarum is almost empty. Only the castle is still there, with a few soldiers, and a few houses. The wind still blows across the hill, as it always has. And from the top of the old banks, you can look down into the valley, and see the great new cathedral rising among the green meadows, pale and beautiful, with the rivers shining around it. The bishop's bold idea has worked.
