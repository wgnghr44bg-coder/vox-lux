Why did people in the Middle Ages build cathedrals? A cathedral is the main church of a bishop, the leader of the church in a large area, called a diocese. The word comes from the Latin word cathedra, which means a chair. Because in the cathedral, there is the bishop's special chair, or throne. But a cathedral was much more than just a big church.

--

For the people of the Middle Ages, a cathedral was a picture of heaven on earth. When you walked inside, you were supposed to feel that you had left the ordinary world behind, and entered the house of God. Everything was designed to lift your eyes and your heart upward. The tall columns, rising like the trunks of great trees. The pointed arches, meeting high above, like hands in prayer. And the windows, full of coloured light.

In the time of our story, a new style of building has spread across Europe. Today, we call it Gothic. It began in France, about a hundred years before, near Paris. And it changed everything. Before, churches were built in a heavy style, with thick walls, round arches and small windows. Inside, they were dark. The new Gothic style used pointed arches, which are much stronger. So the walls could be thinner and taller, and the windows could be much bigger.

{img:pointed-arch-versus-round-arch-diagram-feeling-stone-masons-explaining}
The Gothic builders discovered something wonderful. With pointed arches, ribbed vaults and slender columns, they could build higher and lighter than anyone had ever built before. They could fill the walls with windows. And the light could pour in, through coloured glass, filling the church with glowing light. For them, light was a symbol of God. So the more light, the closer to heaven.

In France, the great Gothic cathedrals are rising at this time. Notre-Dame in Paris. Chartres, with its famous blue windows. Reims, where the kings of France are crowned. Amiens, the tallest of them all. And in England, the builders are learning the new style, and making it their own. They build in a slightly simpler, more elegant way, which is called Early English Gothic. And Salisbury is its most perfect example.

--

{img:villagers-donating-coins-chest-cathedral-building-fund-priest-blessing}
Building a cathedral costs an enormous amount of money. Where does it come from? The bishop gives money from his own income. The priests of the cathedral give a part of their income, every year. The king gives timber from his forests, and gifts of money. Rich nobles and their wives give money, hoping for prayers for their souls. And ordinary people give too. Pennies, eggs, chickens, a day's work. Preachers travel around the diocese, asking for gifts for the new cathedral.

For many people, giving to the cathedral is a way of giving to God. They believe it will help them in this life, and in the next. And they are proud of their cathedral. It belongs to everyone in the diocese, rich and poor. It is the biggest and most beautiful thing that any of them will ever see. It will be there for their children, and their grandchildren, and for hundreds of years after that.

{img:cathedral-silhouette-sunset-town-around-it-tallest-building-medieval}
A cathedral is also the heart of a town. It brings pilgrims, visitors and trade. It needs workers, and workers need houses, food and clothes. Around a new cathedral, a new town grows. Markets, inns, shops and workshops. And so, in the valley of the five rivers, a whole new city is being born, around the great church.

--

For the builders, building a cathedral is the work of a lifetime. Many of the men who began the work in 1220 will never see it finished. Their sons will continue it. And their sons may continue it after them. It requires great patience, and great faith. Each man knows that he is only one small part of a great work, much bigger than himself. And he does his part as well as he can, stone by stone, day by day.
