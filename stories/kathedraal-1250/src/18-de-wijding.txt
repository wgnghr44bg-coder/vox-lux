The years go by. Tom grows up. He finishes his apprenticeship, and becomes a mason. He carves stones for the cathedral, just like his father. The great church grows longer and taller, year by year. The nave is finished. The roof is covered with lead. The windows are filled with glass. And at last, in the autumn of 1258, the main part of the cathedral is ready.

--

On the twenty-ninth of September, 1258, the feast of Saint Michael, there is a great ceremony. The cathedral is to be consecrated. That means that it is blessed and dedicated to God, officially, and becomes a holy place. It is the greatest day in the history of the town. People come from all over the diocese, and from much further away. Lords and ladies, bishops and abbots, priests and monks, merchants and farmers. The streets are packed with people.

And the king himself is coming. King Henry the Third, the son of King John, comes to Salisbury for the consecration, with his queen, Eleanor, and his court. Henry loves churches. He is building a great new abbey church at Westminster, in London. He loves beautiful buildings, and he has given many gifts to Salisbury Cathedral. Timber from his forests, money and fine things for the altars.

{img:king-henry-iii-arriving-salisbury-procession-banners-crowds-1258}
The ceremony is led by the archbishop of Canterbury, the most important churchman in England. It begins early in the morning. The archbishop walks three times around the outside of the cathedral, in a procession, sprinkling the walls with holy water. Then he knocks on the great west door with his staff, and the door opens, and he goes inside, followed by the bishops, the priests, the king, the queen and the great lords.

Inside, the archbishop blesses every part of the cathedral. The altars, the walls, the floor. He anoints twelve crosses on the walls with holy oil, one for each of the apostles. Candles are lit in front of each cross. The choir sings. Incense fills the air. And then there is a great Mass, the first Mass in the finished cathedral, with the king and queen kneeling in front of the altar.

--

{img:cathedral-interior-consecration-mass-candles-choir-light-through-windows}
Tom is inside, with his father and the other masons. They are standing at the back, among the crowd, in their best clothes. Tom looks up. The great pale columns rise all around him, with their dark marble shafts. The pointed arches. The high vaults, with their ribs meeting far above. The silvery windows, full of light. The incense rising in clouds through the sunbeams. And the sound of the choir, filling the whole enormous space.

Walter puts his hand on Tom's shoulder. He has worked on this building for almost thirty years. He has carved hundreds of stones. Some of them are up there, at the top of the columns, the capitals with their curling leaves. And now it is finished. He has tears in his eyes. He does not say anything. He does not need to. Tom understands. And next to them, old Master Nicholas stands very still, looking up at the vaults, with a quiet smile on his face.

{img:old-master-mason-and-masons-standing-proud-finished-cathedral-light}
After the ceremony, there is a great feast. The king gives a feast for the lords and the clergy, in the bishop's palace. And in the meadows, there is a feast for the people, with food and ale, paid for by the bishop. The masons and carpenters are given an extra day's pay, and a feast of their own, in the lodge. There are bonfires and music and dancing, late into the night.

--

That night, Tom walks alone around the outside of the cathedral, in the moonlight. The scaffolding is gone from most of the walls now. The pale stone shines silver under the moon. The great building is quiet and still, after the crowds and the music of the day. Tom touches the wall with his hand. Somewhere in these walls, there are stones he has cut. With his mark on them, a little shape like a bird's foot, the same as his father's, with a small line added. And they will be there, he thinks, long after he is gone.
