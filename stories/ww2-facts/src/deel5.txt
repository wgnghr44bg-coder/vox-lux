HEAD: British soldiers leaving the army were given a complete set of civilian clothes, called a demob suit.
BODY: After the war ended, millions of servicemen and women had to return to ordinary life. This was called demobilisation, or demob for short. Men were released in an order based on their age and how long they had served, so some went home quickly and others waited many months. When a British soldier was finally demobilised, he went to a large clothing depot, where he was given a complete set of civilian clothes. This included a suit, a shirt with two collars, a tie, a pair of shoes, a raincoat and a hat, often a trilby. It was known as the demob suit. Many men kept their demob suits for years, wearing them to weddings, to church and to work, as they slowly settled into peacetime life.
