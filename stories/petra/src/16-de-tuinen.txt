In the middle of the city, beside the main street, on a raised terrace, there is a wonderful place. A great garden, with a pool. The pool is big, as big as a modern swimming pool, and quite deep. In the middle of it, there is a little island, with a pavilion, a small building with columns, open to the breeze. You can reach it by a little boat, or perhaps by swimming.

Around the pool, there is the garden. It is full of trees, and plants, and flowers. Date palms, and fig trees, and pomegranates, and olives, and vines. Roses, and lilies, and herbs, and sweet-smelling bushes. There are paths, and stone benches, and little channels of running water, between the flower beds. In the heat of the day, it is cool, and green, and shady. The air smells of flowers, and wet earth, and water.

--

{img:great-temple-petra-tall-columns-elephant-headed-capitals-courtyard}
Next to the garden, there is another great building, called by people today the Great Temple. It is huge, with tall columns, at the front, and a big courtyard, with more columns around it. The tops of some of the columns are carved with the heads of elephants, with their trunks and their big ears. The Nabataeans have never seen an elephant, most of them. But they have heard about them, from the traders who come from India.

Nobody today is quite sure what the Great Temple was for. Perhaps it was a temple. Perhaps it was a meeting place, for the city council, or for the king. Inside, there is a small theatre, with seats for a few hundred people. Perhaps it was used for meetings, or for music, or for ceremonies. It is one of the mysteries of Petra.

{img:children-sitting-edge-of-pool-feet-in-water-reflections-evening-lamps}
The garden and the pool are open to the people of the city, at certain times. In the evening, when it is cooler, families come to walk there, and sit by the water, and enjoy the fresh air. It is the most beautiful place in Petra. Kamilat and Obodat love it. They sit on the edge of the pool, and put their feet in the cool water, and watch the fish swimming, and the reflections of the trees, and the lamps, in the water.

Hagaru and Maliku sit on a bench, nearby, and talk quietly. About the business, and the children, and the future. About Kamilat, who is growing up so fast, and will soon be old enough to be married. About Obodat, who is so lively, and so restless. About Wahballahi, who is getting old, and tired. And about the Romans, and the news from the north, which worries Maliku a little.

--

The garden is watered by the water system of the city. The water comes in, through pipes and channels, from the spring, and from the cisterns. It flows into the pool, and from the pool into the garden channels, and then away, down the hill. It is a marvel of engineering. Visitors from other lands are always amazed, to find such a green, cool, beautiful place, in the middle of the desert.

{img:pavilion-island-moonlight-still-pool-garden-dark-palms-quiet-night}
At night, the garden is closed, and the gates are locked. The moon rises over the mountains, and shines on the still water of the pool. The pavilion on the island stands silent, in the moonlight. The palms rustle a little, in the night breeze. An owl calls from the cliffs. And the water trickles softly, through the channels, all night long, keeping the garden alive, in the desert.

The garden and pool of Petra were forgotten for many centuries. They were buried under sand, and earth, and fallen stones. Then, in the 1990s and 2000s, archaeologists discovered them, and dug them out. At first, they did not know what they were. They thought the big terrace was a market. Then they found the pool, and the island, and the channels, and the remains of plants. And they realised that it had been a great garden.
