Outside Petra, the desert stretches away, in every direction. It is not all sand, as many people imagine. Much of it is rock, and gravel, and dry, stony hills, with a few tough bushes, and thorny trees. In some places, there are great red sand dunes. In others, there are tall mountains of sandstone and granite, with strange shapes, carved by the wind. And here and there, there are springs, and wells, and green oases.

Some of the Nabataeans still live in the desert, as their ancestors did. They live in black tents, made of goat hair, and move from place to place, with their camels, their sheep and their goats. They are proud people, and free, and they know the desert better than anyone. They are cousins of the people of Petra. They come to the city to trade, and to visit the temples, and then go back to the desert.

--

Wahballahi has a cousin, called Zaidu, who lives in the desert, with his family. Every autumn, Maliku takes the family out to visit him, for a few days. It is one of the highlights of the year for the children. This year, Wahballahi comes too, riding on a quiet old camel, wrapped in his cloak. He says that he wants to sleep in the desert one more time, under the stars.

{img:family-riding-camels-across-red-sand-desert-mountains-sunlit-journey}
They travel for a day, south-east, across the desert, on camels. The children love riding the camels. They sit high up, on the saddles, swaying from side to side. At first, it feels strange, and a little frightening. But they soon get used to it. Obodat says it is like being on a boat, on the sea. Kamilat asks how he knows, since he has never been on a boat. Obodat says he can imagine.

In the evening, they reach Zaidu's camp. It is in a wide valley, among tall red mountains, near a spring. There are four or five black tents, and a flock of goats, and a dozen camels, resting on the sand. Zaidu comes out to meet them, with his wife and his sons, and welcomes them warmly. In the desert, hospitality is sacred. A guest is treated like a king.

{img:bedouin-tent-interior-guests-seated-carpets-coffee-fire-hospitality}
That night, there is a feast. Zaidu kills a young goat, and his wife and daughters cook it, over the fire, with herbs, and bread, baked on a hot stone. Everyone sits on carpets, in the big tent, and eats together, from a great dish, with their fingers. They drink milk, and water, and a little wine, which Maliku has brought. And they talk, and laugh, and sing, until late in the night.

--

Then they lie down to sleep, outside the tent, on blankets, on the soft sand. The night is cool, after the heat of the day. And the sky is full of stars. Thousands and thousands of them. More stars than the children have ever seen, even from the roof of their house in Petra. The Milky Way stretches across the sky, like a river of light. It is completely silent, except for the soft breathing of the camels.

{img:night-sky-milky-way-desert-camp-figures-lying-on-sand-stars-silhouettes}
Wahballahi lies beside the children, and points up at the stars. He tells them their names, the names that the caravan men use, to find their way across the desert at night. This star shows the north. That star rises in the east, just before dawn, in the autumn. These stars, together, make the shape of a camel. When he was young, he says, he led the caravans by the stars, night after night, across the great desert. The stars were his map, and his friends.

The children listen, sleepily. Obodat falls asleep first, in the middle of a sentence. Then Kamilat. Wahballahi lies awake for a long time, looking at the stars, and remembering. Then he too closes his eyes, and sleeps, peacefully, under the great sky of the desert, as he did when he was young.
