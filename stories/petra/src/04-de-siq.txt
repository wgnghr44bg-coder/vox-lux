To enter Petra from the east, where the road comes down from the high desert plateau, the caravans and travellers come through a narrow gorge, called the Siq. It is one of the most remarkable entrances to any city in the world. A long, winding crack in the mountain, more than a kilometre long, with walls of rock on both sides, rising up almost straight, as high as a tall building, or higher.

In places, the Siq is very narrow. Only three or four metres wide. You could almost touch both walls at once, with your arms stretched out. Above, the walls lean together, and you can see only a thin strip of blue sky, far above. The light down below is soft, and dim, and rosy, reflected from the red walls. It is cool, and quiet, and the footsteps echo.

--

{img:siq-floor-paved-stone-water-channel-carved-along-wall-cool-light}
The floor of the Siq is paved with stones, smooth and even, so that the camels and the carts can walk easily. Along both walls, there are channels, carved into the rock, about the height of a man's knee. One of them carries water, from the spring outside the city, all the way into Petra. The other has clay pipes, laid in it, carrying more water. You can hear the water trickling, as you walk.

The walls of the Siq are carved, here and there, with little niches, and shrines, and images of the gods. The travellers stop at them, to say a prayer, and give thanks for a safe journey. There are carved blocks of stone, standing in the niches, which represent the gods. The Nabataeans do not usually make statues of their gods, with faces and bodies. They prefer these simple stone blocks, which they call betyls. A betyl is the house of a god.

{img:life-size-carved-camels-caravan-men-relief-siq-wall-worn-ancient}
Halfway along the Siq, there is a great carving on the wall. Life-size camels, with men leading them, walking toward the city. It is a carving of a caravan, made in honour of the merchants, and the trade that made Petra rich. Obodat loves this carving. Whenever the family walks through the Siq, he stops and stares at it. He pats the stone camels, and says that one day, he will lead a caravan into Petra, just like that.

The Siq is not only a road. It is also a sacred way. On festival days, there are processions through it, with music, and singing, and offerings, to the temples of the city. And at the entrance to the Siq, there is a great arch, built across the top, with carved decorations. It marks the beginning of the sacred city. Everyone passes beneath it, as they come in.

--

When the rains come in the winter, there is a danger. The water rushes down from the mountains, into the valley outside the Siq, and could flood through the gorge, carrying away everything in its path. But the Nabataeans have thought of this. They have built a dam, at the entrance, to turn the water away, into a tunnel, through the mountain, and around the city. So the Siq stays safe, and dry.

{img:siq-opening-first-glimpse-treasury-facade-glowing-light-through-gap}
And then, at the end of the Siq, comes the most wonderful moment. The gorge turns a corner, and suddenly, through a narrow gap between the dark walls, you see a slice of brilliant, glowing pink. A great carved building, shining in the sunlight, like a vision. You walk on, and the gap widens, and there it is, in front of you, filling the whole view. The most famous building in Petra. The great tomb that later travellers would call the Treasury.

Every traveller who comes to Petra remembers this moment, for the rest of their life. Even Kamilat and Obodat, who have seen it many times, still feel a little thrill, every time they come out of the Siq, and see it again.
