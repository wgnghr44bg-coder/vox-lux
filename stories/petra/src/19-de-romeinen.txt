In the year 100 AD, there is news from the north, which worries Maliku and the other merchants. It is about Rome. The Roman Empire is the greatest power in the world. It rules all the lands around the Mediterranean Sea, from Spain to Syria, from Britain to Egypt. And it is all around the Nabataean kingdom. To the north, in Syria. To the west, in Judaea. And to the south-west, in Egypt.

For many years, the Nabataeans have been friends of Rome. They have paid tribute, and sent soldiers to help the Romans in their wars, and traded with them peacefully. The Romans have left them alone, to rule themselves, as a client kingdom. But the Romans are always hungry for more land, and more wealth. And the Nabataean kingdom, with its rich trade, is very tempting.

--

{img:roman-emperor-trajan-bust-marble-laurel-wreath-stern-face}
In Rome, a new emperor has come to power, two years ago. His name is Trajan. He is a great soldier, and a strong ruler. He wants to make the empire bigger, and stronger, and richer. Many people say that he has his eye on the Nabataean kingdom. The merchants of Petra talk about it, in the market, and in the taverns, in low voices. What will happen to us, if the Romans come?

Maliku is not sure. He thinks that perhaps it will not be so bad. The Romans are good for trade. They build roads, and keep the peace, and the Roman cities are full of rich people, who want frankincense. But he is a Nabataean, and proud of his people, and his king, and his gods. He does not want to be ruled by Rome. Nobody in Petra does.

In the end, it happened six years later, in 106 AD. King Rabbel the Second passed away, after a long and peaceful reign. And almost at once, the Roman governor of Syria, sent by Trajan, marched into the Nabataean kingdom, with his soldiers. There was very little fighting. Perhaps the Nabataeans knew that they could not win against Rome. Perhaps they made an agreement. Nobody knows exactly what happened.

The Nabataean kingdom became a Roman province, called Arabia Petraea. Its capital was moved to Bostra, in the north. Petra was still an important city, and Trajan gave it the title of metropolis, or mother city. But it was no longer the capital of a kingdom. There was no Nabataean king anymore. A Roman governor ruled instead, with Roman soldiers, and Roman laws, and Roman taxes.

--

{img:roman-road-milestone-desert-via-nova-traiana-paved-stones-caravans}
The Romans built a great new road, through the province, from Bostra in the north, to the Red Sea, in the south. It was called the Via Nova Traiana, the new road of Trajan. It passed close to Petra. It was paved, and straight, and had milestones, all the way along. It made travel faster and safer. And for a while, Petra continued to grow, and prosper, under the Romans.

The Romans built new things in Petra. They paved the main street, and built a great arch, and new temples, and baths. Some Roman soldiers settled in the city, and married local women. Latin and Greek became more common, and the Nabataean language slowly faded. Over time, the people of Petra became more and more Roman, in their clothes, and their customs, and their names.

{img:petra-under-rome-mixed-crowd-roman-soldiers-nabataean-merchants-street}
But in 100 AD, all of that is still in the future. For now, King Rabbel is still on his throne, and the Nabataean kingdom is still free. The caravans still come, and the incense still flows, and the stonemasons still carve. The people of Petra live their lives, and do their business, and worship their gods, as they have always done. And Maliku decides not to worry too much about the future. He says that nobody can know what tomorrow will bring. Better to enjoy today.
