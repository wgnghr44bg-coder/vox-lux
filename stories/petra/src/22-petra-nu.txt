Today, Petra is one of the most famous places in the world. In 1985, it was made a World Heritage Site, a place of special importance to all of humanity. In 2007, it was chosen as one of the New Seven Wonders of the World, in a vote by millions of people from all over the globe. And every year, hundreds of thousands of visitors come from every country, to see it.

They come through the Siq, just as the caravans did, two thousand years ago. They walk along the same paving stones, past the same water channels, under the same towering red walls. And they come out, at the end, in front of the Treasury, glowing in the sunlight. And they feel the same wonder, and the same amazement, that travellers have always felt, at that moment.

--

{img:today-petra-monastery-hikers-resting-steps-view-tea-stall}
Many of the visitors walk all the way through the city, to the theatre, and the Royal Tombs, and the colonnaded street, and the great temple. And many of them climb the eight hundred steps, up to the Monastery, as Maliku and his children did. At the top, there is a little café now, in a cave, where you can drink sweet tea, and rest, and look at the view. The same view, over the Wadi Araba, that Kamilat remembered all her life.

The local Bedouin people still live in the area. Until the 1980s, some of them lived in the old tombs and caves of Petra itself. Then the government built them a new village, nearby, and they moved there. Many of them now work in Petra, as guides, and camel drivers, and donkey drivers, and selling souvenirs and tea. They know every corner of the ancient city, and every story about it.

{img:today-petra-by-night-treasury-candles-luminaries-siq-silent-crowd}
On some evenings, there is a special event, called Petra by Night. The Siq is lit by hundreds of candles, in paper bags, all along the path. Visitors walk through it in silence, in the dark, by candlelight. At the end, in front of the Treasury, there are more than a thousand candles, glowing on the ground. And someone plays music, on a Bedouin flute, and tells stories. It is very beautiful, and very peaceful.

Petra has also appeared in films. The most famous is a film from 1989, in which the hero, an archaeologist with a hat and a whip, rides through the Siq, on a horse, and comes to the Treasury, in search of a sacred treasure. Many people today know Petra from that film. And some of them come, because of it, to see the real thing.

--

{img:today-archaeologists-excavating-petra-trench-brushes-sandstone-ruins}
Archaeologists are still working in Petra. Only a small part of the ancient city has been dug up so far. There is still much more, buried under the sand and the earth. New discoveries are made almost every year. In 2016, using satellites and drones, archaeologists found a huge monument, hidden in plain sight, near the city, that nobody had noticed before. Who knows what else is still waiting, under the ground?

Petra faces some dangers today. The soft sandstone is being worn away, slowly, by the wind, and the rain, and the salt. And by the millions of feet of visitors, and the hands that touch the walls. Floods are a danger too, as they were in ancient times. So people are working hard to protect Petra, and to repair the old Nabataean dams and channels, so that the city can be preserved, for the future.

And in the evening, when the visitors have gone, and the valley is quiet again, the sun sets behind the mountains, and the red cliffs glow, just as they did two thousand years ago. Red, and pink, and gold, and purple. And then the colours fade, and the stars come out, over the silent city.
