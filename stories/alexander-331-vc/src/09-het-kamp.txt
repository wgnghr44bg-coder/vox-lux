Every evening, when the army stops after a long day's march, a great camp appears, as if by magic. Thousands of tents are put up in neat rows. Thousands of fires are lit. And for one night, a city of fifty thousand people springs up on an empty plain, or by a river, or among the palm trees. And the next morning, it disappears again.

--

The surveyors choose the place for the camp, near water if possible. Then everyone knows where to go. The king's tent is in the middle. It is a big, beautiful tent, almost like a house, with many rooms, carpets and furniture, taken from the Persian king. Around it are the tents of the Companions and the officers. Then come the tents of the soldiers, row after row, each unit in its place. And around the edges, the baggage train, the merchants and the families.

The soldiers sleep in tents made of leather or goat hair, about ten men to a tent. They sleep on the ground, on blankets and cloaks, or on a little straw if they can find it. Nikias shares a small tent with Baeton and two other bematists. It is crowded. One of the bematists snores so loudly that Nikias puts his fingers in his ears. But after a long day of walking, he sleeps anyway.

{img:soldiers-cooking-around-fire-bread-porridge-pots-camp-evening-talk}
In the evening, every group of soldiers cooks its own supper, over a little fire. The main food is bread or porridge, made from wheat or barley. With it, they eat whatever they can get. Cheese, olives, onions, garlic, dried fish, beans, lentils and dates. Sometimes there is meat, when animals are bought or caught. They drink water, or wine mixed with water, or the local beer. And they always add salt, which is very precious.

After supper, the camp is full of life. Soldiers sit around their fires, talking and laughing. They tell stories about home, about their adventures, about the strange places they have seen. They play dice and knucklebones. They sing old songs from Macedonia. Some of them dance. Some mend their clothes, or sharpen their weapons. Some just lie on their backs, looking at the stars.

--

{img:merchants-market-in-army-camp-stalls-food-wine-trinkets-lanterns}
The merchants of the baggage train set up a little market in the camp. They sell food, wine, clothes, sandals, blankets and cooking pots. They buy things from the soldiers too. Souvenirs and treasures from the places they have passed through. There are also barbers, who cut hair and shave beards. And there are fortune tellers, who read the future in the stars, or in the flight of birds.

The camp is noisy until late. But at last, the fires burn low, and the camp grows quiet. Guards walk between the tents, with torches. The horses stamp and snort in their lines. Somewhere, a baby cries, and a mother sings. And then, there is silence, except for the wind, and the crickets, and the soft breathing of fifty thousand people asleep.

{img:quiet-army-camp-night-stars-moon-tents-guard-torch-silhouettes}
Nikias likes the camp at night. Sometimes, when he cannot sleep, he goes out of the tent and sits by the dying fire, looking up at the stars. They are so bright in the clear air of the desert and the plains. Baeton has taught him the names of the constellations. The Great Bear, Orion the hunter, the Pleiades, the seven sisters. They are the same stars he used to count in Pella, from the roof of his father's house.

--

Sometimes his uncle comes and sits with him. He tells Nikias about the battles he has seen. But only a little, and only quietly, because he does not like to talk about them. He prefers to talk about Macedonia. About the mountains and the forests, about the wine harvest and the spring festivals. He says that when the war is over, he will go home, buy a little farm, and grow olives. Nikias says that he will come and help him. His uncle laughs, and ruffles his hair.

In the morning, Nikias is woken by the trumpet, before sunrise. He crawls out of the tent, stretches, and splashes cold water on his face. The camp is grey and misty, and full of yawning people. He eats a piece of bread and a few dates, and ties on his sandals carefully. Then he takes his counting cord, and goes to find Baeton, who is already waiting at the edge of the camp, looking at the road.
