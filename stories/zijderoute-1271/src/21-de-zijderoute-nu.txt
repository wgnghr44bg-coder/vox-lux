Today, more than seven hundred years later, you can still follow Marco Polo's footsteps from Venice across Asia to China.

In Venice there is a little courtyard called the Courtyard of the Million, where the Polo family house once stood. The house is gone, and a theatre stands there now. But a plaque on the wall remembers Marco Polo and his travels.

--

Venice still stands on its islands, with its canals and bridges and palaces. The four bronze horses still stand on the front of Saint Mark's church, though these are copies, and the real ones are kept safe inside. The airport of Venice is named after him: Marco Polo Airport.

In Tabriz, in Iran, the great covered bazaar where Marco walked is still there. It is one of the largest covered markets in the world, with its brick domes, its carpet sellers and its spice merchants, as busy as ever.

--

In the Pamirs, the roof of the world is still wild and lonely. Shepherds still live in felt tents with their yaks and sheep. And the great Marco Polo sheep, with their curling horns, still graze on the high slopes.

Today a great road winds over these mountains from Pakistan into China. It crosses a pass almost five thousand metres high, one of the highest paved roads in the world.

{img:karakoram-highway-mountains-road-trucks-snow-peaks}
In Kashgar, the weekly market still brings people from all the villages around, with donkeys and sheep and melons, and bread from the clay ovens.

--

At Dunhuang, the golden sand dunes still sing when the wind blows over them, just as they did for Marco. The little crescent-shaped lake is still there among the dunes. Visitors ride camels over the sand at sunset.

And the painted caves of Dunhuang are still there in their cliff, after more than fifteen hundred years. In 1900, a monk found a hidden cave, sealed for nine hundred years, full of old books and paintings. Among them was the oldest dated printed book in the world, printed in the year 868.

{img:dunhuang-crescent-lake-dunes-pavilion-camels-sunset}
On the grasslands of Inner Mongolia, the ruins of Xanadu lie quietly in the grass, with the larks singing above them. And in Beijing, Kublai Khan's green hill still rises above its lake, with a white pagoda on top.

--

In Hangzhou, the West Lake is still one of the most beautiful places in China. People still go out on boats, walk under the willows, drink the green tea of the hills, and watch the sun set over the water, as Marco did.

And across Asia, new roads and railways follow the old routes of the Silk Road. Trains and trucks carry goods once again from East to West, and West to East.

The world is very different now. But the mountains and deserts, the oases and rivers are still there. And the story of the young man from Venice who went all the way to the court of the Great Khan, and came back to tell the tale, is still told all over the world.

--

So we come to the end of the long road tonight, with the camel bells growing quiet behind us, and the evening light falling on the lagoon of Venice, where the journey began.

The inns under the stars, the roof of the world, the singing sands, the palace of the Great Khan, and a merchant's son who saw more than he could ever tell.
