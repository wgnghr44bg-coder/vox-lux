Today, more than seven hundred years later, you can still follow the footsteps of Marco Polo, from Venice, across Asia, to China.

In Venice, near the church of San Giovanni Crisostomo, there is a little courtyard, called the Corte del Milione, the courtyard of the Million, where the Polo family house once stood. The house is gone. A theatre stands there now, the Teatro Malibran. But there is a plaque, on the wall, remembering Marco Polo, and his travels.

--

Venice is still built on its islands, in its lagoon, with its canals and its bridges and its palaces. The four bronze horses still stand on the front of the church of Saint Mark, or rather copies of them, while the originals are kept safe inside. And the boats still come and go, across the lagoon, in the evening light.

The airport of Venice is named after him: Marco Polo Airport.

In Tabriz, in Iran, the great covered bazaar, where Marco walked, is still there, rebuilt over the centuries, one of the largest covered bazaars in the world, and a World Heritage Site, with its brick vaults and domes, and its carpet sellers, and spice merchants, and its little shops, as busy as ever.

--

In the Pamirs, in Tajikistan, and Afghanistan, and Pakistan, and China, the high plateau, the roof of the world, is still wild and lonely, with its snowy peaks, and its blue lakes, and its Kyrgyz and Tajik shepherds, in their yurts, with their yaks and their sheep, and the great Marco Polo sheep, with their curling horns, still grazing on the high slopes.

Today, a great road, the Karakoram Highway, winds over the mountains, from Pakistan into China, through the Khunjerab Pass, nearly five thousand metres high, one of the highest paved roads in the world.

{img:karakoram-highway-mountains-road-trucks-snow-peaks}
In Kashgar, the Sunday market still brings people from all the villages around, with their donkeys, and sheep, and horses, and melons, and bread, from the clay ovens, though much of the old city has been rebuilt.

--

At Dunhuang, the great golden sand dunes still sing, when the wind blows over them, as they did when Marco crossed the desert of Lop. And the crescent-shaped lake is still there, among the dunes, with a little pavilion on its shore. Visitors ride camels over the dunes, at sunset.

And the caves of Dunhuang, the Mogao Caves, are still there, in their cliff, with their wonderful paintings, and statues, which have survived for more than fifteen hundred years. In 1900, a monk discovered a hidden cave, sealed for nine hundred years, full of thousands of ancient manuscripts and paintings, including the oldest printed book in the world that has a date, a copy of a Buddhist text, the Diamond Sutra, printed in the year 868.

{img:dunhuang-crescent-lake-dunes-pavilion-camels-sunset}
On the grassland of Inner Mongolia, the ruins of Shangdu, Xanadu, lie quietly in the grass, with the wind blowing over them, and the larks singing. And in Beijing, in Beihai Park, the Green Mount of Kublai Khan, now called Jade Flower Island, still rises above the lake, with a white pagoda on its top.

--

In Hangzhou, the West Lake is still one of the most beautiful places in China. People still go out on the lake in boats, and walk along the causeways, under the willows, and visit the temples, and drink the famous green tea of the hills, Dragon Well tea, and watch the sun set over the water, and the pagodas, as Marco did.

And all across Asia, from China to Turkey, there are new roads, and railways, and pipelines, along the old routes of the Silk Road, and trains, and trucks, carrying goods, once again, from East to West, and West to East.

The world is very different now. But the mountains, and the deserts, and the oases, and the rivers, are still there. And the story of the young man from Venice, who went all the way to the court of the Great Khan, and came back to tell the tale, is still told, all over the world.

--

So we come to the end of the long road tonight, with the camel bells growing quiet behind us, and the evening light falling on the lagoon of Venice, where the journey began.

The caravanserais under the stars, the roof of the world, the singing sands, the palace of the Great Khan, and a merchant's son who saw more than he could ever tell.
