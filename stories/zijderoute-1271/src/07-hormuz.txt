From Persia, the Polos first travel south, all the way to the Persian Gulf, to the great port of Hormuz.

Their plan seems to be to take a ship from Hormuz across the Indian Ocean to India, and then sail on to China. Many merchants go that way.

--

Marco describes the road south. It goes down from the high, cool plains of Persia, through mountain passes, onto a hot plain full of date palms and birds. There are white oxen with humps on their backs, and sheep as big as donkeys, with fat tails.

The plain is often raided by bandits from the hills. Marco tells a strange story about them. He says they can make the day go dark by magic, so nobody sees them coming. Perhaps he is really describing a dust storm. Either way, the travellers keep close together, and watch the hills.

And then, at last, they come to the sea, and to Hormuz.

--

Hormuz is a hot, busy and rich port. Merchants come here from India with spices, pearls, silk and ivory. They sell them to Persian merchants, who carry them inland and on to the West.

Marco writes that the summer heat is so terrible that people leave the town to live in gardens by the river. And every day at noon, he says, a burning wind blows from the desert. When people feel it coming, they go into the water, and stay there up to their necks until it has passed.

{img:shallow-sea-people-standing-in-water-heat-midday-palms}
He writes that the people eat dates, salted fish and onions. They harvest their wheat in March. After that, nothing green can be seen except the date palms, until next winter.

--

But the Polos do not like the ships of Hormuz.

Marco writes that they are bad and dangerous ships. They are not built with iron nails, but sewn together with thread made from coconut husks. They have only one mast and one sail, and no deck. Many of them are lost at sea.

{img:sewn-wooden-dhow-ship-beach-coconut-rope-stitches-close}
So the Polos make a big decision. They will not go by sea. They turn back north, the way they came. They will go to China over land, along the Silk Road. It is a much longer way, through deserts and over the highest mountains in the world. Will it be the right choice?

--

On the way north, Marco tells one of the most famous stories in his book, the story of the Old Man of the Mountain.

The Old Man, he says, was the leader of a secret group who lived in a castle in the mountains of northern Persia. In a hidden valley he made the most beautiful garden in the world. There were golden palaces, fountains of milk and honey, and music everywhere.

He would give young men a sleeping drink, and carry them into the garden. When they woke up, they believed they were in Paradise. Then he gave them the drink again, and carried them out. After that they would do anything he asked, to be allowed back.

Marco says the Mongols had destroyed this group fifteen years before. The story is probably more legend than truth. But it became one of the most famous tales of the Middle Ages.

--

Then the Polos cross the great salt desert of Persia. Marco describes it as seven days with almost no water, and the little there is tastes bitter and makes people ill. There are no houses, no people and no animals. Only sand, stones and salt, shimmering in the heat.

{img:salt-desert-persia-white-crust-shimmering-heat-camels-far}
And beyond it, at last, they come to green land again, and to the city of Balkh, in what is now Afghanistan.
