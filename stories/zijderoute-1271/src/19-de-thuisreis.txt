In 1292, at long last, the Polos get their chance to go home.

The Mongol ruler of Persia has lost his favourite wife. Before she died, she asked him to marry a lady from her own tribe in Mongolia. So he sends messengers to Kublai Khan, to ask for a bride.

--

Kublai chooses a young lady of seventeen, called Kököchin. But the road over land is blocked by wars between Mongol princes. So the messengers decide to go by sea, all the way around the south of Asia.

They ask the Great Khan to let the Polos come with them, because they are experienced travellers. The Khan does not want them to go. But at last, he agrees.

He gives them two golden tablets, letters for the kings of Europe and the Pope, and rich gifts. And he prepares a great fleet. There are fourteen huge Chinese ships with four masts each, carrying six hundred people and food for two years.

--

They sail from the great port of Zaiton, in the south of China.

The voyage is long and hard. They sail south along the coast, and then to the island of Sumatra. There they must wait five long months for the winds to change. They build a camp on the shore with a wooden fence around it, to keep themselves safe.

{img:sumatra-coast-tropical-camp-palisade-ships-anchored-waiting}
Marco describes Sumatra, with its pepper and spices. He sees rhinoceroses, and thinks they must be unicorns. But he says they are very ugly, and nothing like the gentle unicorns of European stories.

--

Then they sail on, past the island of Ceylon, which Marco says is the finest island of its size in the world, with rubies and sapphires. And along the coast of India, where he watches pearl divers diving deep into the sea.

At last, after two years at sea, they reach Hormuz in the Persian Gulf, the port they had left twenty years before.

{img:pearl-divers-boats-calm-sea-india-coast-morning}
Many of the people who set out on the voyage did not survive it. Marco says only eighteen of the six hundred were left. Perhaps that is too low. But it was a very hard journey.

And when they reach Persia, they learn that the ruler who asked for the bride has died while they were at sea. So the young lady marries his son instead. Marco says she wept when the Polos left her, because she had grown so fond of them on the long voyage.

--

From Persia, the Polos travel over land to the Black Sea. There they hear that Kublai Khan has died, at almost eighty. The great protector of their years in China is gone. And they are on their way home just in time.

From the Black Sea they sail to Constantinople, and from there, home to Venice.

They arrive in 1295. They have been away for twenty-four years. Marco is now forty-one.

--

There is a famous story about their return, told many years later.

When they knock on the door of their house, their relatives do not recognise them. They look like Mongols, with long beards and worn, shabby clothes. They speak with a strange accent. Nobody believes these are the Polos. Everyone thought they were dead long ago.

{img:venetian-feast-hall-travellers-cutting-seams-jewels-spilling}
So the Polos invite all their relatives to a great feast. At the end of it, they bring out their old travelling coats, and cut open the seams with knives. And out pours a stream of jewels. Rubies and sapphires, emeralds and pearls, sewn into the linings for safety on the long road. And then everyone believes them.

It is probably just a story. But it is a good one.
