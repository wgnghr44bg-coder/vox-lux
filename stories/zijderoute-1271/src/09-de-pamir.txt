From Badakhshan, the Polos climb into the highest mountains they have ever seen, the Pamirs.

The local people call this place the roof of the world. It is a high, flat land, more than four thousand metres above the sea. All around it rise even higher mountains, covered with snow and ice all year. Here, the great mountain ranges of Asia meet.

--

Marco describes it well. He writes that you ride for twelve days across a high plain with a great lake. The grass there is so good that a thin horse grows fat in ten days. And there are many wild sheep with huge horns.

But it is a lonely, empty place. For twelve days you see no houses, no people and not even birds, because it is so high and so cold. You must carry all your food with you. If you run out, there is nowhere to buy more.

And Marco notices something strange. Up here, he says, fire does not burn as brightly or as hot as in other places. Food does not cook properly. He is right, though he does not know why. High up, the air is thin, and water boils before it gets really hot.

--

After that, he writes, you ride for forty more days over mountains and valleys, across rivers and through empty land. The people who live here are wild hunters who dress in animal skins.

The journey is very hard. The air is thin, so the travellers are short of breath. They have headaches and feel sick and tired. The wind never stops. There is snow even in summer. The paths are narrow and steep, running along the edges of cliffs. One wrong step could be the last.

The animals suffer too. Horses and camels slip and fall. The yaks of the mountains do better. They are big, shaggy black cattle with long hair. They can carry loads through snow and thin air without trouble.

--

A few people live in the Pamirs. They are shepherds who live in round felt tents on the high meadows, with their sheep, goats, yaks and fierce dogs. In summer they move up into the high valleys. In winter they move down to more sheltered places.

They are kind to travellers. They invite them into their tents and give them bowls of hot, salty tea with butter, and yoghurt and bread. They let them sleep by the fire, out of the wind.

{img:inside-felt-yurt-fire-family-tea-bowls-travellers-warm}
Inside the tent it is warm and cosy. The fire burns in the middle, and the smoke drifts out through a round hole in the roof. Bright felt carpets hang on the walls. The family sleeps around the fire on soft mats and furs.

--

At last, after weeks in the mountains, the Polos come down on the other side, and the land begins to change.

Now the rivers flow east, not west. The air grows warmer. There are trees again, poplars and willows, and fields and villages. And ahead of them, at the edge of a great desert, lies a green oasis city. Its name is Kashgar.

{img:descending-mountains-green-valley-poplars-oasis-far-below}
They have crossed the roof of the world. Now they stand at the edge of China. But between them and the Great Khan, there is still a desert so big that people say no one who goes into it ever comes out.
