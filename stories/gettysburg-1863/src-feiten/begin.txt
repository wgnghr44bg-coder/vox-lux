Meet Tillie Pierce. She is fifteen, with dark hair pulled neatly back and a round, serious face. Her father is the butcher on Baltimore Street, in a small Pennsylvania town among apple orchards and wheat fields. On Saturdays she wraps meat in paper behind the counter. On summer evenings she sits on the front step with her sister, counting fireflies. She talks a little too much at school, and she looks out of the window a little too often. And she notices everything.

Her town is called Gettysburg. In the summer of 1863, two great armies will come down its ten country roads, and its name will be known all over the world. Years later, as a grey-haired woman, Tillie will write down everything she saw. Thanks to her, and to a few neighbours like her, we can still walk those streets with them. So what happened to the families of Gettysburg, that summer and in the quiet autumn after it? Let's walk beside Tillie and find out, slowly and gently.

First, a little note from me. These calm stories come out a few times every week. Should you like to hear the next one, you can subscribe, whenever it suits you. And if not, that's perfectly fine too.

I'd also enjoy knowing where these words are finding you. Are you under a quilt in the countryside, or high up in a city flat? And how late is it there? You can tell me in the comments, in just a few words.

Now let the day slip off you, like a coat at the end of a long walk. Rest your head. Let your breath grow long and slow, like a summer evening on a quiet street, when the swallows fly low and the lamps aren't yet lit.
