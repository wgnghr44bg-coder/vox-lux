{img:wagon-arriving-town-evening-wind}
Ike Clanton and Tom McLaury have come to Tombstone on business.

Tom has some beef to sell to the butchers in town, and some money to collect. Ike has come along for the company, and for the saloons.

They leave their wagon and their team at a stable, as the rules require, and walk out into the evening along Allen Street, with their coat collars turned up against the wind.

--

The town is busy, as it always is in the evening.

The lamps are lit in the windows, and the restaurants are full. Men are standing in little groups on the boardwalks, talking and smoking. From the saloons comes the clink of glasses and the soft murmur of voices and the music of a piano.

Ike and Tom go from one saloon to another. They see friends, and have a drink or two, and talk about the cattle business and the price of beef.

--

{img:alhambra-lunch-counter-night}
Late that night, a little after midnight, Ike goes into the lunch room at the back of the Alhambra Saloon, to get something to eat.

It is a small, warm room, with a long counter and a row of stools, and a cook frying eggs and steak over a stove at the back. At this hour, it is full of men who have been out late, ordering coffee and food before they go to bed.

Ike sits down at the counter. A few minutes later, Doc Holliday comes in.

--

Doc has been drinking, and he does not like Ike Clanton at all.

He has heard the things that Ike has been saying about him and about the Earps. He walks up to Ike at the counter and tells him so, loudly, in front of everyone in the room.

Ike says something back. The cook stops frying. The men at the counter put down their forks and watch.

Morgan Earp is there too, sitting at the end of the counter. He gets up and stands nearby, but he does not interfere.

--

The quarrel moves out of the lunch room and into the street.

Doc and Ike stand on the boardwalk in the cold wind, under the light of a lamp, still arguing. Wyatt Earp arrives. Then Virgil comes along, in his role as town marshal, and tells them all to stop it and go home, or he will arrest every one of them.

Doc goes off, still angry, toward his room at Fly's. Ike goes off in the other direction, muttering. The few people who stopped to watch drift away again into the night.

And for a little while, it seems that the trouble is over.

--

But nobody in this story is ready to go to bed.

Not long afterwards, a little group of men sits down to a game of poker at a table in the Occidental Saloon, further along Allen Street.

{img:poker-game-late-night-lamp}
Around the table, under a hanging lamp, are Ike Clanton and Tom McLaury, Sheriff Johnny Behan, Virgil Earp, and another man or two. It seems a strange group of players, after everything that has happened. But in Tombstone in 1881, men who disagree about almost everything will still sit down together to play cards.

They play all through the night.

The cards are dealt and gathered and dealt again. The chips click softly on the green cloth. The lamp burns lower. Outside, the wind blows along the empty street, and the town grows quiet around them.

--

Virgil Earp keeps his revolver on his lap under the table during the game. Ike notices it, and it worries him.

Around dawn, the game comes to an end, and the players stand up, stiff and tired, and push their chairs back from the table.

Virgil goes home to bed. Behan goes home to bed. Tom McLaury goes off to find his room.

But Ike Clanton does not go to bed at all.

--

{img:grey-dawn-allen-street-empty}
It is now the morning of Wednesday, the twenty-sixth of October.

The dawn comes up grey and cold over the Dragoon Mountains. There are heavy clouds across the sky, and the wind is still blowing, sharp and raw, and some people later remember little flakes of snow drifting down in the gusts.

In the town, the ordinary morning begins. The bakers are at their ovens. The milk wagons come in. The mine whistles blow, and the morning shift walks up the hill to the shafts, with their coats buttoned tight against the wind.

--

Ike Clanton walks the streets, tired and cold and angry, still full of the night's whiskey and the night's quarrel.

He tells a bartender that the Earps and Doc Holliday will have to fight him when they show themselves on the street. He tells another man the same thing. He goes from one saloon to another, saying it to anyone who will listen.

Some people take him seriously. Others shake their heads. Ike has talked like this before, and nothing ever came of it.

--

{img:virgil-sleeping-morning-room}
At the little house on the corner of Fremont and First, Virgil Earp is asleep, after his long night at the card table.

Allie has kept the house quiet for him all morning. She moves softly around the kitchen, making coffee, so as not to wake him.

But late in the morning, there is a knock at the door. One of the town policemen has come to tell Virgil that Ike Clanton is out on the street, carrying a rifle and a revolver, and looking for the Earps.

Virgil gets up, gets dressed, puts on his coat, and goes out into the cold.
