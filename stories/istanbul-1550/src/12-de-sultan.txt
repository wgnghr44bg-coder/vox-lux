Every Friday, the sultan rides out of the palace to pray at one of the great mosques of the city. This is the only time that most people in Istanbul ever see him. And so, on Friday mornings, crowds gather along the streets to watch him pass. One Friday, Mehmet and Fatma go with their father to watch.

--

They stand in the crowd near the mosque, waiting. First come soldiers on foot, the Janissaries, in long blue robes and tall white felt hats, with a long piece of cloth hanging down the back. Then come officials of the palace, on horseback, in rich robes of silk. Then come the archers of the sultan's guard, with tall feathers on their helmets. Everything is very orderly and very quiet.

And then, the sultan himself. He rides a white horse, with a saddle covered in gold and jewels. He wears a long robe of white silk, and a large white turban. He is about fifty-five years old. He is tall and thin, with a long neck, a thin face and a drooping moustache. He looks serious and tired. He does not smile. But as he passes, the people bow deeply, and some of them call out blessings.

{img:people-handing-petitions-to-officials-procession-street-ottoman}
Along the way, some people hold up pieces of paper. They are petitions, written requests to the sultan. A widow asks for help. A farmer complains about an unfair tax. A man asks for justice in a dispute with his neighbour. Officials walk beside the sultan's horse, and collect the petitions. Later, they will be read, and many of them will be answered.

Suleiman is called the Lawgiver by his own people. He has worked hard to make good laws for the whole empire. Laws about taxes, about trade, about land and about crimes. He wants the laws to be fair, and the same for everyone. Of course, he is also a powerful ruler, who leads great armies and makes war. But many of his subjects respect him, because they believe he is trying to be just.

--

{img:hurrem-sultan-gardens-palace-portrait-calm-elegant-distance}
Suleiman has a wife, called Hürrem. She came to the palace many years ago, as a young girl from what is now Ukraine. She was very clever and lively, and the sultan fell deeply in love with her. He married her, which was very unusual for a sultan in those days. She is the most powerful woman in the empire. She has built a mosque, a school, a soup kitchen and a hospital for women, in the city.

Suleiman and Hürrem write letters to each other when he is away at war. Some of these letters still exist today. They are full of love. Suleiman also writes poems. He writes under a different name, Muhibbi, which means the lover, or the beloved friend. He writes about love, about God, and about how short and precious life is.

{img:sultan-writing-poetry-pavilion-garden-tulips-scroll-evening-light}
One of his poems has a line that people still quote in Turkey today. He writes that people think that wealth and power are the greatest things in the world. But in this world, there is nothing as precious as health. Everything else passes away, like the wind. It is a quiet, wise thought, for the most powerful man in the world.

--

When the sultan has passed, the crowd slowly breaks up. Fatma is very excited. She has seen the sultan, with her own eyes. She wants to tell everyone at the fountain. Mehmet is thinking about something else. He is thinking that this tired-looking man on the white horse is the one who asked Sinan to build the great mosque. One day, when it is finished, the sultan will come and pray there. And somewhere in the walls, there will be stones that Mehmet helped to carve.

Fatma tells everyone at the fountain the next morning, just as she planned. She describes the white horse, the golden saddle, the tall turban and the serious face. Each time she tells the story, the horse gets a little whiter and the turban a little taller. The other girls listen with open mouths. Nine, sitting at her window, hears every word, and smiles quietly to herself.
