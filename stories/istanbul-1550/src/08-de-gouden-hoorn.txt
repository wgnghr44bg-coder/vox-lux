Istanbul is a city of water. And the most important water of all is the Golden Horn. It is a long, narrow bay that curves into the land, like the horn of an animal. On one side of it lies the old city, with the mosques, the palace and the bazaar. On the other side lies the district of Galata. There is no bridge across the Golden Horn. So everyone crosses it by boat.

--

On the shore, there are many little landing places, with wooden steps going down to the water. And there, waiting for passengers, are the boatmen with their caiques. A caique is a long, slender rowing boat, painted in bright colours, with a pointed front. The boatmen sit in the middle and row with long oars. Passengers sit on cushions at the back. There are thousands of caiques in Istanbul, crossing the water all day long.

One Sunday, Mehmet's father takes him across the Golden Horn to Galata. They want to visit an old friend of his father, a Greek stonemason who lives there. They go down to the landing place and climb into a caique. The boatman is a big man with strong arms. He pushes away from the shore, and begins to row with long, smooth strokes.

{img:boy-and-father-sitting-in-caique-cushions-boatman-rowing-golden-horn}
Out on the water, Mehmet looks around him with wide eyes. The Golden Horn is full of boats. Big merchant ships, with tall masts and furled sails. Fishing boats, coming home with their catch. Barges loaded with wood, stone and grain. And the slender caiques darting in between, like water beetles. Behind them, the old city rises up the hills, with its domes and minarets. And there, on the third hill, he can see the great walls of the new mosque, surrounded by scaffolding.

Further along the Golden Horn, there is the great shipyard of the sultan, called the Arsenal. Here, the warships of the Ottoman navy are built. There are dozens of covered slipways, where new galleys are being built. Thousands of workers hammer and saw. The Ottoman navy is the strongest in the Mediterranean. Its great admiral, Barbarossa, became famous all over Europe, before he passed away a few years ago.

--

{img:galata-tower-genoese-stone-round-tower-houses-hill-1550-afternoon}
Galata is a very different place from the old city. For hundreds of years, it was a colony of merchants from the Italian city of Genoa. They built a tall, round stone tower on the hill, which can be seen from everywhere. It is called the Galata Tower. Today, many Europeans live here. Merchants from Venice, Genoa, France and other countries. There are also Greeks, Jews and Armenians. There are churches and synagogues, as well as mosques.

The streets of Galata are steep and narrow. Some of them are made of stairs. Mehmet hears people speaking Italian, French and many other languages. He sees men in tight European clothes and big hats, with short beards and swords at their sides. Women walk in the streets with uncovered heads. It seems very strange to him. It is almost like visiting another country.

{img:greek-house-galata-family-welcoming-guests-courtyard-vine-table}
The Greek friend of his father is called Nikolaos. He lives in a little house with a vine growing over the courtyard. His wife brings out a plate of fried fish, bread and olives, and a bowl of cherries. The two men talk about their work and their families. Nikolaos has a son of Mehmet's age, called Yannis. The boys go out to explore the streets of Galata together, while the men talk.

--

In the late afternoon, Mehmet and his father take a caique back across the Golden Horn. The sun is setting behind the old city. The domes and minarets are black against the orange sky. The water is golden. Perhaps that is why it is called the Golden Horn, Mehmet thinks. His father says that nobody knows for sure. But tonight, it certainly looks like gold.

Mehmet and Yannis climb up the steep streets to the foot of the Galata Tower. It is much taller than it looked from across the water. A guard stands at the door, and does not let them in. But from the hill, they can see the whole Golden Horn spread out below them, with the old city on the other side. Yannis points out his favourite fishing places. Mehmet points out the new mosque. They both agree that their own side of the water is the best.
