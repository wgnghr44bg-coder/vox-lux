Tonight we travel back almost five hundred years, to the year 1550. We go to one of the greatest cities in the world. It lies on two continents, Europe and Asia, on the shores of a narrow strip of sea. It has been called by many names. The Ottomans, who rule it now, call it Istanbul.

--

It is early morning, just before sunrise. A soft mist lies over the water. Out of the mist rise the great grey domes of the mosques, and the tall, thin towers beside them, called minarets. They look like pencils pointing at the sky. Seagulls are crying over the water. Little wooden boats are already moving across the harbour. And the first pale light is spreading over the hills of the city.

Then, from the top of a minaret, a voice begins to sing. It is the call to prayer. It floats out over the rooftops, slow and beautiful. A moment later, another voice joins it, from another minaret. And then another, and another, from all over the city. Hundreds of voices, rising and falling, echoing across the water, as the sun comes up over the hills of Asia.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

{img:ottoman-istanbul-panorama-1550-hills-mosques-wooden-houses-cypress-trees}
In 1550, Istanbul is the capital of the Ottoman Empire. It is a huge empire, stretching from Hungary in Europe to Iraq in Asia, and from the Crimea to Egypt and Algeria in Africa. And Istanbul is its beating heart. About half a million people live here. That makes it one of the biggest cities in the world, much bigger than London or Paris at the time.

The city is built on seven hills, like ancient Rome. On the hills, there are great mosques, palaces and gardens. Below them, thousands and thousands of wooden houses crowd together along narrow, winding streets. Tall dark cypress trees grow everywhere, in the gardens and the graveyards. And on three sides, the city is surrounded by water, blue and sparkling.

{img:boy-thirteen-ottoman-clothes-looking-at-mosque-construction-scaffolding}
Tonight, we will spend some time in this great city, with an ordinary family. The father is a stonemason, and the son, a boy of thirteen called Mehmet, is learning the same trade. They are working on the building of a new mosque. It will become one of the most beautiful buildings in the world. And it is being designed by the greatest architect of his time, a man called Sinan.

--

We will walk through the Grand Bazaar, with its thousands of little shops. We will take a boat across the Golden Horn. We will visit a steam bath, a hammam, and sit in a garden full of tulips. We will see the old church of Hagia Sophia, and the palace of the sultan. And we will meet the neighbours of Mehmet's family, who come from many different peoples and faiths.

{img:sultan-suleiman-procession-distance-horsemen-crowds-street-respectful}
We will also catch a glimpse of the sultan himself. His name is Suleiman. The Europeans call him Suleiman the Magnificent. His own people call him Suleiman the Lawgiver. He has ruled for thirty years, and he is the most powerful man in the world. But tonight, our story is mostly about ordinary people. So let us begin, on a spring morning in 1550, as the call to prayer drifts over the water, and the city of Istanbul wakes up.

Down by the water, the fishermen are already pulling in their nets. The bakers have been up for hours, and the smell of fresh bread drifts through the streets. In the courtyards of the houses, people are washing their faces at the wells. Somewhere, a donkey brays. Somewhere, a baby cries and is quickly comforted. And slowly, the great city stretches and yawns, like a cat in the first sun.
