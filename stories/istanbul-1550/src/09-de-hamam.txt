Once a week, Mehmet's family goes to the hammam. A hammam is a public steam bath. There are hundreds of them in Istanbul. Almost every mahalle has one. Very few houses have their own bath, so everyone goes to the hammam to get clean. But it is much more than a place to wash. It is also a place to relax, to meet friends and to talk.

--

Men and women go to the hammam at different times, or to different parts of the building. In the mornings, the hammam is for men. In the afternoons, it is for women. Or sometimes, a big hammam has two separate halves, one for men and one for women, each with its own entrance.

Mehmet goes with his father, early on a Friday morning. First, they enter a large, cool hall with a high dome. In the middle, there is a marble fountain, splashing quietly. Around the walls, there are raised platforms with cushions. Here, the men take off their clothes, wrap a cotton cloth around their waist, and put on wooden sandals, called nalin. The sandals are very high, so that their feet do not get wet on the floor.

{img:hammam-cool-entrance-hall-fountain-wooden-platforms-towels-quiet}
Then they walk through a warm room, into the hot room. The hot room is the heart of the hammam. It is a big room made of marble, with a dome on top. In the dome, there are little round holes, covered with glass. The sunlight shines through them in thin beams, like stars, into the steamy room. The air is very warm and damp. The floor is heated from below by a fire.

In the middle of the hot room, there is a large, raised marble platform. It is warm, almost hot. People lie down on it, and let the heat soak into their bodies. They sweat, and their muscles relax. Mehmet lies on his back and looks up at the little stars of light in the dome. After a long week of carrying stones, it feels wonderful.

--

{img:bath-attendant-scrubbing-with-rough-mitten-marble-platform-steam}
After a while, a bath attendant comes. He is a strong man with a rough cloth mitten on his hand. He scrubs Mehmet's back and arms and legs, very hard. Little grey rolls of old skin and dirt come off. Mehmet laughs, because it tickles. Then the attendant covers him with a great cloud of soap foam, made by blowing into a cloth bag full of soapy water. And then he rinses him with bowls of warm water, poured from a marble basin.

Around the walls of the hot room, there are small marble basins, with taps for hot and cold water. People sit beside them and pour water over themselves with metal bowls. Men talk and laugh. Some of them sing a little. The sounds echo softly under the dome. It is one of the most peaceful places in the whole busy city.

{img:women-hammam-afternoon-talking-laughing-copper-bowls-steam-soft-light}
In the afternoon, it is the women's turn. For the women of Istanbul, the hammam is one of the most important places in their lives. It is where they meet their friends and neighbours, away from the men. Ayşe, Nine and Fatma go together, with a bundle of towels, soap, combs and food. They stay for hours. They wash and relax, and then they sit together in the cool hall, eating and talking.

--

The women talk about everything. Who is getting married, who has had a baby, who is ill. They share recipes and remedies. Mothers look at the young girls, to find a good wife for their sons. Sometimes there are special parties at the hammam, for a bride before her wedding, or for a new mother. There is music and singing and delicious food. Fatma loves going to the hammam. She says it is better than the bazaar.

After the bath, everyone feels clean, warm and sleepy. They drink a cool glass of sherbet in the entrance hall, made from fruit juice and sugar, with a little rose water. Then they walk slowly home, through the evening streets, with rosy faces and wet hair. That night, everyone in the family sleeps very well.

The hammam is also a place where news is shared. Men talk about the price of bread, about the sultan's latest campaign, about a ship that has arrived from Egypt. The barber, who also works at the hammam, knows more news than anyone. While he shaves the men's heads and trims their beards, he tells them everything that is happening in the city. Mehmet's father says that the barber knows the news even before the sultan does.
