Every few days, a special visitor comes to the building site. He is an old man, about sixty years old, with a white beard and a large white turban. He walks slowly around the site, looking at everything. He talks to the master builders, looks at drawings, touches the stones and asks questions. When he comes, everyone works a little harder. This is Sinan, the chief architect of the sultan.

--

Sinan has had a remarkable life. He was born in a small village in Anatolia, in a Christian family, probably Greek or Armenian. When he was a young man, he was taken into the service of the sultan, as was the custom then. He became a Muslim, and joined the Janissaries, the sultan's famous soldiers. He learned to be a carpenter and a builder in the army.

As a soldier, Sinan travelled all over the empire, and beyond. He saw many famous buildings, in many lands. He built bridges for the army to cross rivers, and ships to cross lakes. Everyone noticed how clever he was. And when he was about fifty years old, Sultan Suleiman made him the chief architect of the whole empire. It was the most important building job in the world.

{img:sinan-studying-plans-dome-drawing-workshop-assistants-lamplight}
Sinan has already built many mosques, schools, bridges and baths. But the Süleymaniye is his greatest project so far. He wants it to be the most beautiful mosque in Istanbul. He has studied the old church of Hagia Sophia very carefully, with its huge dome. And he wants to build a dome just as wonderful, but in his own way.

Sinan is very careful about every detail. He thinks about the light, and how it will fall through the many windows. He thinks about the sound, and how the voice of the imam will be heard in every corner. He even thinks about the smoke of the thousands of oil lamps that will light the mosque at night. He designs a special little room above the entrance, where the smoke is collected. Then the soot is used to make ink for writing.

--

{img:architect-talking-with-old-stonemason-and-boy-building-site-respect}
One day, Sinan stops by the stonemasons' workshop. He looks at a carved stone that Mehmet's father has just finished. He runs his fingers over the edges. Then he nods and says that it is good work. Mehmet's father bows deeply. He is very proud. Then Sinan turns to Mehmet, who is standing beside his father, covered in dust. He asks the boy how long he has been learning.

Mehmet is so nervous he can hardly speak. He says that he has been learning for one year. Sinan smiles. He says that he himself started as a carpenter, many, many years ago. He tells Mehmet to watch carefully, to be patient and to always do his best work, even on the stones that nobody will ever see. Because God sees them. Then he walks on.

{img:old-man-walking-away-through-building-site-evening-light-dome-rising}
Mehmet never forgets those words. That evening, he tells his whole family about it, three times. Nine says that it is a sign of good fortune. Fatma wants to know exactly what Sinan looks like, and what his voice sounds like, and how big his turban is. And Mehmet's father sits quietly on the sofa, smiling to himself.

--

Sinan will live to be about ninety years old. In his long life, he will design more than three hundred buildings. Mosques, schools, bridges, aqueducts, baths, palaces and fountains, all over the Ottoman Empire. Many of them still stand today. And he will be remembered as one of the greatest architects who ever lived.
