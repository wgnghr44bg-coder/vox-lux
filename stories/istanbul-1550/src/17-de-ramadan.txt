In the autumn, the holy month of Ramadan comes. During Ramadan, Muslims do not eat or drink anything from sunrise until sunset. It is a month of prayer, of patience and of thinking about God and about other people. It is also a month of kindness, when people give food and money to the poor. And in Istanbul, it is a very special time of the year.

--

Before sunrise, while it is still dark, a drummer walks through the streets of the mahalle. He beats a drum and calls out in a singing voice, to wake everyone up. Then the families get up and eat a meal before dawn, called sahur. Mehmet's mother serves bread, cheese, olives, eggs and lots of water. Everyone eats and drinks as much as they can. Then, when the first light appears in the sky, they stop.

The days of Ramadan are long and quiet. People work, but more slowly than usual. They are hungry and thirsty, especially in the afternoon. Mehmet's father and the other workers on the building site work fewer hours. Mehmet is old enough to fast now. It is his first Ramadan of fasting the whole day. The first days are hard. His stomach rumbles, and his mouth is dry. But he is proud, and does not complain.

{img:family-waiting-sunset-iftar-table-dates-bread-soup-window-orange-sky}
In the late afternoon, the whole city waits for sunset. The women prepare the evening meal. The smell of cooking fills the streets. People gather at their tables and wait. Everyone listens for the signal. And then, as the sun sets, the call to prayer sounds from all the minarets at once. It is time to eat.

This meal is called iftar. Mehmet's family breaks the fast with a date and a glass of water, as the Prophet Muhammad did. Then they pray. And then they eat a delicious meal together. Soup, bread, pilaf, vegetables and meat. Rich families invite poor people to share their iftar. The mosques and the soup kitchens give food to anyone who comes. Nobody in the city should be hungry on a Ramadan evening.

--

{img:special-ramadan-bread-pide-bakery-queue-evening-lamps}
During Ramadan, the bakeries make a special round, flat bread, called pide, sprinkled with black seeds. It is only baked during this month. Every evening, just before sunset, there is a long queue at the bakery. The bread is warm and soft, and smells wonderful. Fatma is always sent to fetch it. She carries it home carefully in a cloth, trying very hard not to take a bite before sunset.

After iftar, the evenings of Ramadan are full of life. The great mosques are lit up with hundreds of oil lamps, glowing in the darkness. People walk through the streets, visiting friends and family. They go to the mosques for the special evening prayers. And in the squares, there are storytellers, musicians and shadow puppet theatres. The city stays awake late into the night.

{img:mosque-courtyard-night-ramadan-lamps-glowing-people-strolling-peaceful}
One evening, Mehmet's father takes the whole family to the courtyard of a great mosque, to see the lights. The courtyard is full of people, walking slowly, talking quietly. The lamps hang in the arches, like golden stars. Inside the mosque, hundreds of men are praying, and the soft sound of their voices drifts out through the open doors. Fatma holds her grandmother's hand, and looks up at the lamps with shining eyes.

--

Nine tells the children that Ramadan is the month when the gates of heaven are open. She says that it is a time to forgive people who have hurt you, and to ask forgiveness from those you have hurt. Mehmet thinks about this. He goes to Fatma, and says sorry for taking her red slippers and hiding them, the week before. Fatma forgives him. And then she says sorry too, for telling their mother about it. They both laugh.
