On the morning of the sixth of June, the dawn comes grey and pale over the sea. And the people of the Normandy coast look out of their windows. They see something they can hardly believe.

The sea is full of ships.

--

From one end of the horizon to the other, as far as they can see, there are ships. There are great grey warships and transports, and landing boats of every kind. There are thousands of them, covering the whole sea. It is like a great city that has appeared in the night, out of nowhere.

And then the landings begin on the five beaches along the coast, as the tide comes in.

{img:landing-craft-heading-to-shore-dawn-distant-beach-smoke-grey}
We will not follow the soldiers onto the beaches. It was a long and very hard day for many of them. And many of them did not come home. We will leave them there, with great respect and gratitude, in the grey morning by the sea. And we will think of them quietly, just for a moment.

--

By the evening of the sixth of June, more than a hundred and fifty thousand soldiers have landed in France. They came by sea and by air. The beaches have been taken. The first villages and farms near the coast are free. And the long road to freeing Western Europe has begun.

The farming family in Normandy has spent the whole day in the cellar. The grandmother, the children and the parents sat together on old sacks and boxes. They had a candle, a jug of water and some bread and cheese. The grandmother said her prayers, and the mother held the youngest child on her lap. The noise outside went on all day long. In the evening, at last, it grows quieter.

Then there is a knock at the door. The father goes up the cellar steps slowly. He opens the door and looks out. And there in the farmyard stand two young soldiers. They are tired and dirty and wet. They speak with a strange accent. And they are asking politely for some water.

{img:normandy-farmer-giving-water-soldiers-farmyard-evening-1944}
The father gives them water, and then a jug of cider. The mother brings bread and butter and a whole round cheese. The children come up from the cellar and stare at the soldiers with big eyes. The soldiers smile and give them chocolate from their pockets. One of them shows the children a photograph of his own little sister at home. And the grandmother cries and kisses them both on both cheeks.

--

It is the first day of freedom for the family, after four long years. There will still be many hard weeks all around them, in the fields and hedges of Normandy. But tonight, something has changed.

That night, the farmer goes out again to see his cows in the barn. They are frightened and restless, and they need milking. He talks to them softly and strokes their necks until they are calm. Then he milks them one by one, by the light of his lantern. Afterwards he stands in the orchard in the dusk, under the apple trees. He looks up at the sky. He listens to the guns, far away now. He thinks of his son, the prisoner, so far from home. And he wonders, now, if perhaps his boy will come home at last.
