Tonight we go more than eighty years back in time. We go to the south of England, in the first days of June, in the year 1944.

--

It is early summer, and the countryside is green and full of life. The hedgerows along the lanes are white with wild flowers. The wild roses are just beginning to open, pink and pale. The fields are green, and the hay is growing tall in the meadows. In the villages, the cottage gardens are bright with colour. There are lupins and foxgloves, sweet peas and early roses. And the swallows fly low over the village ponds in the evening light.

But all along the quiet country lanes, under the trees, there is something unusual. There are lorries and tanks, jeeps and big guns, parked one behind another for miles. They are covered with green nets, so they cannot be seen from the air. In the fields there are long rows of tents. And everywhere, in the villages and the towns and the lanes, there are soldiers.

If you like calm stories from the past, like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

For four years, the coast of France across the sea has been occupied by the German army. And for many months, everyone has known that one day the Allies will cross the sea. They will land in France, and they will begin to free Western Europe. Everyone knows that it is coming. But nobody knows when, and nobody knows where.

{img:english-channel-coast-white-cliffs-calm-sea-june-evening-light}
And now, in these first days of June, the whole south of England is waiting. It is as if the whole country is holding its breath. On the white cliffs, people stop on their evening walks and look out across the sea. The water is calm and grey and silver. Somewhere over there, beyond the horizon, lies France.

--

Tonight we will spend the last days and nights before the great day. Later it will be called D-Day. We will not follow the battles themselves. Instead, we will stay gently with the waiting. We will stay with the soldiers in their camps and on their ships, writing letters and playing cards. We will stay with the weathermen, studying their charts through the long night. We will meet the general who must make the hardest decision of the war. We will meet a young woman on a lonely coast of Ireland, reading a weather instrument. We will listen with people in France, who hear a poem on the radio in secret. And we will visit a farming family in Normandy, going to bed on a windy night.

And we will stand with the people of England in their gardens at midnight. They look up at a sky full of aeroplanes, and they say a quiet prayer.

{img:village-church-tower-dusk-english-countryside-june-swifts-quiet}
So let us go back to the beginning of June, 1944. Let us go back to the long, light summer evenings. Let us go back to the lanes full of soldiers and the gardens full of roses. And let us go back to the sea, grey and restless, beyond the cliffs. In a village, the church bell rings for evening, and the swifts are screaming around the tower. The sun goes down slowly over the fields. And everyone is waiting.
