By the beginning of June, every harbour and river and creek along the south coast of England is full of ships.

In Portsmouth and Southampton and Plymouth, and in every little harbour between them, the ships lie at anchor. They are packed so close together that people say you could walk across the harbour from ship to ship without getting your feet wet.

--

There are great grey battleships and cruisers with their guns. There are troop ships and supply ships. There are hospital ships, painted white with red crosses. And there are landing craft, thousands of them, of every size. Some are big flat ships with great doors in the front. The doors open onto a beach so tanks can drive out. Others are little boats for about thirty soldiers, with a ramp at the front that drops down into the water.

Altogether there are nearly seven thousand ships and boats. It is the largest fleet ever gathered in the history of the world.

{img:landing-craft-loading-tanks-hard-ramp-concrete-slipway-soldiers}
Along the shore, special concrete ramps slope down into the water. The landing craft come in and open their doors. Tanks and lorries drive on board. Soldiers march on with their heavy packs.

--

And there are stranger things in the harbours too. Huge blocks of concrete, as big as houses, floating in the water. They are waiting to be towed across the sea. They are parts of two whole artificial harbours. They will be put together off the coast of Normandy. So ships can unload there, even where there is no port.

Nobody has ever done anything like it before. Tens of thousands of workers have built them in secret for months. Most of them had no idea what they were for.

{img:mulberry-concrete-caissons-floating-estuary-tugs-evening-grey}
In the towns, people see the ships and the soldiers and the tanks rumbling toward the docks. And they know. They stand on the seafront and the cliffs. They look at the great fleet filling the harbours and the sea, as far as they can see. And they wonder.

--

At night, the ships lie dark and silent in the harbours. No lights show. Guards walk the decks. Soldiers sleep in hammocks and on the decks. And the sea washes softly against the ships, under the summer stars.
