The story of the Buddha's life was told, and retold, by his followers, for many centuries, before it was written down, and it has many wonderful, and miraculous, details, which grew, over the years. But the heart of the story is simple, and human.

He was born, the stories say, in a garden, called Lumbini, in a grove of sal trees, in blossom, in the spring, at the foot of the Himalayas.

--

His mother, Queen Maya, was travelling, from Kapilavastu, the town of the Shakyas, to her own family's home, to give birth, as was the custom. And on the way, she stopped, in the garden, at Lumbini, to rest, among the flowering trees. And there, standing, holding a branch of a sal tree, she gave birth to a son.

His father, Shuddhodana, the chief of the Shakyas, was overjoyed. They named the baby Siddhartha, which means, the one who achieves his aim. His family name was Gautama.

{img:infant-prince-wise-old-sage-asita-holding-baby-palace-courtyard}
The stories say that a wise old sage, called Asita, came down from the mountains, to see the baby, and held him, in his arms, and smiled, and then wept. And when the king asked him why, he said that he smiled, because the child would become a great teacher, who would show the world the way to freedom. And he wept, because he himself was old, and would not live to hear him.

--

Sadly, a few days after the birth, Queen Maya died. And Siddhartha was brought up, lovingly, by her sister, Mahaprajapati, who became his second mother, and who, many years later, would become the first Buddhist nun.

He grew up, in comfort, and luxury, in his father's house, at Kapilavastu. The stories say that his father had three palaces built for him, one for the hot season, one for the cool season, and one for the rains, and that he wore clothes, of the finest cloth, from Varanasi, and had music, and dancing, and gardens, with lotus pools, and everything he could wish for.

{img:young-prince-siddhartha-sitting-under-rose-apple-tree-meditating-field-ploughing}
There is a lovely story, from his childhood. One day, his father took him, to the ploughing festival, at the beginning of the planting season, where the king, himself, ploughed the first furrow. And the little boy was left, sitting, in the shade, of a rose-apple tree, while everyone else watched the ploughing. And sitting there, quietly, watching the ploughs, and the oxen, and the birds, he fell, naturally, into a deep, calm, peaceful state of meditation, all by himself. And when the nurses came back, they found the shadow of the tree had not moved, to protect him.

--

He learned everything a prince should learn: to ride, and to use a bow, and to read, and to govern. He was handsome, and clever, and kind, and everyone loved him. At sixteen, he married a beautiful cousin, called Yashodhara, and, years later, they had a son, called Rahula.

And yet, the stories say, in the middle of all this comfort, and happiness, the young prince felt that something was missing.
