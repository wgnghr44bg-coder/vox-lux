In India, the most important event of the year is the coming of the rain.

From March, to June, it is the hot season. The sun burns down, from a white sky, day after day. The earth dries, and cracks. The rivers shrink. The ponds dry up. The leaves fall from the trees, and the grass turns brown, and the dust blows, in hot winds, across the plain. Everyone waits.

--

And then, in June, the clouds begin to come, from the south-west, from the sea. At first, a few, high, white clouds. Then more, and more, building up, in great towers, dark, and heavy, and the air becomes thick, and still, and the birds fall silent.

And then, one afternoon, the wind rises, and the sky turns dark, almost black, and there is a crack of thunder, and the rain comes.

It does not rain, as it rains in other places. It pours, in great, warm, heavy sheets of water, roaring, on the thatched roofs, and the leaves, and the fields, so that, in a few minutes, the lanes are rivers, and the courtyards are ponds, and the whole world is water, and noise.

--

And everyone comes out, into the rain, laughing, and the children dance, and shout, and splash, and the old people stand, in their doorways, and lift their faces, to the sky, and the peacocks spread their tails, and dance, and scream, with joy, and the frogs come out, by thousands, and sing, all night long.

The smell of the first rain, on the dry earth, is one of the most wonderful smells in the world, rich, and sweet, and earthy, and the people of India love it.

{img:green-rice-paddies-monsoon-sky-farmers-planting-seedlings-rows}
In a few days, the whole land turns green. The grass grows, the trees put out new leaves, the rivers rise, and the ponds fill, and the farmers go out, into the flooded fields, with their oxen, to plough, and plant the rice.

--

The monsoon lasts for about four months, from June to September. It does not rain all the time. There are storms, and then sunshine, and then more storms. But the roads become mud, and the rivers flood, and travel is difficult, and dangerous.

And so, in the time of the Buddha, the wandering seekers, and the monks, who travel, from place to place, all through the year, stop, for the rainy season, and stay in one place, for three months, so that they do not have to walk through the mud, and trample on the young rice, and the tiny creatures, which come out, everywhere, in the wet.

{img:monk-shelter-grove-rain-falling-meditation-hut-thatch-quiet}
This is the rainy season retreat. And it is the beginning of the great monasteries of Buddhism: places, in groves, given by kings, and merchants, where the monks could stay, together, through the rains, and listen to the teaching, and practise, and live, in peace, while the rain fell, softly, on the roofs, and the leaves, all around them.
