Between the cities of India, along the roads, and the rivers, the merchants travel, with their caravans.

A great caravan may have hundreds of bullock carts, in a long line, creaking slowly along the dusty roads, loaded with goods: cloth, from Varanasi, which is famous for its fine cotton, and later, silk, and salt, and spices, and sugar, and iron, and copper, and pottery, and sandalwood, and perfumes, and precious stones, and horses, from the north-west.

--

The caravans travel slowly, a few kilometres a day, from village to village, and town to town, stopping at night, in a circle, with the carts around them, and fires burning, to keep away the wild animals, and guards, watching, through the night.

They cross forests, and deserts, and rivers, and mountains. There are dangers: wild elephants, and tigers, and snakes, and bandits, and floods, and droughts. In the old stories, the caravan leaders must be wise, and brave, and careful, and know where to find water, in the desert, and how to travel, at night, by the stars.

{img:caravan-camp-night-circle-carts-fires-guards-stars-forest-edge}
There is an old story, told among the Buddhists, of a caravan, crossing a great desert, at night, by the stars, guided by a desert pilot. And on the last night, the pilot falls asleep, and the oxen turn round, and walk back, all night, the way they came, and in the morning, the caravan finds itself where it started, with no more water, and no more wood.

--

But the leader is calm, and wise, and does not despair. He walks around, in the desert, until he finds a patch of grass, growing, and he thinks: there must be water, under here. And he orders the men to dig, and they dig, and dig, until they find a great rock, and they are about to give up. But the leader climbs down, and puts his ear to the rock, and hears the sound of water, flowing, underneath. And he gives a hammer, to a young servant, and tells him to strike the rock. And the boy strikes, and the rock breaks, and the water rises up, cool, and clear, and saves them all.

The story says: do not give up, too soon.

{img:river-trade-boats-ganges-cargo-boatmen-sails-sunset-merchants}
Many goods also travel by river. Along the Ganges, and its great tributaries, there are boats, large, and small, carrying goods, and passengers, up, and down, the river, from city to city, and down to the sea, at the mouth of the Ganges, where ships sail, to the south of India, and to Sri Lanka, and across the sea, to Burma, and beyond.

--

And with the merchants, travel ideas, and stories, and news, and religions. The merchants are among the first, and most generous, followers of the Buddha, because his teaching, of peace, and honesty, and moderation, suits them, and because the monks, too, are travellers, on the same roads, and the merchants, and the monks, become friends, and companions, on the long, dusty roads of India.
