When Siddhartha left his home, and became a wanderer, he walked, south, for many days, until he came to Rajagriha, the capital of Magadha.

And there, early one morning, he walked into the city, with his bowl, for the first time, on his alms round, as the wanderers did, going, quietly, from door to door, and standing, silently, waiting, for whatever the people would give.

--

The stories say that the people of Rajagriha noticed him, at once. He was young, and tall, and handsome, and he walked, so calmly, and with such grace, and dignity, looking down, at the road, in front of him, that they thought he must be someone very special. And the news spread, through the streets, and reached the palace, and the ears of the king.

King Bimbisara went up, onto the roof of his palace, and looked down, and saw the young wanderer, walking, through the streets, below, and he too was amazed, and he sent his servants, to follow him, and see where he went.

{img:king-bimbisara-visiting-young-ascetic-rock-shelter-hill-retinue}
Siddhartha collected his food, and walked out of the city, to one of the five hills, around Rajagriha, and sat down, in the shade of a rock, and ate his meal, quietly. And the servants came back, and told the king. And the king got into his royal carriage, and went out, with his court, to the hill, and walked, up to the rock, where the young man was sitting.

--

He greeted him, and sat down, beside him, and talked with him. And he was so impressed, by the young man's manner, and his words, that he said: You are young, and noble, and you seem to be born to rule. Stay here, in my kingdom. I will give you wealth, and land, and an army, to lead, and you can live a great, and happy, life.

But Siddhartha thanked him, and said, gently, that he was the son of the chief of the Shakyas, and that he had left all of that, a palace, and wealth, and power, and everything he could want, behind him, freely, because he was looking for something else, something that no palace, and no kingdom, could give: a way beyond old age, and sickness, and death, and sorrow.

{img:king-and-wanderer-parting-hillside-sunset-rajagriha-valley-below}
The king understood, and did not try to persuade him any more. But he made him promise one thing: that if he found what he was looking for, he would come back, to Rajagriha, and teach it to him, first.

--

And Siddhartha promised. And he went on, his way, to the forests, and the teachers, and the long years of searching.

And years later, after the night under the Bodhi tree, and the first teaching, in the deer park, the Buddha remembered his promise, and came back, to Rajagriha, with his monks. And the king, now older, came out, with his whole court, to meet him, and heard the teaching, and became his follower, and gave him the Bamboo Grove.

--

They remained friends, the king, and the teacher, for many years. And in the evenings, the stories say, the king would sometimes go, quietly, out of the city, to the Vulture Peak, or the Bamboo Grove, to sit with the Buddha, and listen, and talk, in the cool of the evening, as the sun went down, over the hills, of Rajagriha.
