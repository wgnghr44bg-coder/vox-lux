After leaving home, Siddhartha walked south for many days. At last he came to Rajagriha, the capital of Magadha.

Early one morning he walked into the city with his bowl for the first time. He went quietly from door to door, as the wanderers did. He stood silently at each door and waited for whatever people would give.

--

The stories say the people noticed him at once. He was young and tall and handsome. He walked so calmly and with such grace, looking down at the road in front of him. They thought he must be someone very special. The news spread through the streets. It reached the palace and the ears of the king.

King Bimbisara went up onto the palace roof and looked down. He saw the young wanderer walking through the streets below. He too was amazed. He sent his servants to follow him and see where he went.

{img:king-bimbisara-visiting-young-ascetic-rock-shelter-hill-retinue}
Siddhartha collected his food. He walked out of the city to one of the five hills. He sat down in the shade of a rock and ate his meal quietly. The servants came back and told the king. And the king got into his royal carriage and went out with his court to the hill. He walked up to the rock where the young man was sitting.

--

He greeted him and sat down beside him and talked with him. He liked the young man very much. You are young and noble, the king said. You seem born to rule. Stay here in my kingdom. I will give you wealth and land and an army to lead. You can live a great and happy life.

It was a big offer. But Siddhartha thanked him gently. He said he was the son of the chief of the Shakyas. He had already left a palace and wealth and power behind him. He had left it freely. Because he was looking for something no palace and no kingdom could give. A way beyond old age and sickness and death and sorrow.

{img:king-and-wanderer-parting-hillside-sunset-rajagriha-valley-below}
The king understood. He did not try to persuade him any more. But he asked for one promise. If you find what you are looking for, he said, come back to Rajagriha. And teach it to me first.

--

Siddhartha promised. And he went on his way, to the forests and the teachers and the long years of searching.

Years later, after the night under the great tree and the first teaching, the Buddha remembered his promise. He came back to Rajagriha with his monks. The king, older now, came out with his whole court to meet him. He heard the teaching and became his follower. And he gave him the bamboo grove.

--

The king and the teacher stayed friends for many years. In the evenings, the stories say, the king would sometimes go quietly out of the city to the bamboo grove. He would sit with the Buddha and listen and talk in the cool of the evening, as the sun went down over the hills.
