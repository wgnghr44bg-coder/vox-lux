After the first teaching, the Buddha travelled, and taught, and more, and more people came to listen, and many of them decided to follow him, and become monks.

He went to Rajagriha, the capital of Magadha. And King Bimbisara, who had met Siddhartha, years before, when he first passed through the city, as a young wanderer, and had asked him to come back, if he found what he was looking for, came out, with his whole court, to greet him, and listened to his teaching, and became his follower, and friend.

--

And the king gave him the Bamboo Grove, outside the city, a quiet, cool place, with a pond, as a place for the Buddha, and his monks, to stay. It was the first gift of land to the Sangha.

Soon, there were hundreds of monks, and later, thousands. They came from every kind of family: the sons of Brahmins, and princes, and merchants, and farmers, and craftsmen, and servants. One of the most respected of all, Upali, had been a barber, at the court of the Shakyas. In the Sangha, the old divisions of birth did not matter. The order of seniority depended only on who had joined first.

{img:monks-walking-in-line-alms-round-village-morning-bowls-silent}
The monks lived very simply. They shaved their heads, and wore simple robes, made of pieces of cloth, sewn together, like the patchwork of rice fields, dyed in the colours of earth, and bark, and saffron. They owned almost nothing: three robes, a bowl, a needle, a razor, a belt, and a water strainer, to strain out the tiny creatures, from their drinking water.

--

They did not work, or handle money. Every morning, they walked into the villages, and the towns, in a quiet line, with their bowls, and stood, silently, at the doors, and the people put food in their bowls, and the monks ate it, before midday, and did not eat again, until the next morning.

The rest of the day, they meditated, and studied, and listened to the teaching, and discussed it, and cared for each other, and walked, slowly, up and down, under the trees, in walking meditation.

{img:monastery-grove-evening-monks-gathered-listening-teacher-lamps}
For most of the year, they wandered, from place to place, teaching, and sleeping under trees, and in caves, and groves. But in the rainy season, they stayed together, for three months, in one place, in the groves, and parks, given to them, by kings, and merchants, and ordinary people, which, over time, became the first monasteries, with huts, and halls, and walking paths, and wells.

--

The Buddha himself lived the same simple life, as his monks, walking, all over the plain of the Ganges, for forty-five years, barefoot, with his bowl, teaching, every day, to kings, and farmers, and merchants, and Brahmins, and wanderers, and outcasts, and anyone who came to him, in simple language, with stories, and examples, from everyday life, that everyone could understand.

And he had followers who did not become monks: men, and women, who lived at home, with their families, and their work, and supported the monks, and tried to live by the teaching: not to kill, not to steal, not to lie, not to harm others, and to be generous, and kind, and mindful.
