The river Ganges rises far to the north, in the high mountains of the Himalayas, from an ice cave, at the foot of a great glacier, and rushes down, through the gorges, and the valleys, and comes out, onto the plains, and flows, east, for more than two thousand kilometres, across the whole of northern India, to the sea, at the Bay of Bengal.

Along the way, it is joined by many other great rivers, flowing down from the mountains: the Yamuna, and the Gandak, and the Ghaghara, and the Son, and many more, so that it becomes wider, and wider, and slower, and slower, until, in places, you can hardly see the other side.

--

For the people of India, the Ganges is not just a river. It is a goddess, Ganga, sacred, and pure, who came down from heaven, to the earth, to bless it. To bathe in her waters is to be cleansed, and to die beside her, is a blessing.

The plain of the Ganges is one of the most fertile lands in the world. Every year, in the rainy season, the rivers flood, and spread their rich, soft mud, over the land, and the soil is deep, and dark, and good. Two harvests can be grown, every year, sometimes three.

{img:ancient-forest-clearing-iron-axes-farmers-new-fields-ganges-plain}
In earlier times, much of this plain was covered with thick forest, full of wild animals: elephants, and tigers, and rhinoceros, and deer, and wild buffalo. But now, in the time of the Buddha, the forests are being cleared, with iron axes, and the land is being ploughed, with iron ploughshares, pulled by oxen, and new villages are being founded, everywhere.

--

Iron has changed everything. It makes better axes, and ploughs, and sickles, and tools, so that the farmers can clear more land, and grow more food, and so there are more people, and more villages, and more towns.

Still, the forests are never far away. Between the villages, and the fields, there are groves, of mango, and banyan, and sal, and woods, and jungles, where the wild elephants roam, and the tigers hunt, and the monkeys chatter, in the trees, and the peacocks call, at dusk, and the hermits, and the wandering seekers, live, in little huts, of leaves.

{img:peacock-dancing-feathers-spread-grove-monsoon-clouds-village-edge}
And in the north, on clear days, after the rain, you can see them, far away, on the horizon: the Himalayas, the abode of snow, white, and shining, high above the green plain, like a wall of clouds, the highest mountains in the world.

--

This great plain, of rivers, and fields, and forests, and villages, and cities, is the world in which the Buddha lived, and walked, and taught, for all of his long life, from the foothills of the mountains, in the north, to the banks of the Ganges, in the south.
