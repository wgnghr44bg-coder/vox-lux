For six years, Siddhartha searched.

First, he went to Rajagriha, and learned from two of the greatest teachers of meditation, of his time. He learned everything they could teach him, quickly, and reached the deepest states of calm, and stillness, that they knew. But he was not satisfied. These states were peaceful, but when he came out of them, the old questions, and the suffering, were still there.

--

So he went on, alone, to a quiet place, near a village called Uruvela, beside a river, with a forest grove, and he joined five other seekers, and began to practise the hardest austerities of all.

He fasted, eating only a few grains of rice, or a single bean, a day, until he became so thin, that his ribs stood out, like the beams of an old barn, and his eyes sank deep into his head, like the reflection of stars, in a deep well. He sat, in the heat, and the cold, and held his breath, and went without sleep. And the five companions admired him, and waited, for him to find the truth.

But one day, nearly fainting, he realised that this, too, was not the way. Tormenting the body did not free the mind. It only weakened it. And he remembered the day, as a child, under the rose-apple tree, when he had sat, peacefully, and calmly, and felt a deep, natural happiness. Perhaps, he thought, that is the way.

--

And so, he accepted a bowl of rice, cooked in milk, from a young woman of the village, called Sujata, who brought it to him, as an offering. And he ate it, and bathed in the river, and felt his strength come back.

His five companions were disappointed, and thought that he had given up, and they left him, and went away, to Varanasi.

{img:bodhi-tree-night-full-moon-meditator-seated-calm-river-niranjana}
And Siddhartha walked, to a great pipal tree, near the river, and sat down beneath it, on a seat of grass, given to him by a grass-cutter, facing east, and made a promise to himself: I will not get up from this place, until I have found the answer.

--

It was the evening of a full moon, in the month of Vaishakha, in May.

And through that night, the stories say, he sat, under the tree, in meditation, calmly, steadily, going deeper, and deeper. The stories tell of a great battle, that night, with Mara, the tempter, who tried to frighten him, and distract him, and make him give up, with storms, and armies, and beautiful daughters, and doubts. But Siddhartha did not move. He touched the earth, with his right hand, to call the earth itself as his witness. And the earth trembled, and Mara fled.

{img:dawn-under-bodhi-tree-morning-star-peaceful-figure-golden-light}
And through the watches of the night, his mind became clear, and calm, and bright, like a still lake. He saw, the stories say, the whole chain of his past lives, and the lives of all beings, and how they are born, and die, and are reborn, according to their actions. And he saw, at last, clearly, the cause of suffering, and the way to end it.

--

And as the morning star rose, in the east, at dawn, he became the Buddha: the one who is awake.

He was thirty-five years old. The tree, under which he sat, is called the Bodhi tree, the tree of awakening. And the place, today, is called Bodh Gaya.
