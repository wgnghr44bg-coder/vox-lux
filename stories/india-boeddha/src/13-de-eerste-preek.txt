After his awakening, the stories say, the Buddha stayed under the Bodhi tree, and near it, for several weeks, in peace, and joy, and quiet.

And he wondered whether he should try to teach what he had found. It was so deep, and so subtle, he thought, that perhaps nobody would understand it. But then, the stories say, he thought of all the people in the world, like lotus flowers, in a pond, some still deep under the water, some just reaching the surface, and some standing, above the water, ready to open, to the sun. And he decided that, for those who were ready, he would teach.

--

He thought first of his five old companions, and he walked, more than two hundred kilometres, to find them, at a deer park, at a place called Isipatana, now called Sarnath, near the city of Varanasi, beside the Ganges.

When they saw him coming, they said to each other: here comes Gautama, who gave up the struggle. Let us not stand up to greet him. But as he came closer, they saw something in his face, and his walk, so calm, and bright, and peaceful, that they stood up, without meaning to, and welcomed him, and gave him a seat, and water, to wash his feet.

{img:spotted-deer-grazing-grove-sarnath-evening-light-peaceful}
And there, in the deer park, in the evening, with the deer grazing, nearby, under the trees, he gave his first teaching. It is called, by Buddhists, the turning of the wheel of the teaching.

--

He spoke, first, of the middle way: that a person searching for the truth should avoid both extremes, the life of pleasure, and comfort, which he had known as a prince, and the life of hardship, and self-torment, which he had known as an ascetic, and follow, instead, a middle path, between them.

And then, he spoke of four noble truths. That life contains suffering, dissatisfaction, which everyone feels. That suffering has a cause: craving, and clinging, wanting things to be other than they are. That suffering can end, when craving ends. And that there is a path, to its end: a way of living, with right understanding, and right intention, and right speech, and right action, and right livelihood, and right effort, and right mindfulness, and right concentration.

{img:dharma-wheel-carved-stone-eight-spokes-sunlight-ancient}
Simple words. But the five companions listened, and understood, and one of them, Kondanna, first, and then the others, became his first followers, the first monks. And so, the community of the Buddha's followers, the Sangha, began.

--

The wheel, with eight spokes, for the eight parts of the path, became the symbol of the teaching. Two hundred years later, a great emperor will put a pillar, at Sarnath, with four lions, on top, standing back to back, above a wheel. And today, those four lions are the national emblem of India, and the wheel is in the middle of the flag of India.

And at Sarnath, today, there is a great, round, brick tower, a stupa, called the Dhamek stupa, rising out of the green park, among the ruins of old monasteries, where pilgrims walk, slowly, round, and round, and the deer still graze, in a park, nearby, under the trees.
