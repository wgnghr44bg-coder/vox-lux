Today, more than two and a half thousand years later, the places of the Buddha's life are visited by millions of people, every year, from all over the world.

At Bodh Gaya, in the state of Bihar, there is a great temple, the Mahabodhi Temple, with a tall, pointed tower, of brick, carved, all over, with little niches, and figures, rising high, above the town. It is a World Heritage Site. And beside it, behind the temple, there is a great pipal tree, with its wide, spreading branches, and its heart-shaped leaves, trembling, in the breeze: the Bodhi tree.

--

It is not the same tree, under which the Buddha sat. But it is its descendant, grown from a cutting, of a cutting, of the original tree, more than two thousand years old, which grows, still, at Anuradhapura, in Sri Lanka, brought there, the stories say, by Ashoka's daughter, the nun Sanghamitta.

Under the Bodhi tree, at Bodh Gaya, all day long, and into the night, pilgrims sit, in silence, or chant, softly, in many languages: monks, in saffron robes, from Thailand, and Sri Lanka, and Myanmar, and in maroon robes, from Tibet, and Ladakh, and in grey robes, from Korea, and in black robes, from Japan, and ordinary people, from India, and all over the world. They light little butter lamps, and lay flowers, and prostrate themselves, on the stone, and walk, slowly, round, and round, the temple, in the evening light.

{img:bodhi-tree-leaves-butter-lamps-evening-pilgrims-meditating-bodh-gaya}
At Lumbini, in Nepal, the pillar of Ashoka still stands, and there is a temple, over the place of the birth, and a pool, and a sacred garden, and monasteries, built by many countries. At Sarnath, the Dhamek stupa rises, out of the green park, and the deer still graze, nearby. At Kushinagar, the Buddha lies, on his right side, in his temple, under his golden cloth.

--

At Rajgir, the visitors climb Vulture Peak, at sunset, and the hot springs still flow. At Shravasti, the pilgrims sit, under the trees, of the Jetavana, among the ruins. At Vaishali, a pillar of Ashoka still stands, with a single lion, on the top, looking north, toward Kushinagar.

And the plain of the Ganges is still there, green, and wide, and full of life, with its villages, and its rice fields, and its mango groves, and its cattle, coming home, at the cow-dust hour, and its river, flowing, slowly, toward the sea, past the ghats, of Varanasi, where people still come, at dawn, to bathe, and pray, facing the rising sun.

{img:varanasi-ghats-dawn-ganges-boats-bathers-lamps-mist-today}
So we leave the India of the Buddha, tonight, as the evening falls.

--

The sun is setting, red, and golden, over the plain, and the river. The cattle are coming home, along the lanes, in a cloud of golden dust, with their bells ringing. The smoke of the evening fires is rising, from the villages, and the little clay lamps are being lit, in the doorways. In a quiet grove, outside the city, the monks are gathering, in the hall, and sitting, in silence, as the first stars come out, and the frogs begin to sing. And under a great pipal tree, beside a slow river, the heart-shaped leaves are trembling, softly, in the evening breeze, as the full moon rises, with a little hare, on its face, over the quiet land.
