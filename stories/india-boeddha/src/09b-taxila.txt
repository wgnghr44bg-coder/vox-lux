Far away, in the north-west of India, in what is now Pakistan, beyond the river Indus, there is a famous city of learning: Taxila.

It stands in a green valley, among the hills, on the great road, which runs, from the plain of the Ganges, to the north-west, through the mountains, to the lands of Persia, and beyond. And in the time of the Buddha, it is famous, all over India, for its teachers.

--

Young men come, to Taxila, from all over India, from Rajagriha, and Varanasi, and Shravasti, and further, walking, for months, along the great road, to study with the famous teachers. They study the Vedas, and grammar, and law, and mathematics, and astronomy, and medicine, and archery, and the art of war, and the care of elephants, and many other things.

There is no great university building, with halls, and libraries. Each teacher lives in his own house, with his pupils, who live with him, as part of his family, and work for him, in the house, and the fields, and the forest, and learn from him, by heart, sitting at his feet.

{img:students-fetching-firewood-water-teacher-house-forest-dawn}
The pupils who are rich pay their teacher, at the beginning, with a gift of money. Those who are poor work for him, during the day, fetching water, and firewood, and caring for his cattle, and study, at night, by the light of the fire.

--

The Buddha's friend, the physician Jivaka, studied medicine at Taxila, for seven years. And the stories say that some of the princes of the kingdoms of India studied there, too, and became friends, as students, and remembered each other, all their lives.

Many years later, about a hundred, or two hundred, years after the Buddha, one of the greatest scholars of ancient India, Panini, from near Taxila, will write a grammar, of the Sanskrit language, so complete, and so clever, with nearly four thousand short rules, that scholars still study it, and admire it, today, as one of the greatest works of language, ever made.

{img:ancient-scholar-writing-birch-bark-grammar-rules-lamp-night}
And when Alexander the Great comes, to India, about two hundred years after the Buddha, he will stop, at Taxila, and meet its wise men, and argue with them, and admire them.

--

Today, the ruins of Taxila, in Pakistan, are a World Heritage Site, with the remains of three cities, built, one after another, over many centuries, and of great Buddhist monasteries, and stupas, built later, on the hills around them, where monks, from all over Asia, came, to study, and pray, and live, in peace, among the green hills.
