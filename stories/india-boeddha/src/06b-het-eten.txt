What do the people of India eat, in the time of the Buddha?

Above all, rice. Rice is the great food, of the plain of the Ganges: boiled, and steamed, and made into porridge, and flattened, and puffed, and ground into flour, for little cakes, and sweets. The finest rice, fragrant, and white, is called shali, and is eaten by the rich, and offered to the gods, and the monks.

--

With the rice, there are lentils, and beans, and peas, of many kinds, cooked into soups, and stews, called dal, and vegetables: gourds, and pumpkins, and aubergines, and cucumbers, and radishes, and leafy greens, and lotus roots, and bamboo shoots. And there is milk, and curds, and butter, and ghee, from the cows, and the buffaloes.

And there are spices. The food of India is already full of flavour: ginger, and turmeric, and black pepper, and long pepper, and cumin, and coriander, and mustard seed, and cardamom, and cloves, and cinnamon, and asafoetida, and sesame. There are no chillies yet. They will come, from America, two thousand years later. But the food is spicy, and warm, and fragrant, all the same.

{img:fruit-market-ancient-india-mangoes-bananas-jackfruit-coconuts-baskets}
There is fruit, everywhere: mangoes, above all, the king of fruits, in many varieties, sweet, and golden, and juicy, in the hot season. And bananas, and jackfruit, and coconuts, and dates, and grapes, and pomegranates, and the little sour fruits, of the jujube tree, and the wood apple, and the rose apple.

--

And there are sweets. The people of India love sweet things. They make a kind of sugar, from the juice of the sugar cane, boiled, until it becomes thick, and brown, and sticky, and then hard, like toffee. In fact, the word sugar, and the word candy, come, in the end, from the old languages of India. With it, and with honey, they make sweets, and cakes, of rice flour, and sesame, and milk, and ghee, and coconut.

Meat, and fish, are eaten, by many people, though some, especially among the Brahmins, and the Jains, and later the Buddhists, eat little, or none, because they do not want to harm living creatures.

{img:wedding-feast-ancient-india-guests-sitting-rows-banana-leaf-plates}
At a feast, or a wedding, the guests sit, in long rows, on the floor, or on low seats, and the food is served, on great banana leaves, or plates, made of leaves, stitched together, with little twigs, many dishes, one after another: rice, and dal, and vegetables, and curds, and pickles, and fried cakes, and sweets, and fruit, and milk, sweetened with sugar, and flavoured with cardamom.

--

And everyone eats with their right hand, carefully, and neatly, mixing the rice, and the dal, with their fingers, into little balls. And after the meal, they wash their hands, and their mouths, and chew a little leaf, of the betel vine, wrapped around a piece of areca nut, and a little lime, and spices, which freshens the breath, and helps the digestion, and turns the mouth red, as people in India still do, today.
