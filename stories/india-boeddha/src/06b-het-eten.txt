What do people in India eat in the time of the Buddha?

Above all, rice. Rice is the great food of the Ganges plain. It is boiled and steamed. It is made into porridge. It is flattened and puffed. It is ground into flour for little cakes and sweets. The finest rice is white and smells sweet. The rich eat it. And it is offered to the gods and the monks.

--

With the rice there are lentils and beans and peas of many kinds. They are cooked into thick soups. There are vegetables. Pumpkins and cucumbers and radishes and greens. Lotus roots and bamboo shoots. And there is milk and yogurt and butter from the cows and buffaloes.

And there are spices. The food of India is already full of flavour. Ginger and turmeric and black pepper. Cumin and coriander and mustard seed. Cardamom and cloves and cinnamon. There are no chillies yet. They will come from America two thousand years later. But the food is spicy and warm and smells wonderful all the same.

{img:fruit-market-ancient-india-mangoes-bananas-jackfruit-coconuts-baskets}
There is fruit everywhere. Mangoes above all, the king of fruits. In the hot season they are sweet and golden and juicy. There are bananas and coconuts and dates and grapes and pomegranates. And many small sour fruits from the forest trees.

--

And there are sweets. The people of India love sweet things. They boil the juice of sugar cane until it turns thick and brown and sticky. Then it hardens, like toffee. In fact, the words sugar and candy come in the end from old languages of India. With sugar and honey they make sweets and cakes of rice flour and sesame and milk and coconut.

Many people eat meat and fish. But some eat little or none. They do not want to harm living creatures.

{img:wedding-feast-ancient-india-guests-sitting-rows-banana-leaf-plates}
At a feast or a wedding, the guests sit in long rows on the floor. The food is served on big banana leaves, or plates made of leaves stitched together with little twigs. Many dishes come one after another. Rice and lentils and vegetables. Yogurt and pickles and fried cakes. Sweets and fruit. And milk sweetened with sugar and flavoured with cardamom.

--

Everyone eats with their right hand, carefully and neatly. They mix the rice and lentils with their fingers into little balls. After the meal they wash their hands and mouths. Then they chew a little leaf wrapped around a piece of nut and spices. It freshens the breath and helps the stomach. And it turns the mouth red. People in India still do this today.
