Let us spend a day with a family in a village by the Ganges.

The father is a farmer. He has a few fields of rice and barley, a pair of oxen and two cows. The mother runs the house and looks after the children and the animals. She works in the fields too, at planting and harvest. There are three children and the father's parents. His younger brother and his wife live there too. They all share a house of several rooms around a courtyard.

--

They wake before dawn, when the first birds start to sing and the sky grows pale in the east. The father and the grandfather go down to the river to bathe. They pour water from their cupped hands toward the rising sun and say a prayer.

The mother sweeps the courtyard and smears the floor with fresh cow dung and water. She draws a little pattern at the door with white rice flour, for good luck. Then she lights the fire in the clay stove and milks the cows. She boils the milk and makes breakfast. Rice porridge with milk and a little sugar. Or yogurt and some fruit.

{img:farmer-leading-oxen-to-fields-dawn-boy-following-mango-trees}
Then the father and his brother go out to the fields with the oxen. The eldest son is ten. He takes the cattle to the grazing land by the river. He sits under a tree all day and watches them. He plays his bamboo flute. And he keeps an eye on the edge of the forest. Sometimes a big cat comes near the herd at dusk.

--

The women work at home. They grind grain on a flat stone, rolling a round stone back and forth. They pound rice in a wooden bowl with a long stick to get the husks off. They spin cotton. And they fetch water from the well in clay pots, carried on their heads and hips.

The younger children play in the courtyard and the lane with the other village children. They play with clay toys and carts and balls and tops and little bows. They play with the dogs and goats and calves. They climb the mango trees and swing on the hanging roots of the great tree.

{img:family-midday-meal-courtyard-leaf-plates-rice-lentils-sitting-floor}
At midday the men come home to eat. They sit on mats on the floor in the shade. They eat from leaf plates with their right hands. Rice and lentils and vegetables cooked with spices. Ginger and pepper and cumin and a little butter. Yogurt and pickles and fruit. Then in the heat of the afternoon, everyone rests.

--

In the late afternoon the men go back to the fields. The women go to the river to wash clothes and fetch water. They talk with the other women on the bank. And the children swim and play in the shallows.

In the evening, as the sun sets, the cattle come home from the grazing land. They raise a cloud of golden dust along the lane. Their bells ring. The boy walks behind them with his flute. In India, this time of day is called the cow-dust hour.

{img:cow-dust-hour-cattle-returning-village-sunset-golden-dust-bells}
The family eats supper together by the light of the fire and a little clay oil lamp. Afterwards they sit in the courtyard under the stars. The grandfather tells the children stories. Stories of the gods and heroes. Stories of clever animals. The monkey and the crocodile. The jackal and the lion. And the wise old elephant.

--

Then they sleep on beds woven of string. In hot weather they sleep in the courtyard or on the roof. In the rains and the cool season they sleep inside. The frogs sing in the pond. Far away in the fields the jackals howl. And the night watchman walks through the village, calling out now and then that all is well.
