Let us spend a day with a family, in a village, near the city of Varanasi, beside the Ganges.

The father is a farmer, with a few fields of rice, and barley, and a pair of oxen, and two cows. The mother runs the house, and looks after the children, and the animals, and works in the fields, at the planting, and the harvest. There are three children, and the father's parents, and his younger brother, and his wife, all living together, in a house, of several rooms, around a courtyard.

--

They wake before dawn, when the first birds begin to sing, and the sky grows pale, in the east. The father, and the grandfather, go down to the river, to bathe, and pour water, from their cupped hands, toward the rising sun, and say a prayer.

The mother sweeps the courtyard, and smears the floor with fresh cow dung, and water, and draws a little pattern, at the door, with white rice flour, for good luck. Then she lights the fire, in the clay hearth, and milks the cows, and boils the milk, and makes the morning meal: rice, boiled, or a thin rice porridge, with milk, and a little sugar, from the sugar cane, or curds, and some fruit.

{img:farmer-leading-oxen-to-fields-dawn-boy-following-mango-trees}
Then the father, and his brother, go out, to the fields, with the oxen, and the eldest son, who is ten, goes with them, to look after the cattle, and drive them, out to the grazing land, by the river, where he will sit, under a tree, all day, and watch them, and play his bamboo flute.

--

The mother, and her sister-in-law, and the grandmother, work, at home: grinding grain, on a flat stone, with a round stone, rolling, back, and forth, and husking rice, in a wooden mortar, with a long pestle, pounding, and spinning cotton, on a little spindle, and fetching water, from the well, in clay pots, carried on their heads, and on their hips.

The younger children play, in the courtyard, and the lane, with the other children of the village: with clay toys, and carts, and balls, and tops, and little bows, and arrows, and with the dogs, and the goats, and the calves, and they climb the mango trees, and swing on the roots of the banyan.

{img:family-midday-meal-courtyard-leaf-plates-rice-lentils-sitting-floor}
At midday, the men come home, to eat. They sit on the floor, in the shade, on mats, and eat from leaf plates, or brass bowls, with their right hands: rice, and lentils, and vegetables, cooked with spices, ginger, and turmeric, and pepper, and cumin, and a little ghee, and curds, and pickles, and fruit. Then, in the heat of the afternoon, everyone rests.

--

In the late afternoon, the men go back to the fields, and the women go to the river, to wash the clothes, and fetch water, and talk, with the other women, on the bank, while the children swim, and play, in the shallows.

And in the evening, as the sun sets, the cattle come home, from the grazing land, raising a cloud of golden dust, along the lane, with their bells ringing, and the boy, walking behind them, with his flute. This time of day, in India, is called the cow-dust hour.

{img:cow-dust-hour-cattle-returning-village-sunset-golden-dust-bells}
The family eats the evening meal, together, by the light of the fire, and a little clay lamp, burning mustard oil. And afterwards, they sit in the courtyard, under the stars, and the grandfather tells the children stories: stories of the gods, and the heroes, and the clever animals, the monkey, and the crocodile, and the jackal, and the lion, and the wise old elephant.

--

And then they sleep, on string beds, called charpoys, in the courtyard, or on the roof, in the hot weather, or inside, in the rains, and the cool season, while the frogs sing, in the pond, and the jackals howl, far away, in the fields, and the night watchman, walking through the village, calls out, now and then, that all is well.
