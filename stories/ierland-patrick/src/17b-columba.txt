About a hundred years after Patrick, another great Irish saint carried the faith across the sea to Scotland. His name was Columba. In Irish he was called Colm Cille, the dove of the church.

He was born in the northwest of Ireland, a prince of a royal family. He could have been a king. But he became a monk and a scholar instead, and founded monasteries in Ireland.

--

There is a famous story about him. He secretly copied a beautiful book of psalms that belonged to another monk. That monk said the copy belonged to him, because the book was his. The quarrel went all the way to the High King, who said: to every cow its calf, and to every book its copy. So the copy belonged to the other monk. Some people call it the first copyright case in history.

The story goes on to say that the quarrel grew and led to a battle. Columba was full of sorrow. He decided to leave Ireland as a penance, and never see it again.

{img:currach-twelve-monks-sailing-to-iona-sea-islands-dawn}
In 563 he sailed with twelve companions in a leather boat across the sea, to the little island of Iona off the west coast of Scotland. There he founded a monastery that became one of the most famous in Europe.

--

Iona is a small, beautiful island, with white sand beaches, clear turquoise sea, green fields and wide views of the mountains on the nearby island of Mull. From Iona, Columba and his monks brought Christianity to the north of Scotland. Later his monks went south to England and founded another famous monastery there.

Columba was a great poet and writer of books, and a strong, sometimes fierce leader. All his life he loved Ireland and missed it. A poem said to be by him speaks of a grey eye that looks back on Ireland, and will never see its people again.

{img:old-monk-white-horse-weeping-head-on-shoulder-iona-evening}
A monk who wrote his life tells a beautiful story about his last day. The old saint was walking slowly, and sat down to rest beside the road. The old white horse that carried the milk pails came up to him. It laid its head on his chest and wept, because it knew its master was going to leave it. Columba blessed the horse. And that night, in the church, he died peacefully among his monks.
