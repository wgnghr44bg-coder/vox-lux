Patricius was born, around the year 390, in Roman Britain, somewhere near the west coast, perhaps in what is now Wales, or the north west of England, or Scotland. Nobody knows exactly where. He says that his home was at a place called Bannavem Taburniae, near a little villa, which belonged to his father.

His family were Romans, and Christians, and quite well off. His father, Calpornius, was a deacon of the Church, and a town councillor. His grandfather, Potitus, was a priest. They spoke Latin, and British, and lived in the Roman way.

--

Patricius tells us, in a little book he wrote when he was an old man, his Confession, that as a boy he did not care much about God, or about his studies. He was careless, and lazy, and did not pay attention to his teachers, which he later regretted, because his Latin was never very good.

And then, when he was sixteen, everything changed.

Irish raiders came to his father's villa, in their boats, from across the sea, and took him, with many thousands of other people, he says, and carried him away to Ireland, as a slave.

--

In Ireland, he was sold to a master, and sent to look after his sheep, or his pigs, on a mountain, in the north of Ireland. Tradition says that it was the mountain of Slemish, in what is now County Antrim, in Northern Ireland, a strange, rounded hill, rising out of the plain. Others think it was in the far west, near the wood of Foclut, by the western sea.

For six years, he looked after the animals, alone, on the mountain, in all weathers.

{img:slemish-mountain-rounded-hill-sheep-boy-shepherd-mist-alone}
He was cold, and hungry, and lonely, and far from home. And in his loneliness, he began to pray.

He writes: after I came to Ireland, I was tending flocks every day, and I prayed many times a day. More and more, the love of God, and fear of Him, grew in me, and my faith grew, and my spirit was moved, so that in a single day I would say as many as a hundred prayers, and almost as many at night. I would even stay in the woods, and on the mountain, and before dawn, I would wake up to pray, in the snow, and the frost, and the rain. And I felt no harm.

--

Then, one night, after six years, he heard a voice, in his sleep, saying: you have fasted well. Soon you will go to your own country. And a little later: look, your ship is ready.

So he ran away. He walked, he says, about two hundred miles, across Ireland, to a place he did not know, where there was a ship, about to sail. At first, the captain refused to take him. But then the sailors called him back, and he went with them.

{img:small-ship-leaving-irish-coast-young-man-looking-back-dawn}
After a long and difficult journey, he came home, to his family, in Britain. They welcomed him, and begged him never to leave them again.

But he could not forget Ireland.
