Patricius was born around the year 390 in Roman Britain, somewhere near the west coast. Nobody knows exactly where. He says his home was near a little country house that belonged to his father.

His family were Romans and Christians, and fairly well off. His father was a deacon of the church and a town councillor. His grandfather was a priest. They spoke Latin and lived the Roman way.

--

When he was old, Patricius wrote a little book about his life, called his Confession. In it he tells us that as a boy he did not care much about God or his lessons. He was careless and lazy and did not listen to his teachers. Later he was sorry, because his Latin was never very good.

And then, when he was sixteen, everything changed.

Irish raiders came to his father's farm in their boats from across the sea. They took him, along with many others, and carried him away to Ireland as a slave.

--

In Ireland he was sold to a master and sent to look after sheep or pigs on a mountain in the north. Tradition says it was a strange, rounded hill rising out of the plain, in what is now Northern Ireland.

For six years he looked after the animals, alone on the mountain, in all weathers.

{img:slemish-mountain-rounded-hill-sheep-boy-shepherd-mist-alone}
He was cold and hungry and lonely, far from home. And in his loneliness he began to pray.

He writes: every day I was looking after the flocks, and I prayed many times a day. My faith grew. In one day I would say a hundred prayers, and almost as many at night. I would wake before dawn to pray, in the snow and the frost and the rain. And I felt no harm.

--

Then one night, after six years, he heard a voice in his sleep. You have fasted well, it said. Soon you will go to your own country. And a little later: look, your ship is ready.

So he ran away. Running away was dangerous. A slave who was caught could be punished harshly. But he walked, he says, about two hundred miles across Ireland, to a place he did not know, where a ship was about to sail. At first the captain refused to take him. Patricius turned away and began to pray. Then the sailors called him back, and he went with them.

{img:small-ship-leaving-irish-coast-young-man-looking-back-dawn}
After a long, hard journey, he came home to his family in Britain. They welcomed him with joy, and begged him never to leave them again.

But he could not forget Ireland.
