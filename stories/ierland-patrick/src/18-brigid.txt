After Patrick, the most loved saint of Ireland is a woman, Saint Brigid of Kildare.

Tradition says she was born about twenty years after Patrick came to Ireland. Her father was a chieftain and her mother was a slave woman. She grew up as a servant in her father's house, working in the dairy and looking after the cows.

--

The stories about her are full of kindness and generosity, and full of milk and butter and cows. As a girl, she gave away everything in her father's house to the poor. The butter, the milk, the food, and even her father's precious sword, which she gave to a sick man so he could sell it and buy food. Her father was furious and tried to sell her to a king. But the king was so impressed by her goodness that he refused to buy her. He told her father to let her be.

She became a nun and founded a great monastery at Kildare, in the middle of Ireland, under a great oak tree. The name Kildare means the church of the oak.

{img:kildare-monastery-wooden-church-round-houses-oak-tree-sacred-fire}
It was a monastery for both nuns and monks, and Brigid was its leader. She became one of the most powerful and respected people in Ireland. Kildare became a great centre of learning and art and care for the poor.

--

At Kildare, the nuns kept a sacred fire burning day and night for hundreds of years. Perhaps it went back to the old goddess Brigid, whose spring festival became the feast of Saint Brigid on the first of February.

There are many stories of her wonders. Her cows gave milk three times a day. A single basket of her butter never ran out. She hung her wet cloak to dry on a sunbeam. And when she asked a king for land for her monastery, he laughed. You can have as much as your cloak will cover, he said. So she spread out her cloak, and it grew and grew, until it covered the whole great plain of Kildare.

{img:green-plain-curragh-kildare-horses-grazing-wide-open-morning}
On Saint Brigid's Day, people in Ireland still make Brigid's crosses. They are small crosses woven from rushes, with four arms and a square in the middle. People hang them in their houses and barns for protection for the coming year. The legend says Brigid made the first one at the bedside of a dying chieftain, to explain the cross of Christ to him.

--

Brigid died at Kildare around the year 525. She is one of the three patron saints of Ireland, with Patrick and Columba. Since 2023, her feast day, the first of February, has been a public holiday in Ireland. It is the first Irish public holiday named after a woman.
