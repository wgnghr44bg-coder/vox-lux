In the old Irish stories, there is another world, beside this one: the Otherworld, the land of the sídhe, the fairy people.

The sídhe live inside the hills, and the ancient mounds, and the old ringforts, in beautiful halls, of gold, and crystal, where it is always summer, and there is always music, and feasting, and nobody grows old, or sick, or dies.

--

The old stories say that the sídhe were once the gods of Ireland, the Tuatha Dé Danann, the people of the goddess Danu, a race of gods, and magicians, who ruled Ireland, long ago, before the ancestors of the Irish came, from Spain, across the sea. And when the Irish defeated them, the Tuatha Dé Danann went into the hills, and the mounds, and became the people of the hills, the fairies, and still live there, invisible, beside us.

Some of the greatest of these mounds are the great passage tombs of the Boyne valley: Newgrange, and Knowth, and Dowth, built by the first farmers of Ireland, five thousand years ago, older than the pyramids, which, the stories say, were the homes of the gods: Newgrange was the house of the god Aengus, the god of love, and youth, and beauty.

{img:newgrange-white-quartz-front-stone-mound-boyne-valley-winter-sunrise}
And at Newgrange, on the shortest day of the year, the winter solstice, in December, at dawn, the rising sun shines through a little opening, above the entrance, called the roof box, and sends a beam of golden light, along the long, dark passage, for nineteen metres, into the very heart of the tomb, and lights up the chamber, for seventeen minutes, and then fades away. It has done so, every year, for five thousand years.

--

The fairies can be dangerous, the old people say, if you are not careful. They can steal children, and leave a fairy child, a changeling, in their place. They can lead travellers astray, at night, with lights in the bog. They can take people into their mounds, to dance, for what seems like one night, but when the person comes out, a hundred years have passed.

But they can also be kind, and helpful, to those who respect them. People leave a little milk, or butter, or food, for them, on the doorstep, at night. And they never cut down a lone hawthorn tree, in a field, because it belongs to the fairies.

{img:lone-hawthorn-tree-field-white-blossom-may-stone-wall}
On the night of Samhain, and the eve of May, the fairies come out of their hills, and ride through the countryside, in great processions, with music, and lights. And sometimes, on those nights, people in the old days would hear the music of the fairies, coming from inside the hills: the most beautiful music in the world.
