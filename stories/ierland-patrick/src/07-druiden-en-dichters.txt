Before Patrick came, the religion of Ireland was the old religion, with many gods and spirits and sacred places.

The priests of the old religion are the druids. They are the wise men, and sometimes women, of Ireland. They are priests and teachers, judges and advisers to the kings. They know the old knowledge, the gods and the stars, and the right times to do things. People believe they can read the future in the flight of birds, the clouds and the stars.

--

The druids train for many years. They learn everything by heart, because they never write their knowledge down. They are greatly respected and feared, and they have great power. A king must listen to his druid. In the old stories, the druid even speaks before the king at the feast.

In the stories of Patrick, the druids are his great opponents. They try to stop him with magic, arguments and threats. They have the most to lose if the new faith wins. And over the following centuries, as Christianity spreads, they do lose their power.

{img:filid-poet-reciting-harp-feast-hall-firelight-listeners-rapt}
But there is another learned group in Ireland that does not disappear. They are the poets.

--

The poets of early Ireland do much more than write poems. They are the historians and the keepers of family trees and old stories. They know the history of every family, the deeds of every king, and thousands of lines of stories about gods and heroes, all by heart.

Becoming a master poet takes twelve years of training. It is said the students lay in dark rooms with a stone on their bellies, making up poems in their heads in the dark. Then they came out into the light and recited them.

{img:dark-room-student-poets-lying-composing-candle-stone-quiet}
The poets are honoured and paid by the kings. They are given land and cattle and treated almost like kings themselves. They travel from court to court with their followers and are welcome everywhere.

And they are feared. A poet can make a mocking poem about a king or lord who is mean or unjust. People believe such a poem can shame him so badly that it raises blisters on his face. No king wants to be mocked by a poet.

--

When Christianity came to Ireland, the poets did not disappear. Many became Christians and kept their learning and their stories. Later, monks wrote those stories down. So the old tales of Ireland were saved, and can still be read today. Tales of heroes like Cú Chulainn and Finn, and of the Children of Lir.

And there are the storytellers who tell stories by the fire in ordinary homes on winter evenings. In the Irish countryside, such storytellers went on telling the old tales until the twentieth century.
