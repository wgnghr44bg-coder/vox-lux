Let us spend an ordinary day in a ringfort in Ireland, in the spring of the year 432.

The family are free farmers. They own about twenty cows, some sheep and pigs and a horse, and fields of oats and barley. They share a plough with the neighbours, and a mill.

--

The day begins at dawn. Grey light comes in through the doorway. Birds sing in the hedges, and the cows low in the yard, waiting to be milked.

The mother is up first, as always. She blows the embers back into flame and adds turf and wood. The girls go out to milk the cows into wooden pails. They sit on little stools in the yard and sing to the cows to keep them calm.

Breakfast is simple. Oat porridge from the cauldron over the fire, with milk or butter, and perhaps a little honey.

--

Then the work of the day begins.

The men and boys drive the cattle out to graze. They plough the fields with oxen for the spring sowing. They mend the fences and the banks, cut wood, and dig turf from the bog for the fire. The turf is cut in long slices with a special spade, and laid out on the bog to dry in the wind and sun.

{img:men-cutting-turf-peat-bog-slean-spade-drying-stacks-spring}
The women and girls make butter and cheese. They grind oats and barley on the grinding stones and bake bread. They spin and weave wool, and dye it with plants. Yellow from one plant, blue from another, red from a root. They look after the hens and geese, the calves and the little children.

--

At midday they eat bread, butter and cheese, and drink buttermilk.

In the afternoon, the work goes on. Then a traveller comes to the gate, a man on foot with a staff, a bag and a cloak. He is welcomed in, as all travellers are, and given food, drink and a place by the fire for the night. In Ireland, welcoming guests is sacred. The law says a free farmer must feed his guests and give them a bed.

{img:traveller-welcomed-ringfort-gate-dog-family-greeting-evening}
In the evening, the cattle are brought back into the yard, safe from the wolves. The family gathers by the fire with the traveller for the main meal. Perhaps meat or fish, with bread, milk and ale. And the traveller tells them the news from the far side of the kingdom. Who has died, who has married, which king has raided which. And one more thing. A strange bishop from Britain has come to Ireland, he says, and is going around telling people about a new god. The family looks at each other. What will the king say about that?

--

Then the grandmother tells an old story by the fire. A story about heroes of long ago, or about the fairies who live inside the green mounds under the hills. The children listen wide-eyed until they fall asleep.

The fire is covered with ashes for the night so it will not go out. And the family goes to sleep in their beds around the walls, with the dogs by the door, the cattle breathing in the yard, and the rain whispering on the thatch.
