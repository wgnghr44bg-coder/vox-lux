Over the centuries, many stories and legends grew up around Saint Patrick. They are told all over the world today.

The most famous is the story of the shamrock. Patrick was trying to explain how God can be three, the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, and yet still be one God. So the legend says he picked a shamrock, a little clover with three leaves on one stem. He held it up and said: this little plant has three leaves, and yet it is one plant. In the same way, God is three, and yet one.

--

Nobody wrote this story down until more than a thousand years after Patrick died. But the shamrock has become the symbol of Ireland and of Saint Patrick. On Saint Patrick's Day, people all over the world wear a sprig of shamrock, or something green.

Another famous legend says Patrick drove all the snakes out of Ireland. He stood on a mountain, the story says, rang his bell, and sent every snake on the island into the sea, and they never came back.

In fact, scientists think there have been no snakes in Ireland since the last Ice Age. When the ice melted, the sea rose and cut Ireland off from Britain before the snakes could get there. But perhaps the legend is really a way of saying that Patrick drove out the old religion.

--

The mountain in that story is Croagh Patrick, Patrick's mountain. It is a tall, beautiful, cone-shaped mountain in the far west of Ireland, rising steeply from a bay full of little green islands.

The legend says Patrick climbed it and stayed on top for forty days and forty nights, fasting and praying for the people of Ireland. Demons came to him in the form of black birds to frighten him. But he drove them away by ringing his bell, and throwing it at them.

{img:pilgrims-climbing-stony-path-croagh-patrick-summer-morning}
Every year, on the last Sunday of July, thousands of pilgrims climb Croagh Patrick in his honour. Some climb barefoot up the steep, stony path to the little chapel on top, as people have done for more than a thousand years. On a clear day, from the top, you can see the whole bay with its islands, and the Atlantic Ocean stretching away to the west.

--

Another legend tells of a cave on a little island in a lake in the north. The story says Christ showed Patrick that this cave was the entrance to Purgatory. In the Middle Ages, it became one of the most famous pilgrimages in Europe. Knights came from far away to visit it.

Pilgrims still go to that island every summer, for three days of fasting and prayer. They walk barefoot around old stone circles, and stay awake all night in the church on the island.

{img:lough-derg-island-basilica-lake-grey-sky-boats-pilgrims}
And there are many more stories. About Patrick's bell and his staff, his contests with the druids. In one old tale, two heroes of ancient Ireland come back from the Otherworld after hundreds of years. They walk with Patrick through Ireland, telling him the old stories of every place they pass.
