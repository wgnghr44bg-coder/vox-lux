We do not know exactly when Patrick died. The old Irish records give two different dates. Many historians think it was sometime in the second half of the fifth century, when he was an old man.

But tradition says he died on the seventeenth of March. That is why Saint Patrick's Day is held on the seventeenth of March every year, all over the world.

--

Tradition also says he died at Saul, near the place where he first landed in Ireland, and built his first church in a chieftain's barn, so many years before.

When he died, the story goes, people argued about where he should be buried. So his body was put on a cart pulled by two untamed oxen, and the oxen were left to go wherever they wished. They walked and walked, and at last they stopped at a place a few kilometres away. That place became the town of Downpatrick. And there he was buried.

{img:downpatrick-cathedral-hill-granite-stone-grave-green-grass}
Today, on the cathedral hill in Downpatrick, there is a great rough granite stone lying on the grass, with a cross and the name Patric carved on it. It marks the place where tradition says he lies. People come from all over the world to visit it and leave flowers and shamrocks, especially on the seventeenth of March.

Tradition says that Saint Brigid and Saint Columba, two other great saints of Ireland, were later buried in the same grave beside him.

--

By the time Patrick died, Ireland was changing.

There were churches, priests and bishops all over the north and middle of Ireland. In the generations that followed, Christianity spread over the whole island peacefully, without war. That was very unusual in the history of the world.

{img:early-church-site-round-tower-stone-crosses-green-valley-later}
The old kings remained, and the old laws, the old poets and the old way of life with its cattle, ringforts and feasts. But now there were churches beside the ringforts. And soon there were monasteries, which became the centres of learning, art and books in Ireland.

In the centuries that followed, Ireland would become the island of saints and scholars. It would send its own missionaries to Scotland and England, France, Switzerland and Italy. They would bring faith and learning back to the rest of Europe, in the dark centuries after the fall of Rome.
