The hill of Tara is the most sacred place in ancient Ireland.

It is not very high, only about a hundred and fifty metres above the sea. But it stands alone in the middle of the wide, flat, green plains. From the top, on a clear day, people say you can see half of Ireland. Mountains in the south, hills in the north, and plains stretching away to the west.

--

People have come to Tara for five thousand years. The oldest monument on the hill is a stone tomb built by the first farmers, older than the pyramids of Egypt. After that, over thousands of years, people built great circles of banks and ditches, wooden posts and mounds.

In Patrick's time there are great earthworks on the hill. There is a big oval enclosure called the fort of the kings, with two ring-shaped forts inside it. And there is a long, sunken avenue. The stories say the great feasts of the High Kings were held there, with thousands of guests, each in their proper place.

{img:lia-fail-stone-of-destiny-upright-stone-tara-mound-grass}
On top of one mound stands the Stone of Destiny, a tall, smooth, grey stone. The legend says the old gods brought it to Ireland from one of their magical cities. When the true king of Ireland stood on it, it roared so loudly that all of Ireland could hear.

--

The stories say a great feast was held at Tara every few years, in autumn. All the kings, nobles, poets and lawyers of Ireland came. The laws were announced, and the history of the kingdoms was recited. The poets checked the family trees to make sure they were correct. Quarrels were settled, and peace was kept for the feast. Anyone who broke the peace of Tara by fighting was put to death.

{img:great-assembly-tara-tents-banners-kings-poets-autumn-evening}
Nobody knows how much of this is true and how much is legend. But for the Irish, Tara was, and still is, the symbol of the ancient glory of Ireland. The seat of the High Kings, and the heart of the land.
