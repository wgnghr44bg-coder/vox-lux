Most Irish families in the fifth century live in ringforts.

A ringfort is a round farm, about thirty or forty metres across. A circular bank of earth surrounds it, with a ditch outside, and often a wooden fence or a hedge on top. There are tens of thousands of them all over Ireland. Many can still be seen today in the fields, as round grassy rings, often with trees growing on them.

--

The bank is not really for war. It keeps the cattle in at night, safe from wolves and from cattle thieves. And it shows everyone that a free family lives here.

Inside the bank there are one or two round houses, a few small buildings for storage and animals, and a yard.

The round houses are made of wattle. Wooden stakes are driven into the ground in a circle, and thin branches of hazel or willow are woven between them, like a basket. Sometimes the walls are covered with clay. Sometimes there are two woven walls with moss or straw packed between them, to keep out the cold. The roof is a pointed cone of poles, thatched with straw or reeds. There is no chimney, so the smoke from the fire seeps out through the thatch.

--

Inside, the house is dim and smoky and warm. It smells of turf, wood smoke, food and animals.

In the middle is the hearth, a fire of wood or turf that burns day and night and is never allowed to go out. Around the walls are beds, little woven corners with straw, rushes, hides and woollen blankets. There are wooden tubs and buckets and bowls, a pair of stones for grinding grain, a loom for weaving, and leather bags hanging from the roof.

{img:inside-round-house-central-hearth-family-beds-wattle-walls-dim}
Richer houses have a few precious things. A bronze cauldron hanging over the fire on a chain, a wooden chest, brooches, a fine cloak, perhaps a board game.

--

A bigger family might have a few servants, and perhaps a slave or two, often taken in raids on Britain, just as Patricius was.

In the evening, the whole household gathers around the fire to eat and talk, tell stories, sing and play games. The cattle are safe in the yard inside the bank, and the dogs guard the gate.

{img:crannog-lake-dwelling-island-round-houses-causeway-mist}
Some richer families live on little islands they have built in lakes, out of stones, timber and earth, with round houses on top. They can be reached only by a narrow path or by boat. Raiders cannot easily get to them. On a misty morning, smoke rises from the thatch over the still water, ducks swim around the island, and a fisherman sets out in his little boat.
