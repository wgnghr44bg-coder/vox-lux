The Irish, in the time of Patrick, share their island with many animals.

The most famous Irish dog is the wolfhound: a huge, tall, shaggy, grey dog, the tallest dog in the world, strong enough to hunt wolves, and wild boar, and deer, and gentle, and loyal, and calm, at home. The kings and nobles keep them for hunting, and as guards, and they are greatly prized. Roman writers tell of Irish wolfhounds being sent as gifts to Rome, where the people were amazed at their size.

--

In the old stories, the hero Cú Chulainn got his name, which means the hound of Culann, because, as a boy, he killed the fierce guard dog of the smith Culann, in self-defence, and then, feeling sorry, offered to take its place, and guard Culann's house himself, until a new dog could be raised.

There are wolves in the forests, and wild boar, and red deer, the largest wild animals of Ireland, with their great antlers, and badgers, and foxes, and otters, and pine martens, and hares, and red squirrels. And in the rivers, there are salmon, and trout, and eels.

{img:red-deer-stag-antlers-misty-oak-forest-autumn-morning}
The salmon is the most respected of all the fish. In the old story, there is a salmon, the Salmon of Knowledge, which lived in a pool, in the river Boyne, under a hazel tree, and ate the hazelnuts which fell into the water, and so gained all the knowledge in the world. An old poet, Finnegas, spent seven years trying to catch it, because whoever ate it would know everything.

--

At last, he caught it, and gave it to his young servant, a boy called Fionn, to cook, and told him not to eat any of it. But while Fionn was cooking it, he burned his thumb on the hot skin of the fish, and put his thumb in his mouth, to cool it. And at that moment, all the knowledge of the salmon passed into him.

And ever after, whenever Fionn wanted to know something, he only had to put his thumb in his mouth, and he would know it. He grew up to be Fionn mac Cumhaill, the great hero, the leader of the Fianna, the band of warriors who protected Ireland, and hunted, and fought, and feasted, in the old stories.

{img:boy-cooking-salmon-fire-riverbank-hazel-tree-thumb-legend}
And on the coasts, there are seals, which the people of the west believe are sometimes enchanted people, the selkies, who can take off their sealskins, and come ashore, as beautiful men and women. And the seabirds, puffins, and gannets, and gulls, and guillemots, nesting on the cliffs, in their millions, as they still do today.
