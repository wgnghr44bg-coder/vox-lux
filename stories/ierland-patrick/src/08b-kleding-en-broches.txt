What do the Irish wear in the fifth century?

The main garment is a long tunic of linen or wool, reaching to the knees or the ankles, with long sleeves. It is often dyed yellow or white, and tied at the waist. Over it goes a big rectangular woollen cloak, wrapped around the shoulders and pinned at the chest with a brooch.

--

The cloak is the most important garment. It tells everyone who you are. The law says which colours each rank may wear. A king may wear seven colours, a poet six, a lord four, and so on down to the poor, who wear only one colour, or plain undyed wool.

The cloaks are woven from the family's own sheep's wool and dyed with plants. Some have fringes and coloured borders. The rich wear cloaks of purple, crimson, blue and green, with borders of gold thread.

{img:woman-dyeing-wool-vats-plants-colours-yellow-blue-red-yard}
The brooch that holds the cloak is a little work of art. It is usually a ring with a small gap, and a long pin that goes through the cloak and the ring, and is turned to lock it in place. Ordinary people have brooches of bronze or iron. The rich have brooches of silver or gold, decorated with spirals and animals and coloured enamel.

--

In the centuries after Patrick, Irish craftsmen will make some of the most beautiful brooches in the world. The most famous was made around the year 700, of silver and gold. It is covered in tiny patterns of birds, animals, spirals and knots, so fine it is hard to believe it was made by hand. It is now in the National Museum in Dublin.

The men wear their hair long, often with beards or long moustaches. The women wear their hair long in braids, sometimes with little golden balls at the ends.

{img:ornate-silver-gold-brooch-filigree-spirals-museum-close-light}
They wear leather shoes made from one piece, laced up. In summer, many go barefoot, even nobles.

And everyone, rich and poor, has a cloak against the rain and wind, and sleeps in it at night, wrapped up by the fire. For centuries, the Irish cloak was famous all over Europe. It was big, thick, warm and shaggy, and it kept out even the Irish rain.
