What was the world like, that Patricius came from?

He grew up in Roman Britain, in the last years of Roman rule. For more than three hundred years, Britain had been a province of the Roman Empire, with Roman towns, and roads, and villas, and baths, and temples, and churches, and soldiers, and laws, and coins. Rich families, like Patricius's, lived in villas, country houses in the Roman style, with mosaic floors, and painted walls, and underfloor heating, and baths, and gardens, and farms, worked by slaves, and tenants.

--

They spoke Latin, at least the educated ones, and British, the Celtic language of the island, the ancestor of Welsh. They read Latin books. They were Christians, many of them, by the end of the fourth century, with churches, and bishops, and priests.

But in the years when Patricius was a boy, the Roman world was falling apart. Barbarians were attacking the empire on every side: Goths, and Vandals, and Huns, in Europe, and in Britain, Picts, from the north, and Saxons, from across the North Sea, and Scots, which was the Roman name for the Irish, from the west.

In the year 410, about four years after Patricius was captured, the Roman emperor wrote to the cities of Britain, telling them that they must defend themselves, because Rome could no longer send soldiers to help them. The Roman army left. And slowly, over the following decades, Roman Britain crumbled: the towns emptied, the villas were abandoned, the coins stopped, the roads fell into ruin.

--

By the time Patrick wrote his Confession, as an old man, the Britain of his childhood was gone. The Saxons, and the Angles, from Germany and Denmark, were settling in the east of the island, and would become the English. The British were retreating to the west, to Wales, and Cornwall, and Cumbria, and Brittany, across the sea.

It was the beginning of the time that later people would call the Dark Ages. And in those dark years, the light of learning, and of Latin books, and of the Christian faith, which Patrick carried back to Ireland, would be kept burning, in the monasteries of Ireland, and later carried back again, by Irish monks, to Britain, and Europe.

Archaeologists have found many Roman villas in Britain, with their beautiful mosaic floors, of gods, and dolphins, and the seasons, and Christian symbols, which tell us how families like Patricius's lived, in the last golden years before the end.
