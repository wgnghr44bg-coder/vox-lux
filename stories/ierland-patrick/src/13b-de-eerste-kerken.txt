What were the first churches in Ireland like?

They were small and simple. Little rectangular buildings of oak planks, or woven branches covered in clay, with a thatched roof. Often they were no bigger than a single room, with a small altar at the east end, a door at the west end, and perhaps one little window.

--

Beside the church there was a small house for the priest, a graveyard, perhaps a well, and a cross. At first the crosses were simple wooden ones, or crosses carved on a standing stone. Later, the Irish would carve great stone crosses, covered with pictures from the Bible and spirals and knots, with a ring around the middle. That is the famous Celtic cross.

The priests wore simple woollen robes and carried a staff and a bell. It was a little iron handbell, rung to call people to prayer. It became the sign of the Irish saints. Many of these old bells still survive.

The Irish monks had their own special haircut. They shaved the front of their heads from ear to ear, and let the hair at the back grow long. It was different from the haircut of the monks of Rome, who shaved a round patch on top. Later this little difference would cause great arguments.

--

The services were in Latin, which the people did not understand. But the priests explained them in Irish. People came on Sundays and feast days to hear Mass, and to be baptised, married and buried. And the priests went around the countryside blessing the houses, the cattle, the fields and the wells.

A few hundred years later, the Irish began to build their churches in stone. They were small, steep-roofed and very strong. Some still stand today. One little stone chapel in the far southwest is shaped like an upturned boat. It was built without mortar, from carefully fitted stones, and it has kept out the rain for more than a thousand years.

And later still came the tall, slim round towers beside the monasteries, with pointed stone caps and doors high above the ground. They were bell towers and lookout towers. And they were safe places, where the monks could hide their treasures, and themselves, when raiders came.
