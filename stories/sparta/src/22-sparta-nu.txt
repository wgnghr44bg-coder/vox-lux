Today, there is a small town in the valley called Sparti. It was built about two hundred years ago, on the place where the old city stood. It is a pleasant, quiet town with wide streets and squares.

Orange trees line the streets, and in spring the air smells of their blossom. People sit in cafés in the main square, drinking coffee and talking. Children play on the pavements in the evening. And above the town, the Taygetos mountains rise up, with snow on their peaks in winter. It is the same view the Spartans saw, long ago.

--

At the north end of the town, there is a statue of Leonidas. He stands with his shield and his spear, looking out over the valley. Visitors from all over the world come to take photographs of it. Near the statue, there is a small museum. Inside, you can see the little lead figures from the sanctuary, and the painted masks. You can see the marble soldier with the calm face.

{img:sparti-museum-visitors-looking-clay-masks-glass-case-quiet-today}
If you walk to the edge of town, you come to the old acropolis hill. It is covered with olive trees now. Among them are the low remains of walls and temples. You can find the stone seats of the theatre, and sit there in the sunshine. Lizards sun themselves on the warm stones. And the only sound is the wind in the olive leaves.

--

Down by the river, the sanctuary of Artemis Orthia is quiet and overgrown. There are only a few stones and the seats of the little Roman theatre. The river Eurotas still flows past, clear and cold. Reeds still grow along its banks. And sometimes, people say, you can still see swans there.

{img:eurotas-river-today-reeds-plane-trees-quiet-water-evening-light}
The valley around the town is still full of farms. The olive groves stretch for kilometres along the slopes. The farmers still pick the olives in winter, with nets spread under the trees. The olive oil of Laconia is still famous in Greece. There are orange groves too, and vineyards and fields of vegetables. In spring, the meadows are still red with poppies.

--

Every year, a great running race is held from Athens to Sparta. It follows the path of a famous messenger from long ago. His name was Pheidippides, and he ran all the way to Sparta to ask for help in a war. It is about two hundred and fifty kilometres. Today, runners from all over the world take part. They run through the night and arrive in Sparta the next day. At the end, they touch the foot of the statue of Leonidas. Then local girls give them a crown of olive leaves and a cup of water.

{img:runner-arriving-sparta-statue-leonidas-olive-crown-crowd-dusk-today}
Many of the families in the valley have lived here for a very long time. The grandmothers still tell stories in the evenings, sitting in the doorways. The children still run races along the river. The old men still sit on benches in the sun and watch everything that happens. And in the evening, when the sun goes down behind the Taygetos, the whole valley turns gold, just as it always has.

In the evenings, the people of Sparti walk slowly through the streets and squares. This evening walk is an old Greek custom. Families stroll together, greeting their neighbours. Old couples walk arm in arm. Children run ahead and back again. And the smell of grilled food and orange blossom drifts through the warm evening air.
