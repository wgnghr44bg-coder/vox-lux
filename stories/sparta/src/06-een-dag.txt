Let us follow Agiatis through one ordinary day in spring.

After breakfast, she puts on her cloak and walks out to the family's farm in the valley. It is about an hour's walk, along a path between olive groves and fields. The morning air smells of earth and wild thyme. Birds are singing in the hedges, and bees are busy among the flowers. She walks quickly, with long steps, because she has much to do today.

At the farm, Lykos is already at work in the barley field. He is pulling out weeds, bent over in the rows of green shoots. When he sees Agiatis coming, he straightens up and comes to meet her. They talk about the crops and the weather. The barley looks good this year, he says. If the rain comes at the right time, there will be a fine harvest.

--

Agiatis walks around the farm with him. She looks at the olive trees, which are just beginning to flower. She looks at the vines, with their new green leaves. She checks the goats and the sheep in their pen. Lykos's wife comes out of the farmhouse with a bowl of water and some bread. Agiatis thanks her and drinks.

{img:helot-farmer-barley-field-weeding-spartan-woman-talking-spring}
The two women do not talk very much. They come from very different worlds. Agiatis is free, and Lykos's family is not. Agiatis knows this, and so does Lykos's wife. It is something that lies between them, quiet and heavy, like a stone. But on this spring morning, they talk about the children and the goats. And for a little while, they are simply two mothers standing in the sun.

--

By midday, Agiatis is back home in the village. She eats a simple lunch with her mother-in-law and the children. There is bread and cheese, some boiled beans with olive oil, and a few onions. Afterwards, the grandmother takes a short rest in the shade of the olive tree. The children go outside to play.

{img:spartan-women-talking-at-well-village-noon-water-jugs-shade}
In the afternoon, Agiatis goes to visit a friend in the next street. The women of Sparta visit each other often. They sit in each other's courtyards and talk about everything. They talk about their farms, their husbands and their children. They talk about the news from the assembly, and the latest gossip in the five villages. Spartan women have strong opinions, and they share them freely.

Later, she goes to the river with some other women to watch the girls running races. Kleo is there, with her friends from the village. They race along the track by the river, their feet pounding on the hard earth. Agiatis watches with a small smile. She was a fast runner too, when she was young.

--

As the sun goes down, the men of the village walk off to their common meals. Damon stops by the house on his way and kisses his mother on the forehead. He ruffles Nikandros's hair and asks Kleo how the racing went. I won, she says, of course. He laughs and walks on down the street, his red cloak swinging behind him.

{img:spartan-family-supper-courtyard-lamplight-mother-grandmother-children}
At home, Agiatis eats supper with the grandmother and the children. There is barley porridge, a little fish from the river, and some figs. They eat by the light of a small oil lamp in the courtyard. The evening is warm, and the crickets are singing. The grandmother tells a story about a fox who tried to steal grapes. Nikandros laughs at the funny parts. Kleo pretends she is too old for stories, but she listens to every word.

When the children are in bed, Agiatis sits alone in the courtyard for a while. She looks up at the stars, which are bright and clear tonight. She thinks about her son, and about the day very soon when he will leave. She knows it is the Spartan way. All Spartan boys must go. But tonight, she lets herself feel a little sad. Then she blows out the lamp and goes to bed.

On the way home from the farm, Agiatis stops at a small spring beside the path. The water comes out of the rock, cold and clear, into a little stone basin. She cups her hands and drinks. A small shrine to the nymphs of the spring stands nearby, with a few flowers left by other travellers. She adds a wild flower of her own. Then she walks on, feeling refreshed.
