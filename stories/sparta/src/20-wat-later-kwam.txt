After the great war with Persia, Sparta was one of the two most powerful cities in Greece. The other was Athens. For a while, they were friends. But slowly, they grew suspicious of each other. Athens grew rich from the sea and built a great fleet. Sparta stayed in its valley, with its army and its old ways.

Some fifty years after our story, Athens and Sparta went to war with each other. It was a long and hard war that lasted almost thirty years. In the end, Sparta won. For a short time, it was the strongest city in all of Greece.

--

But Sparta's way of life had a weakness. There were never very many Spartan citizens, and over the years there were fewer and fewer. Fewer men could pay their share of the common meals. More and more land ended up in the hands of a few rich families. And the iron money and the simple life were slowly forgotten. Some Spartans even began to keep gold and silver, secretly.

{img:messenian-farmers-free-again-building-new-city-ithome-mountain}
About a hundred and ten years after our story, Sparta lost a great battle to another Greek city, Thebes. After that, the Thebans helped the helots of Messenia to become free. They built a new city on a mountain called Ithome. For the first time in hundreds of years, the people of Messenia could farm their own land for themselves. It was a great day for them. The old stories say they danced in the streets.

--

After that, Sparta was never as strong again. But it went on as a city for a very long time. Later, the Romans came and ruled all of Greece. Under the Romans, Sparta became a kind of famous old curiosity. Visitors came from all over the Roman world to see it. They wanted to see the famous Spartan ways, which by then were mostly a show for visitors.

{img:roman-era-visitors-sanctuary-artemis-orthia-theatre-seats-watching}
The Romans even built a little theatre beside the sanctuary of Artemis Orthia. Visitors could sit there and watch the old Spartan customs. Rich Romans sent their sons to Sparta to see the training. The Spartans of that time were proud of their past. They kept the old festivals going, and they sang the old songs.

--

Much later, the old city slowly emptied. In the Middle Ages, people moved up into the hills, to a new town called Mystras. It was built on a steep slope, with churches and houses and a castle at the top. The old valley was left to the farmers and the olive trees. The old temples crumbled and were covered by earth and grass.

{img:mystras-hillside-medieval-churches-castle-above-sparta-valley-later}
But the name of Sparta was never forgotten. People all over the world kept telling the stories. They told of the three hundred at Thermopylae, and of the brave Spartan mothers. They told of the black broth and the iron money and the short, clever sayings. Even today, when someone says that a room is spartan, they mean it is very plain and simple. And when someone gives a short, clever answer, we still say it is laconic. That word comes from Laconia, the valley of Sparta.

In the villages of the valley, life went on through all those centuries. Farmers ploughed the fields and picked the olives, as they always had. Children played by the river and splashed in the shallows. And every spring, the poppies came back to the meadows, red and bright, just as they did in the time of Agiatis and Kleo.

Many travellers visited the valley over the centuries. Some came to see the ruins, and some came to see the famous river. They sat under the plane trees by the water and ate bread and olives. They looked at the mountains and tried to imagine the old city. And they went home and told their friends about the quiet, beautiful valley where Sparta had been.
