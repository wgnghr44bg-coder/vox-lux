All this time, far away in the east, something important has been happening. A great king has been gathering an enormous army. He is Xerxes, the king of Persia. Persia is the biggest empire in the world. And Xerxes wants to bring all of Greece under his rule.

His army is huge, bigger than anything the Greeks have ever seen. There are soldiers from dozens of different lands. There are hundreds of ships. In the summer of 480 before Christ, this great army crosses into Greece from the north. And the Greek cities must decide what to do.

--

Many Greek cities decide to stand together against Persia. They ask Sparta to lead them. But there is a problem. It is the time of the Karneia festival, and during the Karneia, the Spartans may not go to war. That is a sacred rule. The Spartan leaders talk for a long time. In the end, they decide that the main army will wait until the festival is over. But one king will go ahead with a small group of men.

{img:king-leonidas-leaving-sparta-small-group-soldiers-dawn-families-watching}
The king who goes is Leonidas. He chooses three hundred Spartan men to go with him. He chooses only men who already have sons, so that their families will go on. Many soldiers from other Greek cities join them along the way. Together, perhaps seven thousand Greeks march north.

The people of Sparta come out to watch them leave. Queen Gorgo stands at the edge of the crowd. People say she asked Leonidas what she should do if he did not return. Marry a good man, he told her, and have good children. Then he turned and walked away with his men. Damon is not among the three hundred. He stays at home, with the main army, waiting for the festival to end.

--

Leonidas and his men go to a narrow pass called Thermopylae. The name means the hot gates, because there are warm springs there. On one side of the pass there are steep mountains. On the other side, there is the sea. The way through is very narrow, only wide enough for a few carts side by side. In such a narrow place, a small army can hold back a very large one.

{img:thermopylae-narrow-pass-mountains-sea-warm-springs-mist-ancient}
For two days, the small Greek army holds the pass. The great Persian army cannot get through. But on the third day, the Persians find a hidden mountain path. They can now go around the Greeks. Leonidas understands what this means. He sends most of the Greek soldiers away, to be safe. But he and his three hundred Spartans stay, with a few hundred others. They stay to give the others time to get away.

We will not follow what happened next. It was a brave and very sad day. And by the end of it, Leonidas and his men had all passed away. We will leave them there, in the narrow pass between the mountains and the sea, with great respect.

--

In Sparta, the news arrives some days later. A messenger comes running into the five villages. People gather in the streets and the agora to hear. The news spreads from house to house, very quietly. In many homes, there is sorrow. Families who have lost someone sit together in silence.

{img:spartan-women-gathered-quietly-evening-courtyard-sad-news-lamps}
Agiatis hears the news at the well. She walks home slowly and tells the grandmother. The grandmother closes her eyes for a long time. She knew some of those men when they were boys. Kleo sits down on the step of the courtyard and puts her face in her hands. Damon comes home early from the common meal. He sits with his family in the courtyard, and nobody says anything for a long while.

Later, the Greeks put up a stone at Thermopylae. On it, they carved a short poem. It said: Go tell the Spartans, passer-by, that here, obedient to their laws, we lie. Those few words became some of the most famous words in the world. And the story of the three hundred has been told ever since, for two and a half thousand years.

In the weeks that follow, the families of Sparta help each other. Neighbours bring food to the houses where someone has been lost. The women sit together in the courtyards and weave and talk quietly. The children play together, a little more gently than before. And slowly, as the weeks pass, the five villages find their way back to everyday life.
