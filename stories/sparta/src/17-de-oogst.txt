In early summer, the barley in the valley turns golden. It is time for the harvest.

On Agiatis's farm, Lykos and his family begin cutting the barley at dawn. They work with curved sickles, cutting the stalks close to the ground. The eldest son cuts beside his father, row after row. Melitta and her daughter follow behind, gathering the stalks into bundles. They tie each bundle with a twist of straw. The little boy carries water to everyone in a small clay jug.

It is hot, hard work. By midday, the sun is blazing, and everyone is sweating. They rest for a while in the shade of an old fig tree. They eat bread and cheese and drink water mixed with a little vinegar. It is refreshing in the heat. Then they go back to the field, and keep cutting until the light fades.

--

When all the barley is cut, it is carried to the threshing floor. This is a flat, round place of hard beaten earth, near the farmhouse. The bundles are spread out on the floor. Then oxen are led round and round over them. The hooves of the oxen shake the grain loose from the stalks.

{img:threshing-floor-oxen-walking-circle-barley-farmhouse-summer-dust}
After that comes the winnowing. On a windy afternoon, the family tosses the grain into the air with wooden shovels. The wind blows away the light straw and dust. The heavy grain falls back down onto the floor in a golden heap. It is beautiful to watch, the grain falling like rain, shining in the sun.

--

Agiatis comes out to the farm to see the harvest. She brings Kleo with her, and the grandmother rides in a small cart pulled by a donkey. The grandmother likes to see the harvest every year. She sits in the shade and watches, remembering the harvests of her youth. Kleo helps to gather up the last bundles. She is surprised how heavy they are, and how quickly her back starts to ache.

{img:spartan-girl-helping-harvest-carrying-barley-bundle-helot-girl-together}
Kleo works beside Lykos's daughter, a girl of ten called Thalia. At first they do not talk. But then Thalia shows her a clever way of tying a bundle quickly. Kleo tries it, and it works. They laugh together. By the end of the afternoon, they are chatting like old friends. Thalia tells Kleo about her little brother and the goats. Kleo tells Thalia about running and the choir.

When it is time to go, Kleo waves to Thalia from the cart. She thinks about her new friend all the way home. She thinks about how hard Thalia works, every single day. And for the first time, she wonders what it would be like to be in Thalia's place.

--

At the end of the harvest, the grain is measured out. Half goes to Agiatis's family, as the law requires. It is carried to the house in the five villages, in big sacks on the backs of donkeys. The other half stays with Lykos's family. It must last them until next year. Lykos looks at his heap of grain carefully. It is a good harvest. There will be enough this year.

{img:harvest-festival-helots-evening-fire-dancing-flute-threshing-floor}
In the evening, the helot families of the valley celebrate the end of the harvest. They light a bonfire beside the threshing floor. They roast a young goat and share bread and new wine. A man plays the flute, and the young people dance around the fire. The old people sit and watch, clapping in time. Lykos sits with his wife and children, tired but content.

The stars come out over the valley, bright and many. The fire crackles and sends sparks up into the night. Melitta sings an old harvest song, and the others join in. It is a song about the golden fields and the kind goddess of the grain. And the sound of it drifts softly across the dark fields, all the way to the river.

The next day, Agiatis sends a gift to Lykos's family. It is a small jar of honey and a piece of good cheese. It is not much, but it is a thank you for the harvest. Melitta opens the jar and lets each child dip a finger into the honey. The little boy licks his finger slowly, with his eyes closed. It is the sweetest thing he has tasted all year.
