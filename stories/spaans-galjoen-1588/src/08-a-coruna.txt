The fleet stays in the harbour of A Coruña for about a month. It is a time of hard work, but also of rest.

A Coruña is a busy port town on a rocky point of land. There is an old Roman lighthouse on the hill above it, called the Tower of Hercules. It has stood there for more than a thousand years. Its light has guided ships safely into the harbour for centuries. Diego looks up at it every morning when he wakes. It makes him feel safe.

--

The carpenters and the caulkers work from dawn to dusk. They repair the broken masts and yards. They fix the planks that were damaged in the storm. They fill the cracks with new rope and hot tar. The sail-makers sit on the deck with big needles, sewing new patches onto the torn sails. The sound of hammers and saws fills the harbour all day long.

{img:ship-carpenters-repairing-mast-yard-tools-sawdust-deck-sunshine}
New food is brought on board, fresh and good this time. There are new biscuits, new barrels of water, and fresh meat and vegetables. The bad food is thrown away. The men are happy to eat fresh bread and fruit again. Some of the sick men are taken ashore to rest and get better. And some new men come on board, to take the places of those who are too ill to sail.

--

In the evenings, when the work is done, the men have some free time. Some of them go ashore to the town. Others stay on board and rest. They play cards or dice on the deck. They mend their clothes and wash them in buckets. They write letters home, or ask Fray Juan to write them, if they cannot write themselves.

{img:friar-writing-letter-for-sailor-deck-evening-quill-paper-lantern}
Diego cannot write. So one evening, he sits down beside Fray Juan and asks him for help. Fray Juan smiles and takes out his paper and quill. Diego tells him what to write, slowly. Dear mother and father, he says. I am well. The ship is big and the food is good, mostly. I have a friend called Andrés. An old sailor called Tomás is teaching me knots. There was a storm, but I was not afraid, not very much. Please give my love to everyone. Your son, Diego.

Fray Juan writes it all down carefully. Then he shows Diego how to write his own name at the bottom. Diego holds the quill and makes the letters slowly, one by one. D, I, E, G, O. It is a bit wobbly, but it is his name. He looks at it for a long time, very pleased. Fray Juan says he will teach him more letters on the voyage. And Diego promises to practise every evening.

--

On Sundays, everyone goes to mass. On the Santa Catalina, Fray Juan sets up a little altar on the deck. The men kneel on the wooden planks, with their caps in their hands. The gentle sound of the waves mixes with the prayers. Afterwards, the men have a better meal than usual, with a little extra wine.

{img:sunday-mass-galleon-deck-friar-altar-sailors-kneeling-harbour}
Tomás takes Diego up to the lighthouse one evening, when they are allowed ashore. They climb the hill together slowly, because Tomás's knees are old. At the top, they sit on a rock and look out over the sea. The sun is going down, and the water is red and gold. The ships of the fleet lie quietly in the harbour below. Tomás tells Diego about his first voyage, long ago, when he was twelve too. I was frightened, he says. Everyone is, at the start. But the sea becomes your friend, in time. Diego listens and nods. And they walk back down to the harbour in the dusk, as the lighthouse begins to glow above them.

While they are in A Coruña, the men are paid some of their wages. Diego gets a few small silver coins. He has never had so much money in his life. He goes to the market in the town with Andrés. He buys a little knife with a wooden handle, because every sailor needs a knife. And he buys a small bag of dried figs to share with his friends. The rest of the money he keeps safely in a little cloth bag around his neck. He wants to bring it home to his mother.
