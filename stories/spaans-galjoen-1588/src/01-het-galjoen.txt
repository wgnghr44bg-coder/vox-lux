Tonight we travel more than four hundred years back in time. We go to the great harbour of Lisbon, at the mouth of a wide river. The year is 1588. And in the harbour, more than a hundred big ships are lying at anchor, waiting to sail.

--

It is early morning in May, and the water is calm and grey. A light mist hangs over the river. The tall masts of the ships rise up through the mist like a forest of bare trees. Their sails are rolled up tight on the long wooden yards. Flags hang still in the windless air. Gulls cry and circle over the water, looking for scraps.

Small rowing boats move between the ships, carrying people and barrels and sacks. A bell rings on one of the ships, and then on another. Somewhere a man is singing a slow song as he works. On the shore, the city of Lisbon is waking up, with its white houses climbing the hills. Church bells ring out across the water. It is quiet and peaceful, and full of quiet waiting.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

{img:galleon-santa-catalina-high-stern-castle-painted-carvings-morning-light}
One of these ships is called the Santa Catalina. She is a galleon, a big Spanish sailing ship with three tall masts. Her back end rises high out of the water, like a little wooden castle. It is painted red and gold and carved with saints and lions. Her sides are dark brown wood, coated with tar to keep out the water. She is about forty metres long. And more than two hundred people will live on board her for months.

Tonight we will sail with the Santa Catalina. We will meet a ship's boy of twelve, who is going to sea for the very first time. We will meet the old sailors and the soldiers, the cook and the carpenter and the priest. We will learn how they ate, and slept, and told the time, and found their way across the sea. We will hear their songs in the evening and their prayers at dusk.

{img:ships-boy-twelve-leaning-rail-galleon-looking-at-lisbon-morning}
This galleon is part of a very large fleet, which the Spanish call the Most Fortunate Fleet. In English, it will later be called the Spanish Armada. It is going to sail north, to England. And later, it will face long storms and cold seas, far from home. There will be moments when much is at stake. But tonight, we will mostly stay with the ordinary people on board, and with their everyday life at sea.

--

So let us go aboard the Santa Catalina, on a spring morning in Lisbon. The deck is wet with dew, and the ropes creak softly as the ship moves on the tide. Sailors are already up, coiling ropes and scrubbing the deck with buckets of seawater. The smell of tar and salt and wood smoke is everywhere.

{img:galleon-deck-morning-sailors-scrubbing-coiling-ropes-mist-lisbon}
At the front of the ship, a thin line of smoke rises from the cooking fire. The cook is boiling a big pot of something for breakfast. A cat walks along the rail, looking for mice. And a boy with dark curly hair stands near the mainmast, looking up at the great masts and ropes with wide eyes. His name is Diego. And this is his first morning at sea.

Diego has never been on such a big ship before. His father's fishing boat at home is small and open, with one little sail. This ship is like a whole village made of wood. There are people everywhere, climbing and pulling and carrying. There are ladders going down into the darkness, and ropes going up into the sky. Diego stands still and tries to take it all in. A sailor hurrying past bumps into him and laughs. Out of the way, little one, he says kindly. And Diego steps aside, smiling shyly, and keeps on looking.
