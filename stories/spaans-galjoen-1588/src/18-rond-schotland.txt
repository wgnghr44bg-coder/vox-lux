In the middle of August, the Spanish fleet sails around the far north of Scotland. This is a wild and beautiful place, at the very edge of the known world.

There are many small islands here, with steep cliffs and green grass on top. Thousands of seabirds nest on the cliffs. There are puffins with their bright orange beaks, and gannets diving into the sea. Seals lie on the rocks and watch the ships go past with big dark eyes. On some islands, there are little stone houses with smoke rising from their roofs. But there are no trees at all, only grass and heather and rock.

--

The weather up here changes quickly. One moment, the sun is shining and the sea is blue. The next moment, a thick fog rolls in, and the men can barely see the front of their own ship. In the fog, the ships of the fleet lose sight of each other. They fire guns and ring bells so they do not run into each other. It is eerie and quiet in the fog, with only the sound of the waves.

{img:thick-fog-at-sea-ship-bell-ringing-sailor-lantern-ghostly-calm}
On the Santa Catalina, the pilot Martín is very careful. He does not know these waters well. There are no good charts of this coast. He keeps the ship far out to sea, away from the rocks. Every day, he measures the sun if he can see it. He marks their position on his chart, with careful little dots. Slowly, the line of dots turns west, and then south.

--

The men are very tired now. Many of them are ill. The barber-surgeon goes from man to man, doing what he can. He gives them his medicines and his kind words. Fray Juan sits with them and prays. Diego and Andrés help where they can. They carry water and blankets to the sick men. They sit with them and talk to them, so they will not feel alone.

{img:ships-boys-carrying-water-blankets-to-tired-sailors-below-deck-lantern}
Diego himself stays healthy, thanks partly to Tomás. The old sailor has a few secret tricks. He has a little bag of dried lemons from Spain, which he has saved for a long time. Every few days, he gives Diego a small piece to suck. Lemons keep you healthy at sea, he says. Nobody else seems to know this. But Tomás learned it on his long voyages, from an old sailor long ago.

--

On one calm evening, the fog lifts, and the sky clears. The sun sets slowly over the sea, very late, because it is summer in the north. The sky glows red and orange for hours. The men come up on deck to look at it. They have not seen the sun for days. After the sunset, the sky stays light, a strange pale blue, almost all night.

{img:northern-summer-night-sky-pale-blue-glow-galleon-silhouette-calm-sea}
Then something wonderful happens. Ribbons of green light appear in the northern sky. They move and ripple slowly, like curtains in the wind. The men stare in wonder. Some of them cross themselves. They have never seen anything like it. It is the northern lights, Tomás tells Diego. I saw them once before, many years ago. They are a gift from God, to remind us that there is beauty everywhere. Diego watches the lights until they fade, and then he goes to sleep with a smile on his face.

One afternoon, a small boat comes out from one of the islands. Two fishermen are in it, with long beards and woollen caps. They speak a language nobody on the Santa Catalina understands. But they hold up some fish and point to the ship. The captain lets them come alongside. The fishermen trade their fish for a few small coins and some cloth. Everyone smiles and nods, and somehow they understand each other. Then the little boat rows away, back to the island. That night, the whole crew eats fresh fish.
