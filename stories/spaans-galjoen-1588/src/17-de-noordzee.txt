After the battle, a strong wind begins to blow from the south-west. It pushes the Spanish fleet away from the coast, north into the North Sea. The ships cannot sail back against it. They cannot reach the Spanish army. And they cannot go back the way they came, through the narrow Channel.

The duke calls his officers together on his ship. They talk for a long time. In the end, they make a difficult decision. They will sail north, all the way around Scotland, and then south past Ireland. That is the only way to get home to Spain. It is a very long way, through cold and stormy waters that few of them know.

--

On the Santa Catalina, the captain tells the crew the plan. The men are quiet. Many of them have never sailed in those northern seas. They have heard stories of fog and storms and rocky coasts. But there is no other choice. And at least, they say, they are going home.

{img:captain-speaking-to-crew-gathered-deck-serious-faces-grey-light}
The English fleet follows them north for a few days. Then it turns back, because the English ships are short of food and gunpowder too. Soon the Spanish ships are alone on the grey sea. The wind blows cold now, even in August. The men take out their warmest clothes and wrap themselves in blankets on the deck.

--

There is a new worry now: food and water. The voyage will be much longer than planned. The duke orders that the food must be shared out very carefully. Each man now gets only half a pound of biscuit a day, and a little water and wine. The horses and mules that were brought for the army must go, because there is not enough water for them. The men are very sad to see them go.

{img:sailors-sharing-small-rations-biscuit-water-cups-cold-wind-deck}
On the Santa Catalina, the men share what they have. The officers give some of their own food to the sailors. Pedro the cook makes the beans last as long as he can, adding more water to the stew every day. Diego is hungry most of the time now. But he does not complain. Tomás tells him that hunger is a good teacher. It teaches you to be grateful for every crumb.

--

The days pass slowly. The sea is grey, and the sky is grey. Sometimes it rains for days without stopping. The men are tired, cold and hungry. But they keep working. The sailors work the sails and pump out the water. The boys turn the sand-glass and sing the hours. And in the evening, the pages still sing the Salve Regina, although their voices are thinner now.

{img:ships-boy-singing-evening-hymn-grey-dusk-sea-men-listening-quietly}
One evening, Diego is the one who sings. The page who usually sings is ill, so Fray Juan asks Diego to do it. Diego is nervous. He stands on the deck in the cold wind and begins to sing, softly at first. Hail, holy queen, mother of mercy. His voice grows stronger as he goes on. The men take off their caps and listen. When he finishes, there is a moment of silence. Then old Tomás nods at him, and wipes his eye with his sleeve.

In the North Sea, the days grow shorter and the nights grow colder. The men wear everything they have to keep warm. They wrap themselves in blankets and old sails. They huddle together around Pedro's cooking fire, holding out their hands. Pedro makes hot soup whenever he can, even if it is mostly water with a few beans. Something hot is better than nothing, he says. And the men agree, holding their bowls in their cold hands and breathing in the steam.
