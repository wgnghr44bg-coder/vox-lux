Far beyond Dilmun, much further to the east, there is a land the Sumerians call Meluhha.

Meluhha is far away across the sea. Today we think it was the land along the river Indus, in what is now Pakistan and western India. Great cities stood there at the same time as Sumer. Cities with straight streets and brick houses and covered drains. People there had their own writing. But nobody today can read it.

--

Ships from Meluhha sometimes come all the way to the harbours of Sumer. They bring wonderful things. Beads of a deep orange-red stone called carnelian. Some are long and thin and have white patterns burned into them. Ivory and fine wood. Gold. And peacocks and monkeys and dogs, strange animals the Sumerians have never seen.

In the royal tombs of Ur, archaeologists found beads that must have come from Meluhha. Long red beads with white lines. The same kind of beads have been found in the cities of the Indus. Proof that the two lands traded with each other, across thousands of kilometres of sea.

{img:carnelian-beads-etched-white-lines-necklace-ur-tomb-close-light}
There were even people from Meluhha living in Sumer. A writer from Ur mentions a village of Meluhha people near the city. Perhaps traders who settled there. One clay seal from Sumer belonged to a man whose job was translator for the language of Meluhha. Imagine that. Four thousand years ago, people were already learning foreign languages to do business.

--

From the east, over the mountains, comes another treasure. A deep blue stone called lapis lazuli. It comes from far away mountains in what is now Afghanistan. It travels for months by donkey and by boat. The Sumerians love it more than any other stone. It is the colour of the night sky with little flecks of gold, like stars. They use it in jewellery and statues and the eyes of gods.

{img:lapis-lazuli-blue-stone-gold-flecks-caravan-mountains-donkeys}
So Ur is connected to the whole known world. Ships and caravans come and go. Goods and ideas and people travel with them. A woman in Ur might wear a necklace with stone from Afghanistan and beads from India and gold from the mountains. She has never seen any of those places. But they have come to her.

--

And one evening, from the roof, the mother of our family sees a ship coming up the river toward the harbour. It is low in the water, heavy with cargo. She stares at it. Is it him? She calls the children. They run to the edge of the roof and look. The ship comes closer. And the daughter shouts. That is father's ship! I can see the red cloth on the mast!

The traders of Meluhha bring wonderful things to the markets of Sumer. There are beads of red carnelian, polished until they shine like little drops of fire. There are pieces of ivory and fine wood. There are little stone seals, carved with pictures of animals. Sometimes, people say, they even bring monkeys and colourful birds. The children of Ur crowd around to look. They have never seen such things before. And they wonder about the faraway land where they come from.

Meluhha was probably the land we now call the Indus Valley, far to the east. Its people built great cities with straight streets and covered drains. They had their own writing, which nobody can read today. Their ships sailed across the sea to trade with Sumer and Dilmun. So, more than four thousand years ago, people from faraway lands were already meeting and trading. They shared goods and ideas, and perhaps a few stories too.
