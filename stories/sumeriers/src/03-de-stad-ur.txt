Ur is a big city for its time. Perhaps thirty or forty thousand people live here. Maybe more.

It is shaped like an oval. A thick wall of mud brick runs all around it. On the west side runs the river Euphrates. On the north side there is a harbour, where boats come in from the river and the sea. A second harbour lies on the west. Canals lead in and out. Ur is a city of water as well as of land.

--

Inside the walls, the city is crowded. Narrow winding streets run between the houses. They are made of beaten earth. They are dusty in summer and muddy when it rains. Rubbish is thrown into the street. Over the years, the street level rises. Some houses have steps going down into them from the street.

In the north of the city, behind a second wall, is the holy area. That is where the great tower stands. Around it are temples and store rooms and the houses of priests. And the palace of the king.

{img:narrow-winding-street-ur-mudbrick-walls-doorways-people-donkey}
The rest of the city is for ordinary people. Houses packed close together. Little shops and workshops. Small shrines at the street corners. Bakers and potters and weavers. Coppersmiths hammering. The smell of bread and dung and spices. Children playing. Dogs sleeping in the shade. And everywhere, donkeys with loads on their backs.

--

Everything is built of mud brick. There is hardly any stone in Sumer. And almost no good timber. But there is mud. Endless mud from the rivers. People mix it with straw and water. They press it into wooden moulds. They let the bricks dry in the sun. And with these bricks, they build everything. Houses and walls and temples and towers.

{img:brickmakers-ur-moulds-straw-mud-rows-drying-sun-river}
Sun-dried brick does not last forever. Rain slowly washes it away. So walls must be repaired again and again. Over hundreds of years, old houses fall down. New houses are built on top of them. The city slowly rises up on its own ruins. That is why the old cities of Sumer are now big mounds of earth. Layer upon layer upon layer. Each layer is a page of history.

--

At night, the gates of Ur are closed. Guards stand on the walls with torches. The streets grow dark and quiet. Only the dogs bark now and then. And from the holy area, the soft sound of the priests' evening hymns drifts over the rooftops. The city sleeps inside its walls, safe until morning.

In the busy streets of Ur, there are little shops and workshops everywhere. A potter sits at his wheel in an open doorway, shaping a jar. A baker sells round flat loaves, still warm from the oven. A barber shaves a man's head on a stool in the street. Donkeys carry heavy loads, and their bells jingle as they walk. Children run between the people, and dogs sleep in the shade. And everyone seems to be talking at once.
