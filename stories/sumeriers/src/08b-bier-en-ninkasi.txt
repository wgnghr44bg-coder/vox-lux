The Sumerians love beer. They have dozens of kinds. Light beer and dark beer. Sweet beer and strong beer. Beer with dates and beer with honey.

Beer is drunk every day by everyone. Workers get beer as part of their pay. Temples give beer to the gods. Kings drink beer at feasts. It is not very strong. It is thick and cloudy and filling. More like a food than a drink.

--

The beer is so thick that bits of grain float on top. So people drink it through long straws. Old pictures show people sitting around a big jar together. Each one has a long reed straw. They all sip from the same jar and talk. Rich people have straws with metal tips that act like filters. One beautiful straw of gold and blue stone was found in a royal tomb at Ur.

There is even a goddess of beer. Her name is Ninkasi. And there is a song for her. It is a hymn of praise. But it is also a recipe. It tells how to make beer, step by step. Soaking the barley. Baking the beer bread. Mixing it with water and sweet dates. Pouring it into the big jar. Letting it bubble.

{img:women-brewers-ninkasi-hymn-big-jars-mixing-bread-dates-courtyard}
The song says, you are the one who pours out the filtered beer. It is like the flow of the two great rivers. People sang it while they brewed. That way they remembered every step. It is one of the oldest recipes in the world. In our own time, brewers have tried to make beer using the song. It turned out quite nice. A little like cider.

--

Brewing is often women's work. Many brewers and tavern keepers in Sumer are women. They make the beer and sell it from their houses. People gather there in the evening. They drink and talk and laugh. The tavern is a place to hear the news. And to meet friends.

{img:tavern-keeper-woman-evening-lamps-customers-straws-ur-house}
There is an old Sumerian proverb. It says, when you do not drink beer, you do not know what is good. Another one is less happy. It says, beer is good, but the road home is long. The Sumerians knew both sides.

--

In our family, the mother brews beer once a week. The kitchen smells of baked bread and sweet dates for days. When the beer is ready, the grandmother tastes it first. She sips through her straw and thinks for a moment. Good, she says. A little sweeter than last week. Your husband will like it. And the mother smiles. And she hopes he comes home soon to taste it.

The mother has her own way of making beer, which she learned from her own mother. She keeps it secret, even from the neighbours. She bakes special loaves of bread and crumbles them into a large jar. She adds water and a little date syrup for sweetness. Then she covers the jar and waits for a few days. The daughter is allowed to help stir it. Her mother says that one day she will teach her the secret too.
