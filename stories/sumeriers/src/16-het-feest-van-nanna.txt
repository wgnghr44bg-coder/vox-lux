And now the day of the great festival has come. The moon god Nanna will travel to Nippur.

Nippur is the holy city of Sumer. It is where Enlil lives, the father of the gods. Every year, the gods of the other cities travel to Nippur to visit him. They bring gifts. They ask for his blessing. And Nanna of Ur makes the journey too. By boat.

--

For weeks, Ur has been getting ready. The god's boat has been repaired and painted. It is decorated with gold and silver and blue stone. Its prow is high and curved. Gifts are loaded on board. Fine cloth and butter and cheese and fish. Lambs and goats. Jars of oil and beer. Baskets of dates. All the best things of Ur, to give to the father of the gods.

On the day of the journey, the whole city comes out. The priests carry the statue of Nanna out of the temple. He is dressed in his finest robe. Music plays. Drums beat. People throw flowers. The statue is placed carefully in the boat. Will it go well? Every year, everyone worries a little. If the god's journey is troubled, it could be a bad sign for the year.

{img:priests-carrying-dressed-statue-to-boat-music-drums-flowers}
Then the boat pushes off. Rowers pull at the oars. The boat moves slowly up the canal, out of the city. People follow along the banks, singing and waving. Children run beside it. For a long way, the crowds walk with their god. Then they stop and watch the boat grow smaller and smaller. Until it disappears among the palm trees.

--

The journey takes several days. A Sumerian poem tells about it. At each city along the way, the boat stops. The people of that city come out to greet the moon god. They offer gifts. And the god blesses them. Then he sails on.

At last he reaches Nippur. The father of the gods welcomes him. There is a great feast. Nanna gives his gifts. And Enlil gives him something in return. A blessing for Ur. Good harvests and fat sheep and full rivers. Long life for the king.

{img:moon-god-boat-returning-ur-crowds-joy-evening-lanterns-banks}
Then the boat comes home. And the people of Ur are waiting. They see it coming up the canal in the evening. They cheer and run to meet it. The god is back. He has brought the blessing. The year will be good. And that night there is feasting in every street of Ur, under the shining moon.

During the festival, the streets of Ur are full of people in their best clothes. Women wear necklaces of coloured beads and long woollen dresses. Men wear fringed skirts and carry walking sticks. Children have flowers tucked behind their ears. There is music on every corner, and the smell of roasting meat everywhere. Our family walks together through the crowds, holding hands so nobody gets lost. And the little one sits on his father's shoulders, to see over everyone's heads.

When the festival is over, the family walks home slowly through the quiet streets. The lamps are going out one by one. The little one has fallen asleep on his father's shoulder. The daughter yawns and leans against her mother. The grandmother walks slowly, holding the eldest son's arm. At home, they climb up to the roof and lie down on their mats. And above them, the moon of Nanna shines down on the sleeping city.
