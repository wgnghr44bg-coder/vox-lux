This is the story of King Shulgi's great run.

It was a festival day. Shulgi was in the holy city of Nippur, in the north of Sumer. That is where the father of the gods has his temple. And on the same day, there was also a festival in Ur, for the moon god. Shulgi wanted to be at both. But Ur and Nippur are far apart. About a hundred and sixty kilometres. A whole week's walk for most people.

--

So Shulgi said, I will run. He rose before dawn in Nippur. He made his offerings in the temple there. Then he tied his belt tight and set out along the road to Ur. Alone, or with a few guards. Down the long straight road beside the canal.

He ran past fields and villages. Past palm groves and reed beds. The sun came up and climbed higher. It grew hot. He ran on. At each rest station, he stopped for a drink of water. Then he ran on again. Farmers in the fields looked up as he passed. Was that the king? Running? They stared after him.

{img:farmers-in-field-looking-up-runner-passing-dust-road-midday}
In the afternoon, clouds gathered. Rain began to fall. Then hail. Heavy hard balls of ice pounding down on the road. The wind howled. It was hard to see. Most people would have stopped. Taken shelter. Waited. But the song says Shulgi did not stop. He ran through the storm like a lion. Like a donkey of the mountains. Like a wild horse.

--

And that evening, as the sun went down, he reached Ur. He ran up to the temple of the moon god. And he made his offerings there too. Both festivals, on one day. The people of Ur cheered him. Then he turned around. And the song says he ran all the way back to Nippur that same night.

Did it really happen? Maybe. Maybe not all of it. Running from Nippur to Ur in one day would be an amazing feat. Running there and back would be almost impossible. But the song was sung for generations. Children learned it at school. And every king after Shulgi wanted to be as strong and fast as he was.

{img:king-arriving-ur-temple-evening-torches-crowd-cheering-tired}
Today, some long distance runners have tried to run the same distance in a day. It can be done. It takes great strength and training. Shulgi was probably a good runner. And a very good storyteller about his own running.
