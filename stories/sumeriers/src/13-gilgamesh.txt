The Sumerians told many stories. The most famous of all is about a king called Gilgamesh.

Gilgamesh was the king of Uruk, the great city not far from Ur. He may have been a real king who lived long, long ago. But over the centuries, the stories about him grew and grew. Until he became the greatest hero of ancient times.

--

In the stories, Gilgamesh is strong and proud. Too proud. He is part god and part man. He builds the great walls of Uruk. But he is also a bully. He makes his people work too hard. So the people pray to the gods for help. And the gods make a wild man to be his equal. His name is Enkidu.

Enkidu lives in the wilderness with the animals. He runs with the gazelles and drinks at the water holes. He is as strong as Gilgamesh. When the two of them finally meet, they fight. They wrestle in the street of Uruk. The walls shake. Doors break. Neither one can win. And at last, both stop. And they look at each other. And they laugh. And from that day, they are best friends.

{img:gilgamesh-enkidu-wrestling-then-embracing-uruk-street-friends}
Together they go on great adventures. They travel to a faraway forest of huge cedar trees, guarded by a giant. They face him together and win. They return home as heroes.

--

But then sorrow comes. Enkidu falls ill. Gilgamesh sits beside him day and night. But he cannot help. Enkidu passes away. And Gilgamesh is heartbroken. He weeps for his friend for seven days. And for the first time, he is afraid. Afraid that one day, he too will die.

So he sets out on a long journey. He wants to find the secret of living forever. He crosses mountains and deserts. He walks through a long dark tunnel under a mountain. He crosses the waters of death. He is looking for one man. A man who, the stories say, was given everlasting life by the gods. A man who survived the great flood.

{img:gilgamesh-alone-crossing-dark-waters-boat-poles-mist-journey}
What he learns from that man is another story. The story of the flood.

--

The story of Gilgamesh was told and retold for two thousand years. In Sumerian and later in other languages. It was written on clay tablets and copied in schools. Then it was forgotten. Until about a hundred and fifty years ago, when people found the tablets again and learned to read them. Today it is called the oldest great story in the world. And it is still read all over the world. Because its questions are still our questions. Friendship. Loss. And how to live, knowing that life does not last forever.

At the end of his long journey, Gilgamesh comes home to Uruk. He has not found a way to live forever. But he looks at his city. At its walls and its gardens and its people. And he understands. This is what lasts. What we build. And the people we love.

The story of Gilgamesh was told and retold for many centuries. In the evenings, storytellers would sit by the fire and tell it to a circle of listeners. They told of his friendship with Enkidu, and of their long journeys together. They told of his search for the secret of living forever. The listeners would lean forward and hold their breath at the exciting parts. And at the end, they would nod quietly, thinking about the lesson of the story.
