Sumer is the southern part of a land called Mesopotamia. That name means the land between the rivers. The two rivers are the Tigris and the Euphrates. Today most of this land is in Iraq.

It is a flat, hot, dry land. It hardly ever rains. In summer, it is so hot that the air shimmers. And yet people have lived here for thousands of years. Because of the rivers.

--

The rivers bring water and rich mud from the mountains far to the north. Long ago, people learned to dig canals. They led the river water out to the fields. With water, the dry land became green and rich. Barley grew tall. Date palms grew thick. Sheep and goats grew fat. There was more food than people needed. And that changed everything.

When there is extra food, not everyone has to farm. Some people can become potters or weavers or builders. Some can become priests or writers or traders. Villages grow into towns. Towns grow into cities. And that is what happened here, in Sumer, more than five thousand years ago.

{img:sumerian-city-states-map-like-ur-uruk-lagash-nippur-rivers}
Sumer is not one country with one king, at least not always. It is a land of many cities. Ur and Uruk. Lagash and Nippur. Eridu and Kish. Each city has its own god and its own temple. Each has its own ruler. They trade with each other. They marry into each other's families. And sometimes they argue over water and land.

--

The people call themselves the black-headed people. Nobody knows exactly why. Maybe because of their black hair. Their language is unlike any other language we know. It is not related to any language spoken today. It is a puzzle.

{img:sumerian-man-woman-woollen-fringed-robes-shaved-head-long-hair}
The men often shave their heads and wear long beards. Or they shave everything. The women wear their hair long and pinned up. Both wear long wrap-around robes of wool. Some robes have rows of fringes, like the fur of a sheep. Rich people wear jewellery of gold and blue stone. And in summer, everyone looks for shade.
