Tonight we go more than four thousand years back. We go to the land of Sumer, in the south of what is now Iraq. The year is around 2100 before Christ. And we go to one of the oldest cities in the world. Its name is Ur.

--

It is early morning. The sun rises over a flat land of rivers and palm trees and reeds. A great river flows slowly past the city. Boats made of reeds glide on the water. Herons stand in the shallows. And above the city, a huge stepped tower rises into the pale sky. On its top stands a small temple. The first light of the sun touches it and turns it gold.

The people of Ur are waking up. Women light their ovens. Men lead donkeys to the fields. Boys run down to the river to fish. Priests climb the tower for the morning prayers. It is a day like any other. And yet these people are doing something new in the history of the world. They are living in cities.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

{img:sumerian-people-market-ur-wool-skirts-baskets-donkeys-morning}
The Sumerians are often called the first civilization. That is a big word. It means people living together in cities. With laws and writing and kings. With temples and schools and trade. The Sumerians were among the very first people on earth to do all this.

They wrote the first writing. They built some of the first cities. They made some of the first wheels and ploughs. They wrote the first stories and the first laws. They counted time in sixties, and we still do. So much of what we take for granted began here, in this flat hot land between two rivers.

{img:sumerian-scribe-reed-stylus-clay-tablet-courtyard-shade-ur}
And yet for thousands of years, the Sumerians were completely forgotten. Their cities turned to mounds of earth. Their language was no longer spoken. Nobody even remembered their name. Only about a hundred and fifty years ago did people start to find them again.

--

Tonight we will walk through Ur in the time of its greatest king. We will climb toward the great tower. We will visit a family in their house. We will sit in a school where boys learn to write on clay. We will sail to faraway lands with the traders. We will hear the oldest story in the world. And we will learn how a whole lost world was dug back out of the earth.

{img:great-ziggurat-ur-reconstructed-stairs-temple-top-golden-light}
So let us walk along the river bank toward the walls of Ur. The air is warm and soft. The palm leaves rustle. The smell of baking bread drifts out from the city. And the great tower stands waiting in the morning sun.

Down by the canal, the first boats are already moving. They are long and narrow, made of reeds tied tightly together and coated with black tar. A man pushes his boat along with a long pole, loaded with baskets of fish. On the bank, women are filling clay jars with water and chatting. A boy drives a small flock of goats through the city gate. And from somewhere inside the walls comes the smell of fresh bread, baking in a clay oven.
