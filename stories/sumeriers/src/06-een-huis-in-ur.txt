Let us visit a family in their house in Ur.

The house is on a narrow street in the middle of the city. From outside, you see only a plain wall of mud brick with a small wooden door. There are no windows on the street. That keeps out the heat and the dust and the noise. And it keeps the family private.

--

Inside the door there is a small room for washing your feet. Then you step into the courtyard. It is open to the sky, paved with baked bricks. In the middle is a drain, so the rain can run away. Around the courtyard are the rooms. A kitchen with a clay oven. A store room for jars of barley and oil and dates. A room where guests are welcomed. And at the back, a small family chapel.

Some houses in Ur have two floors. A wooden gallery runs around the courtyard on the upper level. Stairs of brick lead up to it. The bedrooms are up there. And above them is the flat roof. On hot summer nights, the whole family sleeps on the roof.

{img:family-chapel-small-altar-clay-figures-lamp-ancestors-below-floor}
The family chapel is a special room. There is a little altar and a few clay figures of the family's gods. And under the floor of the chapel, the family buries its dead. Grandparents and great-grandparents lie there, in brick tombs. The family believes they are still part of the household. Every month they pour water and leave a little food for them.

--

This family is a family of traders. The father buys and sells copper and cloth. He travels sometimes by boat down to the sea. He keeps his records on clay tablets in a box in his room. Thousands of letters and contracts have been found in houses like this one in Ur.

{img:trader-father-reading-clay-tablets-box-letters-courtyard-room}
The mother runs the house. She has a few servants to help her. She manages the store room and the kitchen and the money while her husband is away. Their house is busy. Two sons, one daughter, a grandmother. A servant girl. A cat that catches mice in the store room. And a little dog that barks at every visitor.

--

Right now, the father is far away. He left two months ago on a boat for a land across the sea. He should be back soon. The mother checks the harbour every few days. The children ask every morning, is father home today? Not yet, she says. But soon.

In the evening, the mother walks slowly through the house and checks that everything is in order. She closes the store room and makes sure the jars are covered. She lights a small lamp in the family chapel. She looks in on the children, already half asleep. Then she climbs the stairs to the roof and looks out over the city. Somewhere far away, across the sea, her husband is sleeping on a ship. She wishes him a safe journey home. Then she lies down and closes her eyes.
