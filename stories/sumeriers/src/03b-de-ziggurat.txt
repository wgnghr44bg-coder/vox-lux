Above everything in Ur rises the great tower. It is called a ziggurat.

A ziggurat is a stepped tower built of brick. It has several levels, one on top of another. Each level is a little smaller than the one below. At the very top stands a small temple. It looks like a mountain made by people. And that is just what it is meant to be. A holy mountain in the flat land.

--

The great ziggurat of Ur was built by King Ur-Nammu, the father of the present king. It is huge. Its base is about sixty metres long and forty-five metres wide. Inside, it is solid mud brick. Outside, it is covered with a thick skin of baked bricks, set in black tar to keep out the water. Millions of bricks went into it.

Three great stairways lead up the front. One in the middle and one on each side. They meet at a gate high up on the first level. From there, more stairs lead up to the second level and the third. And at the very top is the temple of the moon god.

{img:priests-climbing-ziggurat-stairs-procession-offerings-morning}
The builders were clever. They knew a huge brick tower could collapse if water got inside. So they left little holes in the walls. They placed layers of reed mats between the bricks. Rain water could drain out. The tower could breathe. And it stood strong.

--

They had another trick too. The walls of the ziggurat are not perfectly straight. They lean in very slightly, and they curve a tiny bit outward in the middle. You cannot see it if you do not know. But it makes the tower look stronger and taller. The Greeks used the same trick much later in their temples. The Sumerians thought of it first.

{img:ziggurat-terraces-planted-trees-gardens-green-on-brick-steps}
Some people think there were trees on the terraces. Green trees on a brown tower, like a holy hill. Holes for soil have been found on the levels. Imagine it. A green mountain rising out of the city, with a temple shining on the very top.

--

Only priests may climb all the way to the top. Ordinary people watch from below. On feast days, they see processions going up the great stairs. They hear music and singing. They smell incense drifting down. And they look up at the temple at the top, where their god lives, close to the sky.

{img:ziggurat-at-night-moon-rising-behind-temple-top-city-dark-below}
At night, the moon rises behind the ziggurat. It hangs above the temple on the top. And for the people of Ur, that is the most beautiful sight of all. Because the moon is their god.

Early in the morning, before the city wakes, the priests climb the great stairway of the ziggurat. They carry jars of water, baskets of bread and bowls of incense. At the top, they wash the shrine and sweep the floor. They light the lamps and say the morning prayers. From up there, they can see the whole city spread out below them. They can see the river and the fields and the marshes. And they watch the sun come up over the land.
