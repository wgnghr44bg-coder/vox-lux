At the end of his journey, Gilgamesh finds the man he is looking for. His name is Utnapishtim. And he tells Gilgamesh a story. The story of the great flood.

Long ago, Utnapishtim says, the gods decided to send a flood over the whole earth. People had become too many and too noisy. The gods could not sleep. So they would wash the world clean with water.

--

But one god loved people. He was Ea, the god of the sweet waters and of wisdom. He did not want everyone to be lost. But he had promised to keep the plan secret. So he found a clever way. He went to the reed house where Utnapishtim lived. And he did not speak to Utnapishtim. He spoke to the wall of the house. Reed wall, listen. Build a boat. Leave your things behind. Save life.

Utnapishtim heard it through the wall. And he understood. He built a great boat. Huge and square, with many decks and rooms. He covered it with tar to keep the water out. He loaded it with food and gold and silver. He brought his family on board. And the craftsmen who had helped him. And animals of every kind, wild and tame.

{img:huge-boat-being-built-tar-workers-animals-waiting-reed-houses}
Then the storm came. Black clouds rose from the horizon. Thunder roared. The rain fell and fell. For six days and seven nights the wind blew and the water rose. It covered the fields and the cities and the hills. Even the gods were frightened. They crouched like dogs against the walls of heaven.

--

On the seventh day, the storm grew calm. The sea was still. Utnapishtim opened a window. Light fell on his face. Everywhere there was water. And he sat down and wept.

The boat came to rest on top of a mountain. Utnapishtim waited seven more days. Then he sent out a dove. The dove flew around and came back. There was nowhere to land. He sent out a swallow. It came back too. Then he sent out a raven. The raven did not come back. It had found dry land. So Utnapishtim opened the boat. And everyone came out into the new world.

{img:dove-flying-from-boat-mountain-top-calm-sea-sunrise-hope}
Does this story sound familiar? It is very like the story of Noah in the Bible. A great flood. A man who is warned. A boat full of animals. Birds sent out to find land. When people first read the Sumerian story, about a hundred and fifty years ago, they were amazed. The story was much older than anyone had thought.

--

Why did the people of Sumer tell stories about a great flood? Maybe because floods were real here. The rivers flood every spring. Sometimes they flood very badly. Whole towns are lost. Archaeologists digging at Ur found a thick layer of clean mud in the ground. No pots, no bricks, nothing. Just river mud, laid down by a great flood long ago. People who lived here knew water could be wonderful. And terrible.

{img:archaeologists-pit-ur-thick-clean-mud-layer-flood-1929-ladders}
So they told the story. And at the end, the gods were sorry for what they had done. They promised it would not happen again. And Utnapishtim and his wife were given life without end, far away at the mouth of the rivers.
