Every city in Sumer has its own god. The god of Ur is Nanna, the god of the moon.

The Sumerians believe the moon is a god who sails across the night sky in a boat. Some nights his boat is thin like a sliver. Some nights it is round and full. He watches over the cattle and the herds. He keeps the calendar, because the months follow the moon. And above all, he watches over Ur.

--

Nanna is a gentle god in many stories. He is wise and calm and fair. His wife is the goddess Ningal. Her name means great lady. Their children are the sun god and the goddess of the morning star. So the moon is the father of the sun, in Sumerian belief. The night came first. And the day was born from it.

In the temple at the top of the ziggurat, and in the big temple at its foot, Nanna lives. Not just in the sky. The Sumerians believe the god really lives in his temple, in the form of a statue. The statue is dressed in fine clothes. It is given food every day. Bread and beer and meat and fruit. It is washed and cared for, like a great lord.

{img:temple-of-nanna-courtyard-priests-offering-food-statue-incense}
Hundreds of people work for the temple. Priests and singers. Cooks and bakers and brewers. Weavers and herdsmen. The temple owns fields and flocks and fishing boats. It is not only a place of prayer. It is one of the biggest businesses in the city.

--

The people of Ur love their god. They bring him gifts. They pray to him when they are sick or sad. They thank him when things go well. And every month, when the new moon appears as a thin silver line in the evening sky, there is a little festival. The god has come back. Everyone goes out to look.

The darkest nights are a little worrying. When the moon disappears completely, for a night or two, people wonder. Where has the god gone? Will he come back? The priests say prayers and make offerings. And then, there it is again. A thin silver curve low in the west, just after sunset. People point and smile. Nanna has returned.

{img:family-on-roof-pointing-at-new-moon-evening-ur-crescent}
In a few weeks, the city will hold its greatest festival for the moon. The statue of Nanna will be carried out of his temple. He will travel by boat to visit the father of the gods, in another city. And all of Ur is getting ready. Because if the journey goes well, the whole year will be blessed.
