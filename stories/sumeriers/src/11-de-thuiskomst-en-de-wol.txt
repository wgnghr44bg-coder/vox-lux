The whole family runs down to the harbour. The grandmother cannot run, so she walks as fast as she can. The ship is tying up at the quay. Men are shouting and throwing ropes. And there, at the front of the ship, is the father. He is thinner and his face is brown from the sun. But he is smiling.

He steps off the ship. The children throw their arms around him. The mother holds his hands and does not say anything for a long time. The grandmother arrives last, out of breath. She touches his face. You are late, she says. I know, he says. There was a storm. We waited on Dilmun for two weeks. But I am home.

--

He has brought copper, good copper this time. And a necklace of red beads for his wife. A little ivory comb for his daughter. Dates from Dilmun, the sweetest in the world, for the grandmother. And for the boys, a clay whistle shaped like a bird. That evening, the family eats on the roof. The father tells them about the sea and the island and the springs. And the mother brings out her new beer. He sips it through his straw. It is good, he says. The best I have had in months.

{img:temple-weaving-workshop-ur-many-women-looms-wool-bundles-overseer}
Some of the wool the father traded came from the great temple workshops of Ur. Let us visit one.

--

The temples and the palace of Ur own huge flocks of sheep. Tens of thousands of them. Their wool is brought to big workshops in the city. And there, hundreds of women and girls work at spinning and weaving. Some workshops have more than a thousand weavers.

The work is carefully organised. Some women wash the wool. Some comb it. Some spin it into thread. Some weave it into cloth on big looms. Overseers check the work. Scribes write down how much wool comes in and how much cloth goes out. Every worker gets a ration of barley and oil and wool every month.

{img:spinners-weavers-ur-workshop-children-helping-wool-baskets-light}
The cloth is of many kinds. Fine soft cloth for the gods and the king. Heavy rough cloth for workers. Cloth in natural colours, white and brown and grey. And cloth dyed in bright colours. Red and blue and yellow. It is sold all over Sumer. And it is carried by ships to far lands, to be traded for copper and stone.

--

The weavers' lives are not easy. The days are long. The pay is small. Some of the workers are widows or orphans with nowhere else to go. Some are women taken from other lands. But the workshops give them food and a place. Mothers bring their small children with them. The children play between the looms. And when they are old enough, they learn to spin too.
