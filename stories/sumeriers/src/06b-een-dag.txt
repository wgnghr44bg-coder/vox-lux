Let us spend a day with this family.

The day starts early. Before the sun is up, the servant girl lights the clay oven in the courtyard. The mother grinds barley on a flat stone, rolling a smaller stone back and forth. Soon there is flour. She mixes it with water and salt. She slaps flat round pieces of dough onto the hot inside wall of the oven. They bake in a few minutes. She peels them off. Warm bread for breakfast.

--

They eat in the courtyard as the sky turns pink. Bread and dates. A little cheese made from goat's milk. And beer. Thick, cloudy, a little sweet. Everyone drinks beer in Sumer. Even children drink a weak kind. It is safer than the river water.

Then the day begins. The eldest son, who is twelve, goes off to school. He carries a little basket with his clay tablet and his reed pen. He is not happy about it. School is hard. The younger son, who is seven, goes with the grandmother to the market. The daughter, who is ten, stays home and helps her mother spin wool.

{img:girl-spinning-wool-spindle-courtyard-mother-weaving-loom-shade}
Wool is everywhere in Sumer. There are huge flocks of sheep. Their wool is spun into thread and woven into cloth. The cloth is sold far and wide. Every woman in Ur knows how to spin. The daughter holds a stick wrapped with wool in one hand. She twists the thread with the other, onto a spinning stick that hangs and turns. Round and round. Her thread is still a little lumpy. But it is getting better.

--

At the market, the grandmother buys onions and garlic and fish. She bargains hard. The seller sighs and gives in. The little boy watches a man with a monkey that does tricks. He laughs so hard he hiccups. On the way home, the grandmother buys him a little cake with honey and sesame.

{img:market-ur-grandmother-boy-fish-seller-onions-baskets-monkey-trick}
At midday, it is too hot to do anything. Everyone rests in the cool dark rooms. The streets go quiet. Only the flies are busy.

--

In the late afternoon, the eldest son comes home from school. His hand hurts. Did you get in trouble, his mother asks. A little, he says. I talked when I should have been writing. She shakes her head and gives him a piece of bread.

{img:family-supper-roof-evening-ur-ziggurat-silhouette-sunset-lamps}
In the evening, the family eats on the roof. Lentil stew with onions. Bread. Fish. Cucumbers. The sun goes down behind the ziggurat. The sky turns red and gold. Swallows dart overhead. The grandmother tells a story about the moon god. And as the stars come out, the mother looks toward the harbour. Still no ship. Maybe tomorrow.
