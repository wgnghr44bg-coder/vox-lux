Let us listen to the rain over Tikal.

In May, or June, after the long, hot, dry months, when the sky has been pale, and the air hazy with the smoke of the burning fields, and the earth dry, and dusty, and the reservoirs low, the clouds begin to gather, in the afternoons, great, grey, towering clouds, over the forest.

--

The air becomes heavy, and still, and hot. The birds fall silent. And then, there is a rumble of thunder, far away, and the wind rises, and shakes the trees, and the first drops fall, big, and heavy, and warm, on the dry leaves, and the dusty plazas, and the thatched roofs.

And then the rain comes, all at once, pouring down, roaring, on the forest, and the roofs, and the temples, so loud that people have to shout to be heard, and the plazas are covered with water, and the channels run full, and the water rushes into the reservoirs.

{img:children-standing-doorway-watching-rain-pouring-courtyard}
The children run out into it, laughing, and stand with their faces turned up to the sky. The farmers stand in their doorways, watching, and give thanks, to Chaak, the rain god, because now, they can plant their corn.

--

Every afternoon, for months, the rain comes like this, and then stops, and the sun comes out again, and the forest steams, and drips, and glitters, and smells of wet earth, and leaves, and flowers.

The forest becomes greener, and greener. The frogs come out, in their thousands, and sing, all night long, in the reservoirs, and the swamps, and the puddles, a great chorus, croaking, and peeping, and trilling. The bajos, the low, swampy areas, around the city, fill with water, and become shallow lakes, full of birds, herons, and egrets, and ducks.

--

In the houses, it is cool, and dim, and the rain drums softly on the thatch, and drips from the edges of the roof, and runs away, into the gardens. The women weave, in the shade of the porches, and the old people sleep, in their hammocks, and the children play, inside, and the dogs curl up, by the fire.

{img:mist-rising-forest-after-rain-temple-roof-comb-sunbeams}
The young corn grows, quickly, in the fields, green, and tall, and the beans climb the stalks, and the squash spreads out its great leaves. The farmers go out, between the showers, to weed, and to keep away the birds, and the animals.

--

And at night, the rain falls, softly, on the roofs of Tikal, on the thatch of the houses, and the stone of the temples, and the leaves of the great trees, and on the still water of the reservoirs, making little rings, that spread out, and fade.

And the people of Tikal fall asleep, to the sound of it, as people everywhere have always done, safe, and dry, and warm, under their roofs, while the rain falls, and falls, all through the night, on the great, dark, green forest.
