Let us listen to the rain over Tikal.

It is May or June. The long hot dry months are almost over. For weeks the sky has been pale. The air is hazy with smoke from the burning fields. The earth is dry and dusty, and the ponds are low. Everyone is waiting. Then in the afternoons, clouds begin to gather. Great grey towering clouds rise over the forest.

--

The air becomes heavy and still and hot. The birds go quiet. Then there is a rumble of thunder far away. The wind rises and shakes the trees. The first drops fall, big and heavy and warm. They land on the dry leaves and the dusty squares and the roofs.

And then the rain comes all at once. It pours down and roars on the forest and the roofs and the temples. It is so loud that people must shout to be heard. The squares fill with water. The channels run full. And the water rushes into the ponds.

{img:children-standing-doorway-watching-rain-pouring-courtyard}
The children run out into it, laughing. They turn their faces up to the sky. The farmers stand in their doorways and watch. They thank the rain god. Now they can plant their corn.

--

Every afternoon for months, the rain comes like this. Then it stops, and the sun comes out again. The forest steams and drips and glitters. It smells of wet earth and leaves and flowers.

The forest grows greener and greener. Thousands of frogs come out. They sing all night long in the ponds and the puddles. They croak and peep and trill. The low wet lands around the city fill with water. They become shallow lakes full of herons and ducks.

--

In the houses it is cool and dim. The rain drums softly on the straw roof and drips from the edges. The women weave in the shade. The old people sleep in their hammocks. The children play inside. And the dogs curl up by the fire.

{img:mist-rising-forest-after-rain-temple-roof-comb-sunbeams}
In the fields, the young corn grows fast, green and tall. The beans climb the stalks. The squash spreads out its big leaves. Between the showers, the farmers go out to pull weeds and chase away the birds.

--

And at night the rain falls softly on the roofs of Tikal. It falls on the straw of the houses and the stone of the temples. It falls on the leaves of the great trees. And it falls on the still water of the ponds, making little rings that spread out and fade.

And the people of Tikal fall asleep to the sound of it, as people everywhere always have. They are safe and dry and warm under their roofs. And the rain falls and falls, all through the night, on the great dark green forest.
