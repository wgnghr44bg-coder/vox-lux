Tikal is not the only great city in the Maya world.

In the year 750 there are dozens of Maya cities all over the forest. Each one has its own king and its own temples and palaces. Each has its own gods and its own history. They speak more or less the same language. They use the same writing and count the same calendar. But they are not one country. Each city is its own small kingdom.

--

Only about twenty kilometres north of Tikal lies an old city called Uaxactun. Long ago it was Tikal's rival, until Tikal took it over. Further away there are other cities. Some are friends of Tikal, some are rivals, and some are ruled by Tikal.

And far to the north lies Calakmul, the Snake Kingdom. It is Tikal's great rival.

{img:calakmul-great-pyramid-rising-from-forest-north-distance}
For two hundred years, Tikal and Calakmul were the two great powers of the Maya world. Each had its own friends and its own smaller cities under it. They fought over power and trade and glory.

--

Calakmul beat Tikal long ago. For more than a hundred years, Tikal stayed down. Then Tikal beat Calakmul and rose again. Now, in 750, Calakmul is weaker and Tikal is strong. But nobody in Tikal forgets that the wheel can turn again.

Far to the south lies Copán, in a green valley by a river. It is famous for its carvings. It has a great stairway with more than two thousand signs carved on its steps. They tell the story of its kings. The stones say Copán was founded by a man who may have come from Tikal.

{img:palenque-palace-tower-mist-green-hills-temples}
And far to the west lies Palenque, in green hills above the plains. It has beautiful temples and a palace with a tall tower. Its greatest king ruled for sixty-eight years. He was buried in a great stone coffin, deep inside his temple.

--

The kings of these cities visit each other. They send gifts and marry each other's daughters. They make friends. And sometimes they quarrel and go to war.

Maya wars are mostly small. Warriors from two cities meet with spears and clubs and shields. They wear thick padded cotton for armour. The goal is not to destroy the other city. The goal is to capture its nobles, or even its king. Then the other city must pay. We will leave the battles there, far off in the past, as the stones do, with only a few short words.

--

And the ordinary people of all these cities go on with their lives. The farmers and weavers and potters and traders. They grow their corn and raise their children in the forest, whatever the kings are doing.
