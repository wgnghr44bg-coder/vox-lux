All around Tikal, there is the forest.

It is a tropical rainforest: tall, dense, green, and full of life. The great trees rise thirty, forty, fifty metres high, with their crowns spreading out, high above, into a great green roof, the canopy, so thick that, on the forest floor, below, it is dim, and green, and cool, even at midday, with only a few shafts of sunlight, falling through, onto the ferns, and the leaves.

--

The greatest tree of the forest is the ceiba: a huge tree, with a tall, straight, smooth grey trunk, with great buttress roots, like walls, around its base, and branches spreading out, at the very top, like an umbrella. For the Maya, the ceiba is the sacred tree, the world tree, which stands at the centre of the world, with its roots in the underworld, and its branches in the heavens. It is still the national tree of Guatemala.

There are mahogany trees, and cedars, and sapodilla trees, whose sap, chicle, is chewing gum, and the ramón, or breadnut tree, whose seeds can be ground, and eaten, in times of hunger, and allspice, and wild avocado, and copal trees, whose resin is burned as incense, in all the temples.

{img:howler-monkeys-tree-canopy-dawn-calling-mist-tikal-forest}
And the forest is full of animals.

--

At dawn, and at evening, the howler monkeys roar, from the treetops: a deep, loud, booming, roaring sound, which can be heard for kilometres, and sounds, to someone who has never heard it before, like a jaguar, or a monster. Spider monkeys swing through the branches, with their long arms, and tails. There are toucans, with their enormous, brightly coloured beaks, and green parrots, flying in pairs, and scarlet macaws, and wild turkeys, the ocellated turkey, with its feathers shimmering, blue and green and bronze, like a peacock.

There are coatis, with their long noses, and striped tails, held up, which wander through the forest, and the ruins, in groups, looking for fruit, and insects. There are deer, and peccaries, wild pigs, and tapirs, and armadillos, and anteaters, and agoutis.

{img:jaguar-resting-forest-branch-spotted-coat-dappled-light-calm}
And there is the jaguar: the great spotted cat of the forest, the most powerful animal of the Americas, silent, and secret, and rarely seen. For the Maya, the jaguar is the king of the forest, and the lord of the night, and of the underworld, and the symbol of the power of the kings, who wear its skin, and sit on thrones covered with it, and take its name.

--

At night, the forest is full of sounds: the frogs, and the insects, the crickets, and the cicadas, singing, and clicking, and buzzing, and the owls calling, and the fireflies flickering, among the trees, and the rain, dripping from the leaves.

For the people of Tikal, the forest is everything: their source of wood, and food, and medicine, and dyes, and fibres, and game, and their home. And it is also a place of mystery, and spirits, and danger, at the edge of the human world.
