Let us stay in Tikal for a while, at night.

The sun has set, behind the Temple of the Masks, and the sky over the forest has turned from gold, to red, to deep blue, and then to black. The heat of the day has gone, and the air is soft, and warm, and damp, and smells of flowers, and smoke, and wet leaves.

--

In the Great Plaza, a few torches are burning, on the stairways of the temples, where the priests keep watch, and the guards stand, at the entrances of the palace. The white plaster of the plaza glows faintly, in the moonlight, and the great temples rise up, dark, against the stars.

In the palace, the courtyards are quiet now. The last visitors have gone. The servants are clearing away the cups, and the plates. Behind the curtains, in the narrow, vaulted rooms, the royal family is going to sleep, on their wide stone benches, on their mats, and cushions.

{img:village-houses-night-glowing-fires-through-doorways-forest-dark}
Out in the countryside, around the city, in thousands of little houses, the fires are burning low, and the families are lying down, in their hammocks, and on their mats, wrapped in their blankets, and the grandmothers are telling the last story of the evening, to the children, half asleep.

--

The dogs are curled up, by the doorways. The turkeys are roosting, in the trees. In the gardens, the night flowers are opening, white, and sweet-smelling, and the moths are coming to them.

And in the forest, all around, the night creatures are awake: the owls, and the bats, flitting over the reservoirs, and the frogs, singing, and the crickets, and the kinkajous, and the night monkeys, moving softly through the branches, and, somewhere, far away, the jaguar, walking silently, along a forest path, under the stars.

{img:fireflies-over-reservoir-night-water-lilies-temple-silhouette}
Over the reservoirs, the fireflies are flickering, little green lights, drifting over the still, black water, among the water lilies, which have closed their flowers, for the night.

--

And on the top of one of the temples, a priest is sitting, alone, watching the sky, waiting for Venus to rise, before the dawn, counting the days, and the stars, while the whole city sleeps, below him, in the great, dark, quiet forest.
