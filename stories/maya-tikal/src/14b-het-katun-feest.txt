In the year 751, a great day is coming in Tikal.

A twenty-year period of the Long Count is coming to an end. For the Maya, it is like the start of a new century for us. At Tikal, the end of every twenty years is celebrated with a great festival. And everyone hopes the new period will be a good one.

--

For months before, the people get ready.

The king's builders make a new pair of pyramids. They have flat tops and stairs on all four sides. They face each other across a square, one in the east and one in the west. On the north side, there is a small walled space with no roof, open to the sky. Inside stands a new carved stone with the king's portrait, and an altar. On the south side stands a long low building with nine doorways. The nine doors stand for the nine levels of the world below.

{img:twin-pyramid-flat-top-four-stairways-plaster-new-white-red}
The stone carvers carve the new stone. It shows the king in his finest clothes. He is scattering little drops from his hand, maybe incense, maybe seeds. The glyphs tell the date and the king's name. They tell who his parents were and what ceremonies he held.

--

On the great day, everyone comes. People come from all over the city and from the villages around. Some come from other cities. They wear their best clothes and bring flowers and gifts.

At dawn, a long procession starts at the Great Plaza. The king and queen walk along the white road, with the nobles and the priests. They wear their richest clothes and tall headdresses of green feathers. There are drums and trumpets and rattles and singing. And clouds of incense rise into the morning air.

--

As the sun rises, the king climbs the eastern pyramid. He makes offerings to the sun and the gods. Then he climbs the western pyramid, where the sun will set. Then he goes into the walled space on the north side. He stands before the new stone and the altar. And he makes the offerings that close the old period. Everyone waits in silence. Has he done it right? Will the gods be pleased?

{img:festival-plaza-dancers-feather-costumes-drums-crowd-afternoon}
Then the music starts again. There is dancing and feasting all day and into the night. Dancers in masks dance in the squares, dressed as gods and animals. People eat and drink and talk. The smell of roast turkey and tamales and chocolate and incense fills the air.

--

And the people of Tikal feel that the world has been made new. The old period has ended safely. A new one has begun. The king has done what he must. Now the sun will keep rising and the rain will keep falling. And the corn will keep growing.

The stone set up for this festival still stands at Tikal today, in its little walled space. Nine of these pairs of pyramids have been found at Tikal. Each one was built for the end of a twenty-year period. They are found almost nowhere else in the Maya world.

--

When the festival is over and the people have gone home, the new pyramids stand in the moonlight. They gleam white and red in the silence. And twenty years later, the king's son will build another pair, for the next festival.
