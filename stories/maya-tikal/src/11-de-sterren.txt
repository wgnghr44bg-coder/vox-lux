At night, over Tikal, the sky is full of stars.

There are no city lights. There are only the little fires in the houses and the torches on the temples. The sky is black and deep and clear. Thousands and thousands of stars shine. And the glowing band of the Milky Way stretches right across the sky.

--

For the Maya, the Milky Way is the tree of the world, rising into the sky. It is also the white road, the road the souls of the dead travel along. In some seasons it stands straight up. In others it lies across the sky like a great canoe.

The Maya star watchers sit on top of the temples every night. They know the paths of the sun and the moon and the stars and the planets very well.

{img:venus-morning-star-bright-dawn-sky-over-forest-temple}
Most of all, they watch Venus.

--

Venus is the brightest of the planets. Sometimes it rises before the sun, as the morning star. Sometimes it shines after sunset, as the evening star. And sometimes it disappears for a while into the light of the sun. Where does it go? The Maya want to know exactly when it will come back.

They counted that Venus goes through its whole journey in five hundred and eighty-four days. One old Maya book has tables of Venus. They are correct to within a few hours over many years. It is one of the most amazing things in the history of star watching.

--

The Maya could also predict when the sun or the moon would go dark in the middle of the day or night. Their tables show the days when this might happen. That way the priests could warn the people and hold the right ceremonies.

They knew the length of the year and the month very exactly. And they watched the longest and the shortest days of the year.

{img:maya-astronomers-observing-sky-temple-platform-night-torches-crossed-sticks}
In a city near Tikal, there is a pyramid with three small temples in a row across from it. Stand on the pyramid. On the longest day of the year, the sun rises behind the northern temple. On the shortest day, it rises behind the southern one. And in spring and autumn, it rises right behind the middle one. There is an even older group like this at Tikal itself.

--

For the Maya, the sky is not empty. It is full of gods and stories. The sun and the moon and Venus are gods and heroes. They move across the sky by day. At night they travel through the world under the earth. And the kings on top of their temples, at just the right moment, take part in the great movements of the heavens.

Tonight the stars over Tikal are the same as they were in the year 750. The Milky Way still rises over the temples. And Venus still shines at dawn over the forest.
