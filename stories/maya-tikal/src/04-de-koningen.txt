Tikal has been ruled by kings for more than five hundred years, by the year 750. The stones of Tikal record the names of more than thirty of them, one after another, from the founder, called Yax Ehb Xook, around the year 90, to the kings of the eighth century.

The kings have wonderful names, which tell of their gods, and the animals, and the sky: Great Jaguar Paw. Stormy Sky. Curl Snout. Double Bird. Animal Skull.

--

The greatest king of Tikal, in the memory of its people in 750, is Jasaw Chan K'awiil, who ruled for more than fifty years, from 682 to about 734.

When he came to the throne, Tikal had been through terrible times. For more than a hundred years, it had been defeated, and dominated, by its great rival, Calakmul, the Snake Kingdom, in the north. Its old monuments had been broken, and its kings humbled.

{img:king-dancing-ceremony-plaza-feathers-incense-nobles-musicians}
But in the year 695, Jasaw Chan K'awiil defeated Calakmul, in a great battle, and captured its king, and brought Tikal back to glory. He rebuilt the city, and built great new temples, and set up new monuments, and celebrated great festivals. He was a giant: his skeleton shows that he was tall, much taller than most Maya of his time.

--

He was buried under the Temple of the Great Jaguar, in a great tomb, deep inside the pyramid, with wonderful treasures: jade, more than sixteen pounds of it, in necklaces, and bracelets, and ear ornaments, and a mosaic vessel, and pearls, and shells, and painted pots, and carved bones, with scenes of the gods, and the king's voyage to the underworld, in a canoe, paddled by gods, with animals, an iguana, a monkey, a parrot, and a dog, as passengers.

His son, Yik'in Chan K'awiil, became king after him, and built even greater temples: Temple Four, the tallest of all, about seventy metres high, rising far above the forest, the tallest building in the Americas, before the twentieth century.

{img:temple-iv-tallest-rising-above-forest-canopy-view-other-temples}
In the year 750, Tikal is at the height of its power, rich, and splendid, ruled by the descendants of the great king. Its kings wear headdresses of quetzal feathers, and jaguar skins, and jade, and sit on thrones, covered with jaguar skins, in the palaces of the Central Acropolis, and receive ambassadors, and tribute, and gifts, from other cities, and celebrate the great ceremonies of the calendar, on the tops of the temples, in clouds of incense, before the whole people, gathered in the plazas below.

--

The queens, too, are important. Some Maya queens ruled in their own right. The wives of the kings came, often, from other royal families, from other cities, to make alliances. And they appear on the monuments, beside their husbands, in their rich robes, and jewels, taking part in the ceremonies.
