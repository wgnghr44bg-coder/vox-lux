Let us walk into the heart of Tikal, on a morning in the year 750.

At the centre of the city lies the Great Plaza. It is a wide open square, covered with smooth white plaster. It shines so brightly in the sun that it hurts your eyes. On the east and the west side stand two great pyramids. They face each other. Each one is steep and tall, with a stairway up the front to a small temple on top.

--

On the east side stands the Temple of the Great Jaguar. Archaeologists call it Temple One. It has nine steep levels and rises forty-seven metres high. On top is a small temple with three narrow rooms. Above that rises a tall carved wall called a roof comb. It shows a king sitting on his throne. It makes the temple look even taller. The temple was built about twenty years ago, over the grave of a great king.

On the west side, facing it, stands Temple Two. It is a little smaller. It may have been built for that king's queen.

{img:temple-i-great-jaguar-steep-stairway-roof-comb-red-painted}
On the north side of the plaza is a big raised platform with many small temples. They were built one on top of another, over more than a thousand years. The old kings of Tikal are buried there. On the south side is a large group of palaces. The royal family and the nobles live there. And the king holds his court there.

--

In front of the northern temples stands a long row of carved stones. Each one shows a king in his rich clothes. The signs on it tell his name and his deeds and the dates. In front of each stone lies a round altar stone.

Today the buildings look grey, because they are ruins. But in the year 750 they are covered with smooth plaster and painted. Most of them are a deep rich red. There are details in blue and yellow and green. The whole city glows red and white in the sun. Around it is the green of the forest, and above it the blue sky.

{img:central-acropolis-palace-courtyards-rooms-nobles-red-plaster}
From the Great Plaza, wide raised roads run out across the city. They are made of stone and covered with white plaster. The Maya call them white roads. They lead to other groups of temples and palaces, and to the big water ponds. Along them stand more temples, ball courts, markets and the houses of nobles.

--

All around the centre, for many kilometres, there are the houses of ordinary people. They stand among the trees and gardens and fields. Each family has a few small houses around a little yard. So the city is not packed tight like a city in Europe. It is spread out, like one big garden in the forest.

Today, scientists fly over the forest with a special machine. It sends down beams of laser light that pass between the leaves to the ground. It is called LiDAR. With it they found that Tikal was much bigger than anyone thought. Thousands of houses and fields and roads lie hidden under the trees.
