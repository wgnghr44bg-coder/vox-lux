Let us walk into the heart of Tikal, on a morning in the year 750.

The centre of the city is the Great Plaza: a wide, open square, paved with smooth white plaster, gleaming in the sunshine, so bright that it hurts the eyes. On its east and west sides, facing each other, there are two great temple pyramids, steep, and tall, with stairways running up the front, to small temples on their tops.

--

On the east side, there is the Temple of the Great Jaguar, which archaeologists call Temple One: a pyramid of nine steep levels, rising forty-seven metres high, with a small temple on top, with three narrow rooms, and above it, a great roof comb, a tall decorated wall of stone, carved and painted, with the figure of a seated king, which makes the temple look even taller. It was built about twenty years before, over the tomb of a great king, Jasaw Chan K'awiil.

On the west side, facing it, there is the Temple of the Masks, Temple Two, a little smaller, built, perhaps, in honour of his queen, Lady Twelve Macaw.

{img:temple-i-great-jaguar-steep-stairway-roof-comb-red-painted}
On the north side of the plaza, there is the North Acropolis: a great platform, with many small temples on it, built one on top of another, over more than a thousand years, where the ancient kings of Tikal are buried. And on the south side, there is the Central Acropolis, a great complex of palaces, with courtyards, and rooms, and stairways, where the royal family, and the nobles, live, and the king holds his court.

--

In front of the North Acropolis, there is a long row of stelae, carved stone monuments, each with the portrait of a king, in his rich costume, and the glyphs, telling his name, and his deeds, and the dates. And in front of each stela, a round altar stone.

The buildings are not grey, as they look today, in the ruins. In 750, they are covered with smooth plaster, and painted, mostly a deep, rich red, with details in blue, and yellow, and green, and black, and white. The whole city glows red, and white, in the sun, against the green of the forest, and the blue of the sky.

{img:central-acropolis-palace-courtyards-rooms-nobles-red-plaster}
From the Great Plaza, wide, raised roads, of stone, and plaster, called sacbeob, white roads, run out, across the city, to the other great groups of temples, and palaces, and to the great reservoirs. Along them, there are more temples, and pyramids, and plazas, and ball courts, and markets, and the houses of the nobles.

--

And everywhere, around the centre, for many kilometres, among the trees, and the gardens, and the fields, there are the houses of the ordinary people: little groups of houses, around small courtyards, on low platforms, so that the city is not crowded, like a European city, but spread out, like a great garden city, in the forest.

Today, archaeologists using a new technology, called LiDAR, which uses laser beams, from aircraft, to see through the trees, to the ground below, have found that the city was much larger than anyone had thought, with thousands of houses, and fields, and roads, and walls, and canals, hidden under the forest.
