In the heart of Tikal, near the Great Plaza, there is a ball court.

It is a long narrow lane with a smooth floor. On each side stands a long low building with a sloping wall. The walls face each other. They are painted red and white. Here the Maya play their great game, the ball game.

--

The ball game is one of the oldest games in the world. It was played all over Mexico and Central America for more than three thousand years. In some places in Mexico, a version of it is still played today.

The ball is made of solid rubber. It is heavy and hard and bouncy, about the size of a big melon. It is made from the sap of the rubber tree. The Maya were among the first people in the world to make rubber. Nobody in Europe had ever seen it.

The players must keep the ball in the air. They send it to the other side, bouncing it off the sloping walls. But they may not touch it with their hands or their feet. They hit it with their hips and their thighs and their arms. So they wear thick padded belts around their waists. They wear pads on their knees and arms too. The ball is heavy, and it can hurt.

--

It is a fast and difficult game. The ball flies, the players dive, and the crowd holds its breath. Then it cheers. People make bets on their favourite players, with cacao beans and cloth.

And the game is also holy. For the Maya, the ball is like the sun and the moon. They move across the sky and through the world below the earth. The game is like the great contest between day and night, between light and darkness.

{img:hero-twins-playing-ball-underworld-lords-mythic-scene-painted-vase}
In the old holy book, there is a great story about the ball game.

--

Long ago, there were two brothers who loved to play ball. But they played so noisily that they woke the lords of the world below the earth. The lords invited them down to play. And the lords tricked them. The brothers never came back.

But one of the brothers had two sons, who were twins. They are called the Hero Twins. And they too loved to play ball. And they too were invited down to the world below.

--

But the Hero Twins were clever. The lords set them test after test. They had to spend a night in the House of Darkness. And in the House of Cold. And in the House of Jaguars and the House of Bats. Each time, it seemed they could not escape. And each time, the twins found a trick. They played ball against the lords, and fooled them again and again. In the end, they won.

Then the twins rose up into the sky. They became the sun and the moon. And their father, the corn god, was born again from the earth.

{img:ballcourt-marker-carved-stone-disc-ballplayer-glyphs}
At Tikal, the game is sometimes played just for fun. Sometimes it is played as a great ceremony. And sometimes, long ago, the stones tell us, prisoners of war had to play. For them, losing had a very high price. But that was rare, and we will leave it there, gently, in the past.

--

In the evening, the ball court is quiet again. The players have gone home to rest their bruises. The crowds have gone. The long narrow court lies empty in the fading light. The red walls glow. And the shadows of the temples fall across it.
