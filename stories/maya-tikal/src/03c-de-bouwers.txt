How did the Maya build such great temples? They had no metal tools and no wheels. They had no animals to pull or carry.

They used stone and wood and fire and water. And the strength and skill and patience of thousands of people.

--

The ground under Tikal is limestone. The builders cut it out of the ground close by. They use tools made of a very hard stone called flint. Freshly cut limestone is soft and easy to work. It slowly hardens in the air. Many of the holes they dug later became water ponds.

The workers carry the blocks on their backs. A strap goes across their forehead to help hold the weight. Or they roll the blocks on logs. They lift them with ropes and ramps of earth and wood.

{img:lime-kiln-burning-wood-stones-smoke-workers-forest-edge}
The inside of a pyramid is mostly filled with loose stones and earth. Walls hold it in. Then it is covered with a skin of cut stone blocks. And then with plaster.

--

The plaster is made by burning limestone. The builders pile up wood and stones and burn them for a day and a night. The stone turns into white powder. They mix it with water and sand. It becomes a smooth white paste that hardens like stone. But it takes a lot of wood. To make enough plaster for one big temple, whole parts of the forest are cut down. Every new temple makes the forest a little smaller.

The plaster covers everything. The pyramids and the temples, the squares and the roads and the floors. It is smoothed and polished. And then it is painted, mostly red, with paint made from red earth.

--

On top of each pyramid, the builders make the small temple. Above it they raise the tall roof comb. It has small hollow rooms inside to make it lighter. On the front they shape a big plaster figure of the king on his throne, painted in many colours.

The builders work for the king, perhaps a few weeks every year. They come in the dry season, when there is less work in the fields. It is a kind of tax. In return they get food. And they take part in the great festivals.

{img:architect-scribe-plan-workers-raising-temple-scaffolding-ropes}
There are master builders and plasterers, carvers and painters. Fathers teach the craft to their sons. They plan the buildings very carefully. Many line up with the rising sun and with the stars. And they line up with each other, across the city.

--

The Maya often build on top of old buildings. When a king wants a new temple, he does not tear the old one down. He builds the new one over it, like a shell. So inside many pyramids at Tikal there are older pyramids. And inside those, older ones still. Like the layers of an onion.

So the temples of Tikal grew layer by layer, for more than a thousand years. They became the great buildings that still rise above the forest today.
