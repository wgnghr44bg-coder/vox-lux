What do the people of Tikal look like? What do they wear?

The Maya are not very tall. They have brown skin and black hair and dark eyes. They think a long sloping forehead and a long nose are very beautiful.

--

Ordinary men wear a loincloth. It is a long strip of cotton wound around the waist and between the legs. The ends hang down in front and behind. Sometimes the ends are decorated. On cool evenings, men wrap a cape or a blanket around their shoulders. On their feet they wear sandals.

Women wear a long wrapped skirt. Over it they wear a loose cotton blouse or dress. It is called a huipil. It can be white or coloured, with woven patterns. They wear a shawl over their shoulders or their heads.

{img:maya-man-loincloth-cape-sandals-hair-tied-feather-market}
Men wear their hair long, tied up on top of their heads or in braids. Women wear their hair long too. They braid it and wind it around their heads with coloured ribbons.

--

Both men and women love jewellery. Necklaces and bracelets of shell and stone and seeds. Nobles wear jade. Many wear big round ear plugs of jade or shell or wood. The holes in their ears are stretched slowly from childhood.

Some people decorate their teeth. They file them into little shapes. Or they drill tiny holes in the front teeth and fill them with shiny bits of jade. Some paint their faces and bodies red and black and blue. Some have tattoos.

--

The nobles and the king dress much more richly. They wear fine cotton in deep colours with painted patterns. They wear capes of jaguar skin and feathers. They wear big collars of jade beads. And they wear tall headdresses built on frames of wood. On top are masks of gods and animals and great sprays of green feathers. They nod and shimmer as they move.

{img:king-full-regalia-quetzal-feather-headdress-jade-collar-jaguar-kilt-stela}
At the great ceremonies, the king is almost hidden by his clothes. His headdress is as tall as he is. He wears jade and jaguar skins and feathers. He no longer looks like an ordinary man. He looks like a god, standing on top of the temple in clouds of incense, shining in the sun.

--

And there is colour everywhere. The deep red of the buildings and the clothes. The green of the jade and the feathers. The white of the plaster and the cotton. And a bright blue paint that archaeologists call Maya blue. It has lasted for more than a thousand years without fading. In the year 750, Tikal is not a grey city of stone. It is a city full of colour.
