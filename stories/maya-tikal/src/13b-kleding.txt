How do the people of Tikal look, and dress?

The Maya of the year 750 are not very tall, with brown skin, and black hair, and dark eyes, and they think a long, sloping forehead, and a long nose, are very beautiful, so some of them add a little extra bridge to their noses, of clay, or plaster.

--

The ordinary men wear a loincloth, a long strip of cotton cloth, wound around the waist, and between the legs, with the ends hanging down, in front, and behind, sometimes embroidered, or decorated, at the ends. Sometimes they wear a cape, or a blanket, around their shoulders, in the cool of the evening, and sandals of leather, or fibre, on their feet.

The women wear a long skirt, wrapped around the waist, and a loose blouse, or a long dress, of cotton, the huipil, white, or coloured, and woven, or embroidered, with patterns, and a cloth, or shawl, over their shoulders, or their heads.

{img:maya-man-loincloth-cape-sandals-hair-tied-feather-market}
The men wear their hair long, tied up on top of their heads, or in braids, with a bare patch, sometimes, on the top. The women wear their hair long, too, braided, and wound around their heads, with ribbons of coloured cloth.

--

Both men and women love jewellery: necklaces, and bracelets, and anklets, of beads, of shell, and stone, and seeds, and, for the nobles, jade. And ear ornaments, called ear flares: great round discs, of jade, or shell, or wood, through holes, in their ear lobes, which are stretched, gradually, from childhood, to hold them.

Some people decorate their teeth, filing them into little shapes, or drilling tiny holes, in their front teeth, and filling them with little, shining pieces of jade, or pyrite. Some paint their bodies, and their faces, in red, and black, and blue. And some have tattoos.

--

The nobles, and the king, dress much more richly: in fine cotton, dyed in deep colours, and embroidered, and painted, with patterns, and capes, of jaguar skin, and feathers, and belts, with little masks, of jade, and great collars of jade beads, and tall headdresses, built on frames of wood, and wicker, with masks of gods, and animals, and great sprays of quetzal feathers, nodding, and shimmering, as they move.

{img:king-full-regalia-quetzal-feather-headdress-jade-collar-jaguar-kilt-stela}
At the great ceremonies, the king is almost hidden by his costume: his headdress, as tall as he is, his jade, his jaguar skins, his feathers, his ornaments, his sceptre. He no longer looks like an ordinary man, but like a god, standing on the top of the temple, in the clouds of incense, shining, in the sun.

--

And the colours are everywhere: the deep red of the buildings, and the clothes, the green of the jade, and the feathers, the blue of the paint, made from a clay and indigo, a beautiful, bright blue, which archaeologists call Maya blue, which has lasted, unfaded, for more than a thousand years, the white of the plaster, and the cotton, the black, and the yellow. Tikal, in the year 750, is not a grey city of stone, but a city full of colour.
