The Maya can write.

Of all the peoples of the ancient Americas, the Maya had the most complete writing. With it they could write down anything they could say. Names and dates, stories and poems.

--

Maya writing is made of small square pictures with rounded corners. We call them glyphs. Each one is like a tiny painting. It might show a face, or an animal, or a hand. Little signs are attached to it. Some glyphs stand for a whole word. A jaguar's head means jaguar. Other glyphs stand for a sound, like ba or la or ma. A writer can write the same word in many ways. Sometimes with a picture, sometimes with sounds, sometimes with both. He chooses what looks most beautiful.

There are more than a thousand different glyphs. They are written in pairs of columns, from left to right and from top to bottom.

The writers are important people. They are nobles, sometimes even princes. They are artists and writers and star watchers all at once. They even have their own god, a monkey god of writing and art.

--

They paint with brushes and with pens made of feathers. Their ink pots are cut from shells. In paintings, writers often have a little bundle of brushes tucked into their headdress.

They write on stone, carved on tall stones and altars and stairways. They write on painted pots and on carved jade and bone. And they write books.

--

Maya books are made of paper from the inner bark of the fig tree. The bark is beaten flat and covered with a smooth white coat. Then it is folded like a fan, or like an accordion, between covers of wood or jaguar skin. You can open it page by page. Or you can stretch it out to many metres long.

The pages are painted on both sides with glyphs and pictures, in black and red and blue. There are books about history and the gods. There are books about the stars, the calendar and medicine. And there are books of songs.

{img:maya-codex-pages-unfolded-red-black-glyphs-figures-calendar}
There must have been thousands of these books in every Maya city. But they were fragile. The climate is warm and damp. Almost all of them were lost to time and insects. Much later, Spanish priests burned many more, because they did not understand them.

Today only four Maya books survive. They are all from later times, and all from far away from Tikal. They are kept in museums in Germany, Spain, France and Mexico.

--

For hundreds of years, nobody could read Maya writing. It was a great puzzle.

Then slowly, in the last hundred years, scholars began to understand it. First they worked out the numbers and the dates. Then a Russian scholar showed that many glyphs stood for sounds. Then a woman scholar, Tatiana Proskouriakoff, studied the dates on the stones. She showed that they told the stories of real kings and queens. Their births, their crowning, their wars and their deaths. And then scholars began to read whole sentences.

{img:scholar-desk-notebook-glyph-drawings-lamp-twentieth-century}
Today most Maya writing can be read. And the stones of Tikal have begun to speak again. They tell us the names and stories of their kings and their people.
