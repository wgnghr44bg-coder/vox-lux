Another plant is very precious to the Maya: cacao, the tree that gives us chocolate.

The cacao tree is a small tree, which grows in the shade of taller trees, in the warm, wet forests. Its pods grow straight out of its trunk, and its branches: big, oval, ridged pods, yellow, and orange, and red, and purple, like great, strange fruits.

--

Inside each pod, there are thirty or forty seeds, the cacao beans, wrapped in a sweet, white pulp. The beans are taken out, and left to ferment, for a few days, and dried, in the sun, and roasted, and ground, on a grinding stone, into a thick, dark paste.

And from this paste, the Maya make a drink: chocolate.

{img:maya-woman-pouring-chocolate-drink-vessel-height-foam}
It is not sweet, like our chocolate. It is bitter, and spicy, mixed with water, and ground corn, and chilli, and flowers, and vanilla, and honey. And it is made frothy, by pouring it, from a great height, from one vessel into another, so that it is covered with a thick, rich foam, which is the best part.

--

Chocolate is a drink for kings, and nobles, and great occasions: for feasts, and weddings, and ceremonies, and offerings to the gods. Beautiful painted vessels, for drinking chocolate, have been found in the tombs of the kings of Tikal, and other Maya cities, and some of them still have traces of chocolate, inside, and glyphs, painted on the outside, which say, this is the cup, for the cacao, of the king.

And cacao beans are so valuable that they are used as money. With a few cacao beans, you can buy a tomato, or a tamale. With a hundred, you can buy a turkey, or a good cloth.

{img:painted-maya-cylinder-vase-court-scene-nobles-cacao-glyphs}
The Maya have a god of cacao, and festivals of cacao, and cacao is given to the gods, in offerings, and at the great moments of life.

--

Much later, when the Aztecs ruled in Mexico, cacao was still money, and chocolate still the drink of kings. The Spanish brought it to Europe, in the sixteenth century, where it became the fashionable drink of kings, and queens, and nobles, and later, with sugar, and milk, the chocolate we know today. The word chocolate comes from the languages of Mexico, and cacao, perhaps, from the Maya word, kakaw, written in their glyphs, on their chocolate cups.

So, every time we drink a cup of hot chocolate, before bed, we are sharing something with the kings, and nobles, of Tikal.
