The Maya world is full of gods.

For the Maya, the world has three levels. Above, there are the thirteen layers of the heavens, where the sun, and the moon, and the stars, and the gods of the sky live. In the middle, there is the earth, where people live, flat, and square, with four corners, and four sides, each with its own colour: red for the east, where the sun rises, black for the west, where it sets, white for the north, and yellow for the south. And below, there are the nine layers of the underworld, Xibalba, the place of fear, ruled by the lords of death.

--

And at the centre of the world, joining the three levels together, there is the great world tree, the ceiba, the colour green, with its roots in the underworld, and its trunk in the middle world, and its branches in the heavens, and a great bird, sitting in its top.

The temples of Tikal are like sacred mountains, rising toward the heavens. And the doorways of the temples are like caves, leading into the mountain, and the underworld. And the king, standing on the top of the temple, in the doorway, between the heavens and the underworld, is like the world tree, joining them all together.

{img:chaak-rain-god-axe-lightning-clouds-mask-temple-facade}
There are many gods.

--

There is Itzamnaaj, the old god, the wise one, the first priest, and the first scribe, the lord of the heavens. There is K'inich Ajaw, the sun god, with his great, squinting eyes, and his front teeth filed into a T shape, who travels across the sky, by day, and through the underworld, by night, as a jaguar.

There is Chaak, the rain god, with his long nose, and his axe, which is lightning, with which he strikes the clouds, and makes the rain fall. He is perhaps the most important god of all, for the farmers, because, without rain, the corn will not grow. There is the young maize god. There is Ix Chel, the old goddess of the moon, and of weaving, and of childbirth, and medicine. There is K'awiil, the god of lightning, and power, and abundance, with one leg in the shape of a serpent, whose name is in the names of many kings. Jasaw Chan K'awiil means, perhaps, K'awiil who clears the sky.

--

The gods must be fed, and honoured, and thanked, with offerings: with incense, of copal resin, burned in clay censers, so that the smoke rises up, to the heavens, in great white clouds, smelling sweet, and resinous; and with food, and chocolate, and flowers, and jade, and feathers, and songs, and dances, and prayers.

{img:copal-incense-smoke-clay-censer-temple-doorway-offerings-flowers}
And with blood. The kings, and the queens, on great occasions, offer a few drops of their own blood, on strips of paper, which are burned, so that the smoke rises up, to the ancestors, and the gods. It is the most sacred offering of all, because it is the gift of life itself, and the kings are the ones who must give it, for their people.

--

The Maya also honour their ancestors. The dead are buried under the floors of the houses, or near them, so that they stay close to the family. And the great kings are buried in the temples, and their descendants come to make offerings, and to speak with them, and to ask for their help.

There are spirits everywhere too: in the mountains, and the caves, and the springs, and the trees, and the fields, and the forest. Every person has a spirit companion, an animal, who shares their soul, and their fate. And the kings have the most powerful of all: jaguars, and serpents, and fiery beings.

{img:maya-dance-ceremony-plaza-masked-dancers-drums-conch-trumpets}
And there is music, at every ceremony: the beat of drums, of wood, and turtle shells, and the call of great conch shell trumpets, and long wooden trumpets, and flutes, and rattles, and whistles, and singing, and dancing, in the plazas, before the temples, with the dancers in masks, and feathers, and costumes of the gods.

--

Many of the old beliefs live on, today, among the Maya people, mixed with Christianity. In the villages of Guatemala, and Mexico, people still burn copal, and pray to the saints, and to the old spirits, of the mountains, and the earth, and the rain, and the corn, and count the days of the sacred calendar.
