What do the people of Tikal eat?

Mostly corn, as we have seen, every day, in many forms: tortillas, cooked on the griddle; and tamales, little parcels of corn dough, filled with beans, or squash seeds, or meat, wrapped in leaves, and steamed, in a big pot, over the fire; and drinks of corn, thick, and warm, made from ground corn, and water, sometimes with chilli, or honey, or cacao, drunk from a gourd, in the morning, and carried to the fields, in a little ball of dough, to be mixed with water, from a stream, at midday.

--

The corn is always cooked with lime, or wood ash, first, softened overnight, in a pot of water, with a little lime, made from burnt limestone, or shells. This makes it softer, and easier to grind, and much more nourishing. It is one of the great discoveries of the peoples of Mexico, and Central America, thousands of years old, and it is still done today.

With the corn, there are beans, black, and red, and squash, and its seeds, toasted, and ground, and chilli peppers, of many kinds, and tomatoes, and avocados.

{img:maya-feast-foods-turkey-tamales-squash-fruits-painted-plates-mats}
For meat, there is the deer, which the hunters bring back, from the forest, and the peccary, and the wild turkey, and the tame turkey, which the families keep, in their courtyards, and ducks, and dogs, a special small breed, which the Maya keep, and fatten, for feasts. There are rabbits, and armadillos, and iguanas, and fish, from the rivers, and the lakes, and snails.

--

There is honey, from the stingless bees, little bees, without a sting, which the Maya keep, in hollow logs, hung in the shade, around their houses, and which make a thin, sweet, delicious honey. The Maya beekeepers still keep them, today, in the Yucatan, though they are becoming rare.

There are fruits: avocados, and papayas, and guavas, and the sapodilla fruit, sweet, and brown, and the mamey, with its orange flesh, and the custard apple, and the hog plum, and many others, which grow in the gardens, and the forest, around the houses.

--

At feasts, there is much more: turkeys, roasted, and tamales, of many kinds, filled with meat, and beans, and decorated, and great pots of chocolate, and a drink, called balché, made of honey, and water, and the bark of a tree, fermented, which is drunk at ceremonies.

{img:maya-woman-cooking-tortillas-clay-griddle-comal-smoke-morning}
The cooking is done in the kitchen house, over the fire, of three stones, by the women, who are also the ones who know the recipes, and the plants, and the herbs, and the medicines, and pass them on, to their daughters, and granddaughters.

--

The pots are made of clay, big ones, for storing water, and corn, and cooking tamales, and small ones, for sauces, and chillies. The plates, and bowls, are of clay too, and gourds, cut in half, and dried, make cups, and bowls, and dippers, and bottles.

And so, every day, in thousands of kitchen houses, all over Tikal, the smoke rises, through the thatch, and the smell of corn, and woodsmoke, and chilli, drifts over the city, mixed with the scent of the flowers, and the forest.
