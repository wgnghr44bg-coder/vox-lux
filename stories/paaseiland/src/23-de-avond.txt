So we leave Rapa Nui tonight, around the year 1500, as the evening falls.

The sun is setting over the ocean, to the west. The sky turns gold, then pink, then a deep soft blue. The waves roll gently onto the rocks below the village. Seabirds fly home to their nests on the cliffs. And on the stone platform by the sea, the moai stand in a row, with the last light of the sun on their backs. Their white coral eyes look out over the village, calm and watchful.

--

In the village, the earth ovens have been opened, and the families are eating their supper. The smell of sweet potatoes and fish drifts on the evening breeze. Children are being called in from the shore. The chickens are going into their little stone houses for the night. And smoke rises gently from the fires, into the darkening sky.

{img:family-outside-boat-house-evening-fire-eating-grandmother-stories-stars}
Outside their boat-shaped house, Hina's family is sitting together on the paved stones. Tavake is tired from the quarry, with dust still in his hair. He is telling them that the new moai is almost finished. Soon, he says, it will walk. Vai is weaving a basket by the light of the fire. Mako is trying to make a string figure, and getting his fingers tangled. Hina helps him, patiently.

--

Grandmother Uka is sitting with her back against a warm stone, looking up at the sky. The first stars are coming out over the ocean. She begins to chant softly, an old chant about the canoes that came across the sea, long ago, following the stars. Her voice is low and slow, like the waves. Hina leans against her and listens. Mako stops tangling his string, and listens too.

{img:grandmother-chanting-children-leaning-against-her-night-sky-ocean-moai}
The chant tells of the king Hotu Matu'a and his great canoes. It tells of the dream of the island with three volcanoes. It tells of the white beach of Anakena, and the first sweet potatoes planted in the new land. It tells of the navigators who read the stars and the waves. Hina knows every word. One day, she will chant it for her own grandchildren. And they will chant it for theirs.

--

The stars are bright now, thousands and thousands of them, over the dark ocean. The Milky Way stretches across the sky, like a great river of light. The sound of the waves is soft and steady. Mako has fallen asleep with his head in his grandmother's lap, his string still tangled in his fingers. Hina's eyes are closing too.

Tavake lifts Mako gently and carries him into the house. Vai helps Grandmother Uka to her feet. Hina takes one last look at the moai on their platform, dark against the starry sky. Then she crawls into the warm little house, and lies down on her mat beside her brother. And outside, the ancestors keep watch over the sleeping island, in the middle of the great quiet ocean, under the stars.
