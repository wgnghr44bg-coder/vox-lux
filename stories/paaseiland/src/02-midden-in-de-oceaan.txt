Rapa Nui is one of the most isolated places where people live, anywhere in the world.

It is a small island, shaped like a triangle. It is only about twenty-four kilometres long, and twelve kilometres wide. You could walk across it in a day. At each corner of the triangle, there is an old volcano. The volcanoes are quiet now, and have been for a very long time. Their slopes are covered with grass, and some of their craters are filled with lakes.

--

All around the island, there is ocean. The nearest place where people live is a tiny island called Pitcairn, about two thousand kilometres to the west. The nearest big land is the coast of South America, about three and a half thousand kilometres to the east. That is about as far as from Amsterdam to Cairo, but all across open sea. When you stand on Rapa Nui and look out, you see nothing but water, in every direction.

{img:rapa-nui-coast-black-volcanic-rocks-waves-crashing-cliffs-green-grass}
The island is made of volcanic rock, black and brown and red. Along the coast, there are cliffs and rocky shores, where the waves crash and spray. There are only a few small sandy beaches. The most beautiful is called Anakena, on the north coast. It has soft white sand, palm trees and calm blue water. It is the place where, the old stories say, the first people came ashore.

--

The weather on Rapa Nui is mild, neither very hot nor very cold. It is warm in summer and cool in winter, with rain in all seasons. The wind blows often across the open hills. There are no rivers on the island, because the rain soaks quickly into the porous volcanic rock. The people get their water from the crater lakes, from springs near the shore, and from rain collected in hollows in the rock.

{img:crater-lake-rano-kau-reeds-water-volcano-rim-ocean-beyond}
There are very few kinds of plants and animals on the island. Because it is so far from anywhere, few creatures ever reached it. There were no land mammals at all, before people came. There were seabirds, many of them, nesting on the cliffs and little islands. There were lizards and insects. There were fish in the sea around the island. And, long ago, there were forests of tall palm trees.

--

The people of Rapa Nui have a beautiful name for their island. They call it Te Pito o te Henua. That means the navel of the world. A navel is the little round mark in the middle of your tummy. So the name means the centre of the world. For the people who lived there, alone in the middle of the great ocean, it truly was the centre of their world.

{img:round-smooth-stone-te-pito-kura-navel-of-world-shore-rocks-sunlight}
On the north coast, near the shore, there is a large round stone, very smooth. It is called Te Pito Kura, the golden navel. The old stories say that the first king of the island brought it with him in his canoe, from his homeland far away. People say that the stone feels warm, and that it has special power. Even today, visitors sometimes put their hands on it, and close their eyes, and feel the warmth of the sun in the old smooth rock.

Because the island is so small and so far away, the people of Rapa Nui knew it very well. They knew every hill and every rock, every cave and every spring. They gave names to every place, and told stories about them. This rock was where a famous fisherman sat. This cave was where a girl once hid. This hill was where the first sweet potatoes were planted. The whole island was full of stories.

From the top of the volcanoes, you can see the whole island at once, and the ocean all around it. The people of Rapa Nui sometimes climbed up there to look out to sea. They looked for the seabirds, coming back to nest. They looked for shoals of fish, making the water shimmer. And perhaps, sometimes, they wondered if there were other lands out there, beyond the edge of the sea.
