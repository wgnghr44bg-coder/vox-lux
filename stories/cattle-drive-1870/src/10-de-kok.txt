The most important man on a cattle drive, some cowboys say, is not the trail boss. It is the cook. A good cook can make a crew happy. A bad cook can make them miserable. And Cookie, the old man with the white beard and the bad leg, is one of the best cooks on the trail. The cowboys respect him, and are a little afraid of him.

--

The cook's kitchen is the chuckwagon. Chuck is cowboy slang for food. The chuckwagon was invented a few years before, in 1866, by a famous cattleman called Charles Goodnight. He took an old army wagon, and built a big wooden box at the back of it, called the chuck box, with shelves and drawers, for the food and the cooking things. The back of the box folds down, on a hinge, to make a table, where the cook works.

In the chuck box, there are drawers for flour, sugar, coffee, salt and spices. For beans, dried apples, rice and dried peaches. For tin plates, cups, knives and forks. For medicines, needles and thread, and a bottle of whisky, for emergencies only. Under the wagon, there is a cowhide, stretched like a hammock, where Cookie collects wood and dried cow dung, called cow chips, for the fire, as they go along. On the prairie, there are few trees, and cow chips make a good, hot fire.

{img:dutch-ovens-coals-campfire-sourdough-biscuits-rising-cook-lifting-lid}
Cookie cooks in Dutch ovens. Big, heavy iron pots, with lids, which are put in the coals of the fire, with more coals on the lid. In them, he makes everything. Biscuits, stews, beans, cobblers. His sourdough biscuits are famous. He keeps a pot of sourdough starter, a bubbling mixture of flour, water and wild yeast, which he looks after like a baby. On cold nights, he even takes it into his bedroll with him, to keep it warm.

The food on the trail is simple, and the same almost every day. Beef, from a steer of the herd, which is slaughtered every few days. Beans, cooked for hours with salt pork. Biscuits. Coffee, strong and black, and lots of it. Sometimes, dried fruit, stewed with sugar. And on special days, a cobbler, a sweet pie of dried peaches or apples, baked in the Dutch oven. When Cookie makes a cobbler, the whole crew cheers.

--

{img:cowboys-eating-plates-on-knees-around-fire-cook-pouring-coffee-evening}
Cookie has strict rules, and everyone must obey them. Nobody touches the chuck box without permission. Nobody rides his horse near the cooking fire, because it stirs up dust into the food. Nobody complains about the food. If somebody complains, he has to be the cook the next day. So nobody ever complains. And when Cookie calls them to eat, they must come at once, or go without.

But Cookie is also kind, in his gruff way. He is a doctor, a barber, a dentist and a banker, as well as a cook. He mends the cowboys' clothes, and cuts their hair. He puts medicine on their cuts and blisters. He holds their money for them, in a tin box. And he gives advice, whether they want it or not. When Eli has a terrible blister, from his new boots, Cookie puts some ointment on it, and wraps it up, and grumbles about young fools who do not break in their boots properly.

{img:old-cook-and-boy-cowboy-talking-chuckwagon-tailgate-stars-lantern}
Late at night, when the others are asleep, Eli sometimes sits with Cookie, by the dying fire, and listens to his stories. Cookie has been everywhere. He was a sailor, a soldier, a gold miner in California, and a cook on a steamboat on the Mississippi. He has been up the trail four times. He tells Eli about all of it, in his slow, rough voice, while he smokes his pipe and watches the stars.

--

On the trail, the chuckwagon is home. It is where the cowboys eat, sleep, keep their things, and gather in the evenings. At night, Cookie always points the tongue of the wagon, the long pole at the front, toward the North Star. So that in the morning, Mr Hardin knows exactly which way is north, even on a cloudy day. It is an old cowboy trick. And every morning, the tongue of the wagon points the way to Kansas.
