A cowboy's clothes and equipment are simple, practical and tough. Everything has a reason. Everything is made to last through months of riding, in sun, rain, wind and dust. And most of it comes from the Mexican vaqueros, who invented the cowboy's way of life long before.

--

The most important thing is the hat. A cowboy's hat has a wide brim, to keep off the sun and the rain. A high crown, to keep his head cool. It is made of thick felt, which lasts for years. It can be used as a bucket, to carry water for his horse. As a fan, to light a fire. As a pillow at night. In 1865, a hatmaker called John Stetson made a famous hat for the West, called the Boss of the Plains. Soon every cowboy wanted one. But they were expensive. Eli's hat is an old one of his father's.

Around his neck, a cowboy wears a bandana, a big square scarf of cotton, often red. It has a hundred uses. Pulled up over the nose and mouth, it keeps out the dust, when riding behind the herd. It can be a towel, a bandage, a sling, a hat on a hot day, or a strainer for muddy water. Eli wears a blue one, which Rose made for him.

{img:cowboy-pulling-bandana-over-face-riding-drag-dust-cloud-behind-herd}
He wears a cotton shirt, a vest with pockets, and trousers of strong wool or canvas. Over his trousers, he wears chaps. Leather leggings, from the Spanish word chaparreras. They protect his legs from the thorny bushes of the brush country, and from rubbing against the saddle, and from the horns of the cattle. In the rain, he wears his slicker, the long oiled raincoat, which also covers his saddle.

On his feet, he wears tall leather boots, with high heels and pointed toes. They are not made for walking. They are made for riding. The pointed toe slips easily into the stirrup. The high heel stops the foot from sliding through the stirrup, which could be dangerous, if he fell from his horse. Cowboys hate walking. They say that a cowboy would rather ride a mile to catch a horse than walk a hundred metres to the corral.

--

{img:western-saddle-horn-leather-tooled-rope-coiled-hanging-close}
The saddle is the cowboy's most precious possession. It is heavy, made of wood covered with leather, with a high back and a horn at the front. The horn is for the rope. When a cowboy ropes a steer, he wraps the rope around the horn, so that the horse can hold the steer, not the man. A good saddle can cost a month's wages. Cowboys say that a man can sell his horse, but never his saddle. Eli's saddle is his father's. It is old and worn and comfortable.

The rope, or lariat, from the Spanish la reata, is about twenty metres long, made of braided rawhide or hemp. It has a loop at the end, called a noose. A good cowboy can throw it, and catch a running steer by the horns or the feet, at full gallop. It takes years of practice. Mateo, the vaquero, can make the rope do almost anything. He can make the loop dance in the air, and jump through it. Eli practises every evening. He is getting better.

{img:vaquero-teaching-boy-roping-loop-campfire-evening-practice-fence-post}
There are also spurs, little metal wheels on the heels of the boots, which jingle as the cowboy walks. A good cowboy hardly uses them. Just a touch, to tell the horse what to do. And there is a pistol, which most cowboys carry. Not for fighting, but for emergencies. For rattlesnakes, or for a horse that has broken its leg. Or to turn a stampede, by firing in the air. Mr Hardin does not allow pistols to be worn in camp. They are kept in the wagon.

--

Each cowboy also has a bedroll. A canvas sheet, with blankets inside, rolled up and tied, and carried in the chuckwagon during the day. At night, he unrolls it on the ground, wherever he is, and sleeps in it, under the stars. His saddle is his pillow. His slicker covers him when it rains. And his hat goes over his face, to keep off the morning sun. It is not very comfortable. But after a long day in the saddle, the cowboys sleep like babies.
