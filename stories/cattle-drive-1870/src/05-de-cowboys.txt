A cattle drive of three thousand cattle needs about twelve men. That is not very many, for so many animals. Each man is responsible for about two hundred and fifty cattle. And each man has his own job, and his own place around the herd. Let us meet the men of Mr Hardin's crew, as they sit around the fire, on the first evening.

--

At the head of the crew is the trail boss, Walt Hardin himself. He is about fifty years old, a tall, lean man with a grey moustache, a deeply lined face, and pale eyes that seem to see everything. He has been driving cattle for twenty years. He is in charge of everything. He decides the route, where to camp, where to cross the rivers. He rides ahead of the herd, every day, looking for grass and water. And his word is law.

His second in command is Sam Walker. He is about thirty-five, a Black cowboy, born enslaved on a ranch near the coast. He learned to ride and rope as a boy, and became one of the best cowboys in Texas. After the war, when he was free, he went to work for Mr Hardin. He has been up the trail three times. He is calm, wise and very skilled. Everyone respects him. He is the one who will teach Eli the most.

{img:black-cowboy-1870-skilled-rider-roping-steer-prairie-calm-confident}
There is Mateo Garza, a Mexican vaquero, about forty years old, from the border country near the Rio Grande. The vaqueros are the original cowboys. They were herding cattle on horseback in Mexico long before the Americans came to Texas. Many of the things the cowboys use come from them. The saddle, the lariat, the chaps, the spurs. Even many of the words. Mateo is the best roper in the crew. He can rope a running steer by its horns, or its feet, from far away, with a single throw.

There are the other cowboys. Young men from the farms and ranches of Texas. Jim and Tom, two brothers from Gonzales. A quiet young man called Will, who fought in the war, and does not talk about it. A cheerful Irishman called Paddy, who sings all day. A tall, thin cowboy called Slim, who is always hungry. And Pete, a boy of sixteen, who is the wrangler, the one who looks after the horses.

--

{img:mexican-vaquero-sombrero-lariat-saddle-horse-skilled-texas-1870}
About a quarter of all the cowboys on the trail were Black or Mexican. Some historians think even more. In the cities and the towns, life could be hard for them, because of prejudice. On the trail, it was often a little different. What mattered most was whether you could do your job. Whether you could ride, rope, and handle cattle. Whether you could be trusted. Sam and Mateo are respected because they are the best.

And there is the cook. His name is Henry Pruitt, but everyone calls him Cookie. He is the oldest man on the drive, about sixty, with a white beard and a bad leg, from falling off a horse many years ago. He cannot ride all day any more. So he drives the chuckwagon, the wagon with the food and the cooking things, and cooks for the whole crew. He is grumpy, bossy and very important. Everyone knows that you never, ever, argue with the cook.

{img:old-trail-cook-white-beard-apron-chuckwagon-dutch-oven-grumpy-kind}
The cowboys come from many different backgrounds. But on the trail, they become a team. They eat together, sleep together and work together, for months. They depend on each other. If one man does his job badly, everyone suffers. If one man is in trouble, everyone helps. By the end of the drive, they will be like brothers. Some of them will stay friends for the rest of their lives.

--

On the first evening, sitting around the fire, the men look at Eli, the new boy. Some of them tease him a little. They tell him stories about rattlesnakes in his boots and wolves in the night, to see if he is scared. Eli pretends not to be. Then Sam Walker tells them to leave the boy alone. He sits down next to Eli, and says quietly that everybody is new once. Even him. And that the best thing a new hand can do is keep his eyes open, and his mouth shut, and learn.
