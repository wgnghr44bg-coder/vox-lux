After the Indian Territory, the trail climbs slowly onto the great plains. The land becomes higher, flatter and wider. The trees disappear, except along the creeks. And there is grass. Nothing but grass, in every direction, as far as the eye can see, waving in the wind like the sea. It is called the prairie. And it is one of the most beautiful and lonely landscapes in the world.

--

The grass of the prairie is tall in some places, up to a horse's belly, and short in others. In the spring and early summer, it is full of wild flowers. Purple coneflowers, yellow sunflowers, red Indian paintbrush, blue lupins, white daisies. The prairie is like a great carpet of colour, rolling away to the horizon. And the wind is always blowing, making the grass and the flowers ripple and sway.

The prairie is full of animals. Antelope, the fastest animals in America, with white rumps that flash as they run. Deer in the creek bottoms. Coyotes, which howl at night. Jackrabbits, with enormous ears. Prairie chickens, which dance in the spring. Hawks circling high overhead. And prairie dogs. Little brown animals, a little like squirrels, which live in great towns of burrows, underground, with thousands of them, popping up out of their holes, and chattering.

{img:prairie-dog-town-little-animals-standing-on-mounds-watching-grassland}
Eli loves the prairie dogs. They stand on top of their little mounds, on their back legs, looking around, and calling to each other, with a high, chirping bark. When they see danger, they all disappear, at once, into their holes. And then, after a while, they pop up again, one by one, to see if it is safe. But the cowboys must be careful. The holes of the prairie dogs can trip a horse, and break its leg. So they ride around the prairie dog towns.

And there are the buffalo. In 1870, there are still millions of American bison, which everyone calls buffalo, on the great plains. They live in enormous herds. One day, the cowboys see a herd of buffalo, far away, moving slowly across the prairie. It is so big that it takes all day to pass. The ground seems to be moving. Thousands and thousands of great brown shaggy animals, with their huge heads and humped shoulders, grazing as they go.

--

{img:cowboys-on-horseback-watching-buffalo-herd-pass-awe-sunset-dust}
Mr Hardin stops the herd, to let the buffalo pass. If the cattle get mixed with the buffalo, it could be a disaster. So the cowboys sit on their horses, on a low hill, and watch. Eli has never seen anything so amazing. Sam Walker tells him to remember it. He says that the buffalo are being killed, in great numbers, by hunters, for their hides. And that one day, perhaps, they will be gone. Eli does not believe him. There are so many of them. How could they ever be gone?

But Sam was right. In the next fifteen years, the great herds of buffalo were almost completely destroyed, by hunters with rifles, who killed them in their millions, for their hides and their tongues. By 1890, there were only a few hundred left, out of tens of millions. It was one of the great tragedies of American history. For the Native American peoples of the plains, who depended on the buffalo, it was a catastrophe. Today, thanks to the work of a few people who saved them, there are again about half a million buffalo in America.

{img:sunset-over-endless-prairie-golden-grass-lone-tree-herd-bedding-down}
In the evenings, on the prairie, the sunsets are magnificent. The sky turns gold, then orange, then red, then purple, across the whole enormous horizon. The grass glows. The clouds are on fire. And the herd, bedding down for the night, is a great dark shape against the burning sky. The cowboys sit on their horses and watch, in silence. Some of them take off their hats.

--

Eli writes in his little notebook, that evening, by the light of the fire. He tries to describe the prairie, the flowers, the prairie dogs, the buffalo, and the sunset. But the words are not good enough. Nothing he writes is as big, or as beautiful, as the real thing. So in the end, he just writes, today we saw the buffalo, and the sky was on fire. And then he closes the book, and lies back, and looks at the stars, which are coming out, one by one, over the endless grass.
