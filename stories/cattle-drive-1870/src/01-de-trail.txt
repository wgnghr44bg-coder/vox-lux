Tonight we travel back about a hundred and fifty years, to the year 1870. We go to the wide, open plains of Texas, in the south of the United States. The land is flat and endless, covered with grass, stretching away to the horizon in every direction. And on this land, slowly moving north, there is a great herd of cattle. Thousands of them. With long, curving horns. And with them ride a small group of men on horseback. Cowboys.

--

It is early morning, in spring. The sky in the east is turning pale gold. The stars are fading. A cool wind moves across the grass, making it ripple like water. In a little hollow, by a creek, there is a camp. A wagon, with a white canvas cover. A small fire, with a coffee pot on it, sending up a thin line of smoke. And around the fire, men are sleeping on the ground, wrapped in blankets, with their saddles for pillows.

The cook is already up. He is an old man, with a grey beard and a battered hat, stirring the coals, and putting more wood on the fire. He bangs a spoon on a tin pan, and shouts. Roll out! Come and get it! And one by one, the cowboys wake up, stretch, pull on their boots and their hats, and come to the fire, for coffee and breakfast. Out on the prairie, the cattle are beginning to stir too, getting up from the grass, lowing softly.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

{img:young-cowboy-fifteen-1870-hat-bandana-standing-by-horse-sunrise-prairie}
Among the cowboys, there is a boy of fifteen, called Eli. It is his first cattle drive. He has never been more than a day's ride from home. And now he is on his way to Kansas, almost a thousand kilometres to the north, with three thousand cattle, eleven other men, and about a hundred horses. The journey will take more than two months. And it will change his life.

Tonight, we will ride with Eli on the long cattle drive, up the famous Chisholm Trail, from Texas to Kansas. We will meet the cowboys, and learn how they worked and lived. We will sit by the campfire, and eat beans and biscuits from the chuckwagon. We will ride night watch, singing softly to the cattle under the stars. We will cross great rivers, and wide prairies, and see herds of buffalo, and prairie dogs, and wild flowers.

{img:longhorn-cattle-herd-moving-across-prairie-dust-riders-on-flanks}
There were some dangerous moments on a cattle drive. Rivers in flood. Storms. And sometimes a stampede, when the whole herd suddenly ran, in a panic. We will see these, but gently, as they were told afterwards around the campfire. Because most of the time, a cattle drive was slow, quiet, and very hard work. Riding all day, in the sun and the dust. Watching the cattle. Finding grass and water. And sleeping under the stars.

--

{img:cowboys-riding-alongside-herd-wide-grassland-blue-sky-spring-flowers}
The cattle drives lasted only about twenty years. But they became one of the most famous parts of American history. They gave the world the cowboy, the hat, the boots, the songs, and the dream of the open range. So let us begin, on a spring morning in 1870, on the wide plains of Texas. The coffee is boiling. The cattle are waking. And a boy of fifteen is pulling on his boots, for the first day of the longest journey of his life.
