After about seven weeks on the trail, the herd crosses the border into Kansas. It is a great moment. The cowboys cheer and throw their hats in the air. They have come almost a thousand kilometres, from the hill country of Texas, across the rivers, the Indian Territory and the prairie. And now, there is only a little way left to go. About a hundred and fifty kilometres to Abilene.

--

Kansas is different from Texas. The land is flat and open, like the prairie. But here, there are farms. Homesteaders have come from the east, and from Europe, to farm the land. They have built little houses of sod, which is blocks of grass and earth, cut from the prairie, because there are no trees for wood. They plough the prairie, and plant wheat and corn. And they have fences, to keep the cattle out of their crops.

The farmers do not like the cattle drives. The longhorns trample their crops. And the longhorns carry a disease, called Texas fever, spread by ticks. The longhorns themselves are not affected by it. But it kills the cattle of the farmers. So the farmers of Kansas have made laws, to keep the Texas cattle out of the settled parts of the state. Every year, the line moves further west. And the trail must move with it.

{img:sod-house-homestead-kansas-family-standing-outside-wheat-field-wind}
Mr Hardin is careful. He keeps the herd away from the farms, on the open prairie. He pays the farmers for any damage. And when a farmer comes to complain, he listens politely, and gives him a steer, to make up for it. Some of the farmers become friendly, after that. They sell the cowboys eggs, milk, butter and fresh bread. One farmer's wife, from Sweden, gives Eli a whole loaf of rye bread, warm from the oven, because he reminds her of her son.

Eli is fascinated by the sod houses. They look like little hills, with doors and windows. Inside, they are cool in summer, and warm in winter, but dark, and a little dusty. Snakes and mice sometimes come through the walls. The farmers are poor, and work very hard. Their children run out to look at the herd, barefoot, in the grass. Eli waves to them. He thinks of Rose, at home in Texas, and wonders if she is all right.

--

{img:cowboys-washing-in-creek-shaving-before-town-laughing-splashing-summer}
As they get closer to Abilene, the cowboys start to get excited. They have been on the trail for two months. They are dirty, tired, sunburnt and dusty. Their clothes are in rags. Their hair is long, and their beards are wild. So, on the last day before Abilene, Mr Hardin lets the herd graze in a quiet valley, with a creek. And the cowboys wash. They wash their clothes, their bodies and their hair, in the creek, laughing and splashing like boys.

Cookie cuts everyone's hair, with his big scissors, sitting on a bucket by the chuckwagon. The cowboys shave, with their razors, looking into a little mirror, hanging on the wagon. They put on the clean shirts they have saved, at the bottom of their bags, for this day. Eli does not need to shave yet, much to the amusement of the other cowboys. But he washes his face and his hair, and puts on his clean blue shirt, and his bandana, and feels very grown up.

{img:distant-town-abilene-kansas-1870-railroad-water-tower-smoke-horizon}
In the evening, from the top of a low hill, the cowboys can see Abilene. A little town, on the flat prairie, beside a river. Wooden buildings. A water tower. The smoke of a train, far away, along the railway. And the great cattle pens, beside the tracks, already full of cattle from other herds. After two months of grass and sky, it looks like a city to Eli.

--

That night, the cowboys sit around the campfire, for the last time on the trail. They are quiet. Excited about tomorrow. But also a little sad. The trail has been hard. But it has also been their home, for two months. They have become friends. Tomorrow, they will sell the cattle, and get their wages, and go their separate ways. Paddy plays a slow tune on his fiddle. Eli plays along on his harmonica. And Old Ned, the lead steer, lies by the fire, at the edge of the light, as if he were listening too.
