A cowboy without a horse is not a cowboy. Horses are everything on the trail. The cowboys ride them all day, and often half the night. They depend on them completely. And a good cowboy loves his horses, and looks after them, sometimes better than he looks after himself.

--

On Mr Hardin's drive, there are about a hundred horses, for twelve men. This herd of horses is called the remuda, from a Spanish word meaning change, or exchange. Each cowboy has his own string of six or seven horses, which nobody else may ride. He changes horses two or three times a day, so that none of them gets too tired. A tired horse is a dangerous horse, slow and clumsy, at the moment when you need it most.

The remuda is looked after by the wrangler, Pete. He is sixteen, the youngest after Eli. It is his job to look after the horses, day and night. To drive them along behind the chuckwagon. To bring them in, when the cowboys need fresh horses. To watch them at night, so that they do not wander away. To check their hooves and their backs, for sores and stones. Pete knows every horse by name, and by its character.

{img:cowboy-saddling-horse-morning-gentle-hands-horse-nuzzling-shoulder}
Each horse is different, and has its own job. Some horses are good for the morning, calm and steady. Some are good for the afternoon, strong and patient. Some are good for river crossings, because they are strong swimmers and not afraid of water. And the most precious of all is the night horse. A calm, sure-footed horse, with good eyes, who can find his way in the dark, and stay calm in a storm. A good night horse can save a cowboy's life.

The best horses of all are the cutting horses. These are horses trained to work with cattle. A good cutting horse can pick out a single steer from the herd, and separate it from the others, all by itself, almost without the rider doing anything. It watches the steer, and moves when the steer moves, quick as a cat, turning and stopping, until the steer gives up and goes where the horse wants. It is wonderful to watch. Mateo's horse, a little brown mare called Paloma, is the best cutting horse on the drive.

--

{img:cutting-horse-separating-steer-from-herd-quick-turn-dust-vaquero-riding}
Eli has his mare Dolly, from home, and six other horses from Mr Hardin's remuda. There is a big bay called Sandy, for the mornings. A grey called Smoke, who is a good swimmer. A spotted pony called Patches, who is clever but lazy. And for the night, a quiet black horse called Midnight, who has very good eyes, and never gets frightened. Eli learns all their characters. Which one likes to have his ears scratched. Which one bites. Which one hates rain.

But Dolly is his favourite. She is not the fastest horse in the remuda, or the strongest. But she is the most loyal. She comes when he whistles. She follows him around the camp like a dog. When he is sad, and thinking of home, he goes to her, and puts his face against her warm neck, and tells her about it. And she stands very still, and listens, and blows softly through her nose.

{img:boy-face-against-horse-neck-evening-remuda-quiet-moment-sunset}
At night, the horses are kept near the camp, in a rope corral, or watched by Pete, as they graze. The cowboys keep their night horse saddled and tied near their bedroll, in case they are needed suddenly, in the night. On the open prairie, there are dangers for horses too. Rattlesnakes in the grass. Prairie dog holes, which can break a horse's leg. Wolves, though they rarely attack. And thieves, who sometimes try to steal horses at night.

--

Sam Walker says that a cowboy should always look after his horse before himself. Water the horse before you drink. Feed the horse before you eat. Rub down the horse before you rest. Because without your horse, you are nothing, out here on the prairie. Eli takes this advice very seriously. Every evening, before he eats his supper, he rubs Dolly down with a handful of dry grass, and checks her hooves, and gives her a little corn. And only then does he go to the fire.
