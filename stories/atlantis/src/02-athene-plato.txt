It is Athens, around the year 360 before Christ.

Athens is a famous Greek city. It lies near the sea, in a dry land of hills and olive trees. On a rocky hill in the middle of the city stands the Acropolis. On top of it are white marble temples. The greatest is the Parthenon, the temple of the goddess Athena. It was built about eighty years before our story. It shines in the sun.

--

Athens is a city of talkers. In the market square, people gather to buy and sell. But also to argue and discuss. About politics. About the gods. About what is good and what is true. Athens is a democracy. That means the citizens vote on the laws themselves. So everybody has an opinion. And everybody wants to share it.

In this city lives Plato. He is about sixty-five years old. He comes from a rich old family. When he was young, he was a student of a famous thinker called Socrates. Socrates walked around Athens asking people questions. Simple questions that turned out to be very hard. What is justice? What is courage? What is a good life? People found him annoying. Or wonderful. Usually both.

{img:socrates-talking-young-men-street-athens-barefoot-questions}
Socrates never wrote anything down. But Plato did. He wrote dozens of books. Most of them are written as conversations. People talking and asking questions and searching for answers together. Socrates is often the main speaker. These books are still read all over the world. Plato is one of the most important thinkers who ever lived.

--

Plato loved stories. He used them to explain his ideas. A story about a cave and its shadows. A story about a ring that makes you invisible. A story about the soul as a chariot pulled by two horses. And in his old age, he wrote down the story of Atlantis.

{img:plato-writing-scroll-papyrus-oil-lamp-study-night-athens}
He put it in two of his books. They are called Timaeus and Critias, after the people who speak in them. And in them, the story of Atlantis is told by a man called Critias. Who heard it from his grandfather. Who heard it from a great wise man called Solon. Who heard it from priests in Egypt. A story passed from mouth to mouth across the centuries. Let us follow it back.
