It is Athens, around the year 360 before Christ.

Athens is a famous Greek city, not far from the sea. It lies in a dry and sunny land of hills and olive trees. On a rocky hill in the middle of the city stands the Acropolis. On top of it are temples of white marble, gleaming in the sun. The greatest of them is the Parthenon, the temple of the goddess Athena. It was built about eighty years before our story. From almost anywhere in the city, you can look up and see it shining.

--

Athens is a city of talkers. In the big market square, people gather every morning to buy and sell. Farmers bring olives and figs, cheese and honey and onions. Fishermen shout about the fish they caught at dawn. Potters lay out their painted jars and cups on the ground. But people come to the market for something else, too. They come to talk and to argue. They talk about politics, about the gods, about what is good and what is true. Athens is a democracy, which means the citizens vote on the laws themselves. So everybody has an opinion, and everybody wants to share it.

In this busy city lives Plato. He is about sixty-five years old, with a grey beard and broad shoulders. He comes from a rich and old Athenian family. When he was young, he was the student of a famous thinker called Socrates. Socrates walked around Athens all day, asking people questions. They were simple questions that turned out to be very hard. What is justice, he asked, and what is courage? What is a good life? Some people found him annoying, and some found him wonderful. Usually they found him both at the same time.

{img:socrates-talking-young-men-street-athens-barefoot-questions}
Socrates walked barefoot, even in winter, and he wore the same old cloak every day. He did not care about money or fine clothes. Young men followed him around the streets to listen to his questions. Plato was one of them, and he never forgot his old teacher.

Socrates never wrote a single word down himself. But Plato did, and he wrote a great deal. He wrote dozens of books over his long life. Most of them are written as conversations between people. In them, people talk and ask questions and search for answers together. Socrates is often the main speaker in these books. They are still read all over the world today. Plato is one of the most important thinkers who ever lived.

--

Plato loved stories, and he used them to explain his ideas. He told a story about people in a cave who see only shadows on the wall. He told a story about a magic ring that makes you invisible. He told a story about the soul as a chariot pulled by two horses. And when he was old, he wrote down the story of Atlantis.

{img:plato-writing-scroll-papyrus-oil-lamp-study-night-athens}
He put the story into two of his books, which are called Timaeus and Critias. They are named after the people who speak in them. In these books, the story of Atlantis is told by a man called Critias. Critias heard it from his grandfather, when he was a boy of ten. His grandfather heard it from a great wise man called Solon. And Solon heard it from priests in Egypt, long before. So it was a story passed from mouth to mouth across many years. Let us follow it back, all the way to the beginning.
