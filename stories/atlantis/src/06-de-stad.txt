On the central island, in the middle of all the rings, stood the palace of the kings.

It was built where the house of Poseidon and Cleito had been. Each new king tried to make it even more beautiful than the king before. So over the generations, it grew and grew. Plato says it became a wonder to look at. So big and so beautiful.

--

Around the palace were gardens. Thanks to the hot and cold springs, there was always fresh water. The kings built pools and baths. Some baths were open to the sky. Some were indoors, for winter. There were separate baths for the kings, for ordinary people, for women. And even baths for the horses and the cattle. The water ran from the baths into the gardens. Trees of every kind grew there. Tall and beautiful in the rich wet soil.

On the rings of land, there were temples to many gods. There were gardens and places for exercise. Places where young men ran and wrestled. And on the larger ring, there was a racetrack. A great track for racing horses, going all the way around the island. Remember, Poseidon loves horses. So the people of Atlantis love horses too.

{img:atlantis-horse-racetrack-ring-island-crowds-chariots-mythic}
The guards of the king lived on the rings. The most trusted ones lived on the inner ring, closest to the palace. The kings could always call on them.

--

And beyond the rings, where the great canal reached the sea, there were the harbours. Full of ships. Big warships and trading ships. And there were docks where ships were built and repaired. Plato says the harbours were so crowded that day and night you could hear the noise of people. Shouting and talking and working and trading.

{img:atlantis-harbours-crowded-ships-docks-workers-noise-busy-sea}
Around the whole city, there was one more wall. A great wall that went in a circle around everything, about nine kilometres from the centre. It started at the sea and came back to the sea. Inside it, there were houses. Thousands of houses, packed close together. And people from all over the world.

--

Plato tells it all with such detail. The sizes. The colours. The baths for horses. It feels as if he had been there. Maybe that is why so many people believe it was real. A made-up city usually is not described so carefully. Or maybe Plato was simply a very good storyteller.

{img:student-imagining-city-eyes-closed-lamp-academy-night-dreaming}
At the Academy, the young student from the island has stopped breathing for a moment. He is imagining it all. The rings. The shining walls. The ships. He can almost hear the harbour. He opens his eyes. Around him, the other students are quiet too. The reader goes on.
