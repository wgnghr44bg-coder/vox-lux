Why has the story of Atlantis lasted so long?

Many stories from Plato's time have been forgotten. Many great cities that really existed are only remembered by experts. But everyone has heard of Atlantis. A city that may never have been real. Why?

--

Perhaps because it is a story about loss. Everyone knows that feeling. Something beautiful that is gone forever. A childhood home. A person we loved. A place that has changed. Atlantis is like all of those things together. A beautiful world that sank under the waves. And we can never go back.

Perhaps because it is a story about pride. About what happens when people become too rich and too greedy and forget what is good. Every age has seen that happen. Every age needs that warning. Plato wrote it for Athens. But it fits every time and every place.

{img:sunken-columns-underwater-blue-light-fish-swimming-dreamlike-calm}
And perhaps because it is a mystery. People love a mystery. A question with no answer. Was it real? Where was it? What did Zeus say? Every time we think we know, another possibility appears. Thera. Crete. Helike. Doggerland. Spain. The middle of the ocean. Or only in Plato's head.

--

There is also something true behind it. The sea really does take land. Islands really do sink. Towns really have been swallowed by waves. Coasts really do change. Long ago, people really did live on land that is now under the sea. And they really did have to move, as the water rose.

{img:coastline-changing-sea-rising-old-village-ruins-shoreline-gentle}
In our own time, the sea is rising again. Slowly. Some low islands and coasts are worried about the future. People are building dykes and walls. Planning and preparing. The old story of Atlantis feels a little closer than it used to. A reminder that the sea is patient and powerful. And that we should look after the world we have.

--

But for tonight, Atlantis is a story. A beautiful old story, told by a wise man in a garden under the olive trees. About rings of land and water. About silver temples and golden horses. About kings in dark blue robes, sitting in the dark. And about one day and one night, when the sea came.
