Saïs is a busy city in the green delta of the Nile. Canals run between the fields, full of slow brown water. Palm trees line the river banks, and their leaves rustle in the wind. Farmers lead their donkeys along the paths, loaded with sacks of grain. And in the middle of the city stands a great and very old temple. Its tall stone columns are covered with carvings and painted signs.

Solon sits with the priests in the cool shade of the temple. He wants to show them that the Greeks have old stories too. So he tells them the oldest stories of Greece that he knows. He tells them about the first people who lived there. He tells them about a great flood long ago, and the family who survived it. He counts how many generations have passed since then. He is rather proud of this old history.

--

But one very old priest smiles and laughs gently. Oh Solon, Solon, he says. You Greeks are always children. There is not one old man among you.

Solon is surprised and a little puzzled. What do you mean by that, he asks.

You are young in your hearts and minds, the old priest says kindly. You have no old knowledge, and no truly old stories. Because again and again, great disasters come to your lands. There are floods, and there are times of great heat and drought. They wipe away cities and people and all their writing. Only a few people survive each time. Usually they are the simple shepherds in the mountains, who cannot read or write. And so every time, you must begin again, like children. You forget everything that came before you.

{img:egyptian-priest-reading-old-temple-records-carved-walls-lamp}
But here in Egypt, says the priest, we have been protected. The Nile keeps us safe from both fire and flood. So our temples are very old, and they have stood for a very long time. And we have written down everything that happened, for thousands of years. Our records are carved on the walls and written on rolls of papyrus. And in our old records, there is a story about your own city. It is a story about Athens, a story that you have forgotten.

--

Solon leans forward on the stone bench. Please tell me, he says.

{img:solon-listening-wide-eyes-priest-telling-evening-temple-courtyard}
And the old priest begins his story, slowly and carefully. Nine thousand years ago, he says, your city of Athens was great and noble. And there was another great power in the world at that time. It lay beyond the Pillars of Heracles, out in the great outer ocean. It was an island called Atlantis.

--

The priest speaks slowly and calmly, choosing each word with care. Outside, the evening birds call softly over the river. A young priest walks along the walls and lights the oil lamps one by one. The smell of incense drifts from inside the temple. Solon does not move at all as he listens. He has travelled far and seen many things in his life. But he has never heard a story quite like this one. When the priest is finished, Solon asks him to tell it again, with every detail. And the old priest smiles and begins once more. They sit together until long after dark, while the stars come out over the Nile.
