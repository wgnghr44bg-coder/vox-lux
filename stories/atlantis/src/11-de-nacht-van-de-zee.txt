The end of Atlantis is told in just a few words. In Plato's other book. The one the Egyptian priest told Solon.

Later, says the priest, there were great earthquakes and floods. And in one terrible day and night, the island of Atlantis sank into the sea and disappeared.

--

That is all. One sentence. But people have imagined it ever since. We will imagine it gently. From far away.

Perhaps it began with the earth trembling. A small shaking at first. People stopped and looked at each other. Then went on with their day. Earthquakes happened sometimes. Poseidon was restless. That was all.

Then the sea went strange. It pulled back from the harbours. The ships were left lying on the mud. Fish flapped on the sand. People came down to look. They had never seen anything like it.

{img:people-on-hills-watching-sea-from-distance-dusk-ships-sailing-away}
And the old people remembered the stories. When the sea pulls back, run to the hills. Some ran. Some took to their ships and sailed away. Some stayed and wondered. And then the sea came back. And it did not stop.

--

We will leave Atlantis there. In the dark, under the waves. Its silver temple and its golden statues. Its rings and its canals. Its horses and its ships. Everything gone, in one day and one night. As if it had never been.

{img:calm-sea-morning-after-empty-horizon-gulls-soft-light-peace}
And the next morning, the sun rose over a calm sea. Gulls flew over the water where the island had been. And there was nothing there but waves. Plato says that for a long time afterwards, the sea there was hard to cross. Because of the mud just below the surface. Where the island had sunk.

--

Did anyone survive? Plato does not say. But people have always liked to imagine that some did. That some ships got away in time. That some people fled to the mountains of other lands. And carried with them the memory of their lost home. Stories have been told about it ever since. Of the people who escaped. And of the island under the sea.
