The children of Atlas and his brothers ruled Atlantis for many generations. And slowly, they turned Poseidon's rings into a great city.

The rings of water became canals, wide enough for big ships. The kings had a great canal dug from the sea, straight through the land. It reached all the way to the outer ring of water. This canal was about ninety metres wide, and very deep. Big ships could sail right in from the open ocean. And from the outer ring, they could sail on to the inner rings.

--

The rings of land had strong walls built all around them. Over the rings of water, the kings built wide bridges. And underneath the bridges, there were tunnels cut through the land. These tunnels let ships pass from one ring of water to the next. Each tunnel had a roof over it. So the ships sailed right under the land, as if they were sailing into a cave.

Plato gives us the exact sizes of everything. The outer ring of water was about five hundred metres wide. The ring of land just inside it was the same width. The next ring of water was about three hundred and fifty metres wide. And so it went on, each ring a little smaller, toward the centre. The island in the very middle, with Poseidon's hill on it, was about nine hundred metres across.

{img:ships-sailing-through-covered-tunnel-under-land-ring-atlantis}
Imagine sailing through one of those tunnels on a hot afternoon. The bright sunlight disappears behind you, and it is suddenly cool and dim. The sound of the oars echoes off the stone walls. Water drips from the roof, and the sailors' voices sound strange and hollow. Then there is a circle of light ahead. And the ship glides out into the sunshine again.

The walls around the rings were made of stone of three colours, Plato says. There was white stone, black stone and red stone. It was dug up from under the island itself. The builders used the stones in lovely patterns. Some of the walls were plain and simple. Others were made in stripes and patterns of colour, just for the pleasure of looking at them.

--

And the walls were covered in metal, which shone in the sun. The outer wall was covered with bronze, which glowed like gold. The next wall was covered with tin, silver and grey. And the wall around the central island was covered with a metal called orichalcum. Plato says it glowed like fire, red and gold together.

{img:walls-bronze-tin-orichalcum-gleaming-sunset-rings-water-city}
Imagine sailing into the city from the sea at sunset. You sail down the great canal, with fields and houses on either side. You pass through the first cool tunnel and come out beside the shining bronze wall. You pass through the next tunnel and come out beside the silver wall. And then, straight ahead, you see the central island, with its walls glowing red like fire. Above them, rising on the hill, stands the great temple of Poseidon. No wonder people have dreamed about this city for two thousand years.
