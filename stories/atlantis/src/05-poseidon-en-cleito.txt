In the very beginning, Plato writes, the gods shared out the earth among themselves. Each god received a land of his own to care for. And the god Poseidon received the island of Atlantis.

Poseidon is the Greek god of the sea. He rules the waves and the winds and the storms. The Greeks also believed that he made the earth shake. When the ground trembles, they said, Poseidon is striking it with his great spear. His spear has three points, and it is called a trident. Poseidon also loves horses very much. Some old stories say that he made the very first horse.

--

On the island of Atlantis, near the middle of the coast, there was a wide plain. It was the most beautiful plain in the whole world, Plato says. The soil was rich and dark, and everything grew well there. At the edge of the plain, about nine kilometres from the sea, there was a low hill.

On this hill lived a man and a woman who had been born from the earth itself. They had one daughter, and her name was Cleito. When Cleito was still young, her parents passed away. After that, she lived alone on the hill, among the flowers and the grass.

{img:cleito-young-woman-hill-island-flowers-alone-gentle-light}
Cleito spent her days quietly on the hill. She gathered fruit and flowers, and she watched the birds fly over the plain. In the evenings she sat on the grass and looked out toward the distant sea. She was gentle and kind, and she was not afraid of being alone.

Poseidon saw her one day, and he fell in love with her. He married her, and he wanted to keep her safe always. So he made the hill into a protected place, using his great power. He made rings all around the hill, one inside the other. There were rings of land and rings of water. There were two rings of land and three rings of water. They were perfectly round, like the rings on a target.

--

No ship could reach the hill in the middle. In those days, Plato writes, there were no ships yet, and no sailors. So the hill was safe from everyone in the world. It was like an island inside an island.

Then Poseidon made two springs come bubbling up out of the ground on the hill. One spring gave warm water, and the other gave cold water. And he made the earth around them grow every kind of food in plenty.

{img:two-springs-hot-cold-water-hill-steam-garden-mythic-island}
Poseidon and Cleito had children together, five pairs of twin boys. So they had ten sons in all. When the boys grew up, Poseidon divided the whole island into ten parts. He gave one part to each of his sons. The eldest son got the best part, around the hill with the rings. And he was made king over all his brothers. His name was Atlas. From his name, the whole island was called Atlantis. And the great ocean around it was called the Atlantic.

--

Plato tells us the names of the other sons as well. They are strange old names. Gadeirus was the twin of Atlas, and he ruled the part nearest the Pillars of Heracles. Then there were Ampheres and Euaemon, and Mneseus and Autochthon. There were Elasippus and Mestor, and Azaes and Diaprepes. Plato says these names were translated into Greek from the Egyptian. And the Egyptians had translated them from the language of Atlantis. So these names passed through three languages, across thousands of years. They are like whispers passed down a very long line.
