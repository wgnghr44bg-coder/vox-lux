Tonight we listen to one of the oldest and most famous stories in the world. It is the legend of Atlantis.

It is the story of a great island in the ocean, a very long time ago. It was an island with rich fields, warm springs and wonderful harbours. It had a city built in rings, with rings of land and rings of water. It had temples covered in silver and gold, shining in the sun. It had proud kings and a great many ships. And then, in one day and one night, the sea came. And Atlantis was gone.

--

Is the story true, and did Atlantis really exist? People have asked that question for more than two thousand years. They have searched for the island in every ocean and every sea. They have written thousands and thousands of books about it. And still, nobody knows the answer for sure.

But we do know where the story comes from. It comes from one man, a Greek thinker called Plato. He lived in the city of Athens, about two thousand four hundred years ago. He was the one who wrote the story down, near the end of his long life.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

{img:ancient-athens-evening-acropolis-parthenon-lamps-olive-trees}
Tonight we will travel to Athens, in the time when Plato was alive. We will sit in his garden school, where young people come to learn and think. We will hear how the story of Atlantis travelled from priests in Egypt to a wise man of Athens. We will walk through the city of Atlantis as Plato describes it, ring by ring. We will meet its kings and visit its great silver temple. We will imagine a day in the life of a girl who lives by its harbour. And then, gently, we will hear how the story ends.

{img:plato-old-teacher-beard-garden-academy-students-listening-shade}
After that, we will go looking for Atlantis ourselves, through history and through science. We will visit a volcano in the Aegean Sea that once broke apart. We will walk through a buried town full of painted swallows and dolphins. We will find a Greek town that sank in one night, in Plato's own lifetime. And we will travel to a green land under the North Sea, where people once walked and fished.

Along the way, we will meet a young student at Plato's school. He has come from a small rocky island far away, and he loves the sea. He is shy and quiet, and he listens to everything. He will hear the story of Atlantis for the very first time, just as we do tonight. And he will wonder about it, just as people have wondered ever since.

--

{img:sea-waves-dusk-calm-horizon-ancient-ship-sail-distant-mystery}
So let us begin in Athens, on a warm summer evening. The sun is setting slowly over the sea. The marble temples on the hill glow pink and gold in the last light. Olive trees whisper softly in the evening breeze. Swallows dart over the rooftops, and somewhere a donkey brays. And in a quiet garden outside the city walls, an old man is about to tell a story.
