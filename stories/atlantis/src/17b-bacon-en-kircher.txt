Long before Donnelly, people had already been dreaming about Atlantis. And some of them made beautiful things from those dreams.

About three hundred and fifty years ago, a German priest and scholar called Athanasius Kircher drew a map of Atlantis. He was a man who wanted to know everything. Volcanoes and magnets and music and ancient languages. He had himself lowered into the crater of a smoking volcano in Italy, just to look inside.

--

His map of Atlantis is famous. It shows a big island in the middle of the ocean, between Africa and Spain on one side, and America on the other. The strange thing is that the map is drawn upside down. South is at the top. People looking at it today often turn it around to understand it. It is a beautiful map. With little ships and waves and sea monsters. But it was all imagination.

{img:new-atlantis-bacon-island-utopia-scholars-college-ships-engraving}
A few years before that, an English thinker called Francis Bacon wrote a short book called New Atlantis. It was not about the old Atlantis. It was about a new imaginary island in the Pacific Ocean. A perfect place where everyone was wise and good. And where scientists worked together in a great college to understand nature. To invent new things for the good of everyone.

--

Bacon's island had amazing inventions. Machines to fly and to go under water. Ways to make the weather. Ways to talk across long distances. In the year 1627, that all sounded like magic. Today, much of it is real. Bacon dreamed of a world where science would make life better. And his little book inspired real scientists. Some say it helped to start one of the first great science societies in England.

{img:scholars-gathered-early-science-society-instruments-candles-room}
So Atlantis did not only inspire dreams of the past. It inspired dreams of the future too. Of better worlds. Of new knowledge. Of places where people live wisely and well. Plato would have liked that. Because in the end, that was what his story was really about. Not gold and ships. But how to live well. And what happens when we forget.
