South of Santorini lies a big island called Crete. And on Crete, long ago, lived a people we now call the Minoans.

The Minoans built great palaces, and the biggest one was at Knossos. It was huge and full of twists and turns. It had hundreds of rooms, spread over several floors. There were courtyards and staircases, long corridors and wells of light. There were storerooms with giant clay jars, taller than a man. The palace was so complicated that some people think it started the old Greek story of the labyrinth. That was a great maze where nobody could ever find their way out.

--

The Minoans were a people of the sea, like the people of Atlantis. Their ships sailed everywhere, to Egypt and to the islands and to Greece. They grew rich from their trade across the water. Their towns did not have big walls around them. Perhaps they did not need them. The sea was their wall, and their ships kept them safe.

The Minoans also loved bulls very much. Bulls appear everywhere in their art. There are paintings of young men and women leaping over running bulls. They grab the horns and somersault right over the bull's back. Nobody knows if this really happened, or if it was a kind of dream. But it looks amazing in the paintings. Bulls' horns were carved in stone along the palace walls. And drinking cups were made in the shape of bulls' heads.

{img:minoan-bull-leaping-fresco-acrobat-somersault-bull-colours-knossos}
Do you remember the ceremony of the kings of Atlantis? The bulls wandered freely in the temple grounds. And the kings caught them without any weapons. Some people think this is a faint memory of the Minoans and their bulls.

--

The Minoans were also wonderful painters, full of joy. Their walls show dolphins and fish, lilies and birds. They show ladies with curly hair and fine, colourful dresses. They show princes wearing crowns of feathers. Their art is full of nature and movement and happiness.

In the palace kitchens, cooks baked bread and boiled lentils in big pots. Servants carried jars of olive oil and honey down the long corridors. Children played in the courtyards, and old men sat in the sun. Life in the palace was busy and cheerful, most days.

{img:minoan-dolphins-fresco-blue-sea-fish-queens-room-knossos-light}
And then, slowly, the Minoans faded away. After the eruption of Thera, their world was badly damaged. Ash fell on their fields, and big waves reached their coasts. Many of their ships may have been lost. They went on for a while, but they grew weaker. Later, people from mainland Greece took over their palaces. And slowly, the whole Minoan world disappeared. It was forgotten for three thousand years.

--

Then, about a hundred and twenty years ago, a British archaeologist called Arthur Evans dug at Knossos. He found the great palace and its beautiful paintings. He gave the people their name, the Minoans, after a king in an old Greek story. And people began to wonder about them. Could the Minoans have been the people of Atlantis? They were a rich sea people, living on an island. They had bulls and palaces and many ships. And they were struck by a great disaster from the sea. Some people still believe it today.
