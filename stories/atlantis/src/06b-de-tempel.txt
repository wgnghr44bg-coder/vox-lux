At the very centre of everything stood the temple of Poseidon.

It was built on the old hill where Cleito had lived. It was a holy place. Ordinary people could not go in. There was a smaller shrine for Cleito and Poseidon too. Surrounded by a wall of gold. That was where the ten sons were born. Every year, people brought gifts there from all ten kingdoms.

--

The great temple was huge. About one hundred and eighty metres long and ninety metres wide. And very tall. Plato says it looked a little foreign. Not quite like a Greek temple. More strange and grand.

The outside of the temple was covered with silver. All of it. Except the pointed decorations on the roof. Those were covered with gold. So in the sun, the temple shone silver and gold on top of its hill. You could see it from far out at sea.

{img:temple-interior-ivory-ceiling-gold-poseidon-statue-chariot-horses}
Inside, the ceiling was made of ivory. White and smooth. Decorated with gold and silver and orichalcum. The walls and the columns and the floor were covered with orichalcum. Glowing red and gold.

--

And in the middle stood a statue of Poseidon. Made of gold. So tall that his head touched the ceiling. He stood in a chariot pulled by six winged horses. Around him, riding on dolphins, were a hundred sea nymphs. Spirits of the sea. And around the outside of the temple stood golden statues of all the kings and queens of Atlantis. Rows and rows of them.

There was also a great altar. Fit for such a temple, Plato says. And the palace beside it was just as grand.

{img:golden-statues-kings-queens-around-temple-silver-gleaming-dusk}
It is a dazzling picture. Silver and gold and ivory. Golden horses with wings. A god whose head touches the ceiling. It sounds like a dream. And perhaps that is exactly what it is. A dream of a perfect, wealthy, shining city. Too perfect, maybe. Because in Plato's stories, things that are too perfect and too proud often come to a bad end.

--

The young student at the Academy tries to picture the golden Poseidon. Six winged horses. A hundred sea nymphs on dolphins. He has seen the great temple of Athena in Athens, with its tall statue of gold and ivory. He thinks Poseidon's statue must have been like that, only bigger. Much bigger. He shakes his head in wonder. Could people really have made such a thing?
