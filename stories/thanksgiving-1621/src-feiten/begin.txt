Close your eyes, and you can probably see it. A long table under the autumn trees. A golden roast turkey in the middle, a pumpkin pie, a bowl of cranberry sauce. People in black clothes, with tall hats and shiny buckles, sitting side by side with their new neighbours. It's a lovely picture. And almost every detail in it is wrong.

The real harvest celebration of 1621 was smaller, messier and much more surprising. It lasted three days. There were more Wampanoag guests than English hosts. The main dish was probably venison, a gift from the hunters. And nobody called it Thanksgiving at all. So what was the first Thanksgiving really like? Who was there, what did they eat, and what happened afterwards? Let's walk through that autumn together, slowly, and see.
