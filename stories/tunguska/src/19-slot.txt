We have come a long way tonight.

We began in the great forest, with its mosses and its larches and its slow, quiet rivers.
We met the Evenki, with their reindeer, and their tents, and their old stories.
We looked at the world of 1908, with its railways and newspapers and gentle summer gardens.

We followed a small piece of rock, on a long journey through space, which came at last to the Earth, on a bright June morning.
We heard how the sky flashed, and the forest shook, and the people of Vanavara ran out of their houses.
We saw how the sound travelled around the world, and how, in the nights after, the sky glowed over Europe, pale and silver and still.

We met Leonid Kulik, who walked into the swamp, and looked at a flattened forest, and began to ask questions.
We learned how scientists, over many years, with patience and care, put the story together.
We heard about computers that can rebuild the explosion, and about spacecraft that can move an asteroid, and about a day, in the spring of 2029, when a small, bright star will pass quietly over our heads.

And we learned that the forest has healed.

{img:quiet-forest-path-soft-light-ferns}
It seems to me that there is a gentle lesson in all this.

The Earth is very large, and it is very old.
It has seen many things, and it will see many more.
Sometimes, something very big happens, and it feels like the end of everything.
And then, slowly, the moss grows over it, and the birds come back, and the world carries on.

We are small, and the universe is vast, and that might seem a frightening thing.
But there is also a comfort in it.
We are part of something very large, and very patient, and very beautiful.
We live on a planet that has turned, and healed, and bloomed, for billions of years.
And we are here, tonight, thinking about it, listening to a story, in the quiet.

There are people who watch the sky for us.
There are people who care for the forests.
There are people who remember, and who write things down, and who pass on what they know.
We are not alone in looking after this world.

So, if you are still awake, let your thoughts grow slow and soft.
Imagine that you are standing at the edge of the taiga, in the long pale light of a summer night.
The air is warm, and smells of pine and moss.
The river is murmuring, somewhere near.
The sky above you is a deep, clear, gentle blue, with a few faint stars.

{img:final-stars-over-still-forest-lake}
Nothing is hurrying.
Nothing is asking anything of you.

The trees stand around you, tall and calm.
The ground is soft, and the night is long.
Far away, beyond the hills, a little light is spreading along the edge of the sky, and soon the morning will come.
But not yet.
For now, there is only the quiet, and the stars, and the slow, steady breathing of the forest.

Thank you for spending this time with me.
I hope the story has been a gentle one.
If you enjoyed it, you are always welcome to come back, and listen to another.

Goodnight.
Sleep well.
And may your dreams be as wide and as peaceful as the great northern forest.
