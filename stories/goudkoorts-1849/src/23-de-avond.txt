So we leave the gold fields of California tonight, in the autumn of 1849, as the evening falls.

The sun is going down behind the hills, and the valley is filling with golden light. The river glows red and gold, rushing softly over the rocks. The miners are putting down their picks and shovels, and walking slowly up the bank to their cabins and tents. Smoke rises from the chimneys. And the smell of coffee and frying bacon drifts through the camp.

--

In the company's cabin, the fire is burning in the stone fireplace. Dr. Hale is sitting at the table with his notebook, writing down the day's gold, a few pinches of dust. One of the men is frying flapjacks in a pan. Another is mending his boots by the fire. The fiddle player is tuning his fiddle. Outside, the mules are munching hay, and an owl is calling in the pines.

{img:log-cabin-interior-evening-miners-fire-fiddle-notebook-lamp-cozy}
Samuel is sitting on his bunk, writing a letter to Hannah by the light of a candle. He writes slowly and carefully. He tells her about the cabin and the river, and the little gold they have found. He tells her about the carpentry he has been doing in the evenings. He tells her that he misses her and Ruth every single day. And he tells her that Eli is well, and growing taller, and working hard.

--

Eli is sitting outside the cabin door, on a log, with his drawing book on his knees. He is drawing the river in the sunset, for Ruth. He draws the cabins and the pine trees, the mules and the hills. He draws the first stars coming out above the valley. And at the bottom, he writes: Our camp, Rough and Ready Bar, California. Your brother, Eli.

{img:boy-sitting-log-drawing-sunset-river-valley-stars-coming-out}
Down the river, he can hear the guitar of Tomás's father, playing a slow old song from Sonora. The music drifts softly up the valley, mixing with the sound of the river. Eli stops drawing and listens. Then the fiddle in his own cabin starts to play too, a different song. And for a little while, the two songs drift together over the dark river, in the warm evening air.

--

Far away, on the other side of the continent, the same stars are shining over a little wooden house in Massachusetts. A mother is sitting by the kitchen fire, reading a letter for the third time. A girl is asleep upstairs, with a box of drawings beside her bed. And the sea is whispering softly in the harbour, as it always does.

The stars come out, more and more, over the California hills. The fire burns low in the cabin. One by one, the miners go to sleep in their bunks. Eli comes inside and climbs into his bunk, under Ruth's drawing of the ship. He closes his eyes. And the river goes on singing over the rocks, through the long, quiet night.
