On a cold morning in February 1849, the family travels to Boston. The ship is waiting at the wharf. It is called the Orion, a wooden sailing ship with three masts. It is not new, and not very big. But it is strong, and it has made long voyages before.

The wharf is crowded with people. There are hundreds of men going to California, with their families come to see them off. There are piles of luggage and boxes of tools everywhere. Sailors are loading barrels and crates onto the ship. A band is playing cheerful music. And snow is falling softly on everything.

--

Samuel and Eli carry their bags up the gangplank onto the ship. They find their bunks, in a crowded room below deck. Then they come back up to say goodbye. Hannah hugs Eli for a long time. She tells him to be good, and to listen to his father, and to keep warm. She gives him a little Bible, with her name written inside. Then she hugs Samuel. Two years, she says. Not a day more. He promises.

{img:mother-hugging-son-ship-gangplank-little-sister-crying-1849}
Ruth stands holding her mother's hand. She does not cry, but her lip is shaking. Eli kneels down in front of her. He tells her that he will bring her back a gold nugget, as big as an egg. She laughs a little, through her tears. Then she gives him one more drawing. It is a picture of a ship, with two little figures standing on the deck. She has written underneath: Come home soon.

--

The bell on the ship rings. All the passengers must go on board. The gangplank is pulled up. The sailors untie the ropes. Slowly, the ship moves away from the wharf. The band plays a last song. The people on the wharf wave their hats and handkerchiefs. On the ship, the men wave back. Eli finds his mother and sister in the crowd and waves until he cannot see them anymore.

{img:ship-sailing-out-boston-harbour-winter-grey-sea-snow-receding-town}
The ship sails out of Boston harbour, past the islands and the lighthouse. The town gets smaller and smaller behind them. The wind is cold and the sea is grey. Many of the passengers stay on deck for a long time, watching the land disappear. Some of them are quiet. Some of them are laughing and joking, excited about the adventure. Samuel stands at the rail beside Eli, with his hand on his son's shoulder.

--

At home, Hannah and Ruth take the train back to their town. The house seems very quiet and empty without Samuel and Eli. That evening, they eat supper alone at the kitchen table. Hannah lights the lamp and puts a map of the world on the table. She traces the route of the ship with her finger. Down the coast of America, across the equator, around Cape Horn, and up the other side to California. It is a very long way.

{img:mother-daughter-kitchen-table-world-map-tracing-route-lamp-evening}
Ruth asks how long it will take. Five months, perhaps six, says Hannah. That is the whole spring and the whole summer. Ruth decides she will draw a picture every day, of what she thinks Eli is doing. She will keep them all in a box, and give them to him when he comes home. Hannah thinks that is a lovely idea. She says she will write a letter every week, even though the letters will take months to arrive. And that night, she writes the first one.

On the ship, Samuel and Eli unpack their bags in their bunks. Their bunks are one above the other, in a corner of the steerage. Samuel takes the bottom one, and Eli the top one. Eli pins Ruth's drawing to the wooden wall beside his pillow. Then he takes out his grandfather's compass and looks at the needle. It points north, toward home. And then the ship turns, and the needle swings slowly around.

In the weeks after they leave, Hannah keeps busy. She takes in more sewing, to earn a little extra money. She looks after the cow and the chickens, and the garden in the spring. Ruth helps her, and goes to school, and draws her pictures every evening. The neighbours are very kind. They come to visit, and bring cakes and news. But the house still feels empty without Samuel and Eli.
