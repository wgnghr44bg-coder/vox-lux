Tonight we travel back about one hundred and seventy-five years. We go to California, on the far western edge of America. The year is 1849. And all over the world, people are talking about one thing: gold.

--

It began about a year earlier, on a cold January morning in 1848. In a quiet valley in the foothills of the Sierra Nevada mountains, a group of men were building a sawmill. It stood beside a fast, clear river, called the American River. The man in charge of building it was a carpenter called James Marshall. Every morning, he walked along the channel that carried water to the mill, to check it.

On that morning, he saw something shining in the cold water. He bent down and picked it up. It was a little flake of yellow metal, about the size of a small pea. He picked up a few more. He bit one of them, and it was soft. He hammered one with a stone, and it went flat, but did not break. He thought it might be gold.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

{img:gold-flakes-in-hand-cold-river-water-pan-sunlight-close}
It was gold. The men at the mill tried to keep it a secret. But secrets like that do not stay secret for long. Within a few months, people from all over California were rushing to the rivers to look for gold. And by the end of 1848, the news had reached the rest of America, and the rest of the world. In 1849, hundreds of thousands of people set off for California, hoping to become rich.

These people became known as the forty-niners, after the year 1849. They came from the eastern states of America, from Mexico and Chile, from China and Hawaii, from Europe and Australia. They came by land and by sea. Most of them were young men. Most of them had never seen a gold mine in their lives. And most of them did not become rich. But together, they changed California forever.

Tonight we will follow a father and his son from a small town in Massachusetts, on the east coast of America. We will sail with them on a long voyage, all the way around the bottom of South America. We will arrive with them in the noisy tent city of San Francisco. We will travel up the rivers to the gold fields, and live in a mining camp. We will learn how to wash gold from the sand of the rivers. And we will see what they found in the end, which was not quite what they expected.

--

There will be some exciting moments in this story, in storms at sea and in the hard winter in the mountains. But mostly, it is a story about ordinary people, far from home, working hard and hoping. It is a story about friendship and letters, about patience and luck. And it is a story about a family that missed each other very much.

{img:father-son-1849-standing-ship-deck-looking-west-sunset-hope}
So let us begin in a quiet town on the coast of Massachusetts, in the cold winter of 1848. Snow lies on the roofs and the fields. The harbour is grey and still. And in a little wooden house near the church, a family is sitting around the fire, reading a newspaper. The newspaper says there is gold in California. Gold in the rivers, gold in the hills, gold for anyone who goes to find it. And the father of the family puts down the newspaper, and looks at his son.

The valley where the gold was found was owned by a man called John Sutter. He had come to California from Switzerland, and built a great farm and a fort near the river. He wanted the sawmill to cut wood for his buildings. When he heard about the gold, he was not happy. He knew that people would come and take over his land. And he was right.

In 1849, most people in the eastern states had never been to California. They had only read about it in books and newspapers. They thought of it as a faraway land of sunshine and oranges, of mountains and the Pacific Ocean. Now they also thought of it as a land of gold. And thousands of them decided to see it for themselves.
