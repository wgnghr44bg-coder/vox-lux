Let us meet the family from Massachusetts. They live in a small town on the coast, not far from Boston.

The father is called Samuel Turner. He is about forty years old, a carpenter. He builds houses and barns, and makes doors and windows and furniture. He is a careful, steady man, good with his hands. He is not rich, but he earns enough to keep his family. He has a workshop behind the house, where he works in the winter.

The mother is called Hannah. She is a strong, practical woman, with quick hands and a warm smile. She runs the house and the garden, and keeps chickens and a cow. She sews all the family's clothes. She also takes in sewing for neighbours, to earn a little extra. She can read and write well, and she loves to read the newspapers and books from the town library.

--

They have two children. The son is called Eli, and he is fourteen. He is tall and thin, with sandy hair and freckles. He has helped his father in the workshop since he was small. He can use a saw and a plane, and he knows all the different kinds of wood. He is curious and adventurous. He has read every story about faraway places that he can find. And when he reads about the gold in California, his eyes light up.

{img:boy-fourteen-1848-reading-newspaper-by-fire-excited-father-watching}
The daughter is called Ruth, and she is ten. She is quieter than her brother, with dark braids and serious eyes. She loves to draw, and she draws everything: the birds, the boats in the harbour, the cow in the barn. She is very close to her brother. When she hears that he might go to California, she does not say anything. But she goes up to her room and cries a little.

--

The family is not poor, but life is not easy. Samuel has had less work this year. Some of the families in town cannot afford to build new houses. Prices are going up. Hannah worries about money. She counts the coins in the jar on the kitchen shelf every week. When Samuel reads about the gold in California, he thinks about all the things he could do with a little more money. He could build a bigger house. He could send the children to a good school. He could stop worrying.

{img:kitchen-1848-new-england-family-table-oil-lamp-discussion-evening}
For weeks, the family talks about it in the evenings. Samuel wants to go, and to take Eli with him. Eli is old enough, he says, and strong, and he can help. Hannah is not sure. California is so far away, and the journey is long and hard. What if something happens to them? What if they find nothing? But in the end, she agrees. Two years, she says. Not a day more. And you must write to me every week.

--

Samuel joins a company of men from the town, who are going to California together. There are twelve of them. They each pay a share of the costs. They buy tickets on a ship from Boston, sailing around Cape Horn. They buy tools and tents and food for the journey. They agree to share everything they find in California equally. The leader of the company is a doctor called Dr. Hale, a kind and sensible man.

{img:group-men-1849-company-signing-agreement-table-tools-piled-town-hall}
The ship will leave Boston in February 1849. That gives the family a few more weeks together. They spend them quietly. Samuel finishes his last jobs in town. Hannah sews warm shirts and thick socks for her husband and son. Eli packs and unpacks his little bag again and again. And Ruth draws a picture of the family, all four of them, standing in front of the house. She gives it to Eli, to take with him to California.

On Sundays, the family goes to the white wooden church in the middle of the town. After church, they walk along the harbour, looking at the boats. In summer, they have picnics on the beach and pick blueberries on the hills. In winter, they go skating on the frozen pond. It is a simple, quiet life. And in the evenings, Hannah reads aloud to the family from a book, by the light of the oil lamp.

Grandfather Turner, Samuel's father, was a sailor who sailed all over the world. He died before Eli was born. But Samuel has his old sea chest in the attic. Inside, there is a compass, a spyglass and a few old charts. Eli loves to take them out and look at them. He decides to take the old compass with him to California, for luck.
