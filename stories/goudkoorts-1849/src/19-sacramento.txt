By the summer of 1850, Samuel has made a decision. He talks it over with Eli, and with Dr. Hale, and then he tells the company.

He has seen that the miners in the company are earning only a little from the gold. After paying for food and supplies, there is only a small amount left each month. But as a carpenter, he can earn much more. The towns of California are growing very fast. Everyone needs houses, shops and warehouses. And there are not enough carpenters to build them.

--

So Samuel and Eli leave the company, with their share of the gold, and go down the river to Sacramento. The other men understand. They shake hands and wish them luck. Dr. Hale gives Eli a little book about plants and animals, as a goodbye present. Tomás comes to say goodbye too. The two boys promise to write to each other. Eli gives Tomás his pocket knife. Tomás gives Eli a little carved wooden horse.

{img:boy-saying-goodbye-mexican-friend-gifts-knife-wooden-horse-camp}
In Sacramento, Samuel finds work at once. There is so much building to do. He builds houses and shops, hotels and stables. He builds the wooden pavements, raised above the muddy streets. He hires other carpenters to help him. Eli works beside him every day, learning more and more. Soon, Samuel has his own little building business, with his name painted on a sign.

--

Sacramento is growing very fast. Every week, new buildings go up. There are banks and hotels, churches and theatres, shops of every kind. Steamboats come up the river from San Francisco every day, bringing people and goods. The streets are busy and noisy and full of life. But the town is also built of wood, and close to the river. In the years ahead, it will face both fire and flood. And each time, the people will rebuild it.

{img:carpenter-father-son-building-wooden-house-frame-sacramento-sunny}
Samuel and Eli build a little house of their own, on a quiet street near the edge of the town. It has two rooms and a kitchen, and a porch at the front. There is a little garden at the back, where Eli plants some vegetables. Samuel builds it very carefully, with the best wood he can find. He wants it to be just right. Because he has written to Hannah, and asked her to come.

--

In his letter, Samuel tells Hannah that they will not become rich from gold. But they can have a good life in California. There is plenty of work for a carpenter, and good pay. The weather is warm and sunny most of the year. The land is rich and beautiful. He has saved enough money for her and Ruth to come, by ship and across Panama. He asks her to sell the house in Massachusetts, and to come.

{img:small-wooden-house-porch-garden-sacramento-1850-father-son-painting-fence}
Every evening, after work, Samuel and Eli work a little more on the house. They paint the walls and the fence. They build a table and chairs, and beds for everyone. Eli builds a little shelf for Ruth's drawings. They plant a rose bush beside the porch, because Hannah loves roses. And every evening, they look down the road toward the river, and imagine the day when the boat will come.

Eli goes to a little school in Sacramento in the mornings, for a few months. It is in a wooden building, with a teacher from New York. There are children from many different families: American, Mexican, German, Irish. Eli is older than most of them. But he likes learning again. In the afternoons, he works with his father. And in the evenings, he reads the little book about plants and animals from Dr. Hale.

In the evenings, the people of Sacramento walk along the river and visit each other. There are band concerts on summer evenings and church socials on Sundays. Samuel and Eli make friends with their neighbours, a family from Missouri who run a bakery. The baker's wife invites them for supper every Sunday. She says two men living alone need a proper meal at least once a week.
