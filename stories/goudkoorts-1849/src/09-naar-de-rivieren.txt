To reach the gold fields, the company must travel inland, up the rivers. They buy tickets on a small boat going up the Sacramento River.

The boat is crowded with men, all going to the gold fields. They sit on their bags and boxes, on the deck and below. The boat travels slowly up the river, through wide flat country. On both sides, there are marshes full of tall reeds, called tules. Thousands of birds live there: ducks and geese, herons and cranes. In some places, there are great oak trees along the banks, with grassy meadows under them.

--

After a day or two, they reach a new town called Sacramento. It is the gateway to the gold fields. A year ago, there was almost nothing here. Now there are tents and wooden buildings, shops and hotels, crowded together along the river. Boats unload goods on the riverbank. Wagons and mules carry them away into the hills. Everyone is busy, buying and selling.

{img:sacramento-1849-riverbank-tents-wooden-stores-mule-trains-dusty}
From Sacramento, the company sets off on foot toward the hills. They buy two mules to carry their tools and supplies. Mules are strong and sure-footed, and they can carry heavy loads up steep paths. The men walk beside them. It is hot and dusty, because it is late summer, and there has been no rain for months. The grass is dry and golden. The road is crowded with men going up to the mines, and some men coming back.

--

As they climb into the foothills, the land changes. There are rolling hills covered with oak trees and pine trees. There are deep valleys with rivers running through them. The air is fresher, and smells of pine. In the distance, they can see the high mountains of the Sierra Nevada, with a little snow still on their tops. It is beautiful country.

{img:foothills-sierra-nevada-1849-oak-pine-trail-men-mules-walking-dusty}
Along the way, they meet men coming back from the mines. Some of them look happy, with heavy bags of gold dust. Some of them look tired and thin and disappointed. Some say there is gold everywhere. Some say all the good places are already taken. The company does not know whom to believe. But they keep walking.

--

At night, they camp beside the road. They light a small fire and cook beans and bacon. They sleep on the ground, wrapped in their blankets. The stars are very bright up here, away from the cities. Coyotes howl far away in the hills. Eli lies awake for a while, listening. He thinks about home, and about the long journey they have made. And he thinks that tomorrow, perhaps, he will see gold in a river for the first time.

{img:campfire-night-foothills-men-sleeping-blankets-mules-stars-coyote}
After four days of walking, they come to a river in a deep green valley. It is a branch of the American River, not far from where the gold was first found. There are many tents and cabins along its banks already. Men are working in the river, standing in the water, bent over their pans. Dr. Hale looks around and says: This is the place. And the company begins to unpack.

On the river boat, Eli sits at the front and watches the water. Fish jump out of the river, catching flies. Great flocks of geese fly overhead, honking. On the banks, he sees deer drinking at the water's edge. They lift their heads and watch the boat go by, and then go back to drinking. Eli has never seen so much wild country.
