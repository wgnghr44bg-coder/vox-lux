In March, the rain finally stops, and spring comes to the foothills. It is beautiful. The hills turn bright green, and wildflowers bloom everywhere: orange poppies and blue lupins, yellow buttercups and purple brodiaea. The river goes down, and becomes clear and sparkling again. Birds sing in the oak trees. And the miners go back to work.

The flood has changed the river. It has moved rocks and gravel, and opened up new places. Some miners find that their claims have been washed away. Others find that the flood has brought new gravel, with new gold in it. Everyone is digging again, full of hope.

--

One morning in April, Eli is working at the river, filling buckets of gravel for the rocker. He is digging near a big rock, where the flood has left a deep crack. He pushes his shovel down into the gravel at the bottom of the crack. It hits something hard. He thinks it is a stone. He reaches down into the cold water with his hand to pull it out.

{img:boy-hand-holding-gold-nugget-river-water-dripping-sunlight-amazed}
It is not a stone. It is heavy, much too heavy for its size. It is rough and knobbly, about the size of a walnut. He rubs it on his shirt, and it shines. Yellow. Bright yellow. Eli stares at it. His heart is pounding. He opens his mouth, but nothing comes out. Then he shouts, as loud as he can. Father! Father!

--

Samuel comes running from the rocker, and the other men come too. They crowd around Eli. He holds out his hand. In his palm lies a gold nugget, glinting in the sunshine. Dr. Hale takes it and weighs it in his hand. Then he takes out his little scales and weighs it properly. It weighs nearly three ounces. That is worth about fifty dollars, more than the whole company has found in a month.

{img:miners-crowding-around-boy-nugget-scales-cheering-river-bank}
The men cheer and slap Eli on the back. Samuel hugs his son tightly. Everyone wants to hold the nugget and look at it. Miners from other claims come to see it. It is the biggest nugget found at Rough and Ready Bar for weeks. That evening, the company has a celebration in the cabin. They cook a special dinner, with fresh beef and canned peaches from the store. And they drink a toast to Eli.

--

According to the company's agreement, the nugget belongs to all of them. They will share its value equally. But the other men have a meeting. And they decide something kind. They tell Eli that he may keep a small piece of it for himself. They say he has worked harder than anyone, carrying water all through the year. Dr. Hale cuts off a tiny corner of the nugget with a knife. He gives it to Eli.

{img:boy-holding-small-gold-piece-cabin-firelight-thinking-of-home}
That night, Eli sits by the fire and looks at his little piece of gold. It is smaller than a pea, but it shines in the firelight. He thinks about the promise he made to Ruth, at the wharf in Boston. A nugget as big as an egg, he had said. This one is much smaller. But it is real. And he found it himself, in a California river. He wraps it carefully in a piece of cloth. He will keep it for her, until he sees her again.

The news of the nugget spreads quickly through the camp, and the next camp, and the next. By the end of the week, people are saying that a boy at Rough and Ready Bar has found a nugget as big as a fist. By the end of the month, they say it was as big as a hen's egg. Eli laughs when he hears this. That is how stories grow in the gold fields, says Dr. Hale.
