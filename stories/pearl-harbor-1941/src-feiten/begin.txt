In 1941, the island of Oahu was a quiet corner of the Pacific. It had green mountains, sugar fields, a relaxed town called Honolulu, and a great naval harbour full of grey ships. Thousands of young sailors lived there, alongside families from Hawaii, Japan, China, the Philippines and the mainland. In this story, we will look at everyday life on the island in the months before the seventh of December, at the attack itself, told gently, and at what came after.
