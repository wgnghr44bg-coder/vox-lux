Picture yourself on the deck of a big white passenger ship, early on a soft morning in 1941. Ahead of you, green mountains rise out of the sea, their tops wrapped in cloud. A brown volcanic crater stands at the end of a long golden beach. Boys swim out to the ship and dive for the coins the passengers throw. And on the pier, beside a tall white clock tower, a band is playing slow Hawaiian music.

As you walk down the gangway, someone places a ring of fresh flowers around your neck. It smells sweet and feels cool on your skin. Aloha, they say.
