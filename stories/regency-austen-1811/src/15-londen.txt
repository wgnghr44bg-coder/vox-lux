In the winter, Mr Hartley has to go to London for a few days, on business for the church. And this time, he takes Anne with him. It is her first visit to London. They travel by stagecoach, and stay with an old friend of Mr Hartley's, who lives in a quiet street near Russell Square. For Anne, it is like entering another world.

--

London in 1811 is the biggest city in the world. Almost a million people live there. It is enormous, noisy, crowded, dirty and exciting. The streets are full of carriages, carts, horses, sedan chairs and people on foot. Street sellers shout their wares. Church bells ring. Smoke from thousands of coal fires hangs over the city, making the air grey and foggy, and turning the buildings black.

But there are also beautiful parts of London. The West End, where the rich and fashionable live, is full of elegant squares, with gardens in the middle, surrounded by tall, handsome houses. There are wide streets, with the finest shops in the world. And there is something very new, which amazes Anne. In some streets, the lamps are lit with gas. Bright, clear lights, much brighter than the old oil lamps, glowing in the winter dusk.

{img:gas-lamps-pall-mall-1811-evening-glow-carriages-new-invention}
The first public street with gas lighting in London is Pall Mall, lit up only a few years before, in 1807. People came from all over the city to see it. It seemed like magic. A light that came through pipes, under the street, without oil or candles. Some people were frightened of it. They thought the pipes would explode, or that the gas was poisonous. But soon, more and more streets were lit by gas. And London became a city of light, at night.

Anne and her father walk along the fashionable streets. Bond Street, with its jewellers and its tailors. Oxford Street, which is more than two kilometres long, and full of shops of every kind, with big glass windows, lit up at night, displaying beautiful things. Silks and muslins, china and glass, books and prints, hats and gloves. Anne has never seen so many beautiful things in her life.

--

{img:hyde-park-1811-fashionable-carriages-riders-ladies-strolling-winter-sun}
In the afternoon, they go to Hyde Park, where the fashionable people of London go to ride and drive, between five and six o'clock. It is a great parade. Ladies in elegant carriages, with footmen behind. Gentlemen on fine horses. Dandies in their perfect coats and cravats, walking slowly, looking at everyone. It is like a great outdoor drawing room. Everyone is looking at everyone else.

They visit a famous shop called Gunter's, in Berkeley Square. It is a confectioner's shop, famous for its ices. Even in winter, people come to eat ices at Gunter's. Ladies sit in their carriages outside, and the waiters run across the street to bring them their ices. Anne eats a lemon ice, and a little cake. Her father has a cup of chocolate. It is a wonderful treat.

{img:hatchards-bookshop-piccadilly-1811-shelves-gentlemen-reading-fire}
They also visit a bookshop, in Piccadilly, called Hatchard's, which is one of the most famous bookshops in London. There are newspapers on a table, and a fire, and benches, where gentlemen sit and read. Mr Hartley buys a book of sermons, and Anne buys a book of poems. They go to see the great new British Museum, with its collection of ancient statues and curiosities. And on Sunday, they go to a service in Westminster Abbey, where the singing is so beautiful that Anne has tears in her eyes.

--

London is exciting. But after a week, Anne is tired. Tired of the noise, the smoke, the crowds and the rush. She longs for the quiet lanes of Hampshire, the orchard, and the sound of the church clock. When the stagecoach finally leaves London, and the green fields appear again on both sides of the road, Anne leans back in her seat and sighs with happiness. London, she thinks, is a wonderful place to visit. But a quiet village is the place to live.
