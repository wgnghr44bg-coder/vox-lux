The clothes of the Regency are some of the most elegant in history. After the stiff, wide, heavy dresses of the eighteenth century, the fashion has completely changed. The ladies of 1811 wear light, simple, flowing dresses, inspired by the statues of ancient Greece and Rome. And the gentlemen wear dark coats, white shirts and tall hats.

--

A lady's dress in 1811 has a very high waist, just under the bust, and a long, straight skirt, that falls to the ankles. The sleeves are often short and puffed, for evening, and long and narrow, for day. The neckline is low and square for evening, and filled in with a little lace collar for day. The most fashionable fabric is muslin, a thin, soft cotton, from India. White muslin is the height of elegance. It looks like the white marble of the ancient statues.

But white muslin is thin, and England is cold. So the ladies wear many layers. Under the dress, there is a chemise, a long linen undergarment, and a short corset, called stays, which is much softer than the corsets of earlier times. Over the dress, they wear a short little jacket, called a spencer, or a long coat, called a pelisse. And they wrap themselves in a large shawl, often from India, made of beautiful soft wool, with patterns.

{img:regency-bonnets-shop-window-straw-silk-ribbons-feathers-display}
Outside, every lady wears a bonnet. A hat with a brim around the face, tied with ribbons under the chin. Bonnets are made of straw, or silk, or felt, and trimmed with ribbons, flowers and feathers. Ladies love to change the trimmings of their bonnets, to make them look new. Jane Austen often wrote to Cassandra about trimming bonnets. Indoors, married women and older ladies wear little white caps, of muslin or lace.

On their hands, ladies wear gloves, always, when they go out. Short gloves for the day, and long ones, up to the elbow, for evening. They carry a little bag, called a reticule, because the new dresses are too thin for pockets. They carry a parasol, against the sun, because a pale complexion is fashionable. And on their feet, they wear flat, thin slippers of silk or leather, which are pretty but not very practical in the mud.

--

{img:regency-gentleman-tailcoat-cravat-top-hat-boots-walking-cane-street}
The gentlemen wear a coat, cut short in front and with long tails behind, in dark blue, green, brown or black wool. Under it, a waistcoat, often of a lighter colour. A white linen shirt, with a high collar. And around the neck, a long white cloth, called a cravat, folded and tied in a fashionable knot. They wear tight breeches to the knee, with stockings, or the new long trousers, called pantaloons. And on their feet, tall leather riding boots, polished until they shine.

On their heads, gentlemen wear a tall hat, with a narrow brim, made of beaver fur or silk. They carry a walking cane, and gloves. Their hair is short and natural, often brushed forward in a fashionable style, copied from the statues of Roman emperors. The powdered wigs of their grandfathers are completely out of fashion now. Only old gentlemen, and servants in grand houses, still wear them.

{img:young-women-sewing-dresses-together-patterns-fabric-parsonage-afternoon}
Most ladies of the gentry make many of their own clothes, or at least alter and trim them. They buy fabric in the shops, and sew with a dressmaker, or by themselves. Anne and her mother spend many hours sewing. They look at the fashion plates in the ladies' magazines, which show the latest styles from London, and try to copy them, with cheaper fabrics. Anne is very good at it.

--

For her first ball, in the spring of 1811, Anne is making a new dress. It is of white muslin, with a little pattern of tiny green leaves, embroidered by herself. It has short puffed sleeves, and a green silk sash at the high waist. She will wear long white gloves, white silk slippers, and a green ribbon in her dark curls. She works on it every evening, for weeks. And when it is finished, she tries it on in front of the mirror, and turns around, and cannot quite believe that the young lady in the glass is herself.
