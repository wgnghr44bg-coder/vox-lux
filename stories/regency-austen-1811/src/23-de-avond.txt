So we leave Regency England tonight, in the year 1811, as the evening falls. The sun is setting over the fields of Hampshire. The sky turns pink and gold, and then soft blue. The rooks are flying home to their trees, calling to each other. And the church clock strikes six, slowly and softly, over the quiet village.

--

In the parsonage, the candles are being lit in the drawing room. The fire is crackling. Mrs Hartley is sewing, by the light of a candle. Mr Hartley is reading, with his spectacles on the end of his nose. Kitty is curled up on the sofa, with a book about birds. And Anne is at the pianoforte, playing a gentle little tune, very softly, so as not to disturb anyone.

{img:young-woman-playing-pianoforte-candlelight-drawing-room-family-quiet}
In the kitchen, Mrs Platt is preparing a light supper. Sally is warming the beds with a copper warming pan, full of hot coals. Old Thomas is checking that the horse is comfortable for the night. And outside, in the dark garden, an owl is calling from the orchard, where the apple trees are standing quietly, waiting for spring.

--

A few kilometres away, in a small red-brick house in Chawton, another family is sitting by the fire. An old lady is dozing in her chair. Her daughter is sewing. Their friend is reading. And a lady of thirty-five, in a little cap, is sitting at a tiny table by the window, with a candle and a quill pen, writing a few more lines, by the soft light. Before anyone notices. Before the door creaks.

{img:chawton-cottage-night-candle-in-window-moonlit-lane-quiet}
The village is quiet now. The lanes are empty. The cottages are dark, except for a candle here and there. The moon is rising over the woods, and its silver light lies on the fields, and on the church tower, and on the roofs of the sleeping houses. A fox trots across the lane. And far away, a dog barks, once, and then is silent.

--

The candles in the parsonage go out, one by one. The family goes up to bed. Anne stands for a moment at her window, looking out at the moonlit orchard, and the dark shape of the church tower, and the stars above. She thinks about the ball, and the comet, and the young doctor who makes her laugh. And then she climbs into her warm bed, and pulls up the quilt, and falls asleep, to the slow, gentle ticking of the church clock.
