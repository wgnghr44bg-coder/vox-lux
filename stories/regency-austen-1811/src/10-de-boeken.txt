In the Regency, reading is one of the great pleasures of life. People read everything. Poetry, history, travel books, sermons, newspapers, magazines. And above all, novels. Novels are very popular, especially with young ladies. Some people disapprove of them, and say that they fill young heads with silly romantic ideas. But everyone reads them anyway.

--

Books are expensive in 1811. A new novel, in three small volumes, can cost as much as a week's wages for a working man. So most people do not buy books. Instead, they borrow them from a circulating library. A circulating library is a shop where you pay a yearly fee, and can then borrow books, a few at a time. There are circulating libraries in every town, and in the fashionable places like Bath, they are popular meeting places, where people go to be seen.

The Hartleys belong to the circulating library in the nearby town of Alton. Every week, Anne walks there, and changes her books. She chooses novels for herself, books of poetry for her father, and travel books for her mother, who loves to read about faraway places, especially places where William's ship might be. And Kitty always asks for books about animals and adventures.

{img:young-woman-reading-novel-garden-bench-afternoon-parasol-book-three-volumes}
The most popular novels of the time are often full of drama. Gothic novels, with ruined castles, secret passages, ghosts and villains. Sentimental novels, with heroines who faint and cry a great deal. Jane Austen loved to read these novels. But she also loved to make fun of them. In one of her novels, Northanger Abbey, the heroine reads so many Gothic novels that she starts to imagine mysteries everywhere, in an ordinary country house.

In the autumn of 1811, a new novel appears at the circulating library in Alton. It is called Sense and Sensibility. It is in three volumes. And on the title page, instead of the author's name, it says simply, By a Lady. Nobody knows who the lady is. Anne borrows it, and takes it home. And from the first page, she cannot put it down.

--

{img:sense-and-sensibility-first-edition-three-volumes-by-a-lady-title-page}
Sense and Sensibility is the story of two sisters, Elinor and Marianne Dashwood. After their father dies, they must leave their beautiful home, and move with their mother to a small cottage in the country, with very little money. Elinor is calm and sensible. She keeps her feelings to herself. Marianne is passionate and romantic. She shows her feelings to everyone. Both of them fall in love, and both of them have their hearts tested. And in the end, they both find happiness, in different ways.

Anne reads the whole book in three days. She laughs at the funny parts, and cries at the sad ones. She recognises so many of the characters. The silly neighbours, the selfish relatives, the kind old colonel. It is as if the author had been watching her own village. She thinks that she is a little like Elinor, sensible and quiet. And Kitty, she thinks, will be a Marianne, one day.

{img:jane-austen-holding-printed-copy-own-novel-quiet-smile-cottage-window}
In the cottage at Chawton, Jane Austen holds the three little volumes of her first book in her hands. She has waited for this moment for almost fifteen years. She writes to Cassandra that she can no more forget it than a mother can forget her baby. The book sells well. All the copies are sold within two years, and Jane earns a hundred and forty pounds, the first money she has ever earned by her writing. She is very proud.

--

Anne never guesses that the lady who wrote Sense and Sensibility is the same lady in the little cap, who walked with her in the garden at Chawton. Very few people know. Jane wants to keep it a secret. But her brother Henry is so proud of her that he cannot help telling people. And slowly, over the years, the secret comes out. And when Anne finally learns it, many years later, she laughs, and says that she should have guessed.
