In November, news arrives at the parsonage. William's ship, the Amelia, is coming home to Portsmouth, for repairs, after more than two years at sea. William will have a few weeks of leave. He writes that he will come home for Christmas. Mrs Hartley reads the letter, and bursts into tears of joy. Then she rushes around the house, ordering the cleaning of his room, the airing of his bed, and the baking of his favourite cake.

--

Portsmouth is the great naval port of England, on the south coast of Hampshire, about fifty kilometres from the village. It is the home of the Royal Navy. Its harbour is full of warships. Great ships of the line, with three decks of guns, and fast frigates, and little sloops and cutters. There are dockyards, where ships are built and repaired, with thousands of workers. And the town is full of sailors, officers, shopkeepers and taverns.

Mr Hartley and Anne go to Portsmouth, to meet William. They travel in the little carriage, through the countryside, and over the hills, until they see the sea, grey and shining, and the harbour full of masts, like a forest of bare trees. The air smells of salt, tar and seaweed. Gulls are crying. And everywhere there are sailors, in their short blue jackets and wide trousers, with their hair in pigtails, rolling along the streets.

{img:young-naval-officer-blue-coat-embracing-father-sister-quay-portsmouth}
They wait on the quay, as the boats come in from the ships anchored out in the harbour. And then Anne sees him. A tall young man in a blue naval coat, with gold buttons and a white collar, his face brown from the Mediterranean sun. He jumps out of the boat, and runs up the steps, and embraces his father, and then lifts Anne off her feet and swings her round, as he used to do when she was a little girl. Everybody on the quay smiles.

William has changed. He was a boy of eighteen when he left. Now he is a man of twenty, broad-shouldered and confident, with a deeper voice. But his smile is the same. On the way home, in the carriage, he talks without stopping. About Malta, with its golden stone and its great harbour. About Gibraltar, with its rock and its monkeys. About Sicily, and Naples, and the islands of Greece. About storms and calms, dolphins and whales.

--

{img:naval-officer-telling-stories-family-fireside-parsonage-evening-candles}
At home, Mrs Hartley weeps again, and hugs him, and then makes him eat three pieces of his favourite cake. Kitty hangs on to his arm and will not let go. The servants come out to greet him. Old Thomas shakes his hand. Mrs Platt the cook cries into her apron. And in the evening, by the fire, the whole family sits around him, and he tells them about his life at sea.

He tells them about life on board a warship. The small, dark cabin he shares with another lieutenant. The food, salted meat and hard biscuits, and the occasional treat of fresh fish or fruit from a port. The watches, four hours on and four hours off, day and night. The beauty of the sea at sunset. And the stars in the middle of the ocean, so many and so bright that the sky seems white with them. He does not talk much about the battles. Only that the ship came through safely, and that he is glad to be home.

{img:navy-ship-at-sea-mediterranean-blue-water-sails-dolphins-sunny}
In Jane Austen's novels, naval officers are often the heroes. Brave, honest, good-humoured and kind. In Persuasion, she describes their warmth, their friendliness and their love of home. She knew them well, from her brothers Francis and Charles, who were both naval officers, and both, much later, became admirals. She was very proud of them. And she worried about them, as every family with a son at sea worried.

--

On his first night home, William sleeps in his own old room, in his own old bed, with the window open, so that he can hear the sounds of the countryside. The wind in the trees. An owl in the churchyard. The church clock striking the hours. No waves, no creaking timbers, no ship's bell. He tells Anne the next morning that it was the quietest night of his life. And that he slept like a log for twelve hours, for the first time in two years.
