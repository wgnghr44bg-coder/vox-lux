Tonight we travel back more than two hundred years, to the year 1811. We go to the south of England, to the county of Hampshire. It is a gentle land of green hills, small woods, winding lanes and quiet villages. And in one of these villages, in a small red-brick house by the road, a woman is sitting at a little table, writing a novel. Her name is Jane Austen.

--

It is early morning in spring. A soft mist lies over the fields. The hedgerows are white with hawthorn blossom. Sheep are grazing in the meadows. A cuckoo is calling somewhere in the woods. In the village, the smoke is rising from the chimneys. A farm cart is rattling along the lane. And from the church tower, the bell is ringing seven o'clock.

The village is small and peaceful. There is an old stone church, with a square tower. There is an inn, where the coaches stop. There are cottages with thatched roofs and little gardens full of flowers. There is a parsonage, where the vicar lives with his family. And at the edge of the village, behind tall trees, there is a great old house, belonging to the squire, the most important family in the parish.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

{img:parsonage-garden-1811-young-woman-muslin-dress-bonnet-reading-bench}
This is the world of Jane Austen's novels. A world of country villages and parsonages, of great houses and gardens, of morning calls and evening parties, of letters and walks and balls. A world where young women hoped to marry for love, but also had to think about money and family. Where good manners mattered very much. And where life was, for the most part, calm and gentle.

Tonight, we will spend a year in this world. We will follow a vicar's family in a Hampshire village, not far from the home of Jane Austen. Especially the eldest daughter, a young woman of seventeen called Anne. We will see how they live, what they wear, what they eat and how they pass their days. We will go with them to a ball, and to the elegant city of Bath, and to London.

{img:jane-austen-writing-small-round-table-quill-window-cottage-light}
We will also visit Jane Austen herself, in her cottage at Chawton, and see how she wrote her famous novels. In this very year, 1811, her first novel, Sense and Sensibility, will be published. And we will learn about the times she lived in. The Regency, when a prince ruled England in place of his father. The age of elegant houses, fine carriages and beautiful gardens.

--

There were difficult things in those years too. England was at war with Napoleon, far away across the sea. Many people in England were poor, and worked very hard. We will remember them too. But we will mostly stay in the gentle world of the village and the parsonage, as Jane Austen did in her novels.

{img:regency-country-lane-carriage-trees-spring-blossom-morning-light}
So let us begin, on a spring morning in 1811, in a quiet village in Hampshire. The mist is lifting from the fields. The birds are singing. And in the parsonage, a young woman is opening her window, and looking out at the morning, wondering what the day will bring.

In the lane outside the parsonage, a boy is driving a few cows to the meadow, whistling. A girl is carrying a basket of eggs to the big house. The baker's cart is making its morning round. And an old man is sitting on the bench outside the inn, smoking his pipe, watching the world go by, as he has done every morning for forty years.
