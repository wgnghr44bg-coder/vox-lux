Down by the river at Elton is the mill.

It is a wooden building with a straw roof. It stands beside a channel dug from the river. A small dam and a wooden gate hold the water back. When the gate opens, the water rushes through. It turns the great wooden water wheel on the side of the mill.

--

The wheel turns slowly and steadily. It creaks and splashes and drips. Inside the mill, wooden gears pass the turning to the millstones. There are two great round stones, one on top of the other. The bottom one stays still. The top one turns. The grain is ground between them into flour.

The grain is poured into a wooden funnel above the stones. It trickles down through a hole in the top stone. The flour comes out at the edges, soft and white and warm. It falls into a trough and into the sacks. The whole mill hums and rumbles. The air is full of fine white dust. It settles on everything. And it settles on the miller, so he is white all over, from his hair to his boots.

{img:miller-floury-clothes-taking-toll-grain-bowl-villager-sack}
The mill belongs to the lord. The villagers must bring their grain here and nowhere else. And they must pay the miller. He keeps a small part of their grain, measured with a special bowl.

--

The villagers are not allowed to grind their own grain at home with small hand stones. If they are caught, they are fined at the village court. Many do it anyway, secretly, because it is cheaper. And they listen for footsteps at the door.

The miller is not always popular. People suspect he takes more than his share. Maybe his bowl is a little too big. Maybe he mixes a little sand into the flour. There are many old jokes and songs about millers who cheat. In a famous old book of stories, there is a miller with a thumb of gold. That means he knows just how to press his thumb on the scales.

{img:river-nene-mill-pond-reflections-ducks-evening-calm-willows}
But the mill is also a meeting place. While they wait, the villagers sit on the bank outside in the sunshine. They talk and share the news. The children play by the water. They watch the great wheel turn. And they fish in the mill pond.

--

The mill pond is full of fish. The lord takes eels as part of the rent. Thousands of eels every year. They are caught in woven traps in the mill stream. They are sent to the abbey for the monks to eat on days when they may not eat meat.

In the evening, when the work is done, the miller closes the gate. The great wheel slows down and stops. The mill falls silent. The only sound is the water running over the dam. And the frogs and water birds calling in the reeds.
