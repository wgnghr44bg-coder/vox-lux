The women of Elton work as hard as the men. Often harder.

They run the house and the garden and the yard. They cook and bake and brew. They make butter and cheese. They spin and weave and sew and mend clothes for the whole family. They look after the hens and the geese and the pigs and the cow. They fetch the water and the firewood. And they care for the children and the old and the sick.

--

In the busy seasons, at haymaking and harvest, they work in the fields too. They rake and tie bundles beside the men. Some work for wages on the lord's farm. They are usually paid a little less than the men.

On washing days, the women gather by the river with baskets of linen. They wash it in the shallows. They beat it with wooden paddles on flat stones. They spread it out to dry on the grass and the bushes. And they talk and laugh and share the news of the village.

{img:medieval-woman-making-cheese-dairy-wooden-press-churn-cottage}
Many women of Elton brew ale to sell. In the old rolls, the names of the ale-wives appear again and again. Each one pays a few pennies at the court. It is really a kind of fee that every brewer pays. Brewing is one of the few ways a woman can earn her own money.

--

And some women have land of their own. When a man dies, his widow usually keeps his land. She must manage it. She must organise the ploughing and the harvest. She must pay the rent and do the work, or pay someone else to do it. Many widows appear in the old rolls. They come to the court to stand up for their rights.

A widow with land is a good match. Often she marries again quite soon. And her new husband works the land with her.

{img:medieval-wife-husband-marketing-eggs-cheese-basket-lane-morning}
Marriages in Elton are often arranged by the families. Land and animals and money are involved. The lord must give permission too. But the young people usually choose each other as well. And many marriages are happy ones.

--

The wedding takes place at the church door, in the porch. The priest asks the couple if they take each other freely. The groom gives the bride a ring and some coins. Then they go into the church for the service. Afterwards there is a feast at the bride's house. There is ale and food and music and dancing. And the whole village comes.

Then the bride goes to her new home and her new life. She is a wife now, and soon a mother. She has her own house and garden and fire, in the village where she has always lived.
