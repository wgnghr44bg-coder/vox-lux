On the first of May, the young people of Elton go out to gather the May.

Before dawn, while the stars are still out, they get up quietly. They go out together into the woods and fields. They gather branches of hawthorn covered with white blossom. They gather flowers and green leaves. And they wash their faces in the dew. People say it makes you beautiful for the whole year.

--

As the sun rises, they come back into the village singing. Their arms are full of blossom and green branches. They decorate the doors and windows of the houses and the church. The whole village smells of blossom, sweet and heavy, in the morning air.

On the green they put up a tall pole decorated with flowers and ribbons. It is the maypole. They dance around it in a ring, holding hands. They sing the old songs to the music of a pipe and a drum and a fiddle.

{img:villagers-dancing-ring-around-maypole-green-flowers-pipe-drum}
A girl is chosen to be the Queen of the May. She is crowned with flowers. She sits on a seat covered with greenery and watches the dancing. There are games and races and wrestling and archery. The ale-wives bring out their ale. And everyone eats and drinks and dances all day long.

--

The church does not always approve. Some priests think the May games are too wild. They come from very old times, before Christianity. But the villagers love them. And they go on year after year, for hundreds of years.

Later in spring there is another holiday. And often there is a church ale. That is a big feast held by the village to raise money for the church.

{img:church-ale-feast-trestle-tables-churchyard-villagers-barrels}
The church helpers brew a lot of ale, from grain the villagers have given. They set up tables in the churchyard or in a barn. Everyone comes and pays a little to eat and drink. The money is used to mend the church roof, or buy a new bell, or candles.

--

There is music and dancing and games. Sometimes a travelling singer comes by. He sings old songs and tells stories of knights and outlaws and kings, in the churchyard or on the green.

And in the long light evenings of late spring, the young men and women of Elton walk together. They walk along the river and through the fields, among the flowering hedges and the green grain. And many of the marriages of Elton begin on these May evenings.
