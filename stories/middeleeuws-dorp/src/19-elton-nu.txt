Today, Elton is still there.

It is still a small village in the countryside, beside the river Nene. About seven hundred people live there. Its houses are now built of pale golden-grey local stone. Some have stone roofs, and some still have straw. Gardens full of flowers line the winding lanes and the little green.

--

The church of All Saints still stands in the middle of the village. It is bigger now, with a tall tower added later. In the churchyard, the two old crosses still stand. They are weathered and worn, among the gravestones and the yew trees. They stand just as they did when the villagers of 1300 walked past them on their way to church.

Down by the river there is still a mill at Elton. It is a later one, built of stone, beside the little waterfall. The water still pours over. The river still winds slowly through the green meadows, with their willows and reeds. And cows still graze in the mist on summer mornings.

Near the village stands a great country house with towers and gardens. It was built over many centuries, partly where an old manor house once stood. The same family has lived there for more than three hundred and fifty years.

--

And in the fields around Elton, and all over the middle of England, you can still see the old ridges if you know how to look. The long gentle waves in the grass. They were left by the ploughs of the villagers in their strips, seven hundred years ago.

The old court rolls and accounts of Elton are kept today in archives, carefully stored in boxes in the dry and the dark. Historians have read them and translated them from Latin. They have written books about them. So the people of Elton in the year 1300 are not forgotten. The reeves and the ale-wives and the millers and the ploughmen. Their quarrels and their cows and their weddings.

So we leave Elton tonight, in the year 1300, as the evening falls at the end of a summer day.

--

The ploughmen are coming home slowly from the fields with their oxen, along the lane. Smoke rises from the straw roofs. Women stand in the doorways calling the children in. The geese are being driven home from the green, hissing and grumbling. The church bell rings over the fields. Down by the river, the mill wheel has stopped. The water pours softly over the dam. Mist rises from the meadows as the light fades. And the first stars come out over the quiet village beside the river.
