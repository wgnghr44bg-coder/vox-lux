In the middle of Elton stands the church, the church of All Saints.

It is the biggest building in the village, and the oldest, and the most beautiful. It is built of stone, with thick walls, and a roof of lead, and timber, and a little tower, with bells, and it can be seen, from everywhere, across the fields, and the meadows, and heard, from everywhere, when the bells ring.

--

Inside, it is cool, and dim, and quiet, and smells of stone, and candle wax, and incense. There are no pews, or seats. The people stand, or kneel, on the stone floor, which is covered with rushes, and only the old, and the sick, may sit, on a stone bench, along the wall. Some say that this is where the old saying comes from: the weakest go to the wall.

The walls are covered with paintings, in red, and yellow, and black, and white: pictures of the saints, and of the life of Jesus, and of the Last Judgement, over the chancel arch, with the good souls, going up to heaven, on one side, and the wicked, going down, on the other. Most of the villagers cannot read. So the paintings are their book, which tells them the stories of the Bible.

{img:rood-screen-carved-wood-crucifix-chancel-medieval-candles-altar}
Across the church, between the nave, where the people stand, and the chancel, where the altar is, there is a carved wooden screen, called the rood screen, with a great crucifix, the rood, above it, with the figures of Mary, and Saint John, on each side, lit by candles.

--

In the chancel, beyond the screen, the priest says the mass, every day, at the altar, in Latin, which the villagers do not understand, but they know the words, and the rhythm, and the bells, and they know when to kneel, and when to cross themselves, and when to look up, as the priest lifts the bread, above his head, and the little bell rings.

The church is the centre of village life. Every child of Elton is baptised here, in the great stone font, by the door, a few days after it is born. Every couple of Elton is married here, at the church door, in the porch, and then goes inside, for the mass. And every villager of Elton is buried here, in the churchyard, in the shadow of the tower.

{img:medieval-wedding-church-porch-couple-priest-villagers-ring-spring}
And on Sundays, and the great feast days, everyone comes, in their best clothes, to the mass, and the village gathers, afterwards, in the churchyard, and talks, and arranges business, and hears the news, and the children play among the graves.

--

In Elton's churchyard, today, there are still two old stone crosses, carved more than a thousand years ago, in Anglo-Saxon times, before the Normans came, which were already very old, in 1300, and which the villagers of 1300 would have known, and touched, and passed by, every Sunday.

The church year gives the village its rhythm: Christmas, and Lent, and Easter, and Whitsun, and the feasts of the saints, scattered through the year, each one a holy day, a holiday, when nobody works, and there is mass, and feasting, and games. There are about forty or fifty of them, every year, as well as the Sundays.

{img:medieval-church-tower-bells-evening-swifts-fields-village-roofs}
And the bells of the church ring, every day, to mark the hours, and the prayers, at dawn, and noon, and dusk, so that the people, out in the fields, far from the village, can hear them, and know the time, and stop, for a moment, and say a prayer, and then go on with their work.
