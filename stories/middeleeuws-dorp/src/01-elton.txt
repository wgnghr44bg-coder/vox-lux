Tonight we go more than seven hundred years back. We go to a small village in the middle of England, in the year 1300.

--

It is early morning, in spring. The sun is rising over a wide green valley. A slow river winds between meadows and willows and reeds. Mist lies on the water and over the low fields. Cows stand in it up to their knees, quietly eating. A heron flies slowly over the river. And a cuckoo is calling from the woods on the hill.

On a gentle rise above the river there is a village. It is a cluster of houses with straw roofs, gardens and fences. They stand along a winding lane. In the middle is a stone church with a little tower. Smoke rises from the roofs into the still morning air.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can easily find the next one. And now, let us begin.

--

The village is called Elton. It lies in the east of England, beside the river Nene. A great abbey church stands about ten miles away, in the town of Peterborough.

{img:medieval-cottages-lane-thatched-roofs-crofts-gardens-dawn-smoke}
It is not a famous place. Nothing very important ever happened here. It is simply a village, like thousands of other villages in England in the year 1300. People were born here. They grew up and worked in the fields. They married and had children and grew old.

But we know a lot about Elton. The village belonged to a great monastery, Ramsey Abbey. And the monks of Ramsey kept careful records. They wrote them in Latin on long rolls of parchment, a kind of paper made from animal skin. They wrote down the village quarrels and fines and agreements. And every year they wrote down how much was sown and harvested and sold.

--

Many of these rolls still exist today. In them we can still read the names of the people of Elton, more than seven hundred years ago. And we can read the little stories of their lives. Who brewed ale. Whose cows got into the lord's corn. Who married. Who built a new house. Who quarrelled with a neighbour over a fence.

{img:monk-scribe-writing-parchment-roll-court-record-candle-abbey}
Tonight we will spend a whole year in Elton, from spring to spring. We will walk through the fields and meadows. We will visit the mill and the church and the lord's house. We will meet the village leader and the miller and the woman who brews the ale. We will sit in the village court. We will dance on Midsummer's Eve. We will bring in the harvest. And we will ride to a great fair. And all year long, there is one big question. Will the harvest be good enough to get them through the winter?

--

So let us walk down the lane into the village in the early morning. There is dew on the grass and the smell of wood smoke. A rooster crows. And the church bell rings softly for the morning prayers.
