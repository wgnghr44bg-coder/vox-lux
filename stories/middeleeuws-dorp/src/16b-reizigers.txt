A few miles from Elton runs a great old road. The Romans built it more than a thousand years before. It runs straight from London to the north. In the year 1300 it is still one of the main roads of England.

Along it, and along the smaller roads that join it, come travellers. Some of them pass through Elton.

--

There are pedlars walking from village to village. They carry packs full of little things to sell. Needles and pins and thread. Ribbons and buttons and combs. Little mirrors and knives and small bags of spices. When a pedlar comes, the women of the village gather round. They look and buy and hear the news.

There are friars. They are wandering monks in long grey or black robes, with ropes around their waists. They walk barefoot or in sandals from place to place. They preach in the villages, on the green or in the churchyard. Their sermons are simple and lively, full of stories. The villagers love them. And the friars beg for food and a place to sleep.

{img:friar-preaching-village-green-villagers-listening-sitting-grass}
There are messengers riding fast on horses, with letters for the abbot and the bishop. There are drovers walking cattle and sheep slowly to the markets in the towns, with their dogs and sticks.

--

There are traders with packhorses loaded with wool and cloth and salt and wine. There are carts creaking along the bumpy roads. There are wandering builders and carpenters going to work on a church or a castle far away.

And sometimes there are great people. A lord or a bishop rides by with his whole household. Servants and knights and packhorses and carts full of furniture and kitchen pots. It is a long slow colourful procession. The villagers stop and stare. And they talk about it for weeks.

{img:noble-procession-riders-banners-carts-medieval-road-villagers-watching}
Only ten years before our story, a very sad procession passed along this road. Queen Eleanor, the beloved wife of King Edward, had passed away in the north. Her body was carried slowly all the way to London. At every place where the procession stopped for the night, the king later built a tall beautiful stone cross in her memory. One of them stands not far from Elton. It still stands today.

--

So most people of Elton never travel far. But the world comes to them, along the roads and the river. It brings news and goods and stories and strangers. And then it goes on, down the road and over the hill, and is gone.
