Let us walk through Elton in the year 1300.

The village is built along a winding lane. It runs up from the river, past the church, and on toward the fields and the woods. On each side of the lane stand the houses. There are about a hundred and twenty families. Perhaps five or six hundred people in all.

--

Each house stands on its own little piece of land, with a fence or a hedge around it. A gate opens onto the lane. Behind the house is a long narrow garden. There the family grows vegetables and herbs. They keep a few fruit trees and hens and geese. Perhaps a pig and a cow.

The houses are long and low. They have a frame of wood. The walls are woven branches covered with clay and straw and painted white. The roofs are thick straw or reeds from the river. They are steep and come down low over the walls.

{img:medieval-cruck-frame-house-wattle-daub-thatch-garden-hens}
In the middle of the village stands the church. It is built of pale grey stone, with a little tower. Around it is the churchyard with old yew trees. The graves have no stones. They are just little mounds in the grass.

--

Near the church, by the river, is the lord's house. It is bigger, of stone and wood. It has a great hall and barns and stables and a tower for doves. A wall goes all around it. But the lord himself does not live here. The lord is the head of the abbey, the abbot. He lives at his great abbey, about fifteen miles away across the wet lands. His helpers run the village for him.

Down by the river is the mill. Its great wooden water wheel turns slowly in the stream. The miller's house stands beside it.

{img:medieval-watermill-wooden-wheel-river-nene-willows-morning}
All around the village are the fields. They are great wide open fields with no hedges. They are divided into long narrow strips where the villagers grow their grain. Beyond the fields are meadows by the river. There is common land where all the animals graze. And on the higher ground there are woods. The pigs look for acorns there. And the villagers gather firewood.

--

The village is busy from dawn to dusk. Children drive geese along the lane. Women carry water from the well. Men go out to the fields with their oxen and ploughs. Dogs bark and pigs root in the mud. The blacksmith hammers at his fire. And the church bell rings the hours.

It is a small world, but a complete one. Most people of Elton will live their whole lives here. They will never travel more than a day's walk away. Everything they need is here or close by. Their food and their work. Their families and friends. Their church. And their graves.
