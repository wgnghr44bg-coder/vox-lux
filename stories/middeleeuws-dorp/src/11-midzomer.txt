On the twenty-third of June, it is Midsummer Eve, the eve of the feast of Saint John the Baptist.

It is the longest day, or close to it, and the shortest night, when the sun sets late, after nine o'clock, and the twilight lasts, almost until midnight, and the sky in the north never quite grows dark.

--

On Midsummer Eve, in Elton, as in villages all over England, and Europe, the villagers light bonfires: great fires, of wood, and bones, and brushwood, on the green, and on the hill, above the village. And they dance around them, and sing, and leap over the flames, for luck, the young men, and the girls, holding hands.

The doors of the houses are decorated with green birch branches, and fennel, and Saint John's wort, a little yellow flower, which is said to keep away evil spirits, and lilies, and the lamps are left burning, all night long.

{img:midsummer-doorway-decorated-birch-st-johns-wort-flowers-lamp}
Some people say that, on Midsummer Eve, the fairies are about, and that strange things can happen, in the woods, and the fields, and that, if a girl looks into a well, or a pool, at midnight, she will see the face of the man she will marry. The old women tell stories, by the fire, of the things that happened, on Midsummer nights, long ago.

--

The children are allowed to stay up late, to see the fires, and they run about, in the half-light, waving burning sticks, and making circles of light, in the air, and then fall asleep, on the grass, wrapped in their mothers' cloaks.

And on the next day, the feast of Saint John, there is a holiday, and mass, in the church, and the rents are paid, on Midsummer Day, one of the four quarter days of the year.

{img:short-summer-night-village-fields-glowing-north-sky-embers}
And then, as the fires die down, to glowing embers, in the middle of the night, and the people go home, to bed, the sky, in the north, is still glowing, faintly, with the light of the sun, below the horizon, and the birds begin to sing again, only a few hours later, in the dawn, which comes so early, on midsummer morning.

--

After Midsummer, there is a little quiet time, in the village, between the haymaking, and the harvest. The corn is growing, in the fields, green, and then golden, and the villagers mend their tools, and their carts, and their barns, and weed the fields, and wait, and watch the sky, for the harvest.
