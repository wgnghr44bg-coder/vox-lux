On the twenty-third of June it is Midsummer Eve. It is the evening before the feast of Saint John.

It is the longest day of the year, or close to it. It is the shortest night. The sun sets late, after nine o'clock. The twilight lasts almost until midnight. And in the north, the sky never gets quite dark.

--

On Midsummer Eve, villages all over England light bonfires. Elton does too. Great fires of wood and branches burn on the green and on the hill above the village. People dance around them and sing. The young men and the girls hold hands and leap over the flames, for luck.

The doors of the houses are decorated with green birch branches. They hang up a little yellow flower called Saint John's wort. People say it keeps away bad spirits. And the lamps are left burning all night long.

{img:midsummer-doorway-decorated-birch-st-johns-wort-flowers-lamp}
Some people say the fairies are out on Midsummer Eve. They say strange things can happen in the woods and fields. If a girl looks into a well or a pool at midnight, she will see the face of the man she will marry. Some girls are brave enough to try. They creep to the well in the half dark and look down. The old women tell stories by the fire about Midsummer nights long ago.

--

The children may stay up late to see the fires. They run about in the half light. They wave burning sticks and make circles of light in the air. Then they fall asleep on the grass, wrapped in their mothers' cloaks.

The next day is the feast of Saint John. It is a holiday. There is a service in the church. And rents are paid on Midsummer Day.

{img:short-summer-night-village-fields-glowing-north-sky-embers}
The fires slowly die down to glowing embers. In the middle of the night, people go home to bed. The northern sky still glows faintly. And only a few hours later, the birds start to sing again. Dawn comes so early on midsummer morning.

--

After Midsummer there is a quiet time between the haymaking and the harvest. The grain grows in the fields, first green and then golden. The villagers mend their tools and carts and barns. They weed the fields. And they wait and watch the sky. Will the weather hold until harvest?
