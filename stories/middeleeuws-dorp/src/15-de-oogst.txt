In August, it is harvest time. It is the most important time of the whole year.

The grain in the fields has turned from green to gold. Wheat and barley and oats stand tall and heavy and ripe. They rustle in the warm wind under a blue sky. The whole year depends on the next few weeks. If the harvest is good, there will be bread all winter. If it is bad, there will be hunger.

--

On the first of August there is a feast called Lammas. That means loaf mass. The first loaf made from the new wheat is brought to the church and blessed. People give thanks for the harvest that is coming.

Then the harvest begins. The court has decided the day. Nobody may start before it. And then everyone who can work goes out into the fields. Every man and woman and child. They work from dawn to dusk for weeks.

{img:reapers-row-sickles-cutting-wheat-binders-following-stooks-sun}
The grain is cut with small curved knives. The reaper grabs a bunch of stalks with one hand. With the other he cuts them low, near the ground. He lays them down in a row. It is slow hard work. They bend over all day long in the heat. The sweat runs down. The dust and the straw scratch.

--

Behind the reapers come the binders, often the women. They gather the cut grain and tie it into bundles with a twist of straw. Then they stand the bundles up together in little groups. The heads of grain point up so they can dry in the sun and wind.

The lord's grain must be cut first. On special days, every family must send everyone who can work. Sometimes hundreds of people cut the lord's fields all at once.

{img:boon-work-harvest-lunch-field-bread-cheese-ale-herrings-workers-resting}
On those days the lord gives the workers food and drink in the fields. Bread and cheese and fish and ale. Sometimes meat and soup. At midday they sit down in the stubble in the shade of the bundles. They eat and drink and rest together in the heat.

--

When the grain has dried, it is loaded onto carts. It is carried home to the barns and piled up in big stacks. The stacks are covered with straw against the rain. Everyone watches the sky. One heavy storm now could ruin a whole field. Only when the last cart is in the barn can they breathe easy.

After the harvest, the poor of the village may go into the fields. The old, the widows and the children. They pick up the heads of grain left behind on the ground, one by one. They take them home in their aprons and baskets. It is a precious right. And the court makes sure it is respected.

{img:gleaners-women-children-stubble-field-evening-picking-ears-corn}
When the last bundle is cut in the last field, there is a great shout. The last bundle is decorated with flowers and ribbons. It is carried home in triumph on the last cart. The reapers ride on top, singing.

--

And then there is the harvest supper, the greatest feast of the year. There is meat and pies and bread and cheese and ale. There is music and dancing in the lord's barn or on the green. It goes on late into the night. And the harvest moon rises, huge and golden, over the stubble fields.
