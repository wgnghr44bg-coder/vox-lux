Let us spend a day in May with a family of Elton.

They have about fifteen acres of land in strips across the fields. There is the father, Robert, and the mother, Agnes. There are four children, two boys and two girls. And Robert's old mother lives with them, in a little room at the end of the house.

--

They wake at dawn, at about four o'clock, when the birds start to sing. Light comes in through the doorway and the little window. Agnes gets up first. She blows on the ashes of the fire in the middle of the floor. She adds some sticks and the fire flares up. She hangs the pot over it and warms yesterday's thick soup.

They eat breakfast quickly. A piece of dark heavy bread and a drink of weak ale. Everyone drinks ale, even the children. The water is not always safe.

{img:medieval-father-son-walking-to-fields-hoe-scythe-dawn-mist}
Then Robert goes out to the fields with his eldest son, who is fourteen. Today it is his turn to work on the lord's land. He must weed the barley. He pulls out the thistles and the weeds that would choke the crop.

--

At home, Agnes milks the cow and feeds the hens and the pig. She lets the geese out onto the green. Her youngest daughter, who is seven, must watch them. Then Agnes fetches water from the well in a wooden bucket. She weeds the beans and onions in the garden. She picks herbs and young nettles for the soup.

Her older daughter, who is twelve, sits at the door in the sun, spinning wool into thread. She minds the baby. He sits in the dust playing with a wooden toy and a puppy.

{img:medieval-woman-brewing-ale-vat-cottage-yard-barley-malt}
And then Agnes brews. She is one of the ale-wives of Elton. Several times a month she makes ale in a big wooden tub. When it is ready, she puts a leafy branch on a pole outside her door. That is the sign. Her neighbours come with their jugs to buy it.

--

At midday, the old grandmother takes a basket out to the fields. There is bread and cheese and onions and a jug of ale. Robert and the boy sit at the edge of the strip in the shade of a bush, with the other men. They eat and rest and talk. They look at the young barley. It looks good this year. But it is still a long way to harvest.

In the afternoon they work again until evening. Then the church bell rings, and they walk home through the fields. They are tired and dusty. The sun is low behind them. And the swallows swoop over the grain.

{img:medieval-family-supper-cottage-evening-bench-bowls-door-open-sunset}
At home, Agnes has made supper. It is thick soup of peas and beans and oats and onions and herbs. There is a little bacon in it. It has cooked all afternoon over the fire. There is bread and cheese and ale too. They eat together on benches around the table, with wooden bowls and spoons. The door is open to the long golden May evening.

--

As the light fades, they sit on the bench by the door. They talk with the neighbours over the fence. The children play in the lane until it is dark. Then they go in and bar the door. They cover the fire. They lie down on their straw mattresses under their blankets. And they sleep, while the nightingale sings in the bushes by the river.
