In the north of Mongolia, close to where Genghis Khan was born, there is a mountain. It is called Burkhan Khaldun. It is the most sacred mountain of all the Mongols.

It is not a very high mountain, compared with others. Its slopes are covered with dark forests of pine and larch. Its top is bare and rocky, with patches of snow even in early summer. Two rivers begin on its slopes, the Onon and the Kherlen. These were the rivers of Genghis Khan's childhood. Bears and deer and wolves live quietly in its forests. In the mornings, mist often hangs among the trees, soft and grey.

--

When Temüjin was young, the mountain once gave him shelter. On the day that Börte was taken, he fled up its slopes into the forest. Riders searched the mountain for him for three long days. But the trees and the rocks hid him well. When the riders finally gave up and left, Temüjin came down again. He was safe and unharmed.

He was so thankful that he made a solemn promise. He hung his belt around his neck and took off his hat. He knelt down facing the morning sun. He sprinkled a little milk into the air as a gift. And then he prayed out loud. He said that the mountain had protected his life, though it was as small as a louse. He promised to honour the mountain every single morning. And he promised that his children and their children would honour it too, forever.

{img:young-temujin-praying-kneeling-mountain-forest-belt-around-neck}
And they did honour it, for hundreds of years. The mountain became a holy place for the Mongols. Ordinary people were not allowed to go there at all. Only the khans and their families could climb its slopes. Later there were long years when nobody was allowed to visit. But the people of the steppe never forgot their sacred mountain.

--

Many people believe that Genghis Khan was buried somewhere on or near this mountain. But nobody knows exactly where the place is. The secret was kept very carefully, and it is still kept today. That is another story, and we will come to it a little later.

The herders who live in the valleys near the mountain treat it with great respect. They do not shout or make loud noises when they pass beneath it. They do not cut its trees or hunt its animals without good reason. When they ride by, they often bow their heads a little. The grandmother in our story says the mountain is like an old, wise guardian. It watches over the steppe, she says, and it remembers everything.

{img:sacred-mountain-silent-forest-snow-patches-mist-evening-light}
Today, Burkhan Khaldun is carefully protected, as a World Heritage Site. Mongolians still honour the mountain as their ancestors did. Every few years, there is a great ceremony there. People climb slowly up to the ovoo at the very top. They walk around it three times and leave blue scarves and quiet prayers. The wind carries the scarves fluttering out over the forests. And the mountain stays as quiet and green as it was eight hundred years ago.
