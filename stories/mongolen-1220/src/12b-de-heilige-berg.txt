In the north of Mongolia, near where Genghis Khan was born, there is a mountain. It is called Burkhan Khaldun. It is the most sacred mountain of the Mongols.

It is not very high. It is covered with dark pine and larch forests. Its top is bare and rocky. Rivers begin on its slopes. The Onon and the Kherlen. The rivers of Genghis Khan's childhood. Bears and deer and wolves live in its forests.

--

When Temüjin was young and in danger, he hid on this mountain. The night his wife Börte was taken, he fled up its slopes into the forest. His enemies searched for him for three days. But the mountain hid him. When they finally left, Temüjin came out. He was alive.

He was so grateful that he made a promise. He hung his belt around his neck and took off his hat. He knelt toward the sun. He sprinkled milk into the air. And he prayed. He said that the mountain had protected his life, as small as a louse. He promised that every morning he would honour it. And that his children and their children would honour it too. Forever.

{img:young-temujin-praying-kneeling-mountain-forest-belt-around-neck}
And they did. For centuries, the mountain was a holy place. Ordinary people were not allowed to go there. Only the khans and their families. Later, during some hard times, people were forbidden to visit. But they never forgot.

--

Many people believe Genghis Khan was buried somewhere on or near this mountain. Nobody knows exactly where. The secret was kept very carefully. That is another story, for later.

{img:sacred-mountain-silent-forest-snow-patches-mist-evening-light}
Today, Burkhan Khaldun is protected. It is a World Heritage Site. Mongolians still honour it. Every few years, the president of Mongolia leads a ceremony there. People climb up to the ovoo at the top. They walk around it. They leave blue scarves and prayers. The wind carries them away over the forests. And the mountain stays as quiet and green as it was eight hundred years ago.
