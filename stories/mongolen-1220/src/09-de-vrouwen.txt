Mongol women are strong and free. More than in many other lands at that time.

They ride horses as well as men. They herd the animals. They run the camp. They make the gers and the felt and the clothes. They make all the milk foods. They handle money and trade. And when the men are away, which is often, the women are in charge of everything.

--

Visitors from other lands were surprised by this. Travellers from Europe and China wrote about it. The Mongol women drive the carts, they wrote. They load the camels. They ride like men. They give orders. And their husbands listen to them.

Some women had great power. Hoelun, the mother of Genghis Khan, kept her children alive when everyone left them. Later, she gave advice to her son. Börte, his wife, was his closest adviser. His daughters ruled lands. Later in the empire, some women ruled for a time as regents. One famous Mongol princess even fought as a wrestler. She said she would only marry a man who could beat her. Nobody could.

{img:mongol-woman-riding-galloping-steppe-headdress-confident-free}
Women's work is hard. Up before everyone else. To bed after everyone else. Milking, cooking, sewing, felting, herding, moving camp. Bearing and raising the children. But they are respected for it. The Mongols have a saying. The man is the head of the ger. But the woman is its neck. And the neck turns the head.

--

Mongol women love jewellery. They wear headdresses with silver and coral and turquoise. They wear earrings and rings and bracelets. On special days, married women wear a very tall hat. It is called a boqta. It is like a tall thin cone or a tube, sometimes a metre high. It is decorated with feathers and pearls and silk. Riding into a ger, they must bend down so it does not hit the door frame.

{img:mongol-noblewoman-tall-boqta-headdress-feathers-pearls-silk-ger}
Sarnai has worn hers only twice. At her wedding. And at the great gathering when the khan came to their valley years ago. She keeps it in a painted chest. Sometimes Khulan asks to see it. Sarnai takes it out carefully. Khulan touches the feathers. One day you will wear one, says her mother. Khulan smiles. And puts it back very gently.

--

When the khan's armies were away, the great camp at home was run by women. They decided where to graze the herds. They settled quarrels. They welcomed visitors and sent supplies to the armies. One traveller wrote that the camp moved as smoothly as a city. With carts in long rows and gers on wheels pulled by twenty oxen. And at the front of it all, a woman, deciding where to stop.
