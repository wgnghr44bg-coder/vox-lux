The Mongols are herders, not farmers or craftsmen. They have animals and milk and wool and felt. But many other things they cannot make themselves. Iron and silk. Grain and tea. Pots and needles. So they trade.

--

For a long time, the steppe was dangerous for traders. Raiders could rob a caravan at any time. But under Genghis Khan, the roads are safer. And the khan likes traders. He gives them protection. He lets them travel freely. He even lends them money sometimes.

So caravans come. Long lines of camels, loaded with goods. From China in the east come silk and tea and porcelain and paper. From Persia and the lands in the west come cotton and glass and silver and spices. From the forests in the north come furs. And all of it passes through the Mongol lands. Along the old road that will later be called the Silk Road.

{img:merchant-unrolling-silk-inside-ger-family-admiring-colours}
When a caravan comes near the camp, it is a big event. The children run out to look at the camels. The traders unpack their goods in front of the gers. Bolts of silk in red and blue and gold. Iron pots and knives. Bags of salt and grain. Needles and thread. Little mirrors and beads. The families come to look and touch and bargain.

--

The Mongols pay with animals and wool and felt and furs. A good horse can buy a lot. Sometimes they pay with silver. The traders count carefully. Everyone argues about prices. It is noisy and fun.

{img:caravan-leaving-steppe-sunset-camels-line-dust-trader-waving}
Sarnai buys a little bag of tea and a new iron pot. And a piece of red silk for Khulan. She is almost grown up now. She will need a fine deel for the summer gathering. Khulan holds the silk against her face. It is so soft. She has never touched anything so soft. She thanks her mother again and again.

--

The traders also bring news. They have seen cities and markets and palaces. They tell stories of the far west, where the armies are. They say the khan has taken great cities. They say the armies are moving further. Sarnai listens carefully. Any news of the men? The traders shrug. The armies are huge. They do not know every rider. But they have heard the men are well and the khan is winning. It is something. She thanks them and gives them a bowl of airag for the road.
