Genghis Khan was not always a great ruler. He began life as an ordinary boy called Temüjin. And his childhood was a very hard one.

He was born around the year 1162, beside the river Onon in the north of Mongolia. His father was the chief of a small clan, not a rich or famous one. An old story says that baby Temüjin was born holding something red in his tiny fist. People who heard about it said it was a sign. This child would grow up to be a great man, they said.

--

When Temüjin was about nine years old, his father took him on a long ride. They went to visit another clan, to find a future wife for the boy. That was the custom on the steppe in those days. There Temüjin met a girl called Börte, who was a year older than him. The two families agreed that the children would marry when they were grown. Temüjin stayed with Börte's family for a while, to get to know them. And his father rode home alone across the steppe.

On the way home, his father stopped at a camp of Tatars, who were old rivals of his clan. They gave him food to eat, as travellers were given. But the food made him very ill. He reached home and passed away not long afterwards. Temüjin was called back from Börte's family. He was still only a boy, and now he had no father.

{img:hoelun-gathering-roots-berries-river-children-poverty-steppe}
Then something sad happened. The people of the clan packed up and left. They did not want to follow a widow and her small children. They took most of the herds with them and moved away. Temüjin, his mother Hoelun and his brothers and sister were left almost alone. They had very little left in the world.

--

Those years were very hungry ones for the little family. They lived on roots and wild berries and the small wild onions of the steppe. The boys fished in the river and caught marmots and birds. Their mother Hoelun kept them all alive through those years. She was strong and brave, and she never once gave up. She dug roots with a sharpened stick and mended their clothes by the fire. And she taught her children to always stick together.

{img:mother-teaching-sons-bundle-of-arrows-cannot-break-fire-night}
One evening by the fire, she gave each of her sons a single arrow. Break it, she said, and each boy snapped his arrow easily. Then she tied five arrows together in a bundle. Now break this, she said, and none of them could do it. One arrow breaks easily, she told them. But a bundle of arrows cannot be broken. Stay together, and nobody can break you.

Life stayed difficult for Temüjin for many years. Once he was caught by a rival clan. They put a heavy wooden collar around his neck so he could not run away. But one night, during a feast, he quietly slipped away into the dark. He hid in the river with only his face above the water. A kind man saw him there, in the moonlight. Instead of calling the others, he decided to help him. He hid Temüjin in a cart piled high with wool. The searchers came and looked around, and then they went away. Temüjin was free again.

--

He never forgot those years, not for the rest of his life. He remembered the hunger and the worry, and the people who had left. And he remembered the few people who had been kind to him. When he became powerful, he rewarded every one of them. And he made sure that nobody loyal to him would ever be left alone on the steppe like that.
