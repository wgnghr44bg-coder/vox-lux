Who are the Mongols?

For thousands of years, people have lived on the steppe as herders. They keep sheep and goats and cattle and horses and camels. They live in felt tents that can be taken down and put up again. They move several times a year to find fresh grass for their animals. The steppe is good for animals. But it is too dry and too cold for farming. So people follow the grass.

--

In the time before Genghis Khan, the steppe was divided into many tribes and clans. The Mongols were one of them. There were also the Tatars and the Kereyits, the Merkits and the Naimans, and many more. They spoke similar languages. They lived in similar ways. But they did not get along. They raided each other's herds. They stole each other's horses. They took revenge for old wrongs. Nobody was ever quite safe.

Then Genghis Khan joined them all. Some by friendship and promises. Some by force. In 1206, all the peoples of the felt tents gathered on the steppe. And they named him their great ruler. From then on, they were all called Mongols.

{img:mongol-herder-man-deel-robe-hat-holding-horse-hillside}
The Mongols are tough people. The steppe has no mercy. Winters are very cold, colder than almost anywhere people live. Summers can be hot and dry. Storms come suddenly. Wolves hunt the herds. To live here, you must be strong and clever and ready for anything.

--

They wear long thick robes called deels. The deel is like a coat that wraps across the front and ties at the side. It has a wide sash around the waist. In winter, it is lined with fur. At night it can be used as a blanket. They wear felt boots with pointed toes and fur hats with flaps for the ears. Men and women dress in almost the same way.

{img:mongol-woman-deel-sash-headdress-silver-coral-beads-proud}
Their language is Mongolian. In 1220 it has only just begun to be written down. Genghis Khan ordered it. A scholar from a conquered people taught the Mongols how to write their language with his letters. The letters run from top to bottom in columns. They look like a row of little hooks and loops hanging from a line. Mongolians still use this writing today, alongside newer letters.
