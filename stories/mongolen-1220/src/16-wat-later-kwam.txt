The armies did come home. In 1225, Genghis Khan returned to Mongolia. And with him came many of the riders. Batu among them.

Imagine the day. Sarnai sees a group of riders on the horizon. She knows the shape of one of them before she can see his face. The children run. Batu jumps off his horse. Little Bayar does not recognise him at first. He hides behind his mother. Batu kneels down and holds out his hand. And slowly, Bayar comes to him.

--

Genghis Khan lived for only a few more years. In 1227, far away in the south, he passed away. He was about sixty-five. His sons and grandsons continued what he had started. Under them, the Mongol Empire grew even bigger. It stretched from Korea in the east to Hungary in the west. From Siberia in the north to Persia in the south. It was the largest land empire in history.

The war and conquest caused great suffering in many lands. We remember that with sorrow. But the empire also brought something new. For about a hundred years, people and goods and ideas could travel safely across the whole of Asia. From China to Europe. This time is sometimes called the Mongol Peace.

{img:marco-polo-caravan-arriving-mongol-court-china-later-gifts}
Travellers came from far away. The most famous was a young man from Venice called Marco Polo. He travelled all the way to China, to the court of Genghis Khan's grandson, Kublai Khan. He stayed for many years. When he went home, he wrote a book about it. People in Europe could hardly believe his stories. Paper money! Stones that burn! Cities of a million people!

--

Inventions travelled along the roads too. Paper and printing. The compass. Gunpowder. Medicine and maps and the stars. Foods and spices and stories. East and west met as never before.

{img:mongol-yuan-dynasty-later-palace-china-grand-hall-kublai-court}
Later, the empire broke up into several parts. Each part was ruled by a different branch of the family. Over time, they faded. But in Mongolia itself, the people went on living as they always had. With their gers and their horses and their herds. Moving with the seasons. Under the Eternal Blue Sky.
