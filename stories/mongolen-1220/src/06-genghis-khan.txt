Temüjin grew from a hungry boy into a great leader. Slowly, over many years, he brought the tribes of the steppe together.

He did it in a new way. In the old days, people were loyal to their family and their clan. Rich families ruled. Poor families followed. But Temüjin did things differently. He chose people for what they could do, not for who their father was. A brave poor herder could become a general. A loyal friend could become a great lord. People liked that. Many joined him.

--

He was also known for keeping his promises. And for rewarding loyalty. If you were loyal to Temüjin, he would never forget it. If you betrayed him, he would never forgive it.

In the year 1206, all the peoples of the felt tents gathered by the river Onon. Where Temüjin was born. It was a great meeting called a kurultai. Chiefs came from everywhere. Thousands of gers were set up on the grass. Thousands of horses grazed nearby. And there, the chiefs named Temüjin their great ruler. He took a new title. Genghis Khan.

{img:white-standard-nine-horse-tails-pole-wind-steppe-sacred-banner}
What does Genghis mean? Nobody is quite sure. Maybe ocean. Or strong. Or universal. Khan means ruler. So perhaps, the ruler of everything between the seas.

--

At the kurultai, a great white banner was raised. It was a tall pole with nine white horse tails tied at the top. They flew in the wind. It was the banner of peace. There was also a black banner, for war. The Mongols believed the spirit of the ruler lived in his banner.

From then on, the Mongols were one people. One nation of the felt tents. Old feuds were forgotten, or forbidden. The raiding stopped. A man could ride across the steppe in safety. That had never been true before.

{img:genghis-khan-older-grey-beard-seated-tent-calm-eyes-advisers}
Then the armies rode out. To the south, to China. And later, to the west. That story is long and hard. We will leave it there, far away. Tonight we stay at home on the steppe. In 1220, Genghis Khan is about fifty-eight years old. His beard is grey. He is far away in the west. And at home, life goes on.

--

Genghis Khan himself never learned to read or write. But he understood how important it was. He ordered that the laws and decisions be written down. He had scribes in his camp. He sent written orders across the empire. And he made sure his sons learned the new letters. The boy who once dug roots by the river now ruled from a tent full of writers.
