In midsummer, the people of the steppe gather for games. Later this will be called Naadam. It means games. And there are three of them. Wrestling, archery and horse racing. The three manly games, people call them. But women and children take part too.

--

People come from far away. They ride for days. They put up their gers on the grass by a river. Hundreds of gers. Thousands of horses. Everyone wears their best clothes. Bright deels and fine hats. It is a time to meet old friends and make new ones. To trade and talk and arrange marriages. And to watch the games.

The wrestling is the most loved. The wrestlers wear little vests that cover their arms and back, but leave the chest open. And tight short trousers. And boots. Before a match, each wrestler does a special dance. He spreads his arms and moves them slowly like a great eagle, or a falcon. Then the two wrestlers grab each other. They push and pull and twist. The one who first touches the ground with anything but his feet loses.

{img:mongol-wrestlers-eagle-dance-arms-spread-crowd-watching-grass}
There are no weight classes. A small wrestler can face a huge one. There is no time limit. A match can last for a long time. And the last wrestler standing is the champion. People talk about him for years.

--

The archery is next. Men and women shoot at targets on the ground, far away. When someone hits, the judges sing a little song. They raise their arms and call out the score. Everyone cheers.

{img:mongol-archers-men-women-shooting-targets-judges-singing-arms}
And then the horse race. This is the most exciting. The riders are children. Boys and girls, some only six or seven years old. The horses are young and fast. The race is not on a track. It is across the open steppe. Twenty or thirty kilometres. Over hills and through valleys.

--

Khulan is riding this year. She is older than most. But she is a wonderful rider. Her horse is a grey called Cloud. She has trained him all summer. At the start, dozens of children wait on their horses. They sing a song together to call the horses' spirits. Then they are off. Galloping into the distance until they disappear over a hill.

{img:children-jockeys-galloping-steppe-dust-horses-race-finish-crowd}
Everyone waits at the finish. Long minutes pass. Then a cloud of dust appears on the horizon. Riders. Coming fast. People shout and point. Sarnai stands on tiptoe. Is Khulan there? The first rider comes over the last hill. It is a small boy on a brown horse. Then two more. Then a grey horse. A grey horse with a girl on its back, leaning low over its neck. It is Khulan. She comes in fourth. Out of more than forty. Sarnai shouts with joy. And when Khulan rides up to her, flushed and dusty and grinning, her mother hugs her so hard she almost falls off.
