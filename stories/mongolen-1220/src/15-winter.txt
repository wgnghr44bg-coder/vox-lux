Winter comes to the steppe. And winter in Mongolia is very hard.

By late autumn, the family has moved to their winter place. A sheltered valley behind a hill. The gers are covered with extra layers of felt. The animals have pens made of stone and wood, with roofs of branches, to keep off the worst of the wind. Stacks of dried dung are piled high for fuel. Dried meat and cheese hang in the store ger.

--

Then the cold comes. Real cold. Forty degrees below zero. The air is so cold it hurts to breathe. Your breath freezes on your scarf. The river freezes solid. The snow squeaks under your boots. The sky is very clear and very blue. And at night, the stars are so bright and close you feel you could touch them.

Inside the ger, it is warm. The stove burns all day and all night. The family sits close together on the felt. The children play with ankle bones. The grandmother tells stories. Sarnai sews. Khulan makes felt boots. Temur fixes harness. And the newborn lambs and goats, born too early in the cold, sleep in a corner wrapped in felt.

{img:inside-ger-winter-stove-glowing-family-close-lambs-wrapped-corner}
The greatest danger is called the dzud. It is a terrible winter disaster. Sometimes there is too much snow. It covers the grass so deeply that the animals cannot dig through it. Or the snow melts and then freezes into hard ice. The animals cannot break through. They cannot eat. In a bad dzud, families can lose most of their herds. And that means losing everything.

--

This winter, the snow comes early. Then it rains a little. Then it freezes. A crust of ice covers the steppe. Sarnai looks at it in the morning and her face goes grey. She knows what it could mean. The family works day and night. They break the ice with sticks and axes so the animals can reach the grass. They feed them the hay they have saved. They bring the weakest ones into the ger. Everyone is exhausted. Will the herds survive?

{img:family-breaking-ice-snow-sticks-sheep-grass-underneath-cold-dawn}
Then, after a week, the wind changes. It blows from the south. Warm air comes. The ice cracks and softens. The animals can graze again. Some of the herd has been lost. But most of them have made it. Sarnai sits down on the floor of the ger and cries. Just for a moment. The grandmother puts her hand on her shoulder. We are still here, she says. And so are they.

--

And one evening in the deep winter, a rider comes down the valley. Bells on his belt. A messenger from the Yam. He stops at the ger. He brings words from the west. The khan is coming home. The armies will return next year. Batu is well. He sends his love. Sarnai cannot speak. The children shout and dance around the ger. Even the grandmother laughs. And that night, nobody minds the cold at all.
