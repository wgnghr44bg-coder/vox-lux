In summer, the Mongols drink something special. It is called airag. It is made from the milk of horses.

Mares, the female horses, give milk after their foals are born. In summer, the families milk their mares several times a day. It is not easy. A mare will only let her milk flow if her foal is close by. So the foals are tied in a line near the gers. The mother stays near. The woman brings the foal to its mother for a moment. Then she quickly milks the mare into a wooden bucket. Then the foal can drink the rest.

--

The milk goes into a big leather bag that hangs by the door of the ger. Inside the bag, there is a little old airag from before. That helps the new milk to change. And then comes the important part. Stirring. Someone must stir the milk with a wooden paddle. Again and again. Thousands of times a day. Everyone who walks past the bag gives it a few stirs. Guests stir it. Children stir it. It never stops.

After a day or two, the milk becomes airag. It is a little sour and a little fizzy. It is not very strong. It tastes like a mix of yogurt and lemonade. Mongols drink it in big bowls all summer long. Some drink many litres a day. It is full of goodness. People say it keeps you healthy all year.

{img:leather-bag-airag-hanging-ger-boy-stirring-wooden-paddle}
Airag is offered to every guest. When you visit a ger in summer, the first thing you get is a bowl of airag. It would be very rude to refuse. You take the bowl with your right hand. You drink a little. Then you hand it back.

--

The Mongols also make many things from the milk of cows and sheep and goats and camels. Yogurt and butter. Cream that forms a thick skin on top of boiled milk. Hard dried cheese, cut into pieces and dried on the roof of the ger. It can be kept for months. Children chew it like sweets. It is very hard. You can chew one piece for an hour.

{img:dried-curds-cheese-pieces-drying-on-ger-roof-sun-flies}
The Mongols call these the white foods. Milk and everything made from it. They are pure and good. In summer, people live mostly on white foods. Meat is for winter. And the first airag of the summer is a reason for a little celebration. The family pours a few drops toward the sky and the earth and the four directions. A thank you to the spirits. And then everyone drinks.
