Tonight we travel about eight hundred years back in time, to the wide grasslands of Mongolia. Mongolia lies in the very heart of Asia, far away from any sea. The year is 1220. And the Mongols now rule the biggest land empire the world has ever seen.

--

It is early morning on the steppe, and the air is fresh and cool. The steppe is a huge sea of grass that rolls on and on to the edge of the sky. There are no trees here, and no fences, and no roads at all. There is only grass and gentle hills and a very big sky. A light wind moves over the grass, like slow waves moving over water.

On a gentle slope beside a small stream, there are a few round white tents. A thin line of grey smoke rises from the top of each one. Mist still lies in the low places, soft and white, like wool. Somewhere a horse snorts and stamps its feet in the cold morning air.

A woman steps out of one of the tents, carrying a wooden bucket. She walks across the wet grass to the horses tied in a long line. She kneels down beside a mare and begins to milk her, slowly and calmly. A boy runs past with a big shaggy dog at his heels. Sheep are bleating softly on the hillside. And far away, an eagle circles in the pale blue sky. It is quiet here, very quiet, and very big.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

{img:mongol-family-outside-ger-morning-horses-sheep-blue-sky-wide}
The Mongols are a people of the steppe, and they live together with their animals. They move with the seasons, from one good pasture to the next. And wherever they go, they carry their homes along with them. Less than twenty years before our story, they were only a handful of quarrelling tribes. Then one man brought them all together. His name was Temüjin, but the world would come to know him as Genghis Khan.

Most stories about Genghis Khan are stories about war, about armies and battles and cities. Those stories are true, and that time was very hard for many people. But tonight we will not follow the armies on their long road to the west. We will stay at home, on the quiet steppe, with the families who stayed behind. We will stay with the women and the children and the old people, and with the herders and their horses.

{img:old-mongol-grandmother-sitting-ger-door-spinning-wool-sun}
We will learn how a round felt tent is made, and how it is put up in an hour. We will taste milk that has been turned into a fizzy summer drink. We will ride with children who learn to ride before they can properly walk. We will meet the riders who carry messages across half of a continent. We will watch the great summer games beside the river. And we will follow an old monk from China on his long journey to meet the great khan.

--

{img:eagle-circling-over-steppe-grassland-hills-endless-sky-morning}
There will be a few worries along the way, as there always are. Many of the men are far away, and the winter is never very far off. Everyone wonders when the khan and his riders will finally come home. But for now, the sun is rising slowly over the grass. The horses are snorting softly in the cool morning air. And a new day is beginning, gently, on the steppe.
