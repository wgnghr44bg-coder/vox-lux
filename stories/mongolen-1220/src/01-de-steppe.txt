Tonight we go about eight hundred years back. We go to the wide grasslands of Mongolia, in the heart of Asia. The year is 1220. And the Mongols rule the biggest land empire the world has ever seen.

--

It is early morning on the steppe. The steppe is a huge sea of grass. It rolls on and on, to the edge of the sky. There are no trees here. No fences. No roads. Only grass and hills and sky. A light wind moves over the grass like waves on water. And on a gentle slope by a stream, there are a few round white tents. Smoke rises from the top of each one.

A woman steps out of one tent with a wooden bucket. She walks to the horses tied in a line. She begins to milk a mare. A boy runs past with a dog at his heels. Sheep bleat on the hillside. Far away, an eagle circles in the pale blue sky. It is quiet. Very quiet. And very big.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

{img:mongol-family-outside-ger-morning-horses-sheep-blue-sky-wide}
The Mongols are a people of the steppe. They live with their animals. They move with the seasons, from pasture to pasture. They carry their homes with them. And less than twenty years before our story, they were just a handful of quarrelling tribes. Then one man joined them all together. His name was Temüjin. The world would know him as Genghis Khan.

Most stories about Genghis Khan are about war. About armies and battles and conquered cities. That is true, and it was terrible for many people. But tonight we will not follow the armies. We will stay at home, on the steppe. With the families who stayed behind. The women and children and old people. The herders and the horses.

{img:old-mongol-grandmother-sitting-ger-door-spinning-wool-sun}
We will learn how a round felt tent is made. We will taste milk that has been turned into a drink. We will ride with children who learn to ride before they can walk. We will meet the riders who carry messages across half a continent. We will watch the great summer games. And we will follow an old monk from China on his long journey to meet the great khan.

--

{img:eagle-circling-over-steppe-grassland-hills-endless-sky-morning}
There will be worries. The men are away. The winter is coming. And everyone wonders when the khan and his riders will come home. But for now, the sun is rising over the grass. The horses are snorting in the cool air. And a new day begins on the steppe.
