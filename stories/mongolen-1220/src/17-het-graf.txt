Where is Genghis Khan buried?

The answer is that nobody knows. It is one of the great quiet mysteries of history.

--

When he passed away in 1227, his body was carried slowly back to Mongolia. The old stories say it was a long and secret journey. The riders travelled quietly and made sure that nobody saw them pass. They wanted the place where he rested to stay hidden forever. That way, nobody could ever disturb his long sleep.

The stories say he was buried somewhere near the sacred mountain, Burkhan Khaldun. That was where he had hidden as a young man, and where he had prayed. Some stories say a river was turned aside to flow over the place. Others say that a great herd of horses was ridden over the ground to hide every trace. Then grass and trees grew over it, year after year. And after a while, nobody could tell where the place had been.

{img:forest-hills-near-sacred-mountain-quiet-trees-hidden-place-mist}
For many hundreds of years afterwards, the whole area around the mountain was closed. It was called the Great Taboo, which means a forbidden place. Guards kept everyone away from the forests and the hills. Only the khan's family and their special guards were allowed to enter. Even long after the empire was gone, people stayed away from that place. They did it out of respect for the great khan.

The herders in the nearby valleys told quiet stories about the forbidden forest. Some said you could hear horses there at night, though no horses could be seen. Others said the trees there were older and taller than anywhere else. The grandmothers told the children to leave the forest in peace. And the children always did.

--

In our own time, explorers have gone searching for the grave. They have used satellites and aeroplanes and special cameras. They have walked for weeks through the hills and forests. Some of them have found old graves and stone circles from long ago. But nobody has found the grave of Genghis Khan.

And many Mongolians do not want it to be found at all. Let him rest, they say. He wanted his grave to stay secret, and that wish should be respected. And perhaps they are right about that.

{img:mausoleum-shrine-genghis-khan-white-tents-later-pilgrims-incense}
Far away, in the north of China, there is a shrine for Genghis Khan. It is a large building with three domes, shaped like round gers. People come there from far and near to remember him. Families come, and old people, and small children holding their parents' hands. They light candles and burn sweet incense. They offer bowls of milk and bow their heads. It is not his grave, they know that. But it is a quiet place to remember him.

--

On the steppe, the wind blows softly over the long grass. The forests on the sacred mountain are quiet and dark. Somewhere beneath the trees, perhaps, the great khan sleeps. Nobody knows exactly where. And perhaps that is just as it should be.
