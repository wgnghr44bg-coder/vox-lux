In 1220, Genghis Khan chose a place to be the heart of his empire. It was a valley in the middle of Mongolia, beside the river Orkhon. The place was called Karakorum.

At first, Karakorum was really just a great camp on the grass. There were many gers and tents, set up in long rows. There were storehouses and workshops, where smiths and carpenters worked. It was a place to keep supplies and treasure safe. It was a place where people could meet and talk. But in time, it would grow into something much bigger.

--

The Orkhon valley is a very good place to live. It is green and wide, with a clear river and plenty of good grass. Mountains rise to the west and keep off the worst of the wind. And it lies near the middle of all the Mongol lands. Long before the Mongols, other peoples of the steppe had their capitals here too. It is an old and important place, where rulers have often gathered.

After Genghis Khan, his son built a real city at Karakorum. It had walls and a palace, streets and markets and temples. People from all over the empire came to live there. There were Mongol nobles and Chinese craftsmen, Persian traders and visitors from Europe. There were Muslim and Christian and Buddhist priests, each with their own place to pray. All of them lived together in one city, in the middle of the steppe.

{img:karakorum-silver-fountain-tree-palace-hall-guests-later-wonder}
A visitor from Europe described one wonder of Karakorum a few decades later. A great silver tree stood just in front of the palace. It had been made by a skilled craftsman from Paris who had come to Mongolia. At the top of the tree stood a silver angel holding a trumpet. Four silver lions sat around its roots. And from four silver pipes came four different drinks for the guests. There was mare's milk and wine, a sweet honey drink and rice beer. When the khan wanted his guests to drink, the angel lifted its trumpet. And then the drinks began to flow.

--

But in 1220, all of that still lies in the future. For now, Karakorum is just the place where the great camp gathers. Messengers come and go there, and goods are stored in the warehouses. The family of the khan sometimes stays there for a while. Smoke rises from hundreds of gers, and horses graze by the river. Children play between the carts, and dogs bark at the strangers.

{img:orkhon-valley-today-green-river-horses-stone-turtle-later}
Much later, Karakorum was left empty and almost forgotten. Today, very little of the old city is left. A big stone turtle lies quietly in the grass. Long ago, it held up a tall stone tablet on its back. There are buried foundations, and a scattering of old stones. And next to them stands a beautiful Buddhist monastery, built later from some of the old stones. Its white walls are topped with many small white towers. Horses graze all around it, just as they always have.

--

In 1220, Sarnai has never been to Karakorum. It is many days' ride away from her valley. But Batu told her about it before he rode off to the west. There are hundreds of gers there, standing in long rows, he said. There are carts piled high with goods, and horses as far as the eye can see. There are people there from every land under the sky. Sarnai tried hard to imagine it, sitting by the stove. One day, she thinks, when he comes home, maybe we will go there together. And she smiles quietly at the thought.
