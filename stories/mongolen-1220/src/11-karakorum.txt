In 1220, Genghis Khan chose a place for the heart of his empire. A valley in the middle of Mongolia, by the river Orkhon. The place was called Karakorum.

At first, it was just a great camp. A huge gathering of gers and tents. Store houses and workshops. A place to keep supplies and treasure. A place to meet. But it would grow.

--

The Orkhon valley is a good place. It is green and wide, with a river and good grass. Mountains protect it to the west. And it is near the middle of the Mongol lands. Long before the Mongols, other peoples of the steppe had their capitals here too. It is an old place of power.

After Genghis Khan, his son built a real city at Karakorum. With walls and a palace. Streets and markets and temples. People from all over the empire lived there. Mongol nobles. Chinese craftsmen. Persian traders. European visitors. Muslim and Christian and Buddhist priests. All in one place, in the middle of the steppe.

{img:karakorum-silver-fountain-tree-palace-hall-guests-later-wonder}
A visitor from Europe described one wonder of Karakorum a few decades later. A great silver tree stood in front of the palace. It was made by a craftsman from Paris, who had been brought to Mongolia. At the top of the tree stood an angel holding a trumpet. Four silver lions sat at its foot. And from four silver pipes came four different drinks. Mare's milk and wine and honey drink and rice beer. When the khan wanted drinks, the angel blew its trumpet. And the drinks began to flow.

--

But in 1220, all this is still in the future. Now, Karakorum is just the place where the camp is gathered. Where messengers come and go. Where goods are stored. Where the family of the khan sometimes stays.

{img:orkhon-valley-today-green-river-horses-stone-turtle-later}
Later, Karakorum was abandoned and almost forgotten. Today very little is left. A big stone turtle lies in the grass. It once held up a stone tablet. Buried foundations. A few stones. And next to it, a beautiful Buddhist monastery built later, partly from the stones of the old city. Its white walls are topped with many small white towers. Horses graze around it, just as they always have.

--

In 1220, Sarnai has never been to Karakorum. It is many days' ride away. But Batu told her about it before he left. Hundreds of gers in rows, he said. Carts piled with treasure. Horses as far as the eye can see. People from every land. She tried to imagine it. One day, she thinks, when he comes home, maybe we will go together. And she smiles at the thought.
