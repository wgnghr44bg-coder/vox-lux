In the year 1220, a messenger from Genghis Khan arrives at a mountain temple in the north of China. He brings an invitation for an old monk.

The monk's name is Changchun. That means eternal spring. He is a Daoist master. Daoists believe in living in harmony with nature. They seek a long and healthy life through calm and simple living. Changchun is about seventy years old. He is famous for his wisdom.

--

Genghis Khan has heard about him. The khan is getting older. He has heard that Daoist masters know secrets of long life. Maybe even a medicine to live forever. So he wants to meet this monk. He sends an official with a golden tablet and a polite letter. Come to me, the letter says. I am far away in the west. But I will make your journey safe.

Changchun hesitates. He is old. The journey is thousands of kilometres. Over mountains and deserts and the whole steppe. To a place he has never seen. Many younger men would be afraid. But he decides to go. He chooses about eighteen of his students to come with him. And in the spring, they set out.

{img:monk-and-students-setting-out-carts-horses-leaving-temple-spring}
One of his students writes everything down. Every day of the journey. What they see. Who they meet. What the weather is like. His book still exists. It is one of the best descriptions of the Mongol lands at the time of Genghis Khan.

--

First they travel north, out of China. Then they cross the steppe of Mongolia. The student writes about the wide grass and the gers. The people are friendly, he says. They bring us airag and meat. In one place, they meet Mongol women who greet them warmly. In another, they cross a land of strange rocks. They see the sun rise over endless grass.

{img:monk-caravan-crossing-mongolian-steppe-gers-welcoming-families}
They pass through the great camp of the khan's family. The ladies of the court welcome them. They give the old monk felt and furs and food for the journey. And then he goes on. West. Toward the mountains. Toward the khan.

--

Perhaps they pass through the valley where Sarnai's family lives. Perhaps Khulan and Temur see a strange group riding by. An old man with a long white beard and grey robes, sitting very straight on his horse. Young men in robes riding behind him. Mongol guards in front. The children stare. The old monk smiles at them. And then he rides on, over the hill.
