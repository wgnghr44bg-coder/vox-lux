Today, Mongolia is a country of about three and a half million people. It lies between Russia and China. Its capital is Ulaanbaatar, a busy city with tall buildings and traffic and markets.

But outside the city, the steppe is still there. Wide and green and empty, under the huge blue sky.

--

About one in four Mongolians still live as herders. Like their ancestors. They live in gers. They keep sheep and goats and horses and camels and yaks. They move with the seasons. They make airag in the summer and felt in the autumn. They ride horses. Now some also have motorbikes and solar panels and satellite dishes. A family might have a television in their ger. But the life is still the life of the steppe.

Children still learn to ride before they can walk. They still play with ankle bones. They still herd sheep at seven. And they still race horses across the steppe at the summer games.

{img:naadam-today-ulaanbaatar-stadium-wrestlers-crowd-flags-summer}
Naadam is now the national festival of Mongolia. Every July, there are games all over the country. The biggest is in the capital, in a great stadium. Wrestlers in their little vests do the eagle dance. Archers shoot at targets. And children race horses across the open steppe. The whole country stops to watch.

--

Genghis Khan is a national hero in Mongolia. His face is on the money. The airport is named after him. Near the capital, there is a giant silver statue of him on a horse. It is forty metres tall. You can climb up inside it and stand on the horse's head. From there, you can see the steppe for kilometres around.

So we leave the steppe tonight, in the year 1220, as the evening falls.

--

The sun is setting over the grassland. The sky turns gold, then pink, then deep blue. The sheep and goats are in their pens. The foals are tied in their line. The horses graze on the hillside, their shapes dark against the sky. Smoke rises from the crown of the ger. Inside, the fire glows. Sarnai sits by the stove, mending a boot. Khulan stirs the airag. Temur oils his little bow. Altan and Bayar are asleep under a sheepskin. The grandmother hums an old song. The big black dog lies across the doorway. And outside, the stars come out one by one. More and more. Until the whole sky is white with them. Over the sleeping camp. Over the endless grass. Under the Eternal Blue Sky.
