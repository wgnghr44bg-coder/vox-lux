So we leave Rochdale tonight, in the year 1840, as the evening falls. The mill bell has rung for the last time today. The great steam engine has slowed, and stopped. The thousands of machines are still. And the workers pour out of the mill gates, into the streets, tired, but glad that the day is over. The clatter of their clogs echoes between the houses, as they walk home.

The sky above the town is turning pink and gold, behind the chimneys. The smoke is thinning, now that the boilers are cooling. The first stars are coming out, over the moors. And one by one, the gas lamps are being lit, along the streets, by the lamplighter, with his long pole, going from lamp to lamp.

--

{img:spring-street-evening-windows-glowing-family-entering-house-clogs}
In Spring Street, the Hartleys come home. Grandad Joseph has the fire burning, and the kettle boiling, and a pot of stew bubbling on the range. The family takes off their clogs and shawls, and washes their hands and faces in a bowl of warm water. And they sit down together, at the scrubbed table, for their tea. Bread, and stew, and hot tea with sugar. It tastes wonderful, after the long day.

After tea, they sit by the fire. Tom smokes his pipe, and reads the newspaper. Sarah mends a shirt, by the light of a candle. Ellen reads, quietly, a book of poems. Jack sits on the rag rug, by Grandad Joseph's feet, and listens to one of his stories, about the old days, and the moors, and the weavers, and the boggarts. The fire crackles, and the kettle sings, and the clock on the mantelpiece, which does not work, says ten past three, as always.

--

{img:family-by-firelight-terraced-house-kitchen-grandfather-story-boy-listening}
Then it is time for bed. The children go up first, with a candle, up the narrow stairs. Ellen helps Jack into bed, and tucks him in, and blows out the candle. Then Tom and Sarah go up too, and Grandad Joseph settles down in his bed, in the back room, by the warm wall of the range. And the house is quiet.

Outside, the town is quiet too. The streets are empty. The gas lamps glow in the mist. The mill stands dark and silent, by the river, waiting for the morning. The river runs quietly past it, under the stars. Up on the moors, the wind blows softly over the heather. And far away, an owl calls, from the old woods above the town.
