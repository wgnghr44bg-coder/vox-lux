Under the streets of San Francisco ran a network of iron pipes, carrying water from reservoirs outside the city.

And when the earth moved, those pipes had cracked, and broken apart.

In many parts of the city, when people turned on their taps, only a trickle came out.

[pause]

At the same time, in kitchens and shops and factories, small fires had started.

A stove had tipped over here.

[pause]

A lamp had fallen there.

[pause]

Electric wires had come down, and sparked.

[long-pause]

On any other day, the firemen of San Francisco would have put out fires like these very quickly.

[pause]

They were skilled, and brave, and well trained.

[pause]

But on this morning, they had two great problems.

[pause]

There were many fires, all at once, in different parts of the city.

[pause]

And when they connected their hoses to the hydrants, there was very little water.

[long-pause]

To make matters worse, the fire chief, Dennis Sullivan, had been badly hurt that morning, when part of the building next to his firehouse fell during the earthquake.

[pause]

The man who knew the most about fighting fire in San Francisco could not lead his men.

[pause]

He would not recover.

[pause]

His firemen would have to face the days ahead without him.

One of the fires that morning began in the most ordinary way.

[pause]

In a neighbourhood called Hayes Valley, a woman decided to make breakfast for her family.

[pause]

After such a frightening morning, it must have seemed the most natural thing in the world.

[pause]

She lit her kitchen stove, and began to cook.

[long-pause]

But she did not know that her chimney had been damaged in the earthquake.

[pause]

Sparks escaped through the cracks, and caught the wooden walls of the house.

[pause]

And before long, the whole neighbourhood was in danger.

People came to call it the ham and eggs fire.

[pause]

It is a strangely gentle name, for something that caused so much trouble.

People were simply trying to carry on with their day.
