Close your eyes if you'd like.

[pause]

Tonight, we are travelling back a little more than a hundred years, to a city on the edge of the Pacific Ocean.

[pause]

A city of steep hills, and cable cars, and soft grey fog that rolls in from the sea each evening.

[long-pause]

We are not here to be frightened.

[pause]

We are here to walk slowly through its streets, and to listen to the story of a morning in the spring of nineteen hundred and six, and of everything that came after.

[long-pause]

You don't need to remember any of this.

[pause]

You don't need to follow every name, or every date.

[pause]

Just let the story drift past you, the way the fog drifts over the hills, quietly, and without any hurry at all.

[long-pause]

If you fall asleep before the end, that's alright.

[pause]

The city will still be there.

[pause]

It has a way of always coming back.

[long-pause]

{img:strait-without-bridge}
Let's begin at the water.

[pause]

On the western edge of North America, there is a narrow opening in the coastline, where the ocean meets a great sheltered bay.

[pause]

Today, a tall red bridge crosses that opening.

[pause]

But in our story, there is no bridge yet.

[pause]

Only the water, moving in and out with the tides, and the steep green headlands on either side.

[long-pause]

Sailors called this opening the Golden Gate.

[pause]

Beyond it, the bay spreads out wide and calm, with islands resting in its middle, and gentle hills rising all around its shores.

[pause]

It is one of the finest natural harbours in the world.

[long-pause]

And almost every summer evening, something quiet happens here.

[pause]

Out at sea, a bank of fog gathers.

[pause]

It creeps towards the land, low and white, and then it slips in through the Golden Gate, as if the bay were breathing it in.

[pause]

It spills over the hills, and flows down into the valleys, and covers the rooftops like a soft grey blanket.

[long-pause]

People who live here say you can hear the fog before you see it.

[pause]

Out on the water, the foghorns begin to call, low and slow, one after another.

[pause]

It is a sound that has been lulling this city to sleep for a very long time.

[long-pause]

{img:ohlone-shore}
Long before there was any city, this land was home to the Ohlone people.

[pause]

For thousands of years, they lived in small villages around the bay.

[pause]

They gathered acorns from the oak trees, and ground them into flour.

[pause]

They fished in the shallow water, and collected mussels and oysters along the shore.

[pause]

And they built light boats from bundles of tule reeds, which floated easily across the calm water.

[long-pause]

Over the centuries, the shells from all those meals piled up into great mounds along the shoreline.

[pause]

Some of them were as tall as a house.

[pause]

They were a kind of quiet record, showing how many generations had lived beside this water, and been fed by it.

[long-pause]

{img:presidio-spanish-fort}
In the year seventeen seventy six, Spanish soldiers and missionaries arrived.

[pause]

They built a small fort near the Golden Gate, which they called the Presidio.

[pause]

And a few miles inland, in a sheltered valley where the fog was thinner and the sun came out more often, they built a mission church.

[long-pause]

{img:mission-dolores}
The church was made of adobe, thick walls of sun-dried clay bricks, covered with white plaster.

[pause]

Its roof was held up by great wooden beams, tied together with strips of rawhide.

[pause]

Inside, the ceiling was painted with patterns in soft reds and ochres, in designs taken from Ohlone baskets.

[long-pause]

Its official name was the Mission of Saint Francis of Assisi.

[pause]

But people came to call it Mission Dolores, after a little creek that once ran nearby.

[pause]

It is still standing today, the oldest building in the whole city.

[pause]

And that is something worth remembering, as our story goes on.

[long-pause]

For many years, very little happened here.

[pause]

There was the fort, and the mission, and a few ranches where cattle grazed on the golden hills.

[pause]

Then, near a small cove on the eastern side, a little trading village grew up.

[pause]

It was called Yerba Buena, after a sweet-smelling wild mint that grew on the slopes nearby.

[long-pause]

Yerba Buena was a sleepy place.

[pause]

A few wooden houses, a store or two, and a muddy beach where ships would sometimes anchor to trade for cattle hides.

[pause]

By the middle of the eighteen forties, only a few hundred people lived there.

[long-pause]

In eighteen forty seven, the little village took a new name.

[pause]

It would be called San Francisco, after the mission, and after the great bay beside it.

[pause]

At the time, it hardly seemed to matter.

[pause]

It was still just a small, quiet settlement at the edge of the world.

[long-pause]

But that was about to change, very quickly indeed.
