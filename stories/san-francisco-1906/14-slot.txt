The same sound that people heard in eighteen forty nine, and in nineteen hundred and six, and every foggy evening since.

Beneath the street, the cable is still humming, just as it did more than a hundred years ago.

Past Lotta's Fountain, standing quietly on its corner.

Past the Palace Hotel, where Enrico Caruso once looked out of his window on a spring morning.

Its thick clay walls have stood through more than two hundred years, and through two great earthquakes.

[pause]

It is reflected in the still water of the lagoon, trembling a little when a swan glides past.

[pause]

It is very peaceful here.

[pause]

A place built to celebrate a city that had risen again.

[long-pause]

Deep beneath all of it, the two great plates of the Earth are still moving, slowly, patiently, side by side.

[pause]

About as fast as your fingernails grow.

[pause]

The people of San Francisco know this.

[pause]

They have learned to build for it, and to prepare for it, and then, mostly, to get on with their lives.
