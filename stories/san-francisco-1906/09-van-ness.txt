Van Ness Avenue was one of the widest streets in San Francisco.

It ran from north to south, from the bay down towards the Mission district, like a long, broad river of stone.

[pause]

It was lined with fine houses and churches.

[pause]

[long-pause]

So the soldiers and firemen decided that this was where they would make their stand.

[pause]

They would try to stop the fire here, at the wide avenue, and save the western half of the city.

[long-pause]

To do this, they had to make a firebreak.

[pause]

That meant clearing away the buildings along one side of the avenue, so that the fire would reach an empty space, with nothing left to burn.

[pause]

It was a very hard decision.

[pause]

These were people's homes.

[pause]

[long-pause]

The work went on through the night of the second day, and into the third.

[pause]

Firemen, soldiers and sailors worked side by side.

[pause]

They stretched hoses all the way from the bay.

[pause]

And when the fire finally reached Van Ness Avenue, they fought it with everything they had.

[long-pause]

In some places, the fire jumped across the avenue.

[pause]

Each time, the men raced to put it out.

[pause]

Beating out sparks, spraying water, pulling down burning fences.

[long-pause]

And then, slowly, the fire began to lose its strength.

On the morning of Saturday, the twenty first of April, three days after the earthquake, the last of the fires was finally put out.

The line had held.

[pause]

West of Van Ness Avenue, the houses were still standing.

The railroad company offered free tickets to anyone who wanted to leave, to go and stay with friends or family elsewhere in California, or anywhere in the country.
