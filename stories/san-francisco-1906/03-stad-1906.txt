{img:market-street-1906}
Now let's step into San Francisco in the spring of nineteen hundred and six.

[pause]

It is a big city now, with more than four hundred thousand people.

[pause]

The biggest city anywhere on the western side of the country.

[long-pause]

We'll start on Market Street, the great wide avenue that cuts through the heart of the city, running in a long straight line from the hills down to the bay.

[pause]

It is busy from morning until night.

[pause]

Horse-drawn wagons rattle over the paving stones, loaded with barrels, and crates, and sacks of flour.

[pause]

Men in bowler hats hurry along the pavements, and women in long skirts and wide hats step carefully around the puddles.

[pause]

Newspaper boys call out the headlines on every corner.

[long-pause]

And down the middle of the street, the cable cars come gliding past, their bells ringing clear and bright.

[long-pause]

{img:cable-car-hill}
The cable cars are one of the marvels of San Francisco.

[pause]

The city's hills are so steep that horses could barely climb them, especially on wet days, when the stones were slippery.

[pause]

It is said that a man named Andrew Hallidie once watched a team of horses struggling up a hill with a heavy streetcar, and decided there had to be a better way.

[long-pause]

His idea was simple, and very clever.

[pause]

Beneath the street, in a narrow slot, a thick steel cable runs in an endless loop, pulled along by great wheels in a powerhouse.

[pause]

When the driver of the car pulls a lever, a kind of grip reaches down through the slot and takes hold of the moving cable.

[pause]

And the car is carried smoothly up the hill, no matter how steep.

[long-pause]

If you stand quietly at the side of the road, you can hear the cable humming beneath your feet.

[pause]

A low, steady sound, that runs all day long under the city.

[pause]

Like a heartbeat.

[long-pause]

{img:ferry-building-clock}
At the bottom of Market Street, right at the edge of the water, stands the Ferry Building.

[pause]

It is quite new in our story, finished only a few years earlier.

[pause]

It has a long hall of grey stone, and a tall clock tower rising from the middle, modelled on a bell tower in Spain.

[long-pause]

There is no bridge across the bay yet.

[pause]

So every day, tens of thousands of people cross the water by ferry, from Oakland, and Berkeley, and the towns to the north.

[pause]

The ferries are big white steamboats, with paddle wheels, and open decks where passengers can stand and watch the gulls.

[pause]

In the evening, the Ferry Building clock glows over the water, telling everyone whether they will catch the last boat home.

[long-pause]

{img:palace-hotel-court}
A little way up Market Street stands one of the grandest buildings in the city.

[pause]

It is the Palace Hotel.

[long-pause]

When it opened, some thirty years before our story, it was said to be the largest hotel in the world.

[pause]

It is seven storeys high, with hundreds of rooms, and rows and rows of bay windows looking out over the street.

[long-pause]

In the middle of the hotel is a great open court, covered by a glass roof.

[pause]

Carriages drive straight in from the street, and circle around, so that guests can step down in comfort, out of the wind and the rain.

[pause]

All around the court, balconies with white columns rise up, floor after floor, towards the glass ceiling far above.

[long-pause]

The hotel has its own wells, and great tanks of water on the roof.

[pause]

It has electric lights, and hydraulic elevators, which the guests like to call rising rooms.

[pause]

Kings and presidents and famous actors have stayed here.

[pause]

And in our story, a very famous singer is about to arrive.

[long-pause]

{img:chinatown-lanterns}
Let's walk on, up the slope, into Chinatown.

[pause]

Chinese people had come to San Francisco since the days of the gold rush.

[pause]

They worked in the mines, and on the railroads, and in laundries, and shops, and farms, and fishing camps.

[pause]

Life was often hard for them.

[pause]

There were unfair laws, and much prejudice, and many were not allowed to live anywhere else in the city.

[long-pause]

But inside these few crowded blocks, they built a world of their own.

[pause]

The streets are narrow, and full of life.

[pause]

Paper lanterns hang from the balconies.

[pause]

Shops sell tea, and silk, and dried mushrooms, and herbs in little wooden drawers.

[pause]

There are temples filled with the scent of incense, and theatres where the music of gongs and strings goes on late into the night.

[long-pause]

{img:north-beach-telegraph-hill}
Nearby, on the hills to the north, are the Italian neighbourhoods of North Beach and Telegraph Hill.

[pause]

Here, fishermen mend their nets, and bakers pull warm loaves from their ovens.

[pause]

And in many of the little wooden houses on the hill, families keep barrels of homemade red wine in their cellars.

[pause]

Remember that wine.

[pause]

It will turn out to be important.

[long-pause]

{img:nob-hill-mansions-1906}
Above Chinatown rises Nob Hill.

[pause]

This is where the richest people in the city have built their mansions.

[pause]

Railroad owners, and silver kings, and bankers.

[pause]

Their houses are enormous, with towers, and turrets, and stained glass windows, and gardens behind stone walls.

[pause]

On the very top of the hill, a brand new hotel called the Fairmont is almost finished, with white stone walls gleaming in the sun.

[long-pause]

{img:golden-gate-park}
And far to the west, where the city meets the ocean, there is Golden Gate Park.

[pause]

Not so long ago, this land was nothing but sand dunes, blowing in the wind off the sea.

[pause]

People said nothing would ever grow there.

[long-pause]

But a young engineer, and later a patient Scottish gardener named John McLaren, set out to prove them wrong.

[pause]

They planted grasses to hold the sand in place.

[pause]

Then shrubs, and then trees, thousands and thousands of them.

[pause]

Slowly, year by year, the dunes turned into meadows and forests.

[long-pause]

Now the park is a place of lakes and lawns, and winding paths beneath tall cypress trees.

[pause]

There is a conservatory full of tropical flowers, under a white glass dome.

[pause]

There is a small herd of buffalo, grazing in a paddock.

[pause]

And near the ocean end, a Dutch windmill turns slowly in the sea breeze, pumping water for the gardens.

[long-pause]

On Sunday afternoons, families come here to picnic, and to row boats on the lake, and to listen to the band playing in the music stand.

[pause]

It is a peaceful, green place.

[pause]

And in the days to come, it will become a home to many thousands of people.

[long-pause]

That is the city, in the spring of nineteen hundred and six.

[pause]

Busy, and proud, and full of life.

[pause]

A little noisy, a little rough around the edges, but always looking forward.

[pause]

Nobody here is thinking about the ground beneath their feet.
