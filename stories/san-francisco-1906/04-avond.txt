It is the evening of Tuesday, the seventeenth of April, nineteen hundred and six.

[pause]

The day has been mild, and the evening is clear and still.

On Mission Street, a crowd is gathering outside the Grand Opera House.

[pause]

Carriages pull up one after another, and ladies step down in silk gowns and long gloves, with feathers in their hair.

[pause]

Gentlemen in black evening coats hold the doors open.

[pause]

Everyone is talking about the same thing.

[long-pause]

The Metropolitan Opera company has come all the way from New York, travelling across the country by train, with its singers, and its orchestra, and whole railway cars full of costumes and scenery.

[pause]

And tonight, the most famous tenor in the world is singing.

[pause]

His name is Enrico Caruso.

[long-pause]

{img:caruso-stage-carmen}
Caruso was born in Naples, in the south of Italy, not far from Mount Vesuvius.

[pause]

He grew up in a poor family, one of many children, and as a boy he sang in church, and in the streets, for a few coins.

[pause]

Now, he is famous everywhere.

[pause]

People queue for hours to hear him, and his voice can be heard on gramophone records in homes all over the world.

[long-pause]

Tonight the opera is Carmen.

[pause]

Caruso plays Don José, a soldier who falls in love.

[pause]

The theatre is full, from the stalls all the way up to the highest balconies.

[pause]

Thousands of people, sitting in the warm golden light, listening in silence.

[long-pause]

When the last notes fade away, the audience rises, and the applause goes on and on.

[pause]

Flowers are thrown onto the stage.

[pause]

Caruso bows, and smiles, and bows again.

[long-pause]

Afterwards, he goes to supper with friends, and then returns to his rooms at the Palace Hotel.

[pause]

It has been a long evening, and a happy one.

[pause]

He will sleep well tonight.

The last cable cars rattle home to their barns.

On Nob Hill, the great mansions stand dark and silent behind their walls.
