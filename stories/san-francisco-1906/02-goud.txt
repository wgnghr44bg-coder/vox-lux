He tested them as well as he could, and before long, there was no doubt.

At first, the news travelled slowly.

[pause]

People heard the rumours, and shrugged.

[pause]

Then a shopkeeper walked through the streets of San Francisco, holding up a small bottle of gold dust, and calling out the news to anyone who would listen.

[pause]

After that, nothing could stop it.

Within weeks, the little town had almost emptied.

[pause]

Shopkeepers closed their stores.

[pause]

Sailors left their ships.

Even the newspaper stopped printing, because everyone who worked there had gone off to look for gold.

[long-pause]

And then, from all over the world, people began to arrive.

[pause]

They came from the eastern states of America, crossing the plains and the mountains in covered wagons.

[pause]

[pause]

They came from Mexico, and Chile, and China, and Australia, and France, and Germany, and Ireland.

[long-pause]

They were called the forty-niners, after the year eighteen forty nine, when most of them arrived.

[pause]

And almost all of them came through the Golden Gate, into the harbour of San Francisco.

Hundreds of them, their tall masts standing together like a leafless forest.

As soon as they dropped anchor, their crews jumped ashore and headed for the gold fields, leaving the ships floating empty in the bay.

So the people of San Francisco found other uses for them.

[pause]

As the town grew, the shoreline was filled in with sand and rubble, and new streets were built out over the water.

[pause]

Some of those old ships were simply buried where they lay.

[pause]

Even today, when builders dig down beneath the streets of the city's financial district, they sometimes find the wooden hull of a gold rush ship, resting quietly in the old mud.

In just two years, the population of San Francisco grew from about a thousand people to more than twenty thousand.

[pause]

There was nowhere near enough room for them all.

People lived in tents, and in shacks made of packing crates, and in canvas houses that glowed like lanterns at night, when a candle was lit inside.

[long-pause]

[pause]

Prices were astonishing.

[long-pause]

And because nearly everything was built of wood and canvas, fire was a constant worry.

In the first years of the gold rush, the young city burned several times.

[pause]

[pause]

[pause]

Building, and rebuilding, and never quite giving up.

In fact, the seal of the city shows a bird called a phoenix, rising from the flames.

It is a reminder of those early days, and of a city that would not stay down for long.

By the end of the nineteenth century, San Francisco had become the greatest city on the western coast of America.

[pause]

It was a place of ships and trade, of new arrivals and big dreams.

[pause]

A city that had grown up almost overnight, and had never stopped moving since.
