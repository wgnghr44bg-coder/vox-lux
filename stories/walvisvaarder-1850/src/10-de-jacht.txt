When the whales are sighted, the captain gives the order to lower the boats. The whaleboats swing out from the davits and drop into the sea. The crews climb down into them. Six men in each boat. Four oarsmen, the harpooner at the front, and the mate at the back, steering. Will is one of the oarsmen in Mr. Swift's boat. Joaquim is his harpooner.

--

As we said at the beginning, we will not follow the hunt closely. It was hard and dangerous work, and it ended the lives of many whales. Instead, let us watch from a distance, from the deck of the ship, the way the cook and the carpenter and the cabin boy watched it.

{img:distant-whaleboats-small-on-wide-sea-sails-up-ship-deck-watching}
The boats spread out over the sea, rowing quietly toward the whales. Sometimes the men put up small sails, so they can move without making a sound. From the ship, the boats look tiny, like little beetles on a wide blue table. The whales are still far away. Their spouts rise and fall in the sunlight. The men in the boats row for a long time. The sun climbs higher. The ship follows slowly behind.

--

In the boat, Will rows with all his strength. He sits with his back to the whales, as all oarsmen do. So he cannot see where they are going. He can only see the face of Mr. Swift, at the back of the boat, watching the sea ahead. Mr. Swift speaks quietly, encouraging them. Row, lads, row softly. Steady now. Will's heart is pounding. His arms are aching. And somewhere behind him, very close now, he hears a great, deep breath, like the sigh of a giant.

{img:whale-surfacing-near-small-boat-mist-of-breath-calm-sea-awe}
Will looks over his shoulder, just for a second. And he sees the whale. It is so close, he could almost touch it. A vast, dark, wrinkled back, rising from the water like a small island. A cloud of warm mist drifts over the boat, smelling of the sea. For a moment, everything is perfectly still. The whale, the boat, the men. And Will feels something he will remember all his life. Not fear, exactly. Something more like wonder.

--

What happens next, we will leave to the whalemen. Some whales escaped that day, diving deep into the ocean and swimming away. One did not. In the late afternoon, the boats come back to the ship, slowly, towing the whale behind them. The men are exhausted. Their hands are bleeding from the ropes. They are wet through, and some of them are shaking. Nobody says very much.

{img:tired-whalemen-climbing-back-aboard-ship-evening-light-quiet}
That evening, Will sits alone at the rail, looking at the sunset. He is glad that everyone in his boat is safe. But he also feels strange and quiet inside. Joaquim comes and sits beside him. He does not say anything for a while. Then he says, softly, that he always feels this way too, after a hunt. That the whale is a great creature, and that one should always respect it. Will nods. And they sit together, watching the last light fade from the sky.

Later, the old sailors tell Will that this feeling never quite goes away. Joaquim has been whaling for many years, and still he says a quiet prayer for the whale afterwards, in Portuguese. Many whalemen respected the whales deeply. They admired their size and strength, and their care for each other. They did their work because it was their living. But many of them, in their hearts, loved the whales too.
