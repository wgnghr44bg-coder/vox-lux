The ship that Will is joining is called the Hannah Grey. She is a whaling ship of New Bedford, about thirty-five metres long, with three tall masts. She is not new. She has already made five long voyages around the world. But she is strong and well built, and her captain knows her well.

--

A whaling ship looks a little different from other sailing ships. Along her sides, hanging from wooden arms called davits, there are four small boats. These are the whaleboats. They are long, narrow and light, pointed at both ends, made of thin cedar planks. When a whale is seen, the whaleboats are lowered into the sea, and the men row out in them. A fifth boat is kept on deck, as a spare.

{img:whaleboats-hanging-davits-ship-side-oars-coiled-rope-tubs-close}
In the middle of the deck, there is something very strange, for a wooden ship. It is a big brick oven, with two huge iron pots set into the top. This is called the try-works. It is where the oil is boiled out of the whale blubber, at sea. Under the bricks, there is a shallow tank of water, to protect the wooden deck from the heat. When the try-works are not in use, they are covered with a wooden lid.

--

Below the deck, there are several parts of the ship. At the front, in the forecastle, the ordinary sailors live and sleep. It is a small, dark, triangular room, with bunks along the walls, one above the other. There is a table in the middle, and a little lamp hanging from the ceiling. About twenty men live there, with their sea chests. It is crowded and stuffy, and it smells of tar and wet wool and old tobacco.

{img:forecastle-whaling-ship-bunks-sea-chests-sailors-lamp-dim-crowded}
In the middle of the ship, below the deck, there is the steerage, where the harpooners and the boatsteerers live. They have a little more space than the ordinary sailors. And at the back of the ship, there are the cabins of the officers. The captain has the biggest cabin, with windows in the stern looking out over the sea. There is also a little dining room, where the officers eat together.

--

At the very bottom of the ship is the hold. This is where the barrels of oil are stored, thousands of them. At the start of the voyage, the hold is full of barrels of food and water, and empty barrels waiting to be filled with oil. There are also spare sails and ropes, wood and iron, tools and everything else the ship will need for three or four years. A whaling ship is like a little floating town, carrying everything it needs.

{img:ship-hold-barrels-stacked-lantern-cooper-working-casks-dim-light}
When Will first goes on board, he is amazed by how small everything is. The ship looked so big from the wharf. But inside, every space is full. He finds his bunk in the forecastle, a narrow wooden shelf just long enough for him to lie down. He puts his sea chest beside it. He takes out his grandfather's scrimshaw and holds it in his hand. Then he goes up on deck, to look at the harbour, and the town, and the ship that is now his home.

On deck, there are other things Will has never seen before. Near the back of the ship, there is a little wooden house, with a door and windows. It is the entrance to the captain's cabin. There is a large wheel, for steering the ship, and a compass in a wooden box, called the binnacle. And everywhere, there are coils of rope, neatly laid down on the deck in round spirals, so that they can run out freely without tangling.

The ship has a figurehead too, at the very front, under the long pole called the bowsprit. It is a carved wooden woman in a blue dress, with her hair blowing back, as if she is looking out over the sea. Sailors believe a figurehead watches over the ship and keeps her safe. Every few years, someone repaints her dress and her face, so she always looks fresh and bright.
