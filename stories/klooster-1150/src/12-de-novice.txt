How does a man become a monk at Rievaulx?

In the older Benedictine monasteries, many monks were given to the monastery as little children, by their parents, and grew up there. But the Cistercians do not accept children. A man must be at least fifteen, and later eighteen, and must come of his own free will.

--

When a young man comes to the gate, and asks to become a monk, he is not welcomed at once.

Saint Benedict wrote that the newcomer should not be given an easy entrance. He should be made to wait, at the gate, for four or five days, and treated rather roughly, to see if he really means it, and is patient. Then, if he persists, he is allowed into the guest house, for a few days.

And then, if he still wishes to stay, he is taken to the novices' quarters, and becomes a novice.

--

The novice spends a year learning the life of the monastery, under the care of an older monk, the master of novices. Aelred himself was master of novices at Rievaulx, before he became abbot.

The novice learns the Rule of Saint Benedict, which is read to him three times during the year. He learns the psalms by heart, all hundred and fifty of them. He learns the chant, and the services, and the customs of the monastery, and the sign language. He learns to read Latin, if he does not already know it. He learns to keep silence, and to obey, and to work.

{img:master-of-novices-teaching-young-monks-psalms-room}
And at the end of each part of the year, he is asked again: do you still wish to stay? He may leave at any time, and many do. The life is hard.

--

At the end of the year, if the novice still wishes to stay, and the monks agree that he is suitable, he makes his profession.

In the church, in front of all the monks, he promises stability, to stay in this monastery for the rest of his life. Conversion of life, to live as a monk. And obedience, to his abbot. He writes his promise on a piece of parchment, in his own hand, or, if he cannot write, someone writes it for him, and he makes a cross. And he lays it on the altar.

{img:young-monk-kneeling-altar-profession-parchment-candles}
Then he sings, three times, a verse from the psalms: receive me, O Lord, according to your word, and I shall live, and do not disappoint me in my hope. And all the monks sing it after him. And he prostrates himself, at the feet of each of the monks, one by one, and asks them to pray for him.

--

Then he is given the white robe, the cowl, of a Cistercian monk. And from that day, he belongs to the monastery, for the rest of his life.

Everything he owned before, he must give away, to the poor, or to the monastery. He keeps nothing. He has no money, no property, nothing that is his own, not even a book, or a pen. Everything he needs is given to him by the abbot: his clothes, his bed, his food, his tools.

What does a monk own? The Rule says: a cowl, and a tunic, and shoes, and stockings, and a belt, and a knife, and a pen, and a writing tablet, and a needle, and a handkerchief. And a mattress, a blanket, a cover, and a pillow. Nothing more.

--

Why do men choose this life? For many reasons.

Some, like Aelred, feel that the world is empty, and long for God. Some want to save their souls, and to make up for their sins. Some are looking for peace, and quiet, and safety, in a violent world. Some are poor, and want security. Some are drawn by the beauty of the life, the singing, and the silence, and the order. Some come because their friends are coming, or because they have heard a great preacher, like Bernard, and been moved.

{img:young-monks-walking-cloister-together-silent-spring}
And once they have come, most of them stay, for the rest of their lives, in the valley of the Rye, among their brothers.
