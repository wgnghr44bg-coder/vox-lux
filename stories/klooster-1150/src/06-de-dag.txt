After the services of the night and the dawn, the day of the monks goes on, in a fixed rhythm, which is the same every day, all year round, with small changes for the seasons, and the feasts.

The day is divided by the services in the church, which the monks call the Divine Office, or the Work of God, the opus Dei. Seven times a day, and once in the night, the bell rings, and the monks stop whatever they are doing, and go to the church, to pray and sing.

--

After Lauds, at sunrise, there is Prime, the first hour. And then the monks go to the chapter house, for the daily meeting, the chapter.

They sit on stone benches, around the walls, with the abbot in his seat, at the end. A monk reads a chapter of the Rule of Saint Benedict, aloud. Then the abbot speaks, about the Rule, or about the life of the monastery. Then the names of monks who have passed away, in other monasteries, are read, and the monks pray for them.

And then comes the chapter of faults. Any monk who has broken one of the rules, by being late for a service, or breaking a dish, or speaking when he should be silent, comes forward, and kneels, and confesses it, and receives a small penance, from the abbot. Other monks may also point out, kindly, the faults of their brothers. It is a way of keeping the community honest, and humble.

--

Then, in the morning, there is work.

The Cistercians believe, like Saint Benedict, that work is a kind of prayer, and that monks should work with their hands, as the apostles did. Idleness, Benedict wrote, is the enemy of the soul.

{img:monks-working-fields-hoes-white-habits-tucked-up-sunny}
So the monks go out to work, in silence: in the gardens, and the orchards, and the fields, digging, and planting, and weeding, and harvesting. Or in the workshops, mending shoes, and clothes, and tools. Or in the kitchen, or the bakehouse. Or cleaning the buildings. At harvest time, everyone, even the abbot, goes out to help bring in the crops.

--

At about nine o'clock, the bell rings for Terce, the third hour. Then there is the Mass, the most important service of the day, when the monks gather around the altar, and the priest offers the bread and the wine.

At midday, there is Sext, the sixth hour. And then, in summer, the main meal of the day, dinner, in the refectory. In winter, during the fasting season, dinner is later, in the afternoon.

After dinner, in summer, the monks may rest for a while, on their beds, in the dormitory, because they rose so early, and the days are long. It is called the meridian, the midday rest.

--

In the early afternoon, there is None, the ninth hour. And then more work, or reading.

Every monk must spend some hours each day in lectio divina, holy reading. He sits in the cloister, on a stone bench, or in a little carrel, a small wooden desk, against the wall, with a book from the library, and reads, slowly, aloud, in a low murmur, a few lines at a time, from the Bible, or the writings of the saints, and thinks about them, and prays.

{img:monk-reading-book-cloister-carrel-afternoon-sun-quiet}
It is not like our reading, fast, for information. It is slow, and repeated, like chewing, the monks say, so that the words sink deep into the heart, and nourish the soul.

--

In the late afternoon, as the sun goes down, there is Vespers, the evening service, with psalms, and the beautiful song of Mary, the Magnificat, sung by all the monks, as the light fades in the church.

Then a light supper, in summer. In winter, during the fast, there is no supper, only a drink, and a little bread.

And then the monks gather in the cloister, or the chapter house, and one of them reads aloud, from a book of the lives of the saints, or the sayings of the old monks of the desert, by the light of a lamp. This is called the collation.

--

And last of all, as darkness falls, there is Compline, the last service of the day, the completion, short and quiet, with the psalms of the night. The monks pray for protection, during the night, and sing a hymn to Mary, the Salve Regina, hail holy queen, softly, in the dark church.

And then the abbot sprinkles each monk with holy water, as they leave the church. And the Great Silence begins, which lasts all night, until the morning. Nobody speaks, at all, for any reason.

{img:monks-climbing-stairs-dormitory-candle-evening-silence}
The monks climb the stairs to the dormitory, and lie down on their beds, in their robes, and go to sleep, at about seven or eight o'clock in the evening, in winter. A little lamp burns all night, in the dormitory. And outside, the river flows, and the owls call, in the woods of the valley.
