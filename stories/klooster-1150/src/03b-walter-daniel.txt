Much of what we know about Rievaulx in the time of Aelred comes from one monk, Walter Daniel.

Walter came to Rievaulx as a young man, in about 1150, the very year of our story. He was clever, and educated, and had trained as a doctor, it seems, before he became a monk. He was a bit proud, and quick-tempered, and liked to argue. But he loved Aelred deeply, and Aelred loved him, and was patient with him.

--

Walter lived at Rievaulx for the rest of his life. He was with Aelred for seventeen years, and was with him when he passed away. And soon afterwards, he wrote a book about Aelred's life, in Latin, called The Life of Aelred.

It is a wonderful book, full of little details about life at Rievaulx: how Aelred talked, and walked, and prayed, and worked. How he treated his monks. How he endured his illness. What happened when difficult monks came. And many little stories, some funny, some sad, some strange.

Some people at the time complained that Walter had exaggerated, and made Aelred sound too perfect, like a saint in a story. So Walter wrote a letter, to defend his book, and added more details, and the names of people who had seen what he described.

--

Walter tells us, for example, that Aelred had a little room built, next to the infirmary, when he became too ill to walk, with a little bath, or pool, beneath the floor, filled from a spring, where he would sit in cold water, in his youth, to calm his passions.

He tells us that, in his little room, Aelred would let the monks come in, freely, every day, and sit around him, and talk with him, and that nobody else in the monastery was allowed to talk so freely, except in Aelred's room.

{img:old-monks-and-young-gathered-sick-abbot-room-warm-light}
He tells us that Aelred was so gentle, that once, when a difficult monk shouted at him, and insulted him, in front of the others, Aelred simply waited, until the man had finished, and then answered him quietly and kindly, and the man was ashamed, and asked his forgiveness.

--

And he tells us about Aelred's last days, in January 1167, in great detail: how he gave his last advice to his monks, and gave them his books, and his little cross, and his relics. How he lay on the floor, on a hair cloth and ashes, as monks did when they were dying. How the monks gathered around him, and sang. And how, at the very end, Walter himself held Aelred's head in his hands.

{img:winter-dawn-valley-abbey-snow-quiet-bell-tower}
Thanks to Walter Daniel, and his book, the monks of Rievaulx, who lived and died nearly nine hundred years ago, are not just names, or ruins. We can still almost see them, and hear them, in the valley, in the cloister, around their abbot's bed, talking, and laughing, and praying.
