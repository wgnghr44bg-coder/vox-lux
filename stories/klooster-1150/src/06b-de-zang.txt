The monks of Rievaulx spend many hours a day singing. And their singing is one of the most beautiful things about their life.

They sing Gregorian chant, which is named after Pope Gregory the Great, who lived about five hundred years before, although the chant itself is probably older, and grew slowly, over the centuries, in the churches and the monasteries of Europe.

--

Gregorian chant is very simple. It is sung by all the monks together, in unison, all singing the same note, at the same time, without harmony, and without any instruments: no organ, no harps, no bells, only the human voice.

The melodies are long, and flowing, and calm, rising and falling gently, like waves, or like breathing. There is no steady beat, like in modern music. The music follows the rhythm of the Latin words. Sometimes, one syllable is sung on many notes, in a long, winding melody, especially on the word Alleluia.

{img:chant-manuscript-square-notes-four-line-staff-red-lines}
The music is written in the great choir books, in little square notes, on four lines, in black ink, with the words underneath. But most of the monks know the chant by heart, because they sing it every day, for years.

--

The Cistercians wanted their chant to be pure, and simple, and correct, like everything else in their life.

So, in Bernard's time, they sent monks to Metz, in France, which was said to have the most authentic chant, to copy the books. And later, Bernard himself oversaw a revision of the chant, removing what he thought were later additions, and too elaborate melodies, so that the chant would be simpler, and closer to what he believed was the original.

In the church, the monks stand in their stalls, in two rows, facing each other across the choir. One side sings one verse of a psalm, and the other side answers with the next. Back and forth, back and forth, for hours, like a long, gentle conversation.

--

Aelred himself wrote about singing, in one of his books, The Mirror of Charity.

He complained about some churches, where the singers showed off, with strange voices, and trills, and high notes, and twisted melodies, and dramatic gestures, so that the people listening forgot to pray, and only admired the singers. He said that the singing should be simple, and humble, and help the heart to pray, not distract it.

{img:empty-abbey-church-choir-stalls-soft-light-dust}
At Rievaulx, the chant is sung slowly, and quietly, and simply, in the plain stone church, which echoes the voices softly, so that the sound seems to float in the air, and fill the whole building.

--

For a monk, the singing is not a performance. It is prayer.

Saint Benedict wrote that the monks should sing in such a way that their mind is in harmony with their voice. And that nothing should be put before the Work of God.

{img:monk-alone-church-dawn-singing-quietly-candle}
Many people today, who are not monks, and not religious, still listen to Gregorian chant, on recordings, to help them relax, or to sleep. Something in its long, calm, flowing melodies, sung by many voices as one, seems to quiet the mind, as it did for the monks of Rievaulx, nine hundred years ago.
