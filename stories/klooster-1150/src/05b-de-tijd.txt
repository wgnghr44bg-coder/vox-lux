How do the monks know when to get up, in the middle of the night, in the year 1150, when there are no clocks?

Mechanical clocks, with weights and gears, will not be invented for another hundred and fifty years. So the monks must find other ways to tell the time.

--

The monk responsible for waking the others is the sacristan, who looks after the church. He must be careful, and reliable, and he does not sleep much.

On clear nights, he watches the stars. The old monastery books explain which stars rise at which times, in different months, and how to tell the hour by looking at them, through a certain window, or over a certain roof. When a particular star appears over the roof of the dormitory, it is time to ring the bell.

{img:clear-winter-night-stars-over-monastery-roof-orion}
On cloudy nights, he uses candles, marked with lines, which burn down at a steady rate, so many lines an hour. Or a water clock: a vessel of water, with a little hole, through which the water drips slowly, into another vessel, marked with the hours. Or he simply recites psalms, in the dark, a fixed number of them, knowing how long each one takes.

--

In the daytime, there are sundials, on the south wall of the church, simple ones, with lines carved in the stone, and a stick, casting a shadow, to show the hours of the services.

And there is the bell. Everything in the monastery is ruled by the bell. When the bell rings, every monk stops what he is doing, at once, even in the middle of writing a letter, Benedict says, and goes to the church.

{img:simple-sundial-carved-church-wall-shadow-stick-sunlight}
The monks do not think of time as we do, in minutes and seconds. They think of it in the hours of prayer, and the seasons, and the feasts, and the light and the dark. Their time is measured not by machines, but by the sun, and the stars, and the singing.

--

Sometimes, of course, the sacristan makes a mistake, and wakes the monks too early, or too late.

The old monastic books tell what to do then. If the monks are woken too late, the night office must be shortened, so that the morning service can begin at dawn. And the sacristan must confess his fault, at the chapter, the next morning, in front of everyone.
