In 1150, the abbot of Rievaulx is a man called Aelred.

He is forty years old. He has been abbot for three years. And he will become one of the most loved and most famous abbots in the history of England. Many years later, he will be honoured as a saint.

--

We know a great deal about Aelred, because one of his monks, Walter Daniel, who lived with him for seventeen years, wrote a book about his life, soon after his death. And because Aelred himself wrote many books, and letters, and sermons, which still exist.

Aelred was born in 1110, in the town of Hexham, in Northumberland, in the far north of England, near the Scottish border. His father was a priest, as his grandfather had been, because in those days, before the rules were made stricter, some English priests still married and had families.

{img:hexham-abbey-town-northumberland-1110s-river-tyne}
As a boy, Aelred was clever, and gentle, and full of charm, and everyone liked him. When he was about fourteen, he was sent to the court of the king of Scotland, King David the First, at Roxburgh, to be brought up with the king's son, Henry, as a companion.

--

At the Scottish court, Aelred did very well. King David liked him, and trusted him, and made him his steward, in charge of the royal household and the king's table, when he was still a young man. He could have had a great career at court, and become a bishop, or a great official.

But he was not happy. He felt that the life of the court was empty, and that he wanted something more. He later wrote that, in those years, he was outwardly cheerful, but inwardly troubled, and that he longed for a real friend, and for God.

In 1134, when he was twenty-four, King David sent him on business to the archbishop of York. On the way, he stayed with Walter Espec, the lord of Helmsley, who told him about the new monastery of the white monks, in the valley nearby, at Rievaulx, which had been founded two years earlier.

--

Aelred went to visit. And the next day, as he was riding home, past the monastery, on the hillside above the valley, he stopped, and looked down at it, for a long time. And then he turned his horse around, and rode down into the valley, and asked to become a monk.

He gave up everything: his place at court, his career, his wealth, his friends. And he put on the plain white robe of a novice, and began a new life.

At Rievaulx, Aelred was a model monk. He was humble, and hard-working, and kind, and patient. He was not strong, physically. He suffered from illness for much of his life, from painful stones in his kidneys, and later from arthritis. But he never complained.

--

He was soon given important work. He was sent to Rome, on business for the abbey, in 1142. He became master of the novices, teaching the young men who came to join. He was sent to found a new monastery, at Revesby, in Lincolnshire, as its first abbot. And in 1147, when he was thirty-seven, the monks of Rievaulx chose him as their abbot.

As abbot, he is kind, and wise, and gentle. He is famous for his patience, and for never losing his temper, and for his love of his monks. Walter Daniel writes that Aelred never sent anyone away from the monastery, in all his years as abbot, even the most difficult monks. He said that it was the glory of the house of Rievaulx that it was a home for the weak as well as the strong, a mother of mercy for all.

{img:abbot-listening-to-young-monk-garden-bench-gentle}
Under Aelred, Rievaulx grows larger than ever. Men come from all over England and Scotland, and even from abroad, to join, because they have heard of his kindness. By the end of his life, there are, Walter Daniel says, a hundred and forty monks and five hundred lay brothers, so many that on feast days, the church is as crowded as a hive of bees.

--

Aelred writes books too, many of them, in Latin.

The most famous is called On Spiritual Friendship. It is a book about friendship, written as a conversation between Aelred and some of his monks, sitting together, talking. Aelred says that friendship is one of the greatest gifts of God, and that a true friend is a treasure. He says that God himself is friendship, and that whoever lives in friendship lives in God.

{img:three-monks-sitting-talking-cloister-garden-sunlight}
This was a new and unusual idea, for a monk, in the twelfth century. Many monastic rules warned the monks against having special friendships. But Aelred believed that friendship, between people who love God, was a way to God. His book is still read today, all over the world.

--

Walter Daniel describes him in 1150: a slight, slender man, with a gentle face, and a quiet voice, walking in the cloister, among his monks, or sitting in his little room, reading, or writing, or talking with a friend.

He still has twenty more years to live, at Rievaulx. And they will be the happiest years of the abbey's history.
