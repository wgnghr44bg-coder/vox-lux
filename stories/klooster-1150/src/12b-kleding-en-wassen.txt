The monks of Rievaulx wear a habit of plain, undyed wool, the natural greyish white colour of the sheep.

They wear a long tunic, down to the ankles, with long sleeves, tied with a belt of leather or cord. Over it, for work, a scapular: a long, narrow piece of cloth, hanging down in front and behind, over the shoulders, like an apron, with a hood. And over everything, in the church, and in cold weather, the cowl: a great loose robe, with very wide sleeves, and a hood, which falls in long, soft folds, almost to the ground.

--

The cloth is woven from the wool of the abbey's own sheep, by the lay brothers, and fulled, and cut and sewn, in the monastery's own workshops. It is rough, and plain, and warm, and lasts for years.

Under the tunic, the monks wear nothing much: the Cistercians, at first, do not even wear underclothes, because Saint Benedict does not mention them. On their feet, they wear shoes or boots of leather, made in the monastery's tannery, and woollen stockings. In winter, in the cold church, at night, their feet are frozen.

{img:monastery-laundry-river-washing-habits-wooden-tubs}
Each monk has two tunics, and two cowls, so that one can be washed while the other is worn. The clothes are washed in the monastery's laundry, by the river, in great wooden tubs, with water, and lye, made from wood ash. And when they are worn out, they are given to the poor.

--

The monks' hair is cut short, and the top of the head is shaved, in a circle, called the tonsure, leaving a ring of hair around the head, like a crown. It is a sign that the monk has given his life to God.

The monks are shaved, all together, by one of the brothers, about once every few weeks, in the cloister, sitting in rows, while they sing psalms. The water is heated in the kitchen, and carried out in basins. In winter, it is a cold business.

The lay brothers do not shave their beards. They keep them, as a sign that they are lay brothers, not monks.

--

How often do the monks wash?

They wash their hands, and their faces, every morning, and before every meal, at the great stone basin in the cloister, with running water, piped from a spring. They wash their feet, often, especially on Saturdays, when the monks whose turn it has been to serve in the kitchen wash the feet of all the brothers, as Christ washed the feet of his disciples.

{img:saturday-foot-washing-cloister-monks-humble-basins}
But full baths are rare, in the twelfth century, for monks. Saint Benedict wrote that baths should be allowed to the sick, as often as necessary, but to the healthy, and especially to the young, more rarely. So the healthy monks bathe only a few times a year, before the great feasts, like Christmas and Easter.

--

The monks sleep in their habits, in the dormitory, on simple beds: a wooden frame, with a mattress filled with straw, a pillow, a woollen blanket, and a cover. They sleep in rows, along the walls, a little apart from each other, with a lamp burning all night, at the end of the room.

In winter, the dormitory is bitterly cold. There is no fire. The wind blows through the cracks. Sometimes snow drifts in, under the doors. The monks sleep wrapped in their cowls and their blankets, and wake at two in the morning, to go down to the church.

{img:dormitory-winter-night-lamp-rows-beds-frost-window}
It is a hard life. But many monks of Rievaulx live to be old, in the clean air of the valley, with their simple food, and their regular hours, and their peace of mind.
