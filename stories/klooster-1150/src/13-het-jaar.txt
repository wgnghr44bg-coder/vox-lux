The life of the monastery follows not only the hours of the day, but the seasons of the year, and the great feasts of the Church.

The monks' year begins in the late autumn, with Advent, the four weeks before Christmas, a time of quiet waiting, and preparation, and fasting. The days grow short and dark. The monks rise in the darkness, and go to bed in the darkness. The services are long, and the church is cold.

--

Then Christmas comes, the great feast of the birth of Christ.

On Christmas Eve, the monks sing the night office, the most solemn of the year. At midnight, there is the first Mass of Christmas, by candlelight. Then a second Mass at dawn, and a third in the day. The church is lit with extra candles. There is a better meal, in the refectory, with a pittance, perhaps of fish, or cheese, or eggs. The monks may rest a little.

After Christmas, the winter goes on. Snow falls on the moors, and in the valley. The river freezes, at the edges. The monks work indoors, in the workshops, and the scriptorium, and the cloister, copying books, and mending clothes, and making tools. The lay brothers look after the animals, in the barns, and the sheep on the snowy hills.

--

At the beginning of February, there is Candlemas, the feast of the Purification of Mary, when all the candles for the year are blessed, and the monks walk in procession, around the cloister, each carrying a lighted candle.

Then comes Lent, the forty days of fasting before Easter.

{img:candlemas-procession-cloister-monks-lighted-candles-snow}
In Lent, the monks eat only one meal a day, in the evening, of bread and vegetables. They keep even stricter silence. They pray more, and read more. At the beginning of Lent, each monk is given a book from the library, to read through the year. The church is bare. The crosses are covered with cloth.

--

And then, at the end of Lent, comes Holy Week, and Easter, the greatest feast of all.

On the Thursday before Easter, the abbot washes the feet of the monks, and of poor men, brought in from the gate, as Christ washed the feet of his disciples. On Good Friday, the monks walk barefoot, and kneel before the cross. And on Easter night, a new fire is lit, outside the church, and a great candle, and the light is carried into the dark church, and the monks sing, for the first time since the beginning of Lent, the joyful word: Alleluia.

{img:easter-new-fire-church-doorway-night-monks-great-candle}
Easter brings the spring. The days grow longer. The monks go out to work in the fields and gardens again. There are two meals a day again. The lambs are born, on the hills. The birds sing in the woods. The primroses and the bluebells come out, under the trees, on the banks of the river.

--

In summer, the days are long, and the work is hard.

The monks rise at about one in the morning, because the night is so short, and the services must be finished before dawn. They work in the fields, haymaking, in June, and weeding, and the lay brothers shear the sheep, on the granges, and pack the wool, in great sacks, for the merchants. After the midday meal, the monks rest, in the dormitory.

Then, in August and September, the harvest. Everyone works, monks and lay brothers together, even the abbot, from dawn to dusk, cutting the corn with sickles, and binding it into sheaves, and carrying it to the barns. Sometimes, at harvest, the monks even sing the services in the fields, so as not to lose the good weather.

--

After the harvest, in the autumn, the apples and pears are picked, in the orchards. The pigs are taken into the woods, to eat the acorns and the beechnuts. The new ale is brewed.

On the fourteenth of September, the feast of the Holy Cross, the winter timetable begins again, with one meal a day.

{img:orchard-apple-picking-monks-baskets-autumn-light-ladder}
And on the first of November, there is All Saints, and on the second, All Souls, when the monks pray for all the dead: their own brothers, and their families, and their benefactors, and all the souls of the faithful departed. They go out to the monks' cemetery, east of the church, and pray beside the graves.

And then Advent comes again. And the year begins once more, the same as before, and as it will be next year, and the year after, for as long as the monastery stands.
