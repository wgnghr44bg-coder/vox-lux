For every animal brought to Rome for the games, there are people who look after it. These keepers and trainers live with the animals in the enclosures outside the city. They feed them and care for them, sometimes for months, before the games.

--

The keepers are skilled people who know their animals well. They know how to feed them, calm them, move them and treat them when they are ill. Many come from the same lands as the animals. Africans who know lions and elephants. Indians who know tigers. Egyptians who know crocodiles and hippos.

Some keepers grow very fond of their animals. There are old stories of lions who loved their keepers, and elephants who recognised their old trainers years later in the crowd.

The most famous story was told by a Roman writer who said he heard it from someone who was there. A runaway slave called Androcles was hiding in a cave in Africa. A lion came in, limping, with a big thorn in its paw. Androcles was terrified. But he carefully pulled out the thorn. And the lion became his friend. For three years it lived with him and brought him food.

--

Later, Androcles was captured and sent to Rome to face the beasts in the arena. And the lion had been captured too, and sent to Rome. When the lion was let into the arena, it ran toward Androcles. The crowd held its breath. And then the lion stopped. It recognised him. It lay down at his feet, wagging its tail like a dog, and licked his hands.

The crowd was amazed. The emperor asked Androcles to explain. When he told the story, the crowd shouted for him to be set free, and to be given the lion. Afterwards, the writer says, Androcles walked through the streets of Rome with the lion on a light leash. People gave him money and said: here is the lion who was a man's friend, and the man who was a lion's doctor.

{img:man-walking-lion-on-leash-roman-street-crowd-wonder-calm}
It is probably only a story. But it shows that the Romans, too, could be moved by kindness and friendship between people and animals.
