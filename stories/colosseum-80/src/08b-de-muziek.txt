The games in the Colosseum are never silent. There is always music.

A band plays throughout the shows. There are long, straight trumpets. There are great curved horns shaped like a big letter G, which wrap around the player's body and make a deep, loud, booming sound. And there is the water organ.

--

The water organ is a wonderful instrument, invented in Egypt three hundred years before. It has rows of bronze pipes of different lengths, and a keyboard. Pumps push air through the pipes, and water in a tank underneath keeps the pressure steady. It makes a loud, sweet, powerful sound that fills the whole amphitheatre. It is the ancestor of the church organ.

An old floor picture made of tiny stones, found in North Africa, shows the music at the games. A woman plays the water organ. Two men play the great curved horns. Another plays a trumpet, beside the arena.

The music follows the action, like music in a film today. It is slow and calm at the beginning, when the fighters circle and watch each other. It gets faster and louder as things get exciting. And there is a great fanfare at the end, when the winner is announced.

--

The trumpets also announce the start of the games, the arrival of the emperor, the entrance of the gladiators in their grand parade, and each new part of the show.

When the emperor enters his box, the whole crowd stands up, cheers and claps in rhythm. They chant his name and wish him good luck and long life, in great choruses, like football crowds today.

{img:crowd-standing-cheering-emperor-arriving-box-trumpets-sunlight}
Imagine the sound of it. Fifty thousand people cheering and chanting, with the trumpets, the horns and the great water organ, all echoing around the huge stone bowl of the amphitheatre. The sound rolls out over the roofs of Rome, so people far away in the streets can hear it, and know the games have begun.
