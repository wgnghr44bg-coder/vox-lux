The games in the Colosseum are never silent. There is always music.

A band plays, throughout the shows: trumpets, called tubae, long and straight. Great curved horns, called cornua, shaped like a big letter G, which wrap around the player's body, with a crossbar on top, and make a deep, loud, booming sound. And the water organ, the hydraulis.

--

The water organ is a wonderful instrument, invented in Alexandria, three hundred years before, by an engineer called Ctesibius. It has rows of bronze pipes, of different lengths, and a keyboard, and the air is pushed through the pipes by pumps, and kept at a steady pressure, by water, in a tank, underneath. It makes a loud, sweet, powerful sound, which can be heard all over the amphitheatre. It is the ancestor of the church organ.

A mosaic, found in a villa at Zliten, in Libya, shows the music at the games: a woman playing the water organ, and two men playing the great curved horns, and another playing a trumpet, beside the arena, while the gladiators fight.

The music follows the action, like the music in a film today: slow, and calm, at the beginning, when the gladiators are circling, and watching each other. Then faster, and louder, as the fight becomes exciting. And then a great fanfare, at the end, when the winner is announced.

--

The trumpets also announce the beginning of the games, and the arrival of the emperor, and the entrance of the gladiators, in the grand procession, and the changes between the different parts of the show.

And when the emperor enters his box, the whole crowd stands, and cheers, and claps, in rhythm, and chants his name, and his titles, and wishes him good luck, and long life, in great choruses, like the chants of football crowds today.

{img:crowd-standing-cheering-emperor-arriving-box-trumpets-sunlight}
Imagine the sound of it: fifty thousand people, cheering, and chanting, and the trumpets, and the horns, and the great water organ, all echoing around the huge stone bowl of the amphitheatre, and out over the roofs of Rome, so that people far away, in the streets, could hear it, and know that the games were going on.
