Today, the Colosseum is the most visited monument in Italy and one of the most famous buildings in the world.

Every year, more than seven million people come to see it from every country on earth. That is more people each year than ever saw it in all the centuries of the games. Not long ago, it was chosen as one of the New Seven Wonders of the World.

--

You walk to it along a wide avenue from the Forum, or come up out of the underground station right in front of it. And there it suddenly is, enormous, rising above you, with its great arches and its broken outer wall glowing gold in the sun.

Inside, you can walk through the corridors where the crowds once streamed, and climb to the upper levels. You can look down at the arena, with its rebuilt wooden floor at one end, and the maze of underground passages open to the sky. You can see the remains of the seats, and the marble seats of the senators. And you can see the numbers carved above the entrance arches.

In the corridors there is a little museum with things found in the Colosseum and its drains. Coins and pottery, animal bones, and the remains of food people ate at the games two thousand years ago. Olives and peaches, grapes and nuts, chicken bones and mussel shells.

--

Every year at Easter, the Pope leads a procession by torchlight at the Colosseum, with thousands of people, in memory of the early Christians.

And at night, the Colosseum is lit up golden against the dark sky. Whenever a country somewhere in the world stops using the death penalty, the lights of the Colosseum are changed from white to gold for a night, as a sign of hope and life.

{img:colosseum-night-golden-light-quiet-street-cypress}
Next to it, you can still see the ruins of the gladiator school below the level of the street, with the curved rows of seats of its little practice arena. The arch of Titus still stands on the Sacred Way. And the places where the great fountain and the giant statue once stood are marked on the ground.

--

So we leave the great amphitheatre for tonight, as the evening light turns its old stones gold, and the swallows circle high above the arches where the sailors once hauled on the ropes of the sunshade.

The builders with their ox carts, the gladiators in their school eating their barley porridge, the baker and his son in the high seats, and two tired fighters walking out together, both free.
