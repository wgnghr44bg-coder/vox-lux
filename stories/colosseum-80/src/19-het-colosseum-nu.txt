Today, the Colosseum is the most visited monument in Italy, and one of the most famous buildings in the world.

Every year, more than seven million people come to see it, from every country on earth: more people, every year, than ever saw it in all the centuries of the games. In 2007, it was chosen as one of the New Seven Wonders of the World.

--

You approach it along a wide avenue, from the Forum, or come up out of the underground station, called Colosseo, right in front of it, and there it is, suddenly, enormous, rising above you, with its great arches, and its broken outer wall, glowing gold, in the sunshine.

Inside, you can walk through the corridors, where the crowds once streamed, and climb the stairs, to the upper levels, and look down at the arena, with its rebuilt wooden floor, at one end, and the maze of the hypogeum, open to the sky, below. You can see the remains of the seats, and the marble seats of the senators, with their names. You can see the numbers, carved above the entrance arches: twenty-three, twenty-four, twenty-five, fifty-two, fifty-three.

In the corridors, there is a little museum, with things found in the Colosseum, and its drains: coins, and pottery, and the bones of animals, and the remains of the food that people ate, at the games, two thousand years ago: olives, and peaches, and grapes, and figs, and nuts, and chicken bones, and the shells of mussels.

--

Every year, on Good Friday, the Pope leads the Way of the Cross, at night, by torchlight, at the Colosseum, with thousands of people, in memory of the early Christians.

And at night, the Colosseum is lit up, golden, against the dark sky. Since 2000, every time a country in the world abolishes the death penalty, or a person condemned to death is spared, the lights of the Colosseum are changed, from white, to gold, for a night, as a sign of hope, and life.

{img:colosseum-night-golden-light-quiet-street-cypress}
Beside it, the ruins of the Ludus Magnus, the gladiator school, can still be seen, below the level of the street, with the curved rows of seats, of its little practice arena. The Arch of Titus still stands, on the Sacred Way. The remains of the Meta Sudans fountain, and the base of the Colossus, are marked on the ground, near the Arch of Constantine.

--

So we leave the great amphitheatre for tonight, as the evening light turns its old stones gold, and the swallows circle high above the arches, where the sailors once hauled on the ropes of the awning.

The builders with their ox carts, the gladiators in their school eating their barley porridge, the baker and his son in the high seats, and two tired fighters, walking out together, both free.
