What do the Romans eat at the games?

The games last all day, from morning to evening, and the crowd gets hungry. So people bring food in baskets and bags. And they buy food from sellers who walk up and down the rows, and from stalls around the outside of the amphitheatre.

--

Archaeologists digging in the drains of the Colosseum have found out what people ate two thousand years ago. People dropped their leftovers on the floor, and the rain washed them into the drains.

They found the stones and seeds of olives, figs, grapes, melons, peaches, plums and cherries. Walnuts, hazelnuts and pine nuts. Chicken bones and pig bones. The shells of mussels, oysters and snails.

The sellers offer hot sausages, bread and cheese, boiled chickpeas and roasted nuts, honey cakes, fruit and pastries, and wine mixed with water.

--

And sometimes the emperor gives food to the crowd. Baskets of bread, meat, cakes, fruit and wine are handed out along the rows. A few years later, the next emperor will hold a great feast in the Colosseum for the whole crowd. Sweet cakes, dates, figs and nuts will rain down on the people from ropes stretched over the seats. And the crowd will shout with joy.

A poet describes it. A whole day of feasting in the amphitheatre, rich and poor, men and women, children and senators, all eating together, as if the whole city were one great family at one great table.

{img:sweets-dates-figs-raining-down-from-ropes-crowd-reaching-joy}
So for ordinary Romans, a day at the games was not only a day of shows. It was a day out, a picnic and a festival, with friends and family, food and wine, sunshine and the shade of the great sunshade, and noise and music and excitement.
