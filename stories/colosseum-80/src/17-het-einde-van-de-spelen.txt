The games in the Colosseum went on for more than four hundred years.

Emperor after emperor gave great games there. One emperor, after a great victory, held games that lasted a hundred and twenty-three days. Another emperor even fought in the arena himself as a gladiator, which shocked the Romans.

--

But slowly, over the centuries, things changed.

About three hundred years after the Colosseum opened, the Roman Empire became Christian. The Christians did not approve of the gladiator games, and Christian writers wrote against them. The games also became harder to pay for, as the empire grew poorer and was attacked by enemies.

There is a story about a monk called Telemachus. He came to Rome from the East, and during a gladiator fight, he ran down onto the sand and tried to separate the fighters. He begged them to stop. The angry crowd threw stones at him. And the emperor was so moved when he heard of it that he banned the gladiator games forever.

We do not know if the story is true. But it seems the gladiator fights did end around that time.

--

The animal hunts went on a little longer. The last one we know of in the Colosseum was held more than a hundred years later, when a Gothic king ruled Italy after the fall of the Western Roman Empire.

After that, the Colosseum fell silent. The great building that once held fifty thousand cheering people stood empty, in a Rome that had shrunk from a million people to perhaps thirty thousand, living among the ruins.

{img:empty-colosseum-grass-growing-arena-6th-century-quiet-ruin}
Grass grew in the arena. Trees took root in the seats. Birds nested in the arches. And the people of Rome began to use the great building for other things.

--

{img:shepherd-goats-grazing-ancient-ruins-rome-middle-ages-quiet}
In the early Middle Ages, shepherds grazed their goats and sheep among the ruins of ancient Rome, in the old Forum, which they now called the cow field. And the great amphitheatre stood above them, silent, enormous and mysterious. It was a building from another age that nobody could build any more. People began to say it had been built by giants, or by magic.
