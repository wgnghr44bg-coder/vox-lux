The emperor Titus, in the year 80, is forty years old.

He is the elder son of Vespasian. He grew up at the emperor's court and became a soldier and a general. He fought in Britain and Germany, and led the Roman army that captured Jerusalem ten years ago. He is clever, charming and handsome. He is a good speaker. He can write fast in shorthand, copy anyone's handwriting, sing and play the harp.

--

Before he became emperor, many Romans were afraid of him. He had commanded the emperor's bodyguard, and people said he was harsh. And he was in love with a foreign queen. The Romans still remembered Cleopatra, and did not want another foreign queen in Rome.

But when Titus became emperor, he surprised everyone. He sadly sent the queen away, because the Romans would not accept her. And he became one of the most loved emperors in Roman history.

An old writer says Titus never sent anyone away without giving them some hope. Once, at dinner, he realised he had done nothing kind for anyone all day. And he said: my friends, I have lost a day.

--

But his short reign is full of disasters.

Only two months after he became emperor, the volcano Vesuvius, near Naples, erupted. It buried the towns of Pompeii and Herculaneum. Titus went there himself and sent help, money and officials for the survivors.

{img:vesuvius-distant-bay-of-naples-quiet-smoke-plume-79ad-far}
Then this spring, while Titus was away helping the people near Naples, a great fire broke out in Rome. It burned for three days and destroyed the great temple of Jupiter and many other buildings. And after that came a terrible sickness.

Titus gave his own money, and even the furniture of his own palaces, to help rebuild Rome. He did everything he could for the people.

--

And now, in the summer of 80, he will open the great new amphitheatre with a hundred days of games. He wants to cheer up the people of Rome after all their troubles, and show them that their emperor cares about them.

At the games, he will throw little wooden balls into the crowd. Inside each one is a ticket for a gift. Food or clothes, silver or gold, a horse, and sometimes even a house.

{img:wooden-gift-balls-thrown-into-crowd-amphitheatre-hands-reaching}
Titus will reign for only two years. In September of next year, he will fall ill with a fever on a journey to his family's home in the hills, and die at forty-one. The people of Rome will mourn him as if they had lost a member of their own family. But tonight, nobody knows that yet.

His brother Domitian will become emperor after him. He will finish the last parts of the amphitheatre, including the tunnels under the arena.
