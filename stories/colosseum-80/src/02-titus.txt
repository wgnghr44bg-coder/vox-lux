The emperor Titus, in the year 80, is forty years old.

He is the elder son of Vespasian. He was brought up at the court of the emperors, with the son of the emperor Claudius, and was a soldier, and a general, in Britain, and Germany, and Judaea, where he commanded the Roman army that captured Jerusalem, in the year 70. He is clever, and charming, and handsome, and a good speaker, and can write fast in shorthand, and imitate anyone's handwriting, and sing, and play the harp.

--

Before he became emperor, many Romans were afraid of him. He was the commander of the Praetorian Guard, the emperor's bodyguard, and was said to be harsh. And he was in love with a foreign queen, Berenice, the sister of the Jewish king Agrippa, and the Romans, remembering Cleopatra, did not want another foreign queen in Rome.

But when Titus became emperor, he surprised everyone. He sent Berenice away, sadly, because the Romans would not accept her. And he became one of the most loved emperors in Roman history.

The historian Suetonius says that Titus never sent anyone away from an audience without some hope. And that once, at dinner, he remembered that he had done nothing kind for anyone all day, and said: my friends, I have lost a day.

--

His short reign was full of disasters.

In the summer of 79, only two months after he became emperor, the volcano Vesuvius, near Naples, erupted, and buried the towns of Pompeii, and Herculaneum, and Stabiae. Titus went there himself, and sent help, and money, and officials, to help the survivors, and rebuild.

{img:vesuvius-distant-bay-of-naples-quiet-smoke-plume-79ad-far}
Then, in the spring of 80, while Titus was away, in Campania, helping the people there, a great fire broke out in Rome, and burned for three days, and destroyed the temple of Jupiter on the Capitol, and the Pantheon, and many other buildings. And then there was a plague, one of the worst in Roman history.

Titus gave his own money, and the furniture of his own palaces, to help rebuild Rome. He did everything he could for the people.

--

And in the summer of 80, perhaps in June, he opened the great new amphitheatre, with a hundred days of games, to cheer up the people of Rome, after all their troubles, and to show them that their emperor cared for them.

He gave away, at the games, little wooden balls, thrown into the crowd, with tickets inside them, for gifts: food, and clothes, and silver, and gold, and horses, and even slaves, and houses.

{img:wooden-gift-balls-thrown-into-crowd-amphitheatre-hands-reaching}
Titus reigned for only two years. In September of the year 81, he fell ill, with a fever, on a journey to the Sabine country, the home of his family, and passed away, at the age of forty-one. The people of Rome mourned him, it is said, as if they had lost a member of their own family.

His brother, Domitian, became emperor, and finished the last parts of the amphitheatre, and added the underground passages, beneath the arena.
