There are many kinds of gladiators, each with their own armour, weapons and way of fighting. Each kind usually fights one particular other kind, so the fight is balanced and exciting to watch.

--

One kind wears a great bronze helmet with a wide brim and a tall crest shaped like a fish. He carries a large curved shield, like a Roman soldier's, and a short sword. He has a padded sleeve on his sword arm and a guard on one leg. He is heavy, strong and slow.

Another kind is called the Thracian. His helmet has a high crest topped with a griffin's head. He carries a small square shield and a short curved sword. He has tall guards on both legs, because his shield is small. He is quick and nimble.

{img:retiarius-net-trident-light-armour-shoulder-guard-sand}
The strangest of all is the net man. He has almost no armour. No helmet and no shield, just a metal guard on his left shoulder. He fights with a net, which he tries to throw over his opponent, and a trident, a long fishing spear with three points. He is the fastest gladiator of all. And because he wears no helmet, the crowd can see his face and recognise him.

--

The net man usually fights the chaser. The chaser wears a smooth round helmet with tiny eyeholes, so the trident cannot catch on it, and carries a large shield and a sword. Their fight is like a fisherman trying to catch a fish. The net man dances around, throwing his net, while the chaser tries to get close.

There are other kinds too. Fighters with a small round shield and a spear, like Greek soldiers. Fighters on horseback. Even fighters on chariots. And many more.

{img:gladiator-helmets-bronze-crests-griffin-fish-museum-light}
The armour and helmets are often beautiful, decorated with gold, engravings and coloured plumes. Some helmets found in the gladiator barracks at Pompeii, buried by the volcano, are among the most beautiful armour that survives from ancient times.

--

The crowd has its favourites. Some people cheer for gladiators with big shields. Others cheer for gladiators with small shields. Like football fans today, they argue about which is better. The emperor Titus himself is a fan of the small shields, and jokes with the crowd about it.

And gladiators have stage names, like wrestlers or actors today. The Flame. The Dove. The Little Arrow. Triumph. Names the crowd shouts and writes on the walls.
