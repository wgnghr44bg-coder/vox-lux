There are many different kinds of gladiators, each with their own armour, and weapons, and style of fighting. And each kind usually fights against one particular other kind, so that the fight is balanced, and interesting to watch.

--

The murmillo wears a great bronze helmet, with a wide brim, and a tall crest, shaped like a fish, and carries a large, curved, rectangular shield, like a Roman soldier's, and a short sword, the gladius. He has a protective sleeve, of padded linen, or leather, on his sword arm, and a short guard on one leg. He is heavy, and strong, and slow.

The thraex, the Thracian, wears a helmet with a high crest, topped by a griffin's head, and carries a small, square shield, and a short, curved sword, like a sickle. He has tall guards on both legs, because his shield is small. He is quick, and agile.

{img:retiarius-net-trident-light-armour-shoulder-guard-sand}
The retiarius, the net man, is the strangest of all. He has almost no armour: no helmet, no shield, only a metal guard on his left shoulder, and a padded sleeve on his left arm. He fights with a net, which he tries to throw over his opponent, and a trident, a long, three-pronged fishing spear, and a dagger. He is the fastest, and the most mobile, of the gladiators. Because he does not wear a helmet, his face can be seen, and the crowd can recognize him.

--

The retiarius usually fights against the secutor, the chaser, who wears a smooth, round helmet, with tiny eyeholes, so that the trident cannot catch in it, and carries a large shield, and a sword. The fight between them is like a fisherman trying to catch a fish: the net man dancing around, throwing his net, and the chaser trying to catch him, and get close.

There are other kinds too: the hoplomachus, with a small round shield, and a spear, like a Greek soldier. The provocator, the challenger. The eques, on horseback, who fight only against each other, at the beginning of the games. The essedarius, who fights from a chariot, like the Britons. And many more.

{img:gladiator-helmets-bronze-crests-griffin-fish-museum-light}
The armour, and the helmets, are often beautiful, decorated with gilding, and engravings, and feathers, and coloured plumes. Some of the helmets, found at Pompeii, in the gladiator barracks there, buried by Vesuvius, in the year 79, are among the most beautiful pieces of armour that survive from the ancient world.

--

The crowd has its favourites. Some people support the gladiators with large shields, called the scutarii. Others support the gladiators with small shields, the parmularii. Like football supporters today, they argue about which is better, and cheer for their side. The emperor Titus himself was a supporter of the Thracians, the small shields, and joked with the crowd about it, at the games.

And the gladiators have stage names, like wrestlers, or actors: Flamma, the flame. Columbus, the dove. Hermes. Spiculus, the little arrow. Triumphus. Names that the crowd shouts, and writes on the walls.
