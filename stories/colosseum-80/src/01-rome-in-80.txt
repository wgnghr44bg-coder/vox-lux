Tonight we are going nearly two thousand years back, to the city of Rome, in the year 80 after Christ.

--

In a valley between the hills of Rome, where a few years before there was a lake in the gardens of an emperor, a vast new building now stands: an enormous oval of pale travertine stone, four storeys high, with eighty arches on each level, and statues looking out from every arch. It is the greatest building the Romans have ever made, and in this year, it is about to open.

The Romans call it the Flavian Amphitheatre. We call it the Colosseum. And tonight, we will spend a little time around it: with the builders and the sailors who raise its great awning, with the gladiators in their training school, with the animal keepers and the crowds, and with the ordinary people of Rome.

If you enjoy slow journeys into the past, you are welcome to subscribe to the channel, so that you can find the next one easily.

--

Rome, in the year 80, is the capital of an empire that stretches from Britain to Syria, from the Rhine to the Sahara, around the whole Mediterranean Sea. It is the largest city in the world, with perhaps a million people.

It has changed a great deal in the hundred and twenty years since the time of Julius Caesar. The Republic is gone. Rome is ruled by emperors. And it has become a city of marble, and gold, and great buildings: temples, and baths, and forums, and aqueducts, and theatres, and palaces.

--

But the last few years have not been easy.

Sixteen years before, in the year 64, a great fire burned for nine days, and destroyed much of the city. The emperor at the time, Nero, used the burned land, in the middle of the city, to build himself an enormous palace, called the Golden House, with gardens, and woods, and vineyards, and a great artificial lake, and a gigantic bronze statue of himself, more than thirty metres tall, called the Colossus.

{img:golden-house-gardens-lake-colossus-statue-nero-palace-sunset}
The Romans hated it. Nero had taken the heart of their city for himself.

Then, in the year 68, Nero died, and there was a terrible year of civil war, with four emperors, one after another. And the last of them, a tough, practical, good-humoured old soldier, called Vespasian, won, and became emperor, and brought peace.

--

Vespasian decided to give the land of Nero's palace back to the people of Rome. He drained Nero's lake, and on the spot where it had been, he began to build a great amphitheatre: a place of entertainment, for the people, the largest in the world.

He did not live to see it finished. He passed away in the summer of 79, at the age of sixty-nine. It is said that, as he felt death coming, he joked: oh dear, I think I am becoming a god. Because the Romans made their dead emperors into gods.

{img:vespasian-old-emperor-portrait-practical-face-humorous-calm}
His son, Titus, became emperor. And in the year 80, he finished the amphitheatre, and opened it, with a great festival of games, which lasted for a hundred days.
