Tonight we are going nearly two thousand years back, to the city of Rome, in the year 80 after Christ.

--

In a valley between the hills of Rome, where a few years ago there was a lake in an emperor's garden, a huge new building now stands. It is an enormous oval of pale stone, four storeys high, with eighty arches on each level and statues looking out from the arches. It is the greatest building the Romans have ever made. And this year, it is about to open.

The Romans call it the Flavian Amphitheatre. We call it the Colosseum. Tonight we will spend a little time around it. With the builders, and the sailors who raise its great sunshade. With the gladiators in their training school, the animal keepers and the crowds, and the ordinary people of Rome.

If you enjoy slow journeys into the past, you are welcome to subscribe to the channel, so that you can find the next one easily.

--

Rome in the year 80 is the capital of an empire that stretches from Britain to Syria, all around the Mediterranean Sea. It is the largest city in the world, with perhaps a million people.

It has changed a lot since the time of Julius Caesar. Rome is now ruled by emperors. It has become a city of marble and gold, with temples, baths, aqueducts, theatres and palaces.

--

But the last few years have not been easy.

Sixteen years ago, a great fire burned for nine days and destroyed much of the city. The emperor at the time, Nero, took the burned land in the middle of the city and built himself an enormous palace on it. It was called the Golden House. It had gardens, woods, vineyards, a great artificial lake, and a giant bronze statue of Nero himself, more than thirty metres tall. It was called the Colossus.

{img:golden-house-gardens-lake-colossus-statue-nero-palace-sunset}
The Romans hated it. Nero had taken the heart of their city for himself.

Then Nero died, and there was a terrible year of civil war, with four emperors one after another. The last of them won. He was a tough, practical, good-humoured old soldier called Vespasian. He became emperor and brought peace.

--

Vespasian decided to give the land of Nero's palace back to the people of Rome. He drained Nero's lake. And on that very spot he began to build a great amphitheatre, a place of entertainment for the people, the largest in the world.

He did not live to see it finished. He died last summer, at sixty-nine. It is said that as he felt death coming, he joked: oh dear, I think I am becoming a god. Because the Romans turned their dead emperors into gods.

{img:vespasian-old-emperor-portrait-practical-face-humorous-calm}
His son Titus became emperor. And now, in the year 80, Titus must finish the amphitheatre and open it. He plans a great festival of games that will last a hundred days. But this year has already brought one disaster after another. Will Rome be ready to celebrate?
