In the Middle Ages, the Colosseum became a strange kind of village.

People built houses, workshops and stables inside the arches and under the vaults. Blacksmiths, shoemakers and carpenters lived and worked there. There was even a little church built into it. Later, a powerful Roman family turned it into a fortress, with walls and towers.

--

Then, in 1349, a great earthquake struck Rome. The outer wall on the south side collapsed in a huge heap of stone. That is why the Colosseum today has a complete outer wall only on the north side.

For the next four hundred years, the Colosseum was used as a quarry. The Romans took its stones to build palaces, churches and bridges. Much of the stone of some of the great buildings of Rome came from the Colosseum. They dug out the iron clamps too, leaving the holes. Little by little, the great building was disappearing. Would anything be left?

{img:renaissance-carts-carrying-stones-from-colosseum-builders-1500s}
Then, about two hundred and seventy-five years ago, the popes decided to protect it. A pope declared the Colosseum a sacred place, in memory of the early Christians who were believed to have died there. He set up a great cross in the arena. Historians today are not sure how many Christians really died in the Colosseum itself. But that decision saved the building from being taken apart completely.

--

Later, the popes and then the Italian government repaired the Colosseum. They built great brick supports at each end of the surviving wall to hold it up. Travellers, writers and artists came from all over the world to see it, especially by moonlight, which was thought to be the most romantic way to see it.

The English poet Lord Byron walked there by moonlight and wrote about it. The American writer Mark Twain called it the king of all European ruins. And plant experts found more than four hundred different kinds of plants growing in the ruins. People liked to believe some of their seeds had come long ago from distant lands, in the fur and food of the animals brought for the games.

{img:moonlit-colosseum-19th-century-travellers-lanterns-romantic-ruins}
Most of those plants were later cleared away to protect the stones. But in spring, there are still wild flowers growing in the cracks and on the high ledges of the Colosseum.
