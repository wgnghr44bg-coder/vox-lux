In the summer of the year 80, the emperor Titus opens the great amphitheatre with games that last a hundred days.

People come to Rome from all over the empire and beyond to see them. The poet who wrote about the games says no people was so far away that it did not send someone. Farmers from the mountains of the Balkans. Riders from the far east. People from the source of the Nile and the shores of the Atlantic. Arabs and Ethiopians. All speaking different languages, he says. And yet all speaking with one voice when they cheered Titus as the father of his country.

--

These are the greatest games Rome has ever seen. An old historian says nine thousand animals, tame and wild, were shown during the hundred days. There were gladiator fights, hunts and shows of every kind. And it is said there was even a sea battle.

Because at the very beginning, before the tunnels under the floor were built, the arena could be flooded with water and drained again. The Romans put on a show of an old sea battle, with small real ships rowed by men, and horses and bulls trained to swim.

{img:flooded-arena-small-ships-water-show-crowd-amazed-distant}
Scholars still argue whether this really happened in the Colosseum or somewhere nearby. But several old writers describe water shows at the opening games. Imagine the crowd's amazement, seeing ships sail where moments before there had been sand.

--

On the last day of the games, an old writer says, Titus wept openly in front of the people. Perhaps he was tired. Perhaps he already knew he was not well. A year later, he was dead.

The poet wrote that the Colosseum was the greatest wonder of the world. Greater than the pyramids of Egypt and the walls of Babylon. Let Egypt stop boasting about its pyramids, he wrote. Every work gives way to Caesar's amphitheatre. Fame will speak of this one work instead of all the others.

{img:crowd-leaving-colosseum-evening-arches-lamps-streets-filling}
And in the evenings, during those hundred days, the streets around the Colosseum were full of people going home after the games. They talked and laughed and argued about the gladiators and the animals. They stopped at the taverns and food stalls, in the warm summer night.
