Who were the gladiators?

The name comes from gladius, the short sword of the Roman soldier. Gladiators were trained fighters. They fought each other in pairs in the arena, to entertain the people of Rome and of cities all over the Roman Empire.

--

The games began long ago as part of funerals. About two hundred and fifty years before the Colosseum, at the funeral of a Roman nobleman, his sons had three pairs of slaves fight in his honour. Over the centuries, the games grew larger and grander. They became the most popular entertainment in the Roman world. Rich men, politicians and emperors gave games to win the favour of the people.

Most gladiators were slaves, prisoners of war or condemned criminals. They had no choice. They were bought, owned and trained by a manager, who hired them out to the men giving the games.

But some gladiators were free men who chose this life, for the money, the fame and the excitement. They signed a contract and swore a terrible oath. For a while, they gave up their rights as free citizens.

--

In a strange way, gladiators were both looked down on and admired.

The Romans thought of them as people of the lowest kind. And yet they were also heroes and celebrities, like sports stars today. Their names were written on walls. Their pictures were painted on lamps, pots and glass cups. Children played at being gladiators.

{img:clay-oil-lamp-gladiator-picture-glass-cup-souvenir-names}
And here is something that surprises many people. Most gladiator fights did not end in death. Gladiators were expensive to buy, train and feed. Their owners did not want them killed. So the fights had rules and referees. Usually a fight ended when one fighter was wounded or exhausted, or gave up by raising one finger to ask for mercy. Very often mercy was given, and the loser was sent away to fight another day.

--

Historians who have studied gravestones and old writings think that in the time of the Colosseum, perhaps only one fight in five or ten ended in death. A gladiator might fight two or three times a year. If he was skilful and lucky, he could hope to survive for years. And perhaps, one day, he would win his freedom.

When a gladiator won his freedom, he was given a wooden sword as a sign that he was free. Some freed gladiators became trainers or referees. Some even went back to the arena, because they missed the excitement and the fame.

We will not watch the fights tonight. Instead, we will spend time with the gladiators in their school and their daily lives. How they lived and trained, ate and slept. And how they hoped, every single day, for that wooden sword.
