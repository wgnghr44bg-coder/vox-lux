After the games, many Romans go to the baths.

The new baths of Titus, opened at the same time as the Colosseum, stand just above it on the hillside. They are large and splendid. There are great halls with high curved ceilings, walls of coloured marble and floors of tiny stone pictures. There are pools and fountains, statues and gardens, exercise yards, libraries, and rooms for massage and for talking.

--

Romans go to the baths every day if they can, in the afternoon after work, before dinner. It costs almost nothing, the smallest coin. Children go free. And on special days, the emperor sometimes pays for everyone.

First the bathers undress in the changing room and leave their clothes in little holes in the wall. Sometimes a slave guards them, because thieves are common. Then they exercise in the courtyard. They run, wrestle, lift weights, or play ball games.

Then they go into the warm room, and then the hot room, full of steam. It is heated by hot air flowing under the floors and inside the walls, from great furnaces below, kept burning by slaves. They sweat, and scrape their skin clean with a curved metal tool and oil. Then they jump into the cold pool. And then they swim in the great outdoor pool, or sit and talk and relax.

--

The baths are a place to meet friends, gossip, do business, hear the news and the latest jokes, and talk about the games and the gladiators. Sellers walk around calling out: sausages, cakes, drinks! Masseurs slap and knead. Poets recite their verses to anyone who will listen.

One Roman thinker lived above a bath house and complained about the noise. The grunting of weightlifters, the slapping of masseurs, the shouts of ball players, the cries of sausage sellers. And the man who loved the sound of his own voice, singing in the bath.

{img:bathers-relaxing-pool-edge-marble-statues-evening-glow}
And then, clean and refreshed, the Romans go home to dinner in the evening, as the lamps are lit. The streets fill with people coming home from the baths, the games and their work, in the warm summer evening.
