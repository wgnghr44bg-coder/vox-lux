A year before the Colosseum opened, in the summer of 79, something happened in the south of Italy which shocked the whole Roman world.

Around the beautiful Bay of Naples, there were rich towns, and villas, and vineyards, and farms, on the green slopes of a mountain, called Vesuvius. Nobody knew that it was a volcano. It had been quiet for hundreds of years. Its slopes were covered with vineyards, and woods, and gardens, and the wine of Vesuvius was famous.

--

There had been earthquakes, seventeen years before, in the year 62, which had damaged the towns of Pompeii and Herculaneum, and the people were still repairing their houses, and temples, when, on a day in late summer, or perhaps autumn, of the year 79, the mountain woke up.

We will watch only from very far away, as a young man did, who was there, across the bay, at the town of Misenum: Pliny the Younger, who was seventeen, and later wrote two letters about it, to the historian Tacitus, which still exist. They are the first eyewitness description of a volcanic eruption in history.

{img:distant-tall-cloud-umbrella-pine-shape-over-bay-misenum-view}
He wrote that, at about one o'clock in the afternoon, his mother pointed out to his uncle a cloud of unusual size and shape, rising from a mountain, across the bay. It rose very high, he said, like an umbrella pine tree: a tall trunk, and then branches, spreading out at the top. Sometimes it was white, and sometimes dark, and patchy.

--

His uncle, Pliny the Elder, a famous scholar, and writer, and the admiral of the Roman fleet at Misenum, wanted to see it more closely, as a scientist, and then, when he received a message, asking for help, from a friend, on the other side of the bay, he took the warships, and sailed across, to rescue people. He did not come back. He passed away, on the shore, at Stabiae, peacefully, his nephew says, as if asleep.

The towns of Pompeii, and Herculaneum, and Stabiae, were buried, under metres of ash, and pumice, and rock. They were forgotten, for more than sixteen hundred years, until they were found again, in the eighteenth century.

{img:pompeii-excavated-street-forum-columns-vesuvius-behind-quiet-later}
And because they were buried so quickly, they were preserved, almost as they were, on that day, in the year 79: the streets, and the houses, and the shops, and the paintings on the walls, and the graffiti, and the bread in the ovens, and the gladiator barracks, with the helmets, and the armour, of the gladiators who had trained there.

Today, Pompeii is one of the most visited places in the world, and the best picture we have of an ordinary Roman town, in the time of the Colosseum.
