One of the people who watched the opening games of the Colosseum and wrote about them was a poet called Martial.

He was born in Spain, in a little town in the mountains. As a young man he came to Rome to make his fortune as a poet. He lived there for thirty-five years.

--

He was not rich. For many years he lived in a little flat on the third floor, up a long flight of stairs. He often complained about it in his poems. About the noise, the stairs, the cold and the rent. And about the bakers, the schoolteachers and the money changers who kept him awake from dawn with their racket.

He wrote very short, witty, clever poems of a few lines each. They are about everything. His friends and his enemies, the people of Rome and their habits, their dinner parties and their slaves, the gladiators and the games. More than fifteen hundred of them survive.

His first book was a little collection of about thirty poems about the opening games of the Colosseum, written for the emperor Titus. It is one of the main ways we know about those games.

--

Martial is funny and sharp, sometimes rude, and sometimes very tender. He wrote a beautiful little poem for a little slave girl who died at almost six. He asked the earth to lie lightly on her, because she had been so light on it.

He wrote about the simple life he longed for. A little house in the country, a good fire, sleep that makes the night short, and a mind at peace.

{img:spanish-hill-town-countryside-vines-old-poet-garden-peace}
In the end he got it. After thirty-five years in Rome, tired and older, he went home to his little town in Spain. A kind lady gave him a house, a garden and a farm. There, he wrote, he slept as long as he liked. He no longer had to wear a toga. And he no longer heard the noise of Rome.
