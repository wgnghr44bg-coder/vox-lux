One of the people who watched the opening games of the Colosseum, and wrote about them, was a poet called Marcus Valerius Martialis. We call him Martial.

He was born in Spain, around the year 40, in a little town, called Bilbilis, in the mountains of Aragon. He came to Rome, as a young man, to make his fortune as a poet, and lived there for thirty-five years.

--

He was not rich. He lived, for many years, in a little apartment, on the third floor of a building, on the Quirinal Hill, up a long flight of stairs, and complained about it, often, in his poems: about the noise, and the stairs, and the cold, and the rent, and the bakers, and the schoolteachers, and the money changers, who kept him awake, from dawn, with their noise.

He wrote epigrams: short, witty, clever poems, of a few lines, about everything: his friends, and his enemies, and his patrons, and the people of Rome, and their habits, and their vices, and their meals, and their houses, and their slaves, and their dinner parties, and the gladiators, and the games. More than fifteen hundred of them survive.

His first book was the Book of Spectacles, a little collection of about thirty poems, about the opening games of the Colosseum, in the year 80, written for the emperor Titus. It is one of the main sources we have for those games.

--

Martial is funny, and sharp, and sometimes rude, and sometimes very tender. He wrote a beautiful little poem, for a little slave girl, called Erotion, who passed away, at the age of almost six, asking the earth to lie lightly on her, because she had been so light on it.

He wrote about the simple life that he longed for: a little house in the country, a good fire, a quiet wife, sleep that makes the night short, and a mind at peace.

{img:spanish-hill-town-countryside-vines-old-poet-garden-peace}
In the end, he got it. After thirty-five years in Rome, tired, and older, he went home, to Spain, to his little town of Bilbilis, where a kind lady gave him a little house, and a garden, and a farm. And there, he wrote, he slept as long as he liked, and did not have to put on a toga, and did not hear the noise of Rome any more. He passed away there, around the year 104.
