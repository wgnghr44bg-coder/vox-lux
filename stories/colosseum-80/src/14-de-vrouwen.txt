Women are an important part of life around the Colosseum, in many ways.

In the amphitheatre, they sit at the top, in the wooden gallery, separate from the men, as the old law says. Only the priestesses of Vesta and the women of the emperor's family sit at the front, near the arena.

--

But women are passionate fans of the games and of the gladiators. On the walls of the gladiator barracks in Pompeii, there are scratched messages. One gladiator is called the sigh of the girls. Another is called the doctor of the night-time girls. The gladiators were the heartthrobs of their time.

A Roman writer a few years later tells a story, perhaps exaggerated, about a senator's wife. She left her husband, her children and her comfortable life, and ran away to Egypt with a gladiator. He was not even handsome, the writer says, with a scarred face, a bad eye and a lump on his nose. But he was a gladiator. And that was enough.

{img:pompeii-wall-graffiti-gladiator-names-scratched-plaster}
And there were women gladiators too.

--

At the opening games, an old historian says, women took part in the animal hunts. And in later games, women fought as gladiators in the arena. The next emperor held games at night, by torchlight, with women gladiators.

There is a stone carving found in Turkey, now in a museum in London. It shows two women gladiators fighting each other with shields and swords, without helmets. Their stage names are carved above them: Amazon and Achillia. The words say both were set free with honour, after a fight that ended in a draw.

{img:stone-relief-two-women-gladiators-amazon-achillia-museum-light}
Many Romans did not approve of women gladiators. About a hundred and twenty years later, an emperor banned them. But for more than a century, some women fought in the arenas of the Roman world.

--

And women worked in all the other jobs around the games. They sold food, drink, cushions and souvenirs outside the Colosseum. They ran the taverns, inns and cook shops where the crowds ate. They wove the cloth for the sunshade, the costumes and the banners. And in the gladiators' homes, wives, mothers and daughters waited after every fight for news.
