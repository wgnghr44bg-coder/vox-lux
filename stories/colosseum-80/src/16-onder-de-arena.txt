A few years after the opening, the emperor Domitian, the brother of Titus, made a great change to the Colosseum.

He built, under the floor of the arena, a great underground world, called the hypogeum: two levels of tunnels, and passages, and rooms, and cages, and machines, under the wooden floor of the arena, covered with sand. After that, the arena could no longer be flooded for sea battles. But it became a stage for spectacular shows.

--

In the hypogeum, there are long corridors, of brick, and stone, running the length of the arena, with rooms on either side, where the gladiators wait, before their fights, and the animals are kept, in cages, and the scenery is stored.

And there are lifts: about sixty of them, wooden cages, raised and lowered by ropes, and pulleys, and capstans, great wooden wheels, turned by teams of workers, or slaves, pushing on wooden bars. The lifts carry animals, and gladiators, and scenery, up from the underground, to trapdoors, in the floor of the arena, which open suddenly, so that, to the amazement of the crowd, a lion, or a bear, or a whole forest, or a mountain, appears out of nowhere, in the middle of the arena.

{img:wooden-lift-capstan-workers-rope-pulley-cage-underground}
In 2015, a team of archaeologists and engineers built a full-size working copy of one of these lifts, and tested it, in the Colosseum, and lifted a wolf, safely, up into the arena, to show how it worked.

--

The hypogeum was a busy, dark, crowded, noisy place, on the days of the games, lit by torches, and oil lamps, full of people working: the stage hands, and the animal keepers, and the carpenters, and the engineers, and the men turning the capstans, hundreds of them, all working together, to make the show run smoothly, like the backstage of a great theatre.

Today, the floor of the arena is gone, and you can look down into the hypogeum, from above, and see the maze of brick walls, and passages, and the grooves where the lifts once ran. And in recent years, part of the floor has been rebuilt, of wood, so that visitors can stand on it, and imagine what the gladiators saw, as they came out into the arena, and looked up at the fifty thousand faces, rising all around them.

{img:reconstructed-arena-floor-wooden-visitors-standing-looking-up-tiers}
The underground passages also connected the Colosseum to the gladiator school, the Ludus Magnus, next door, and to the stores, and the barracks, so that everything could be moved, in and out, without the crowd seeing it.
