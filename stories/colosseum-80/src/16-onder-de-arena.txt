A few years after the opening, the next emperor made a big change to the Colosseum.

Under the floor of the arena, he built a great underground world. Two levels of tunnels, passages, rooms, cages and machines, all under a wooden floor covered in sand. After that, the arena could no longer be flooded for sea battles. But it became a stage for amazing shows.

--

Underground there are long brick corridors running the length of the arena, with rooms on each side. Gladiators wait there before their fights. Animals are kept there in cages. Scenery is stored there.

And there are lifts, about sixty of them. They are wooden cages raised and lowered by ropes and pulleys. Teams of workers turn great wooden wheels to work them. The lifts carry animals, gladiators and scenery up to trapdoors in the arena floor. The trapdoors open suddenly. And to the crowd's amazement, a lion, a bear, or even a whole forest appears out of nowhere in the middle of the arena.

{img:wooden-lift-capstan-workers-rope-pulley-cage-underground}
A few years ago, a team of archaeologists and engineers built a full-size working copy of one of these lifts. They tested it in the Colosseum, and lifted a wolf safely up into the arena, to show how it worked.

--

On game days, this underground world was busy, dark, crowded and noisy, lit by torches and oil lamps. Stage hands and animal keepers, carpenters and engineers, and hundreds of men turning the wheels, all worked together to make the show run smoothly. Every lift had to rise at exactly the right moment. One mistake, and the whole show would go wrong in front of fifty thousand people.

Today the arena floor is gone. You can look down into the underground world from above, and see the maze of brick walls and passages, and the grooves where the lifts once ran. Part of the floor has been rebuilt in wood. Visitors can stand on it and imagine what the gladiators saw as they came out into the arena and looked up at fifty thousand faces rising all around them.

{img:reconstructed-arena-floor-wooden-visitors-standing-looking-up-tiers}
The underground passages also linked the Colosseum to the gladiator school next door, and to the storerooms and barracks. So everything could be moved in and out without the crowd seeing it.
