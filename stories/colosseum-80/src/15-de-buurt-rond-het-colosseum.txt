Let us walk around the outside of the Colosseum in the year 80, and look at the neighbourhood.

The amphitheatre stands in a valley between three of the hills of Rome. On one of them live the emperors. Around the building lies a wide paved square, with a ring of tall stone posts. Perhaps they held the ropes of the sunshade, or helped to control the crowds.

--

To the west, toward the Forum, stands a great fountain. It is a tall cone of brick and marble. Water bubbles out at the top and runs down its sides into a round basin. Travellers stop there to drink, wash their faces and rest in the cool spray.

Next to it stands Nero's giant bronze statue, now with the head of the sun god, thirty metres tall, its bronze rays shining in the sun.

{img:baths-of-titus-steps-colonnade-bathers-entering-afternoon-sun}
On a hillside to the northeast, Titus has built new public baths on part of Nero's old palace grounds. He opens them at the same time as the Colosseum. After the games, people can go there to bathe and relax, for free or for a tiny fee.

--

And on the slope along the Sacred Way into the Forum, there will soon be a great white marble arch, built after Titus's death to celebrate his victory in Judaea.

Inside the arch there are carvings. Titus rides in his victory chariot, crowned by the goddess of Victory. And there is the victory parade through Rome, carrying the treasures of the temple of Jerusalem. The great golden lampstand with its seven branches, the silver trumpets and the golden table.

{img:arch-of-titus-white-marble-relief-procession-menorah-sacred-way}
The arch still stands today. The carving of the seven-branched lampstand can still be seen. It has become a symbol of the Jewish people. For many centuries, Jews living in Rome would not walk under that arch, out of sorrow.

--

All around the Colosseum there are streets, houses, shops, taverns and workshops. There are the barracks of the sailors who work the sunshade, and the gladiator schools. There are storerooms for scenery and weapons, a hospital for wounded gladiators, and the offices of the people who organise the games.

On game days, the whole neighbourhood is full of crowds and noise. On other days it is quieter. You hear the gladiators training in their schools, the animals roaring faintly in their enclosures, and the sailors mending the canvas of the sunshade in the sunshine.
