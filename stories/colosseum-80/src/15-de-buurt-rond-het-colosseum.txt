Let us walk around the outside of the Colosseum, in the year 80, and look at the neighbourhood.

The amphitheatre stands in a valley, between the Palatine Hill, where the emperors live, the Caelian Hill, and the Esquiline Hill. Around it, there is a wide paved square, of travertine, with a row of tall stone posts, around the building, which may have been used to hold the ropes of the awning, or to control the crowds.

--

To the west, toward the Forum, there is a great fountain, called the Meta Sudans, the sweating turning post: a tall cone of brick and marble, shaped like the turning posts of the chariot racecourse, from which water bubbles out at the top, and runs down the sides, into a round basin. Travellers stop there, to drink, and wash their faces, and rest in the cool spray.

Beside it, there is Nero's giant statue, the Colossus, now with the head of the sun god, shining in the sun, thirty metres tall, with its bronze rays.

{img:baths-of-titus-steps-colonnade-bathers-entering-afternoon-sun}
On the slope of the Esquiline Hill, to the north east, Titus has built new public baths, the Baths of Titus, on part of the land of Nero's Golden House, and opened them at the same time as the Colosseum. The people can go there, after the games, to bathe, and exercise, and relax. Free, or for a tiny fee.

--

And on the slope of the Palatine, along the Sacred Way, leading into the Forum, there will soon be a great arch of white marble, the Arch of Titus, built by his brother Domitian, after his death, to celebrate his victory in Judaea, and the capture of Jerusalem.

On the inside of the arch, there are carvings: Titus, in his triumphal chariot, crowned by the goddess Victory. And the triumphal procession of the year 71, carrying the treasures of the temple of Jerusalem through the streets of Rome: the great golden lampstand, the menorah, with its seven branches, and the silver trumpets, and the golden table.

{img:arch-of-titus-white-marble-relief-procession-menorah-sacred-way}
The Arch of Titus still stands today, and the carving of the menorah can still be seen, and has become one of the symbols of the Jewish people, and of the state of Israel. For many centuries, Jews living in Rome would not walk under the arch, out of sorrow.

--

All around the Colosseum, there are streets, and houses, and shops, and taverns, and workshops, and the barracks of the sailors who work the awning, and the gladiator schools, and the storerooms for the scenery, and the weapons, and the hospital for the wounded gladiators, and the mortuary, and the offices of the people who organize the games.

On the days of the games, the whole neighbourhood is full of life, and noise, and crowds. And on the other days, it is quieter, with the sound of the gladiators training, in the schools, and the animals roaring, faintly, in their enclosures, and the sailors mending the canvas of the awning, in the sunshine.
