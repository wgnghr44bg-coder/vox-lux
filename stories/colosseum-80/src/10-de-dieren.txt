The games in the Colosseum are not only about gladiators. There are also animals, thousands of them, from every part of the Roman Empire and beyond.

The Romans bring wild animals to Rome from all over the world. Lions and leopards from North Africa. Tigers from India and Persia. Bears from Germany. Elephants from Africa. Crocodiles and hippos from Egypt. Giraffes, ostriches and zebras. Wild boar, deer, bulls and bison.

--

Catching and moving these animals is a huge business. Hunters and soldiers catch them alive with nets, pits and traps. They put them in wooden cages and carry them by ox cart and by ship across the Mediterranean to the port of Rome, and then up the river to the city.

At the port, there are floor pictures in the offices of the shipping companies showing elephants and other animals being loaded onto ships. In a great country house in Sicily, there is a long floor picture made three hundred years later. It shows lions, tigers and antelopes being caught, and an elephant walking up a plank onto a ship.

{img:mosaic-great-hunt-elephant-gangplank-ship-tigers-cages}
In Rome, the animals are kept in enclosures outside the city walls. Keepers and trainers look after them until the day of the games.

--

Some animals are trained to do tricks. The Romans love to see elephants walk on tightropes, or write in the sand with their trunks, or kneel before the emperor. There are lions trained to catch hares and carry them gently in their mouths without hurting them. There are dancing bears, and bulls that let boys stand on their backs.

A poet who was at the opening games of the Colosseum wrote a little book of poems about them. He says an elephant knelt down by itself before the emperor Titus, as if it understood that he was a god.

{img:trained-elephant-kneeling-arena-sand-crowd-emperor-box-sunlight}
In the mornings there are also hunts, where trained hunters face wild animals with spears and bows in the arena. Sometimes the arena is decorated like a forest or a mountain, with real trees, rocks and bushes that rise up through trapdoors in the floor.

--

We will not watch the hunts. But it is sad to think of the thousands of animals taken from their homes all over the world and brought to Rome. In the centuries that followed, so many were caught for the games that some animals disappeared from parts of the Roman Empire. The elephants of North Africa. The lions of Greece. The hippos of the lower Nile.
