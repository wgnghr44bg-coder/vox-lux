On hot days, in summer, the Colosseum has a roof: a great awning, of cloth, called the velarium, which can be pulled across the top, to shade the spectators from the burning sun.

It is one of the most remarkable things about the building.

--

Around the top of the outer wall, there are two hundred and forty wooden masts, held in stone brackets, and sockets, which can still be seen today. From the masts, ropes stretch across, toward the middle, like the spokes of a wheel, or the rigging of a ship. And along the ropes, great strips of canvas, or linen, are pulled, and unfurled, to cover most of the seats, leaving an opening in the middle, over the arena, so that the sun shines on the sand, and the spectators sit in the shade.

Nobody is quite sure exactly how it worked. Engineers today still argue about it.

{img:sailors-misenum-fleet-hauling-awning-ropes-top-of-wall-wind}
But we know who worked it: sailors. More than a thousand sailors from the Roman navy, from the great fleet at Misenum, near Naples, are stationed in Rome, in a barracks near the Colosseum, just to work the awning. Because sailors know about sails, and ropes, and wind, and climbing. They haul on the ropes, and climb the masts, and adjust the canvas, as the sun moves across the sky, and the wind changes, like sailors on a great ship.

--

When it is windy, the awning cannot be used, because the wind would tear it, or pull down the masts. On such days, the spectators bring their own hats, with broad brims, and little parasols. And the advertisements for the games, painted on the walls of Pompeii, sometimes promise: there will be awnings. Because that made people more likely to come.

The awning is often coloured. The poet Lucretius describes the sunlight shining through coloured awnings, red, and yellow, and purple, in a theatre, and colouring the whole crowd, and the stage, and the senators, so that everything glows in the colour of the awning.

{img:coloured-light-through-awning-crowd-glowing-red-yellow-amphitheatre}
And on the hottest days, there are fountains, and sprays of perfumed water, scented with saffron, and balsam, sprayed over the crowd, to cool them, and to mask the smells of the crowd, and the animals.

--

Imagine sitting high up in the Colosseum, on a summer afternoon, in the cool, coloured shade of the great awning, with fifty thousand other people, and the smell of saffron in the air, and the sound of the sailors' calls, high above, as they pull on the ropes, and the bright sunlight shining on the sand of the arena, far below.
