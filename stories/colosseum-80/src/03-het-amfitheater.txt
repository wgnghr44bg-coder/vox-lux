The Colosseum is the largest amphitheatre ever built.

An amphitheatre is a round, or oval, open-air theatre, with seats all around, rising up in rows, and a flat space in the middle, the arena, where the shows take place. The word arena means sand, because the floor is covered with sand.

--

The Colosseum is an oval, about a hundred and eighty-nine metres long, and a hundred and fifty-six metres wide, and nearly fifty metres high: as tall as a building of fifteen storeys today. It can hold perhaps fifty thousand people, or more, sitting on stone seats, in rows, all around the arena.

The outside wall has four storeys. The bottom three have eighty arches each, one above the other, framed by half-columns, of three different styles: at the bottom, the plain, sturdy Tuscan style. In the middle, the Ionic style, with its scroll-shaped tops. And above, the elegant Corinthian style, with its leaves. The top storey has no arches, but small square windows, and tall, flat pilasters, and brackets, to hold the great poles of the awning.

{img:statues-in-arches-upper-storeys-colosseum-sunlit-travertine}
In each of the arches of the second and third storeys, there is a statue: gods, and heroes, and emperors. The whole outside is built of travertine, a creamy white limestone, from quarries at Tibur, about thirty kilometres away. In the sunshine, it glows, pale gold.

--

There are eighty entrances, at ground level, each with a number carved above it, which can still be seen today. Every spectator has a ticket, a little piece of pottery, or bone, with the number of his entrance, and his section, and his row, and his seat. So fifty thousand people can come in, and find their places, in about fifteen minutes, and leave again, just as quickly, through the corridors, and staircases, which the Romans called vomitoria, because they let the crowds pour out.

Four of the entrances are special: the two at the ends of the long axis, for the gladiators, and the processions. And the two at the ends of the short axis, for the emperor, and his guests, and the most important people.

{img:numbered-entrance-arches-crowd-entering-tokens-morning-sun}
Inside, the seats are arranged according to rank, in rings, one above another. In the front row, nearest the arena, on a wide platform, called the podium, there are the senators, in their white togas with purple stripes, on wide marble seats, with their names carved on them. And the Vestal Virgins, the priestesses of Vesta. And the emperor, in his special box, the pulvinar, in the middle of one of the long sides.

--

Behind them, there are the knights, the rich men of Rome. Then the ordinary citizens, in their white togas, which they must wear. Then, higher up, the poorer citizens. And at the very top, in a wooden gallery, under the colonnade, the women, and the slaves, and the poorest people. Augustus, many years before, had made a law that women must sit at the top, separately.

The higher you sit, the further you are from the arena, and the less important you are. But from the top, you have a wonderful view, over the whole amphitheatre, and over the roofs of Rome, to the hills.
