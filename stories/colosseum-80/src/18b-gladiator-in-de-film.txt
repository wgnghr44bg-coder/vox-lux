The Colosseum, and the gladiators, have fascinated people ever since the games ended, and especially in the last hundred years, in books, and paintings, and films.

In the nineteenth century, the French painter Jean-Léon Gérôme painted famous pictures of the games, which showed the gladiators saluting the emperor, and the crowd, with their thumbs, and gave people the idea, which has lasted ever since, that the crowd decided the fate of a defeated gladiator by turning their thumbs up, or down. In fact, historians are not sure what gesture the Romans really used. Perhaps a thumb hidden in the fist meant mercy, and an outstretched thumb meant the opposite. Nobody knows for certain.

--

The famous words, which, people say, the gladiators spoke to the emperor, before they fought: Hail, emperor, those who are about to die salute you, are recorded only once, in Suetonius, and not by gladiators at all, but by prisoners, condemned to fight in a sea battle, on a lake, for the emperor Claudius. It was probably not a regular custom.

In 1960, there was the film Spartacus. And in 2000, the film Gladiator, directed by Ridley Scott, with Russell Crowe as a Roman general, who becomes a gladiator, and fights in the Colosseum, which was rebuilt, on film, with computers, to look as it did when it was new. The film was enormously popular, and won many prizes, and made millions of people curious about ancient Rome, and the Colosseum.

{img:tourists-gladiator-costumes-outside-colosseum-photographs-sunny}
In 2024, there was a second film, Gladiator II, which even showed a sea battle, in the flooded arena of the Colosseum, with sharks, which is certainly not true to history, but shows how the old stories of the opening games still capture people's imagination.

--

Today, outside the Colosseum, men dressed as gladiators, and Roman soldiers, in plastic armour, and red cloaks, pose for photographs with tourists, for a few coins, as people dressed as gladiators may have done, in some way, two thousand years ago, on the same square, selling souvenirs, and little clay figures, to the crowds on their way to the games.
