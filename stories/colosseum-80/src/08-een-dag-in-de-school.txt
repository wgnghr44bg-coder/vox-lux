Let us spend an ordinary day in the gladiator school, beside the Colosseum, in the spring of the year 80, a few weeks before the great games.

The day begins at dawn, with a horn, or a bell. The gladiators get up, from their straw mattresses, in their little cells, and wash, at the fountain, in the courtyard, and eat breakfast.

--

What do gladiators eat? In 2014, scientists studied the bones of gladiators, found in a gladiators' cemetery, at Ephesus, in Turkey, and discovered that they ate mostly barley, and beans, and very little meat. Roman writers called them hordearii, the barley men. A diet of barley and beans, with plenty of carbohydrates, may have given them a layer of fat, to protect them from cuts, in the arena.

And the scientists found something else: the gladiators' bones had a lot of strontium, which may mean that they drank a special drink, made of plant ashes, mixed with water, or vinegar, which the Roman writer Pliny mentions, as a drink for gladiators, to strengthen their bones, and help them recover from injuries. A kind of ancient sports drink.

After breakfast, the training begins.

--

The trainers, called doctores, many of them former gladiators, who have won their freedom, teach the gladiators to fight, in their own particular style. Each kind of gladiator has its own trainer, and its own techniques.

The beginners start with wooden swords, and wicker shields, which are heavier than the real ones, and practise against a wooden post, the palus, striking it, again and again, for hours, to build up their strength, and learn the movements. Then they practise in pairs, with wooden weapons, slowly at first, then faster.

{img:pairs-practising-wooden-weapons-sand-trainer-watching-shade}
They practise footwork, and blocking, and striking, and falling, and getting up again. They run, and lift weights, and wrestle. The training is hard, and long, and repetitive, like the training of athletes, or soldiers, or dancers, today.

--

At midday, they rest, and eat again, and bathe, and are massaged, with oil, by the masseurs, and their injuries are treated by the doctors.

The doctors of the gladiator schools are among the best in the Roman world, because they have so much experience of injuries. A hundred years later, the great Greek doctor Galen, the most famous doctor of the ancient world, will begin his career as the doctor of the gladiators, at Pergamum, in Asia Minor, and will say that he learned more about the human body there than anywhere else. He was proud that, in his time, very few of his gladiators died of their wounds.

{img:afternoon-gladiators-resting-shade-colonnade-dice-talking}
In the afternoon, more training. And then, in the evening, supper, and rest, and talk, and games of dice, and knucklebones, and letters home, for those who can write, and sleep, in their little cells, with the sound of the city outside, and the great dark shape of the Colosseum, rising against the stars, next door.
