Let us spend an ordinary day in the gladiator school beside the Colosseum, in the spring of the year 80, a few weeks before the great games.

The day begins at dawn with a horn or a bell. The gladiators get up from their straw mattresses in their little cells. They wash at the fountain in the courtyard and eat breakfast.

--

What do gladiators eat? A few years ago, scientists studied the bones of gladiators buried in a gladiators' cemetery in Turkey. They found that the gladiators ate mostly barley and beans, and very little meat. Roman writers even called them the barley men. A diet like this may have given them a layer of fat that protected them from cuts in the arena.

The scientists found something else too. The bones suggest the gladiators drank a special drink made of plant ashes mixed with water or vinegar, to make their bones stronger and help them heal. A Roman writer mentions it. It was a kind of ancient sports drink.

After breakfast, the training begins.

--

The trainers are often former gladiators who have won their freedom. Each kind of gladiator has its own trainer and its own way of fighting.

Beginners start with wooden swords and wicker shields, which are heavier than the real ones. They strike a wooden post again and again for hours, to build up strength and learn the movements. Then they practise in pairs with wooden weapons, slowly at first, then faster.

{img:pairs-practising-wooden-weapons-sand-trainer-watching-shade}
They practise footwork and blocking, striking and falling and getting up again. They run, lift weights and wrestle. The training is hard, long and repetitive, like the training of athletes or soldiers or dancers today. And every man knows that the games are only weeks away now.

--

At midday they rest, eat again and bathe. They are massaged with oil, and the doctors treat their injuries.

The doctors of the gladiator schools are among the best in the Roman world, because they see so many injuries. A hundred years later, the most famous doctor of the ancient world will start his career as a doctor for gladiators. He will say he learned more about the human body there than anywhere else.

{img:afternoon-gladiators-resting-shade-colonnade-dice-talking}
In the afternoon there is more training. Then in the evening comes supper and rest. The men talk and play dice, and some write letters home. Then they sleep in their little cells, with the sounds of the city outside, and the great dark shape of the Colosseum rising against the stars next door.
