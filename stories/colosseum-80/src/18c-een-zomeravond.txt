Let us end with a summer evening, in the year 80, during the hundred days of the opening games.

The games are over for the day. The crowds have gone home, through the eighty arches, into the streets. The amphitheatre is quiet now, empty, enormous, in the soft evening light, with the last of the sun shining on its upper storeys, and the statues in its arches, and the masts of the awning, black against the pink sky.

--

The sailors have rolled up the great awning, and climbed down, and gone back to their barracks, nearby, to eat, and rest. The workers are sweeping the corridors, and the seats, and raking the sand of the arena, smooth again, for tomorrow. The gladiators who fought today are back in their school, next door, eating their supper, and having their wounds treated, and talking quietly, about the day.

In the animal enclosures, outside the walls, the keepers are feeding the animals, and the elephants are drinking, and the lions are lying, quietly, in the cool of the evening.

{img:colosseum-dusk-empty-arena-raked-sand-pink-sky-swallows}
And in the streets around the Colosseum, the people of Rome are walking, slowly, in the warm evening air, to the baths, and the taverns, and home, talking about the games, and the gladiators, and the emperor, while the swallows swoop and circle, around the high arches, and the first lamps are lit, in the windows of the apartment buildings, and the sky turns from pink, to violet, to deep blue, and the first stars come out, over the great, quiet amphitheatre, and the sleeping city.
