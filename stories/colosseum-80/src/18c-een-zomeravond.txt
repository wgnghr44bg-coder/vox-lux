Let us end with a summer evening in the year 80, during the hundred days of the opening games.

The games are over for the day. The crowds have gone home through the eighty arches into the streets. The amphitheatre is quiet now, empty and enormous in the soft evening light. The last of the sun shines on its upper storeys, on the statues in the arches, and on the masts of the sunshade, black against the pink sky.

--

The sailors have rolled up the great sunshade, climbed down and gone back to their barracks to eat and rest. Workers sweep the corridors and the seats, and rake the sand of the arena smooth again for tomorrow. In the school next door, the gladiators who fought today eat their supper, have their wounds treated, and talk quietly about the day. Tomorrow, others will walk through the tunnel.

In the animal enclosures outside the walls, the keepers feed the animals. The elephants drink, and the lions lie quietly in the cool of the evening.

{img:colosseum-dusk-empty-arena-raked-sand-pink-sky-swallows}
And in the streets around the Colosseum, the people of Rome walk slowly in the warm evening air, to the baths, the taverns and home. They talk about the games and the gladiators and the emperor. Swallows swoop and circle around the high arches. The first lamps are lit in the windows. The sky turns from pink to violet to deep blue. And the first stars come out over the great quiet amphitheatre and the sleeping city.
