London Bridge is one of the wonders of England. It is the only bridge over the Thames in London. It is more than three hundred and fifty years old in 1599, built of stone, with nineteen arches. And on top of it, there is a whole street of houses and shops. Some of them are four or five storeys high, hanging out over the river on both sides.

--

Walking across London Bridge is like walking down a busy, narrow street. There are shops on both sides, selling everything. Gloves, hats, needles, pins, cloth, silk and haberdashery. The shopkeepers live above their shops. In some places, the houses are built across the street, like a tunnel. In others, there are gaps, where you can look out over the river. It is so crowded that it can take an hour to walk across.

In the middle of the bridge, there is a little chapel, and a big, beautiful house called Nonsuch House. It was built about twenty years before, in pieces, in the Netherlands, and brought to London by ship, and put together on the bridge, with wooden pegs, without a single iron nail. It is painted in bright colours, with towers and onion domes, and many windows. It is one of the most admired buildings in London.

{img:nonsuch-house-colourful-towers-onion-domes-on-bridge-sunlit}
The arches of the bridge are narrow, and the stone piers are very wide, standing on big wooden platforms called starlings. So the river is squeezed through the arches. When the tide is going out, the water rushes through the arches like a waterfall, foaming and roaring. The difference in the height of the water on the two sides of the bridge can be more than a metre. Going through the arches in a boat is called shooting the bridge. It is very exciting, and a little dangerous.

Many passengers get out of their boats before the bridge, and walk around it, and get back in on the other side. The watermen say that London Bridge was made for wise men to go over, and fools to go under. But some young men love to shoot the bridge, for the thrill. Ned dreams of doing it one day. His mother says that he certainly will not.

--

{img:waterwheels-under-london-bridge-arches-river-foam-pumping-water}
Under some of the arches, there are big waterwheels, turned by the rushing water. They work pumps, which push water from the river up through pipes into the houses of the city. The water is not very clean, of course. But it is useful for washing and cleaning. The wheels make a great creaking and splashing noise, day and night. People who live on the bridge get used to it, and cannot sleep without it.

At the southern end of the bridge, there is a great stone gateway. And on top of the gateway, sadly, there is something that every visitor to London remembers. But we will not look at that. We will look instead at the view from the bridge, over the river. Upstream, the river curves away to the west, past the palaces and gardens. Downstream, there is the Pool of London, crowded with ships, and the grey walls of the Tower.

{img:view-from-london-bridge-ships-pool-tower-sunset-gulls-river}
Bess and Ned often walk across the bridge, to visit their aunt, who lives in Southwark, on the other side. They love to stop at the gaps between the houses, and lean over the wall, and look down at the rushing water below, and the boats shooting through the arches. They watch the gulls and the swans, and the ships coming in from the sea. And they count the boats, and argue about whose boat is the biggest.

--

There is a famous old song about the bridge. London Bridge is falling down, falling down, falling down. Children sing it, holding hands, in a circle. It is probably very old already in 1599. The bridge has been damaged many times, by floods, fires and ice. But it has always been repaired. And it will stand, with its houses and its shops, for more than two hundred years more, until a new bridge replaces it.
