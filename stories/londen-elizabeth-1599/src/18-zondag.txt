On Sundays, the shops of London are closed. Nobody works. And everyone goes to church. In fact, it is the law. Everyone must go to church on Sunday, to their own parish church. If they do not go, they must pay a fine. The bells of more than a hundred churches ring out over the city, calling the people to prayer.

--

The Wood family puts on their best clothes. Thomas wears his good black doublet and a starched white ruff around his neck. Margaret wears her best gown, of dark blue wool, with a white lace collar, and a hat. Bess has a new bodice, embroidered by her mother, and a clean white cap. Ned's hair has been combed, by force. And even Kit and Joan are dressed in their Sunday best.

Their parish church is St Mary-le-Bow, in Cheapside. It is an old church, with a tall tower, and famous bells. People say that a true Londoner, a Cockney, is someone born within the sound of Bow Bells. The bells ring every evening too, at nine o'clock, as the curfew, to tell the apprentices to stop work and go home.

{img:interior-church-1599-whitewashed-walls-pulpit-pews-congregation-sermon}
Inside, the church is plain and simple. The walls are whitewashed. Most of the old paintings and statues have been removed, because of the changes in religion in England, earlier in the century. There is a pulpit, from which the minister preaches. There are wooden benches and pews, where the families sit. The important families sit at the front. The poor sit at the back, or stand. And on the walls, there are boards painted with the Ten Commandments.

The service is in English, from the Book of Common Prayer. There are prayers, readings from the Bible, and psalms, which everyone sings together. And there is a sermon. Sometimes the sermon is very long, an hour or even two hours. The minister speaks about God, about sin and goodness, about how people should live. Some ministers are wonderful speakers. Others are very dull. Ned sometimes falls asleep, and Margaret pokes him with her elbow.

--

{img:family-walking-home-after-church-cheapside-sunday-afternoon-sunny}
After church, there is Sunday dinner. It is the best meal of the week. Roast beef, if they can afford it, with a pudding and vegetables, and a pie. Sometimes they invite friends or neighbours. The Dutch family from next door. Or Margaret's sister, the aunt from Southwark, with her husband and her children. The table is crowded and noisy. Everyone talks at once. And the cat sits under the table, waiting for scraps.

In the afternoon, people rest, or go for walks. In summer, they walk in the fields outside the city walls, or along the river. Young men and women walk together, and flirt a little, under the eyes of their parents. Old people sit in the sun. Children play quietly, because it is Sunday, and loud games are not allowed. Some people read the Bible. Some people sing psalms. Some people simply sleep.

{img:father-reading-bible-aloud-fireside-family-listening-sunday-evening}
In the evening, Thomas reads from the big family Bible, by the fire. He reads slowly, following the words with his finger. The Bible is in English, which is still quite new. For centuries, the Bible was only in Latin, and ordinary people could not understand it. Now, anyone who can read can read it for themselves. The family listens. Bess follows the words over her father's shoulder. Ned looks at the pictures.

--

Sunday is a quiet day, and a good one. After a week of hard work in the shop, and the noise of Cheapside, it is a day of rest, of family, of prayer and of peace. And when the bells of Bow ring out at nine o'clock, and the family goes up to bed, everyone feels calm and rested, ready for another week.
