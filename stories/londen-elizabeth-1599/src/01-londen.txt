Tonight we travel back more than four hundred years, to the year 1599. We go to London, the capital of England, on the banks of the river Thames. It is the time of Queen Elizabeth the First. She has ruled England for more than forty years. And London is busy, crowded, noisy, smelly and full of life. One of the most exciting cities in the world.

--

It is early morning in late summer. A soft mist lies over the river. Out of the mist rise the church towers and spires of the city, more than a hundred of them. The great old cathedral of St Paul's stands on its hill, above all the rest. Seagulls cry over the water. And across the river stretches London Bridge, with houses and shops built right on top of it, so many that it looks like a street floating over the water.

On the river, the first boats are already moving. Little rowing boats, carrying passengers from one bank to the other. Barges loaded with wood, hay and coal. Fishing boats coming back with their catch. The boatmen call out to each other. And from the city, the bells begin to ring. One church, then another, then dozens, all over London, calling people to wake up and begin the day.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

{img:cheapside-street-1599-timber-houses-shop-signs-crowds-carts-morning}
In the streets, people are waking up. Shopkeepers are opening their shutters. Apprentices are sweeping the doorsteps. Water carriers are walking from house to house with big wooden tankards of water on their shoulders. Carts are rattling over the cobbles, bringing food into the city from the countryside. And in a narrow street called Cheapside, in the heart of the city, a family of glovers is getting ready for the day.

Tonight, we will spend some time with this family. The father is a glover, who makes and sells fine leather gloves. His wife helps him in the shop. They have a daughter of thirteen, called Bess, and a son of eleven, called Ned. We will see their house and their shop, their food and their friends. We will walk with them through the markets and along the river. And we will go with them to church on Sunday.

{img:globe-theatre-1599-new-round-wooden-theatre-bankside-flag-flying}
We will also visit a brand-new building, on the other side of the river. It is a round, wooden theatre, built just this year, with a flag flying from its roof. It is called the Globe. And in it, the plays of a man called William Shakespeare are being performed for the first time. We will stand in the crowd, and watch one of them, in the afternoon sun.

--

And we will catch a glimpse of the queen herself. She is sixty-five years old, and has ruled England since 1558. She has never married. She has seen her country through many dangers. And her people love her, and call her Good Queen Bess, and Gloriana, and the Virgin Queen. Her reign is often called the golden age of England.

{img:queen-elizabeth-i-1599-white-face-red-hair-pearls-ruff-dignified-portrait}
London in 1599 was not always pleasant. It was dirty and crowded, and there were many poor people. Diseases spread easily. But it was also a city of energy, of new ideas, of trade with the whole world, of music and poetry and theatre. So let us begin, on a late summer morning in 1599, as the bells ring out over the Thames, and the great city wakes up.
