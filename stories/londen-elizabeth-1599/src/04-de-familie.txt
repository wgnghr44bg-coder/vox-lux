In Cheapside, the busiest street of London, there is a narrow timber house, with a shop on the ground floor. Above the door hangs a painted wooden sign, in the shape of a glove. There are no house numbers in London in 1599. So every shop has a sign, to show what it sells, and people find their way by the signs. This one is the shop of Thomas Wood, glover.

--

Thomas is about forty years old. He is a tall, thin man, with a short brown beard and careful hands. He makes gloves of soft leather. Plain gloves for workers, warm gloves for winter, and fine gloves for ladies and gentlemen. The finest ones are made of thin, soft kidskin, embroidered with silk and gold thread, and perfumed with sweet scents. Gloves are very fashionable. Rich people give them as presents, and carry them in their hands to show off.

Thomas is a member of the Company of Glovers. In London, every trade has its own company, or guild. The bakers, the butchers, the goldsmiths, the drapers, the fishmongers and all the others. The company makes rules about the trade, about prices, quality and training. It looks after its members, and helps their widows and children if they are in need. Every year, it holds a great feast, in its hall.

{img:mother-sewing-embroidery-gloves-window-light-daughter-watching}
His wife is called Margaret. She is a small, lively woman, with quick blue eyes and a ready laugh. She works in the shop too. She does the fine embroidery on the gloves, in silk and gold thread, with patterns of flowers, birds and leaves. She is the best embroiderer in the street. She also runs the house, does the cooking, keeps the accounts, and knows everybody's business in Cheapside.

Their daughter, Bess, is thirteen. She helps her mother with the embroidery, and is already very skilled. She also helps in the shop, serving customers, and she can read and write a little, which her father taught her. She is curious about everything. She loves to stand at the shop window and watch the people go by in the busy street. And she has a secret wish. She would love, one day, to see the queen up close.

--

{img:boy-eleven-tudor-clothes-reciting-lines-pretending-actor-attic}
Their son, Ned, is eleven. He goes to a small school in the mornings, where he learns to read, write and do sums, and a little Latin. In the afternoons, he is supposed to help his father in the workshop. But Ned has another passion. He loves plays. Whenever he can, he slips away to watch the actors, in the inn yards and the theatres. He knows long speeches by heart. And he recites them in the attic, to the cat.

There is also an apprentice in the house, a young man of seventeen called Kit, from a village in Kent. He is learning the trade of the glover from Thomas. He will live with the family for seven years, working for them, and in return, they will feed him, house him and teach him. At the end, he will become a glover himself. Kit is shy and hardworking. And he is a little bit in love with Bess, though he would never say so.

{img:family-supper-table-tudor-house-candles-pewter-plates-evening}
And there is a maid, a cheerful country girl called Joan, who helps with the cooking and the cleaning. She sleeps in a little room under the roof. Like many maids in London, she came from the countryside, to earn some money before she marries. She sends most of her wages home to her mother. She sings while she works, old country songs, which Bess loves to hear.

--

The family is not rich, but they are comfortable. They have a good house, enough food, and some savings. They have fine clothes for Sundays. They are respected in their street. Thomas is a member of his company, and hopes one day to be one of its masters. And in the evenings, when the shop is closed, they sit together by the fire, and talk about the day, and the news, and the queen, and whatever else is happening in the great city outside.
