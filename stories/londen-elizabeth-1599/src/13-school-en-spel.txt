Every morning, Ned goes to school. It is a small school, in a room above a shop, a few streets away. It is called a petty school, where young children learn to read, write and count. The schoolmaster is an old man with spectacles and a long black gown. He is strict, but not unkind. About twenty boys sit on long benches, with their books on their knees.

--

The youngest children learn to read with a hornbook. It is a little wooden board, with a handle, like a small paddle. On it, there is a sheet of paper, with the alphabet and the Lord's Prayer printed on it. And over the paper, there is a thin, clear sheet of cow's horn, to protect it from dirty fingers. The children point to the letters and say them aloud, again and again, until they know them by heart.

The older boys, like Ned, learn to write with a quill pen and ink, on paper. They learn sums. And they begin to learn a little Latin, which is the language of scholars, lawyers and churchmen. Clever boys may go on to a grammar school, where they will learn much more Latin, and Greek, and read the old Roman poets and writers. William Shakespeare went to a grammar school, in the town of Stratford, where he learned to love the old stories.

{img:schoolboys-reciting-latin-schoolmaster-pointing-book-morning-light}
Ned is not a very good pupil. He is bored by sums, and finds Latin grammar terribly dull. But he loves one thing about school. Sometimes, the schoolmaster lets the boys act out little scenes from old Latin plays, to help them learn the language. Then Ned comes alive. He acts every part, with all his heart, and makes the other boys laugh. The schoolmaster shakes his head. But secretly, he smiles.

Most girls in 1599 do not go to school. Some girls from rich families have tutors at home, and learn to read, write, sing, play music, speak French and dance. The queen herself was one of the most educated women in Europe. But most girls learn at home, from their mothers. They learn to cook, sew, embroider, keep house and look after children. Some learn to read a little, from the Bible. Bess learned to read from her father, in the evenings, by the fire.

--

{img:tudor-children-playing-street-games-hoop-spinning-top-hopscotch-sunny}
When they are not at school or at work, the children of London play. In the streets, in the courtyards and in the fields outside the walls. They play with hoops and sticks, spinning tops and marbles. They play hide and seek, blind man's buff, and leapfrog. They play a game called hopscotch, which was already old in 1599. They fly kites. They play football, a rough game with few rules, which often ends with bruises.

Girls play with dolls, made of wood or cloth, which they dress in little gowns. They play skipping games and clapping games, with songs. They play cat's cradle, with a loop of string. And they play at being grown-ups. Running a shop, cooking a meal, holding a wedding. Bess is too old for most of these games now. But sometimes she still plays cat's cradle with the little girl next door.

{img:fields-outside-london-walls-children-flying-kites-windmills-summer}
On holidays, the people of London go out into the fields outside the walls, to the north and east of the city. There, there are green meadows, archery grounds, windmills and little ponds. People walk, have picnics, play games, practise archery and dance. In winter, when the ponds freeze, they go skating, with skates made of animal bones tied to their shoes. The children love it.

--

One summer holiday, Thomas takes the whole family out to the fields at Moorfields, just north of the City wall. They bring a basket of bread, cheese, cold chicken and plums. They sit on the grass and eat. Ned flies a kite, which Kit made for him. Bess makes a chain of daisies. Margaret and Thomas sit together in the sun, and do nothing at all, for once. And the windmills turn slowly on the hill, in the warm summer breeze.

On the way home, as the sun sets, the windmills stop turning, one by one. The family walks back through Moorgate, into the city, with the crowds of other people coming home from the fields. Ned carries his kite. Bess wears her daisy chain. And they all agree that it was the best day of the summer.
