The day begins early in London. At about four or five in the morning, the bells of the churches ring. Thomas and Margaret get up first. Then Joan, the maid, who lights the fire and fetches water. Then Kit, the apprentice, who must open the shop and sweep the doorstep. And last of all, the children, who must be called three times before they come down.

--

People in 1599 do not wash very much. They think that too much water is bad for the health, because it opens the skin to diseases. Instead, they wash their hands and faces in a bowl of cold water, and rub their bodies with a clean linen cloth. They change their linen shirts often, because they believe that clean linen draws the dirt out of the skin. Rich people use perfumes, to cover the smells. Everyone else simply gets used to them.

Bess puts on her clothes. First, a long linen shift, which is like a long shirt, and which she also sleeps in. Then a bodice, laced up the front, and a skirt of wool. Over that, an apron, and a white linen cap on her head, because women and girls always cover their hair. And on her feet, leather shoes. Ned puts on a linen shirt, a doublet, which is like a short jacket, and woollen breeches and stockings. Then he tries to escape without combing his hair.

{img:tudor-breakfast-bread-ale-cheese-family-quick-meal-kitchen-dawn}
Breakfast is simple and quick. Bread, a piece of cheese, perhaps some leftover meat from the day before, and a mug of ale. Ale is drunk by everyone, even children, because the water in London is often not safe to drink. It is a weak, slightly sweet ale, made by brewers in the city. Then everyone goes to work. Thomas and Kit in the workshop. Margaret and Bess at the embroidery. Joan at the housework. And Ned to school.

Outside, Cheapside is already busy. It is the widest and grandest street in the City. On both sides, there are shops, many of them goldsmiths, with their windows full of gold and silver plates, cups and jewellery. Other shops sell cloth, silk, lace, books, spices and every kind of luxury. In the middle of the street, there are market stalls, selling food. And there are great stone crosses and fountains, called conduits, where people come for water.

--

{img:conduit-fountain-cheapside-water-carriers-women-buckets-gossip}
The conduits are very important. They bring fresh water from springs outside the city, through lead pipes, into the middle of London. People come with buckets and jugs to fetch water. Or they pay water carriers, called cobs, to bring it to their houses. The water carriers walk all day, with huge wooden tankards on their shoulders, crying out, fresh water, fresh water. The conduit is also a great place for gossip. All the news of the street is passed on there.

At eleven o'clock, or a little before, it is time for dinner, the main meal of the day. The shop is closed for an hour. The whole family, with Kit and Joan, sits around the table in the hall. Thomas says a grace. And then they eat. After dinner, everyone goes back to work, until the evening. In summer, when the days are long, work goes on until seven or eight o'clock.

{img:apprentices-playing-football-street-evening-shops-closing-laughter}
In the evening, when the shops are closed, the streets are full of people again. Apprentices play football in the streets, with a leather ball stuffed with straw, which is not allowed, but they do it anyway. Old men sit on benches outside their doors. Women talk at their windows. Children play games. And the smell of supper drifts out from the kitchens.

--

Then the curfew bell rings, at about nine o'clock. The apprentices must go home. The gates of the City are closed. The watchmen begin to walk the streets with their lanterns and their bells, calling out the hour and the weather. Nine o'clock, and a fine summer night. And the family goes up to bed, by the light of a single candle, as the great city slowly falls asleep.

Bess likes the mornings best. When she opens the shutters of the shop, the street is still cool and grey, and the first sellers are arriving with their baskets. A milkmaid comes by with two pails on a yoke, calling out. A baker's boy runs past with a tray of fresh bread on his head. And the sparrows hop along the gutter, looking for crumbs. Then the sun comes up over the rooftops, and Cheapside fills with light.
