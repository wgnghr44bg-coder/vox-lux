So we leave London tonight, in the year 1599, as the evening falls. The sun is setting over the river, in the west, behind the palaces of the Strand. The water of the Thames turns red and gold. The last boats are crossing to Southwark. And the swans are gliding slowly toward the reeds, to sleep.

--

On Bankside, the flag on the roof of the Globe has been taken down. The play is over. The crowds have gone home, across the bridge and in the boats. The actors are taking off their costumes and their paint. And the empty theatre stands quietly by the river, its round shape dark against the sky, waiting for tomorrow's play.

{img:cheapside-dusk-shops-closing-shutters-lantern-glow-quiet-street}
In Cheapside, the shops are closing. The shutters are being pulled up and locked. The apprentices are sweeping the doorsteps one last time. In the glover's house, Margaret is putting away her embroidery, and Thomas is counting the money from the day. Joan is in the kitchen, stirring a pot of soup. And the smell of supper drifts out into the evening street.

--

The bells of Bow ring out for the curfew. Nine o'clock. The gates of the City close. The watchmen begin their rounds, with their lanterns and their bells, calling out softly. Nine o'clock, and a fair night, and all is well. In the houses, the fires are covered. The candles are lit, one by one, in the upstairs windows. And then, one by one, they go out again.

{img:girl-at-small-attic-window-night-rooftops-london-stars-st-pauls-tower}
High up under the roof, in a small room, a girl of thirteen is looking out of her little window. Over the rooftops of Cheapside, she can see the dark tower of St Paul's against the stars. She can hear the river, far away, and the soft calls of the watchmen. She is thinking about the queen in her golden barge, and the play at the Globe, and the globe of Master Pieter, spinning under her finger. And she is smiling.

--

In the next room, her brother is already asleep, with a little printed play under his pillow. The cat is curled up at the foot of his bed. Downstairs, the fire glows softly under its cover of ashes. The great city is quiet now. And over all of London, over the river and the bridge, the churches and the theatres, the palaces and the little timber houses, the moon is rising, calm and silver, in the autumn sky.
