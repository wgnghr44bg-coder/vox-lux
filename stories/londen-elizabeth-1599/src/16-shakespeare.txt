In 1599, William Shakespeare is thirty-five years old. He is an actor, a playwright and a shareholder in the Globe. He is already well known in London. His plays are popular. But he is not yet the most famous writer in the world, as he will become. He is simply a successful man of the theatre, working hard, writing new plays, and acting in them.

--

Shakespeare was born in 1564, in Stratford-upon-Avon, a small market town in the middle of England, by a river. His father, John Shakespeare, was a glover, just like Thomas Wood. He made gloves, and sold them in a shop on Henley Street. He was also an important man in the town, and was even mayor for a while. William grew up among the leather and the gloves, just like Bess and Ned.

William went to the grammar school in Stratford, where he learned Latin, and read the old Roman poets. At eighteen, he married a woman called Anne Hathaway, who was eight years older than him. They had three children, a daughter called Susanna, and twins, called Hamnet and Judith. Then, some time in the next few years, William left Stratford and came to London, to work in the theatre. Nobody knows exactly when or why.

{img:stratford-upon-avon-glovers-shop-henley-street-timber-house-river}
In London, he became an actor, and then began to write plays. At first, plays about English history, about kings and wars. Then comedies, full of love, mistaken identities and funny servants. Then tragedies, like Romeo and Juliet. His plays were full of wonderful language, unforgettable characters and new words. Many words and phrases we still use today were first written by Shakespeare. Or at least, he was the first to write them down.

Shakespeare's family stayed in Stratford. He visited them when he could, perhaps once a year, a journey of several days on horseback. In 1596, his only son, Hamnet, passed away, at the age of eleven. It was a great sorrow. Some people think that it can be felt in the plays he wrote afterwards. Shakespeare used his money to buy the second-biggest house in Stratford, called New Place, for his family.

--

{img:shakespeare-walking-bankside-river-evening-thinking-globe-in-background}
In 1599, Shakespeare is working on several plays. Julius Caesar has just been performed at the Globe. He is writing a comedy called As You Like It, set in a magical forest, with the famous lines, all the world's a stage, and all the men and women merely players. And he may be starting to think about a new tragedy, about a young prince of Denmark, called Hamlet. Some people think that its title is a little like the name of his lost son.

He lives in rented rooms, near the theatres. He is a quiet man, people say. Friendly, gentle and witty. He does not drink too much, or fight, or make a fuss. He works. In the mornings, perhaps, he writes. In the afternoons, he acts, or watches the rehearsals and the plays. In the evenings, perhaps, he sits in a tavern with the other actors, and talks about the plays, the actors, the audience, and the news of the day.

{img:boy-actor-meeting-playwright-stage-door-globe-shy-hopeful-afternoon}
After the play, Ned waits by the back door of the Globe, where the actors come out. He wants to see them up close. One by one, they come out, in their ordinary clothes, laughing and talking. And among them, there is a man of medium height, with a high forehead, a small beard and kind, tired eyes. One of the other actors calls him Will. Ned is too shy to speak. But the man notices him, and smiles, and asks if he liked the play. Ned says yes. Very much. The man nods, and says that's good, and walks on.

--

Ned never knows for sure if that was really Shakespeare. But he believes it was. And for the rest of his life, he tells the story. How, one afternoon in September 1599, outside the Globe, the greatest writer in the world asked him if he liked the play. And how he said yes. And how the great man smiled. It is a small story. But it is Ned's story. And he never gets tired of telling it.
