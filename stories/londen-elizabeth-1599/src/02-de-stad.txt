London in 1599 is the biggest city in England, by far. About two hundred thousand people live here. That is more than ten times as many as in any other English town. And it is growing fast. Every year, thousands of people come from the countryside, looking for work, for adventure, or for a new life.

--

The old heart of London is called the City. It is surrounded by an old stone wall, built by the Romans more than fourteen hundred years before, and repaired many times since. There are seven great gates in the wall, with names like Aldgate, Bishopsgate, Moorgate and Ludgate. At night, the gates are closed. Inside the walls, the streets are narrow and crowded, and the houses are tall, with upper floors that hang out over the street.

But London has long since grown beyond its walls. Outside the gates, there are new streets and suburbs, spreading out into the fields. To the west, along the river, there is the Strand, where the great nobles have their palaces, with gardens running down to the water. And further west, there is the city of Westminster, with its old abbey, its royal palace, and the halls where Parliament meets.

{img:westminster-abbey-1599-palace-river-thames-barges-strand-mansions}
On the south side of the river, across London Bridge, there is a district called Southwark. It is outside the control of the City. So all kinds of things that are not allowed in the City happen there. There are inns, taverns, gardens and fairs. There are rings for bear-baiting, a cruel sport which we will not visit. And there are the theatres, where crowds come every afternoon to see plays.

The river Thames is the great highway of London. There are very few bridges. In fact, there is only one, London Bridge. So everyone crosses the river by boat. There are thousands of small boats, rowed by boatmen called watermen, waiting at the stairs along the river. If you want to go somewhere, you go down to the water and call out, and a waterman rows you there.

--

{img:ships-tower-of-london-pool-of-london-merchant-ships-masts-sails-quays}
Below London Bridge, the river is full of ships. Big sailing ships, from all over the world. From the Netherlands, France and Spain. From the cities of the Baltic and Russia. From Italy and the eastern Mediterranean. Some English ships have even sailed all the way around the world, like the famous Golden Hind of Sir Francis Drake. They bring wine, spices, silk, sugar, timber and many other goods. And they take away English wool and cloth.

At the eastern edge of the City, by the river, there stands the Tower of London. It is an old castle, built more than five hundred years before by William the Conqueror. Its great White Tower rises above the walls. It is a royal fortress, a prison, an armoury, a treasury and a mint, where coins are made. And there is even a little zoo, with lions, which visitors can pay to see.

{img:tower-of-london-white-tower-walls-moat-river-ravens-afternoon-1599}
The streets of London are noisy all day long. Street sellers call out their goods, each with their own song. Hot pies! Fresh oysters! Ripe cherries! New brooms! Who will buy my lavender? Bells ring. Dogs bark. Carts rattle. Hammers bang in workshops. And people talk, shout, laugh and argue, in English, and in Dutch, French, Italian and other languages, because many foreigners live here too.

--

The streets are also dirty. There are no proper drains. Rubbish and dirty water are thrown into the street, and run down a gutter in the middle. People walk close to the walls, to avoid being splashed. The richest people ride in coaches, or are carried in chairs. But most people walk. And they wear thick shoes, or wooden overshoes called pattens, to keep their feet out of the mud. London is not a clean city. But it is never, ever boring.
