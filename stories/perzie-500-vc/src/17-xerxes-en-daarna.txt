After Darius, his son Xerxes became king, in 486 before Christ. He was about thirty-two.

Xerxes finished many of his father's buildings at Persepolis: the Gate of All Nations, with its great winged bulls, and the Apadana, and he built his own palace, and began the great Hall of a Hundred Columns, the largest hall at Persepolis, with a hundred columns, in ten rows of ten, where the king received his army officers.

--

On the Gate of All Nations, Xerxes carved an inscription, in three languages, which can still be read today: I am Xerxes, the Great King, King of Kings, King of countries containing all kinds of men, King of this great earth far and wide. By the favour of Ahura Mazda, I built this gateway of All Nations. Much other good construction was built in Persepolis, which I built, and which my father built. Whatever good construction is seen, all that, by the favour of Ahura Mazda, we built.

After Xerxes, there were other kings: Artaxerxes, and Darius, and Artaxerxes again, and others. For a hundred and fifty years more, the Persian Empire went on, rich, and powerful, and mostly at peace, the greatest power in the world.

{img:hall-of-hundred-columns-forest-of-columns-persepolis-afternoon}
The kings went on building at Persepolis, and Susa, and Babylon. The roads went on carrying messengers, and merchants, and travellers. The satraps went on governing the provinces. The farmers went on digging qanats, and planting orchards. The Magi went on tending the sacred fires. And every spring, the peoples of the empire came to celebrate the New Year.

--

There were troubles too: rebellions, in Egypt, and elsewhere, and quarrels in the royal family, and plots in the palace. But the empire held together.

And many Greeks came to Persia, and worked for the Persian kings, as doctors, and soldiers, and craftsmen, and advisers. Even Themistocles, the Athenian general who had defeated Xerxes at Salamis, later fled from Athens, when the Athenians turned against him, and went to Persia, and the Persian king welcomed him, and gave him three cities to rule, in Asia Minor, where he lived out his life in peace.

{img:greek-exile-received-persian-court-king-friendly-gesture}
And Persian ideas, and Persian art, and Persian gardens, and Persian ways of governing, spread across the world, and were copied, by the Greeks, and later by Alexander, and the Romans, and many others.
