In the year 500 before Christ, the King of Kings is Darius, the first of that name. He is about fifty years old. He has ruled for twenty-two years.

He was not the son of a king. When Cyrus died, his son Cambyses became king, and conquered Egypt. And when Cambyses died, in 522, there was confusion, and rebellion, and a man claiming to be Cambyses's brother seized the throne. Darius, a young nobleman of the royal family, an officer of the guard, with six other Persian nobles, overthrew him, and became king.

--

Herodotus tells a charming story about how Darius was chosen. The seven nobles agreed that they would all ride out of the city, at sunrise, and the one whose horse neighed first would be king. And Darius's clever groom, the night before, took his master's horse to the place, with a mare it was fond of. So in the morning, when they came to the place, Darius's horse neighed at once. And Darius became king.

It is probably only a story.

In his first years, Darius had to fight many rebellions, all over the empire, and won them all. He recorded his victories in a great inscription, carved high on a cliff, at a place called Behistun, in the west of Iran, in three languages, Old Persian, and Elamite, and Babylonian, with a great relief, showing him, with his foot on the defeated rebel, and the other rebels, roped together, before him, and above, the winged symbol of his god. In the nineteenth century, a British officer, Henry Rawlinson, climbed the cliff, on ladders, and ropes, to copy it, and it helped scholars to decipher the cuneiform writing of the ancient world.

--

Darius is a great organizer. He divides the empire into provinces, called satrapies, each governed by a satrap, a governor, usually a Persian nobleman. He sets the tribute that each province must pay, in silver, or gold, or goods, fairly, according to its wealth. He introduces a common system of weights and measures. And he issues the first gold coins of the empire, called darics, with a picture of the king, running, with a bow and a spear.

He builds roads, and canals, including a canal from the Nile to the Red Sea, so that ships can sail from the Mediterranean to the Indian Ocean. He sends explorers, to sail down the river Indus, and around Arabia.

{img:gold-daric-coins-king-archer-running-hand-close-light}
The Greeks called him Darius the shopkeeper, because he was so interested in money, and taxes, and accounts. But it is a sign of how well he ran his great empire.

--

In his inscriptions, Darius speaks about himself, and his beliefs. He says that he is a friend of what is right, and not a friend of what is wrong. That he does not wish the weak to be wronged by the strong, or the strong to be wronged by the weak. That he rewards the man who cooperates, and punishes the man who does harm. That he is not hot-tempered, and controls his anger.

And above all, that he hates the Lie, and loves the Truth. Because for the Persians, the greatest of all sins is to tell a lie.

{img:king-darius-listening-petitioners-audience-hall-courtiers-calm}
And he says that everything he has done, he has done by the will of the great god, Ahura Mazda, the Wise Lord, who made the earth, and the sky, and man, and happiness for man.
