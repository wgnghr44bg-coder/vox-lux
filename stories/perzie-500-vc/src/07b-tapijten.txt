The Persians are famous all over the world for their carpets. And the oldest carpet in the world may come from the time of our story.

In 1949, a Russian archaeologist was digging in frozen tombs high in the mountains of Siberia. In one tomb, which had been filled with ice for nearly two and a half thousand years, he found a carpet, almost perfectly preserved.

--

It is a knotted carpet, just like a modern Persian carpet. It is about two metres square, made of wool, with more than a million knots. Its colours are deep red, blue, yellow and green. In the middle there is a pattern of squares with stars. Around it run borders. In one border, deer are grazing. In another, horsemen ride and walk beside their horses, like the processions on the walls of Persepolis.

Nobody knows exactly where it was made. Some think the nomads of the north made it themselves. But many experts think it was made in Persia, and traded far to the north.

{img:weaver-women-carpet-loom-knotting-wool-courtyard-sunlight}
To make a knotted carpet, weavers stretch long threads on a loom. Then they tie little knots of coloured wool around them, one by one, row after row, and cut the ends short to make the soft surface. A good carpet can have hundreds of knots in every square centimetre. A family of weavers working together may need months or even years to finish one.

--

The wool comes from mountain sheep and is spun by hand. The colours come from plants and insects. Red comes from the root of a plant, and from tiny insects. Blue comes from indigo. Yellow comes from saffron. Brown comes from walnut shells.

The nomads, moving with their flocks, make carpets too. They use little looms they can roll up and carry on their camels. They make carpets for the floors of their tents, and bags and covers for their animals, with bold patterns that show where they come from.

{img:nomad-tent-interior-carpets-cushions-family-tea-evening}
In the palaces of Persepolis, the floors are covered with carpets and the walls with hangings of wool, silk and gold thread. The king sits on carpets, walks on carpets and gives carpets as gifts.

Two and a half thousand years later, Persian carpets are still among the most beautiful things made by human hands, and they are found in homes all over the world.
