The Persians are famous, all over the world, for their carpets. And the oldest carpet in the world may come from the time of our story.

In 1949, a Russian archaeologist, Sergei Rudenko, was digging in the frozen tombs of the Scythian nomads, in the Altai mountains, in Siberia, at a place called Pazyryk. In one of the tombs, which had been filled with ice, for nearly two and a half thousand years, he found a carpet, almost perfectly preserved.

--

It is a knotted pile carpet, like a modern Persian carpet, about two metres square, of wool, with more than a million knots, beautifully made, in deep red, and blue, and yellow, and green. In the middle, there is a pattern of squares, with stars. Around it, there are borders: one with a procession of deer, grazing, and another with horsemen, riding, and walking beside their horses, like the processions on the walls of Persepolis.

It was made around the fourth or fifth century before Christ. Nobody knows exactly where. Some think it was made by the Scythians themselves. But many experts think that it was made in Persia, or by Persian craftsmen, and traded far to the north, along the routes of the steppes.

{img:weaver-women-carpet-loom-knotting-wool-courtyard-sunlight}
To make a knotted carpet, the weavers set up the warp, long threads, on a loom, upright, or flat on the ground. Then they tie little knots of coloured wool, one by one, around the warp threads, row after row, and cut the ends short, to make the soft pile. A good carpet may have hundreds of knots in every square centimetre, and take months, or years, to make, by a family of weavers, working together.

--

The wool comes from the sheep of the mountains, and the plains, spun by hand. The colours come from plants and insects: red from the root of the madder plant, and from tiny insects, called kermes. Blue from indigo. Yellow from saffron, and other plants. Brown and black from walnut husks, and oak galls.

The nomads, moving with their flocks, make carpets too, on little looms that they can roll up, and carry on their camels: carpets for the floors of their tents, and bags, and covers for their animals, with bold patterns, and bright colours, that tell where they come from.

{img:nomad-tent-interior-carpets-cushions-family-tea-evening}
In the palaces of Persepolis, and Susa, the floors are covered with carpets, and the walls with hangings, of wool, and silk, and gold thread. And the king sits on carpets, and walks on carpets, and gives carpets as gifts.

Two and a half thousand years later, Persian carpets are still among the most beautiful things made by human hands, and are found in homes all over the world.
