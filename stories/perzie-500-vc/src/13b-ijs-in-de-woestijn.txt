In the hot, dry lands of Persia, the people found a clever way to have ice in summer, in the middle of the desert.

They built ice houses, called yakhchals, which means ice pits: great domes of thick mud brick, like huge beehives, rising from the desert, over a deep pit, dug into the ground. The oldest ones may go back to the time of the Persian kings, or a little later.

--

In the winter, on cold, clear nights, the people let water flow, from a qanat, into long, shallow channels, or pools, beside the ice house, in the shade of a tall wall, built on the south side, to keep off the sun. The water freezes in the night. In the morning, the workers break the ice, and carry it into the pit, under the dome, and pack it with straw, and sawdust, to keep it cold.

The walls of the dome are very thick, two metres or more, made of a special mortar, of sand, and clay, and egg whites, and lime, and goat hair, and ash, which keeps out the heat. And the shape of the dome lets the warm air rise, and escape, through a hole at the top, while the cold air sinks down into the pit.

{img:workers-carrying-ice-blocks-into-dome-pit-straw-cold-morning}
And so, in the middle of the burning summer, when the desert is so hot that the air shimmers, the people can take ice from the yakhchal, and cool their water, and make cold drinks, and even a kind of frozen dessert, of ice, and rose water, and fruit, and noodles, which the Persians still love today, called faloodeh.

--

Together with the qanats, which bring water under the desert, and the wind catchers, which cool the houses, the ice houses show how the people of Persia learned to live, comfortably, in one of the hottest and driest lands on earth, using only mud, and water, and wind, and the cold of the winter nights.

Some of these old ice houses still stand today, in the deserts of Iran, around the cities of Yazd, and Kerman, with their great brown domes, like giant beehives, against the blue sky.
