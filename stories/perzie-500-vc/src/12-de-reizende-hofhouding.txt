The King of Kings does not live in one place. He has several capitals, and the whole royal court moves between them, with the seasons.

In the winter, the court lives at Susa, in the warm lowlands of Elam, in the south west of Iran, near the border of Iraq, where the winters are mild, and the summers burning hot. Darius has built a great palace there, with walls of glazed bricks, in bright colours, blue, and yellow, and white, and green, showing lions, and griffins, and rows of archers, the royal guard, in their long robes, with their spears and their bows.

--

In the spring, the court moves to Persepolis, for the New Year festival, and to Babylon, the ancient city, on the Euphrates, the richest and largest city of the empire.

And in the summer, when the lowlands are too hot, the court moves north, up into the mountains, to Ecbatana, the old capital of the Medes, where the air is cool, and fresh, among the green valleys, and the snow-capped mountains. Herodotus says that Ecbatana had seven walls, one inside the other, each painted a different colour: white, black, purple, blue, orange, silver, and gold.

When the court moves, it is like a whole city moving. The king, and his family, and his wives, and his children, and the nobles, and the officials, and the guards, and the servants, and the cooks, and the musicians, and the horses, and the chariots, and the wagons, and the camels, and the mules, carrying tents, and furniture, and treasure, and food, and everything needed for the royal life.

--

On the road, the king travels in a chariot, or in a covered carriage, with curtains, or on horseback. In front of him, there are the guards, and behind him, the long procession, stretching for kilometres along the road.

And Herodotus says that, wherever the king goes, he drinks only water from one river, the Choaspes, which flows past Susa. The water is boiled, and stored in silver jars, and carried with the king, in wagons pulled by mules, wherever he goes, so that he never has to drink any other water.

{img:silver-water-jars-mule-wagons-royal-road-escort-sunlight}
At night, the court camps, in great tents, like palaces of cloth, with carpets, and hangings, and couches, and lamps. The king's tent is magnificent, with columns of gilded wood, and hangings of embroidered cloth.

--

At Susa, in 1901, French archaeologists found, written on a clay tablet, an ancient code of laws, carved on a tall black stone pillar: the laws of Hammurabi, king of Babylon, who lived twelve hundred years before Darius. It had been carried to Susa as war booty, by an Elamite king, long before. And it is now in the Louvre museum, in Paris.

And at Susa, too, according to the Bible, lived Esther, the Jewish girl who became queen of Persia, and saved her people, in the time of a Persian king called Ahasuerus, which may be Xerxes, the son of Darius. The story of Esther is still remembered every year, by Jews all over the world, at the festival of Purim, with costumes, and noise, and gifts of food.

{img:susa-mound-citadel-plain-river-palms-ancient-town-evening}
And at Susa, as in all the royal capitals, the king's palaces are surrounded by gardens, and parks, and orchards, and the people of the town gather at the gates, every morning, to see the king's officials go in and out, and to present their petitions, and to hear the news of the empire.
