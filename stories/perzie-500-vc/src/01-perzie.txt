Tonight we are going two and a half thousand years back, to the heart of the Persian Empire, in the year 500 before Christ.

--

On a great stone terrace, at the foot of a mountain, on a wide plain in the south of what is now Iran, a new city of palaces is rising: forests of tall stone columns, great staircases carved with endless processions of people, and gateways guarded by winged bulls with human faces. It is called Persepolis, and it belongs to Darius, the King of Kings, ruler of the largest empire the world has ever known.

Tonight, we will walk slowly through his world: his palaces and gardens, his roads and messengers, and the lives of the ordinary people who built it all.

If you like these slow journeys into the distant past, you are welcome to subscribe to the channel, and to come back for the next one.

--

In the year 500 before Christ, the Persian Empire stretches from the borders of Greece in the west, to the river Indus, in what is now Pakistan, in the east. From the deserts of Egypt and Libya in the south, to the steppes of Central Asia in the north.

It covers perhaps five and a half million square kilometres, and rules over perhaps fifty million people: almost half of all the people in the world, at that time, some historians think. It includes what is now Iran, and Iraq, and Turkey, and Syria, and Lebanon, and Israel, and Egypt, and Afghanistan, and parts of Pakistan, and Central Asia, and the Caucasus, and the Greek cities of the coast of Asia, and even, for a while, Thrace, in Europe.

--

Within it live dozens of peoples, speaking dozens of languages: Persians, and Medes, and Elamites, and Babylonians, and Assyrians, and Arabs, and Jews, and Phoenicians, and Egyptians, and Lydians, and Ionian Greeks, and Armenians, and Bactrians, and Sogdians, and Scythians, and Indians, and many more.

And over all of them rules one man: the Great King, the King of Kings, Darius, of the family of the Achaemenids.

{img:fars-province-green-valley-mountains-shepherds-flocks-spring}
The Persians themselves come from a region in the south west of Iran, called Parsa, or Persis, from which the Greeks gave them their name. It is a land of high mountains, and green valleys, and wide plains, hot in summer, and cold and snowy in winter, with rivers running down from the mountains, and orchards, and fields of wheat and barley, and flocks of sheep and goats, and herds of horses.

--

Only about seventy years before our story, the Persians were a small people, ruled by their neighbours, the Medes. Then, in a single lifetime, under a great king called Cyrus, they conquered the Medes, and the rich kingdom of Lydia, in Turkey, and the great city of Babylon, and built an empire.

How they did it, and how they ruled it, is one of the most remarkable stories of the ancient world.

{img:persian-nobles-long-robes-cylindrical-hats-walking-colonnade-sun}
And unlike most of the empires of the ancient world, the Persians are remembered, even by their enemies, the Greeks, not only for their power, and their wealth, but also for their good roads, their fair laws, their tolerance of other peoples and other religions, and their love of truth, and gardens, and horses, and beautiful things.
