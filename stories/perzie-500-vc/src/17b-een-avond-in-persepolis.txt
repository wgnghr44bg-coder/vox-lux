Let us end our visit with an evening at Persepolis, in the spring of the year 500 before Christ, after the New Year festival.

The guests have gone home, to their provinces, along the royal roads, with their gifts from the king. The great hall is quiet. The sun is going down behind the plain, and its last light turns the stone columns gold, and then pink, and the shadows of the columns stretch long across the terrace.

--

From the workers' village, below the terrace, there is the smell of bread baking, and smoke rising, and the sound of children playing, and voices, in many languages, as the stone cutters, and the carpenters, and the weavers, finish their work for the day, and go home to their families, with their rations of grain, and wine.

On the terrace, the guards change, at the Gate of All Nations, and lamps are lit, in the palaces, little clay and bronze lamps, with olive oil, or sesame oil, glowing in the windows.

{img:workers-village-below-terrace-evening-smoke-children-families}
In the gardens, below the terrace, the water flows in the stone channels, and the nightingales begin to sing, in the trees, and the scent of the roses, and the orange blossom, drifts up on the evening air.

--

In the royal apartments, the king sits with his family, and listens to music, a harp, and a flute, and a singer, singing the old songs of the Persians, about the heroes of long ago.

And on the mountain, behind the terrace, the stars come out, one by one, over the Mountain of Mercy, and the great dark plain, and the far mountains, and the whole, vast, sleeping empire, from the river Indus, to the shores of the Aegean Sea.
