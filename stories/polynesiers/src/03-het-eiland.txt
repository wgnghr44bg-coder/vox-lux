Raiatea is a beautiful island. It rises out of the sea, green and steep, with tall mountains in the middle, covered in thick forest, and waterfalls, and mist. Around the island, there is a wide, calm lagoon, of clear turquoise water. And around the lagoon, there is a coral reef, where the great waves of the ocean break, in long white lines of foam, with a deep, steady roar, day and night.

On the shore, under the coconut palms, there are villages. Houses with walls of woven leaves, and thatched roofs, made of pandanus leaves, or palm fronds. Some are small, for families. Others are big, for meetings, and ceremonies, and for the chiefs. The houses have no walls on some sides, so that the cool sea breeze can blow through. The floors are covered with soft woven mats.

--

{img:polynesian-village-thatched-houses-palms-women-weaving-mats-children}
Around the villages, there are gardens, and plantations. The Polynesians are skilled farmers. They grow taro, in wet fields, flooded with water from the streams. Taro is a plant with big green leaves, shaped like hearts, and a starchy root, which is cooked, and pounded into a paste. They grow breadfruit, on tall trees, with big green fruits, as big as a man's head. And bananas, and yams, and sugarcane, and coconuts, everywhere.

They keep pigs, and dogs, and chickens. And they fish. In the lagoon, they fish with nets, and spears, and traps made of woven cane, and hooks made of shell and bone. Outside the reef, in the open ocean, they fish from canoes, for tuna, and bonito, and mahi-mahi, and sometimes, sharks. The ocean gives them food, every day. They thank it, and the gods of the sea, for its gifts.

{img:fishermen-outrigger-canoe-lagoon-throwing-net-morning-calm-water}
Raiatea is a sacred island. In the old stories, it is called Havaiki, the homeland, the place where the gods first made the world, and where the Polynesian people began. On the eastern shore, there is a great sacred place, called a marae. A wide open courtyard, paved with stones, with tall upright slabs of coral and stone, and a raised stone platform at one end. Here, the priests make offerings to the gods, and the great chiefs are honoured.

The marae is the centre of the island's life. Here, the people gather for festivals, and ceremonies. Here, the chiefs meet, from Raiatea, and from the other islands around. And here, the voyagers come, before they set out on a great journey, to ask the gods for a safe passage, and a good landing. The canoes leave from the beach in front of the marae, through a gap in the reef, which the people call the sacred pass.

--

Life on Raiatea is good, in many ways. The climate is warm all year round. There is plenty of food, from the land, and the sea. There is time for music, and dancing, and games, and storytelling. The people are strong, and healthy, and handsome. Later, when the first Europeans came to these islands, they thought it was a paradise.

{img:polynesian-dance-evening-firelight-drums-dancers-beach-palms}
But there are also problems. The island is not very big. And over many generations, the number of people has grown. Now, there is not quite enough land, for everyone. The chiefs sometimes quarrel, over land, and power. And younger sons, and their families, have no land of their own. So some people decide to leave. To sail away, and find a new land, where they can make a new beginning.

This is one of the reasons why the Polynesians are always voyaging. Not only for adventure, although they love adventure. But because they need new land. And because, in their hearts, they are explorers. They want to know what lies beyond the horizon. And they are not afraid to go and find out.
