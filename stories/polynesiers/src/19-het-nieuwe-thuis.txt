The first weeks in the new land are very busy. There is so much to do. Houses to build, gardens to plant, fishing grounds to find, and the land to explore. Everyone works, from dawn until dark. But it is happy work. They are building their new home, in a beautiful, rich, empty land. Everything they do is the first time that anyone has ever done it, here.

First, they build shelters. Simple houses, of wooden poles, tied together with sennit, and thatched with grass, and leaves. Later, they will build bigger, better houses. But for now, they need somewhere to sleep, out of the rain. And it rains a lot, on this side of the island, in the afternoons. Soft, warm rain, which makes everything grow.

--

{img:taro-terraces-being-dug-by-stream-men-women-working-digging-sticks-mud}
Then they make the gardens. Along the stream, they dig flat terraces, and lead water into them, with little channels, to make wet fields for the taro. It is hard work, with wooden digging sticks, and their hands. On the higher, drier land, they clear the forest, and plant bananas, and sugarcane, and yams, and paper mulberry. And along the beach, they plant the coconuts, in long rows. One day, there will be palm trees here, hundreds of them.

The plants grow well. The soil is rich, from the old volcanic ash, and the rain is plentiful, and the sun is warm. Within a few weeks, the taro has new leaves. The banana shoots are growing. The sugarcane is sprouting. Mahana is delighted. She says that the land is happy to have them. And she still talks to the plants, every day.

{img:native-hawaiian-forest-birds-red-honeycreeper-ohia-lehua-flowers-ferns}
The land is full of wonders. In the forests, there are trees they have never seen, with bright red flowers, like little brushes, which the birds love. There are giant ferns, taller than a man. There are birds of every colour, singing in the trees, which have never seen people before, and are not afraid of them. There are no snakes, no dangerous animals, no mosquitoes. Only birds, and insects, and the plants, and the sea.

Ru and Hina explore the coast, in a small canoe, which Tane has made, from a tree in the forest. They find other bays, and beaches, and streams, and good fishing places. They climb a hill, and look out to the north-west. And on the horizon, on a clear day, they see another island. And beyond it, perhaps, another. Ru says that this land is not just one island. It is a chain of islands, stretching away to the north-west. A whole new world.

--

As the months pass, the little settlement grows. More houses are built. The gardens spread along the stream. The pigs and the chickens have babies. A baby is born, too, to one of the young couples. The first child of the new land. The whole settlement celebrates, with a feast. The priest gives the baby a name, which means born under the new star.

{img:old-navigator-sitting-on-rock-overlooking-ocean-sunset-girl-beside-quiet}
Ru grows older, and slower. The long voyage has tired him. He spends his days sitting on a rock, above the beach, looking out at the sea, to the south. Thinking about Raiatea, and his sister, and his old friends. He knows that he will not see them again. He is too old for another voyage. But he is content. He has done what he came to do. He has brought his family to a new home.

And he has passed on his knowledge, to Hina. She knows the stars now, and the swells, and the birds, and the clouds. She knows the way from Raiatea to this land, and the way back. One day, Ru says, she will sail back to Raiatea, to tell them about this land. And she will bring more people here. And she will be a great navigator, like him. Hina is not sure she can be as great as him. But she promises to try.
