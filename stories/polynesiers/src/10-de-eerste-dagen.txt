The first days at sea are busy, and a little strange. Everyone must get used to life on the canoe. The movement of the waves, up and down, and side to side, all day and all night. The cramped space, with so many people, and animals, and baskets. The sun, the wind, the salt spray. Some people are seasick, at first, and lie on the platform, feeling miserable. Tavi is sick on the first day. But by the third day, he is fine, and hungry again.

The canoe sails north-east, at first, into the trade wind. Ru has a plan. He knows that the winds near the equator will push them west. So he sails east first, to make room. Then, when they reach the right place, he will turn north, and let the wind carry them to the land. It is like aiming an arrow, a little to one side, because you know the wind will blow it back.

--

{img:steersman-holding-great-steering-paddle-watching-navigator-at-bow-sunlight}
Life on board soon settles into a routine. The crew is divided into watches, like on a ship. Some people work, while others rest. The steersmen take turns at the steering paddle, a few hours at a time. Others handle the sails, adjusting them to the wind. Others bail out the water, which comes in through the joints of the hulls, and over the sides, from the waves. Bailing never stops, day or night.

Ru hardly sleeps. He is the navigator, and he must always know where they are. He sits at the front of the canoe, or at the back, watching the sea, and the sky, and the sun, and the stars. He sleeps only for short moments, an hour here, an hour there, sitting up. When he sleeps, Hina watches for him. She keeps the course, by the stars or the swells, and wakes him if anything changes.

{img:girl-sitting-at-bow-watching-horizon-wind-in-hair-concentrating-ocean}
Every morning, at sunrise, Ru studies the sky in the east. He watches where the sun rises, and compares it with the stars that rose there, just before dawn. He feels the swells, and notes their direction. He remembers how far they have sailed, how fast, and in which direction. And from all of this, he works out where they are. Not with numbers, or maps. But in his head. He imagines the canoe as standing still, and the islands moving past it, around the edge of the ocean.

It is very hard to explain, and very hard to learn. Hina asks him how he does it. He says that it is like walking through your own house, in the dark. You cannot see. But you know where everything is. You know how many steps to the door, and where the table is, and where your bed is. The ocean is his house. He has been walking through it, in the dark, for fifty years.

--

The people on the canoe pass the time in many ways. They talk, and tell stories, and sing. They play games, with string, making shapes between their fingers. They weave, and mend the nets, and make sennit. They fish, with long lines, trailing behind the canoe. They sleep, in the little house, or on the platform, under the sky. And they watch the ocean, for hours, in every mood and colour.

{img:voyagers-string-figures-games-singing-afternoon-shade-platform-calm-sea}
The ocean is never the same, from one hour to the next. In the morning, it is grey, and silver, and then blue. At midday, it is a deep, brilliant blue, almost purple, so clear that you can see far down into it. In the evening, it turns gold, and pink, and orange, with the sunset. And at night, it is black, with the reflections of the stars, and sometimes, little sparks of green light, glowing in the waves, made by tiny living creatures.

Tavi loves the glowing waves. At night, he leans over the edge of the platform, holding on tightly, and trails his hand in the water. Every movement of his hand makes the water sparkle, with little green lights, like fireflies. He shows Popo, who is not impressed. But Hina is. She sits beside her brother, and they make patterns in the glowing water, together, under the stars.
