On the second day after they turn west, the signs begin to appear. In the morning, Hina sees a group of birds. Small, dark birds, with white caps on their heads, flying low over the water, fishing. They are noddies. Ru watches them carefully. He says that noddies do not fly far from land, perhaps fifty kilometres, at most. In the evening, they fly back to land, to sleep. So, in the evening, they must watch where the birds go.

All day, the voyagers watch the birds, and wait. More birds come. Terns, and noddies, and a big brown booby, which lands on the mast, and sits there, looking at them, for an hour. Then, in the evening, as the sun goes down, the birds stop fishing. They rise up from the sea, and gather together, and fly away, all in the same direction. To the west, and a little north. Ru points. That way, he says. The land is that way.

--

{img:stationary-cloud-cap-on-horizon-green-tint-underneath-sea-sunset}
That night, nobody sleeps much. Everyone is excited, and nervous. They sail on, slowly, through the dark, toward the west. Ru does not want to go too fast, in case they come too close to the land, in the dark, and hit the rocks. He feels the swells, and notices that they are changing. The swell from the north is weaker. Something is blocking it. Something big, to the north-west. Land.

In the morning, they look out to the west. At first, there is nothing. Just the sea, and a few clouds, on the horizon. But then Ru notices something. One of the clouds is not moving. All the other clouds are drifting slowly across the sky, with the wind. But this one stays in the same place. And it is tall, and thick, and its underside is a strange colour. Not grey, but a little green. Ru says that the cloud is sitting on a mountain. A very high mountain. And the green is the reflection of the forests below.

{img:snow-capped-volcano-peak-rising-above-clouds-distant-ocean-morning-light}
The canoe sails on, toward the cloud. All morning, the voyagers stare at it. Then, in the middle of the day, Hina sees something, below the cloud, on the horizon. A dark line. A shape. She looks, and looks, and looks. It does not move. It is not a wave. It is not a cloud. She grabs her grandfather's arm, and points, and cannot speak. Ru looks, for a long time. Then he nods. And he says, quietly, there it is.

Then everyone sees it. A great mountain, rising out of the sea, far away. Its top is hidden in the clouds. But then the clouds part, for a moment, and they see something that none of them, except Ru, has ever seen before. The top of the mountain is white. Covered with something white, and shining, in the sun. Tavi asks what it is. Ru says it is snow. Frozen water, that falls from the sky, on the highest mountains. It is cold, he says, colder than anything you can imagine.

--

The voyagers cheer, and shout, and cry, and embrace each other. They have been at sea for more than three weeks. They have crossed thousands of kilometres of open ocean. And they have found the land. Chief Tama blows the conch shell, a long, joyful note, across the water. The priest chants a prayer of thanks, to the gods of the sea, and the sky, and the navigators. And Ru sits down, suddenly, on the platform, and closes his eyes. He is very tired.

{img:voyagers-celebrating-on-canoe-embracing-conch-shell-mountain-ahead-joy}
Hina sits beside him, and takes his hand. She asks him if he is all right. He opens his eyes, and smiles at her. He says that he is just resting. He says that he was not completely sure, until now. He says that every voyage is a mystery, until the land appears. And he says that she was the first one to see it. So she gets the first coconut.

Tavi is disappointed, for a moment. Then he says that he saw the noddies first, yesterday, so he should get a coconut too. Everyone laughs. Chief Tama says that everyone on the canoe will get a coconut, from the new land, when the trees have grown. Even Popo. It will take about six years, Mahana says. Tavi says that he can wait.
