For the rest of that day, and all the next, the canoe sails toward the land. It grows bigger, and bigger, and more and more amazing. It is not one mountain, but two. Two great mountains, huge and gentle, rising up from the sea, higher than anything the voyagers have ever seen. Their tops are in the clouds, with snow. Their sides are covered with forests, dark green, and ferns, and grass. And along the coast, there are cliffs, and waterfalls, falling straight into the sea.

As they come closer, they see more. Streams, and rivers, running down the valleys. Beaches, some of white sand, and some of black sand, made of crushed volcanic rock. Strange trees, that they do not know. Birds, everywhere, of every colour. Red, and yellow, and green, and black. Singing, in the forests. And on one side of the island, they see something very strange. A great black land, with no plants on it at all. And smoke, rising from it.

--

{img:distant-volcano-smoke-black-lava-field-coast-glowing-dusk-voyagers-watching}
Ru tells them what it is. It is the place where the ground is still being made. Fire comes up from inside the earth, and pours out, like a red river, and then cools, and becomes black rock. The new land is still being born, he says. It is a young land, and alive. The Polynesians later believed that the goddess of the volcano lived there, in the fire. Her name was Pele.

They sail along the coast, looking for a good place to land. Ru is looking for a bay, with a beach, and a stream of fresh water, and flat land for gardens, and a gap in the reef. On the second evening, they find one. A wide, curving bay, with a beach of white sand, and shady trees, and a stream, running down from the mountains, into the sea. Chief Tama says, this is the place.

{img:first-footsteps-on-new-beach-chief-wading-ashore-family-following-dawn}
They anchor in the bay, for the night, and wait for the morning. Nobody sleeps. Then, at dawn, they paddle the canoe slowly into the shallow water, and up onto the beach. The priest goes first. He wades ashore, and kneels on the sand, and chants a prayer, to the gods of this new land, asking for their welcome, and their protection. He makes an offering, of a pig, and some coconuts, and red feathers. Then Chief Tama steps ashore. And then everyone else.

Hina steps off the canoe, into the warm, shallow water. It feels strange to stand on solid ground again, after so many weeks at sea. Her legs feel wobbly, as if the land is moving, like the waves. She walks up the beach, slowly, and kneels down, and puts her hands in the warm sand. It is soft, and white, and real. She has never been so happy in her life.

--

Tavi runs up and down the beach, shouting, with Popo barking beside him, both of them wild with joy. The other children join him. The grown-ups unload the canoe, carrying the baskets, and the plants, and the animals, up the beach, into the shade of the trees. The pigs are let out of their cages, and run around, squealing. The chickens flutter and squawk. Everyone is laughing, and talking, and crying, all at once.

{img:mother-planting-first-taro-in-new-soil-stream-bank-family-watching-morning}
Mahana goes straight to the stream, with her plants. She tastes the water. It is cold, and fresh, and sweet. She looks at the soil, along the bank. It is dark, and rich, and moist. She kneels down, and digs a small hole, with her hands, and plants the first taro, in the new land. She pats the soil around it, gently. And she whispers to it, that it is home now, and it can rest, and grow.

That evening, the voyagers light a great fire, on the beach, and cook a feast. Fish, from the bay, and a pig, and the last of the taro, and the last green coconuts from the canoe. They eat, and sing, and dance, and give thanks. And when the stars come out, Ru shows Hina the orange star, high above, right at the top of the sky, shining down on the new land. That is the star of this island, he says. Remember it. One day, you will need it, to bring other people here.
