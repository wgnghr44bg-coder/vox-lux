Let us meet a family who lives in Florence, in a narrow street not far from the cathedral.

The father is called Piero. He is a stonemason, about forty years old. He works in the great workshop of the cathedral, called the Opera del Duomo. The workshop looks after the cathedral and all its buildings. Piero cuts and shapes stone for repairs. He carves decorations and steps and window frames. His hands are strong and covered with little scars, and his hair is always grey with stone dust. He is a calm, patient man who loves his work.

The mother is called Caterina. She is a silk weaver. She has a loom in the main room of the house, where she weaves silk cloth for a merchant. The merchant brings her the silk thread, and she weaves it into beautiful fabric, with patterns of flowers and leaves. It is slow, careful work. She is paid for each length of cloth she weaves. She also runs the house and looks after the family.

--

They have two children. The son is called Matteo, and he is thirteen. He is an apprentice in the workshop of a painter, near the river. He grinds colours, prepares wooden panels and sweeps the floor. He is learning to draw and to paint, little by little. He dreams of becoming a great painter one day, like the famous masters of Florence.

{img:boy-apprentice-painter-workshop-grinding-pigments-stone-slab-1500}
The daughter is called Bianca, and she is eleven. She is curious about everything. She asks questions all day long. Why is the sky blue? How do birds fly? How did they build the dome? She helps her mother with the silk, winding the thread onto little bobbins. But she would much rather be outside, exploring the city. She loves to visit her father at the cathedral workshop and watch the stonemasons at work.

--

The grandmother lives with them too. She is Caterina's mother, and her name is Lucia. Everyone calls her Nonna. She is about seventy, small and round, with bright eyes and white hair under a linen cap. She was once a silk weaver too, and she taught Caterina everything she knows. Now her fingers are stiff, but she still spins a little and sews. And she tells the best stories in the whole street.

{img:italian-grandmother-1500-linen-cap-spinning-window-sunlight-smiling}
Nonna Lucia remembers the old days, when Lorenzo the Magnificent ruled Florence. She saw him once, riding through the streets on a fine horse, with his friends and his servants. She remembers the great festivals of those years, with parades and music and fireworks. She remembers when the baptistery doors were new and shining. And she remembers the day the great dome was finished, when she was a little girl, sitting on her father's shoulders.

--

The family is not rich, but they are not poor either. Piero earns a steady wage from the cathedral workshop. Caterina earns a little from her weaving. They have enough to eat, and a little to spare. On feast days, they can buy meat and good wine. And Piero has been able to pay for Matteo to become an apprentice. That is something he is very proud of.

{img:florentine-family-supper-1500-table-bread-wine-soup-candles-evening}
In the evenings, the family eats together at a wooden table, by the light of an oil lamp. There is bread and soup, beans with olive oil, and sometimes a little cheese or fish. Piero tells them about his day at the cathedral. Matteo tells them about the painter's workshop. Bianca asks a hundred questions. And Nonna Lucia tells a story about the old days, while Caterina clears the table and listens with a smile.

The family also has a cat, a fat tabby cat called Moro. He spends his days sleeping in the sun on the loggia, and his nights hunting mice in the courtyard. He likes to sit on Caterina's silk when nobody is looking, which makes her cross. He is especially fond of Nonna Lucia, who gives him little pieces of cheese. In the evenings, he sleeps curled up on her lap by the fire.

On Sundays, the family goes to mass at their parish church, a little old church at the end of the street. After mass, they walk along the river, or visit relatives. Sometimes they walk out of the city gate, into the countryside. They pick wild flowers and watch the farmers at work. They have a picnic of bread and cheese under an olive tree. It is the best day of the week.
