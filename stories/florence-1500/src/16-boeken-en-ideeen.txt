Florence around 1500 is a city of books and ideas. It is one of the most important centres of learning in Europe.

About fifty years before our story, a new invention arrived in Europe: the printing press. Before then, every book had to be copied out by hand, letter by letter. It took months to copy one book. Books were very rare and very expensive. Now, with the printing press, hundreds of copies of a book can be made quickly. Books become cheaper, and more people can read them.

--

In Florence, there are now several printing shops. They print books of poetry and history, prayers and sermons, and old books from Greece and Rome. There are bookshops near the cathedral, where people can browse and buy. Rich people still also have beautiful handwritten books, made by skilled scribes and decorated with tiny paintings in gold and colour.

{img:scholars-humanists-reading-greek-latin-texts-library-wooden-desks-light}
The scholars of Florence are called humanists. They study the books of ancient Greece and Rome. They learn Latin and Greek. They read the old poets and philosophers, historians and scientists. They believe that these old books contain great wisdom, which had been forgotten for a long time. And they believe that people should use their own minds, to understand the world and to live good lives.

--

One of the most interesting people working in the government of Florence around 1500 is a man called Niccolò Machiavelli. He is about thirty years old. He works as a secretary and diplomat for the city. He travels to other cities and countries, to talk with kings and dukes. He watches them closely and thinks about how power works. Later, he will write a famous book about it. It is called The Prince. People still read it and argue about it today.

{img:machiavelli-1500-official-desk-palazzo-letters-quill-thinking-window}
There are also new ideas about the world itself. In 1492, a sailor called Christopher Columbus sailed west across the Atlantic and found new lands. Another explorer from Florence, called Amerigo Vespucci, sailed there too, a few years later. He wrote letters about these lands, saying they were a new world, not part of Asia. His letters were printed and read all over Europe. And a mapmaker named the new lands after him: America.

--

Bianca hears about this from her father, who heard it from a merchant at the cathedral. A man from Florence has sailed to a new world, he says, across the great ocean. There are lands there that nobody in Europe knew about. There are strange animals and plants, and peoples who live in different ways. Bianca's eyes grow wide. She asks if she can go there one day. Her father laughs. Perhaps, he says. The world is getting bigger every day.

{img:girl-looking-at-new-world-map-merchant-shop-window-florence-curious}
On the way home from the market, Bianca stops at a little shop that sells maps and prints. In the window, there is a new map of the world. It shows Europe and Africa and Asia, and, far to the west, a strange new coastline. Bianca stands and looks at it for a long time. The shopkeeper comes out and tells her about the new lands. Bianca listens to every word. And that night, she dreams of ships and oceans and faraway shores.

The great library of the Medici was full of beautiful old books. Cosimo de' Medici had collected them from all over the world. He sent people to search for old Greek and Latin books in monasteries and libraries far away. Many of these books had been almost forgotten. Thanks to him and others, they were saved and copied. Some of them would otherwise have been lost forever.

In the evenings, the scholars of Florence meet in each other's houses and gardens. They read poems aloud and discuss ideas. They talk about Plato and the meaning of beauty. They talk about the stars and the planets, about the soul and about love. Sometimes there is music, played on a lute or a harp. These evenings can go on late into the night, by the light of candles.
