In the autumn of 1888, London was the largest city in the world.

[pause]

Nearly six million people lived within its borders, spread across a landscape of grand avenues and, not far away, some of the poorest streets in Europe.

[long-pause]

The city had grown faster than almost anyone could plan for.

[pause]

In just a hundred years, London's population had multiplied many times over, pulled inward by factories, by trade, by the endless promise that city life offered something better than the countryside ever could.

[long-pause]

[pause]

[long-pause]

[pause]

There were theatres, and grand hotels, and shops with gleaming windows full of silk and silver.
