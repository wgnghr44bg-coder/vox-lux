Let us walk through Jerusalem, in the spring of the year 33.

The city is built on two hills, with a valley between them, and surrounded by deep valleys on three sides: the Kidron valley, to the east, between the city and the Mount of Olives, and the Hinnom valley, to the south and west. Around it, there are strong walls, with towers, and gates.

--

On the western hill, there is the Upper City, where the rich and the powerful live: the families of the high priests, and the nobles, and the wealthy merchants, in fine houses of stone, with courtyards, and gardens, and painted walls, and mosaic floors, and their own ritual baths, cut into the rock, for purification.

At the north western corner of the Upper City, there is the palace of King Herod the Great, now used by the Roman governor when he comes to Jerusalem: a great fortress-palace, with three tall towers, named after Herod's brother, his friend, and his wife, and gardens, and pools, and fountains, and colonnades, and banqueting halls, for hundreds of guests.

In the valley between the hills, and on the slope of the eastern hill, below the Temple, there is the Lower City, the old City of David, where most ordinary people live, in small stone houses, crowded together, along narrow, winding, stepped streets, going up and down the hills.

--

There are markets, and bazaars, and workshops, everywhere. There is a street of the bakers, and a street of the cheesemakers, and a market for sheep, and a market for wood, and a market for wool, and a market for clothes. There are potters, and weavers, and dyers, and tanners, outside the walls, because of the smell, and stone cutters, and goldsmiths, and perfume makers.

At the south end of the Lower City, there is the Pool of Siloam, a great stepped pool, fed by a spring, through a tunnel, cut through the rock, seven hundred years before, by King Hezekiah, where people come to wash, and purify themselves, before going up to the Temple. And a great stepped street runs from the pool, up the valley, to the Temple Mount. Archaeologists have found both of them, in recent years.

{img:pool-of-siloam-stepped-stone-pool-pilgrims-washing-morning}
North of the Temple, there is the Antonia fortress, a great square castle, with four towers, built by Herod, and named after his friend, Mark Antony, where the Roman soldiers are stationed, looking down over the courts of the Temple, to keep order, especially at the festivals, when the crowds are huge, and riots can easily break out.

--

And there is water: the Pool of Bethesda, north of the Temple, with five porches, where the sick lie, waiting for the water to stir, hoping to be healed. Aqueducts, bringing water from springs, near Bethlehem, to the city, and to the Temple. Great cisterns, cut into the rock, under the Temple Mount, and under the houses, to store the winter rains, for the long dry summer.

{img:pool-of-bethesda-porches-colonnade-sick-waiting-water-light}
And outside the walls, on every side, there are gardens, and orchards, and olive groves, and vineyards, and tombs, cut into the rock of the hillsides, and on the Mount of Olives, to the east, the olive trees, silver-green, covering the slopes, and the little village of Bethany, on the far side of the hill, on the road to Jericho.
