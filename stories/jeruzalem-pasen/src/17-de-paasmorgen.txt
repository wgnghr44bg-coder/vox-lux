And then, the Gospels tell, very early in the morning, on the first day of the week, the Sunday, when it was still dark, the women went to the tomb, carrying the spices, which they had prepared: Mary Magdalene, and Mary the mother of James, and Salome, and Joanna, and others.

On the way, they were worried, and said to each other: who will roll away the stone for us, from the door of the tomb? Because it was very large.

--

But when they came to the garden, in the first grey light of dawn, they saw that the stone had already been rolled away. And the tomb was empty.

The Gospels tell the story in different ways, with different details, as people do, who remember something extraordinary. They tell of a young man, in a white robe, or two men, in dazzling clothes, angels, sitting in the tomb, who said to the women: do not be afraid. You are looking for Jesus of Nazareth, who was crucified. He is not here. He has risen. Why do you look for the living among the dead?

And the women ran, the Gospels say, trembling, and astonished, with fear, and great joy, to tell the disciples. And Peter, and John, ran to the tomb, and John ran faster, and got there first, and looked in, and saw the linen cloths lying there, and the cloth that had been around his head, folded up, by itself. And they believed.

--

The Gospel of John tells the most beautiful part of the story.

Mary Magdalene stayed outside the tomb, weeping, after the others had gone. And she turned around, and saw a man standing there, in the garden, in the early morning light. She thought he was the gardener. And he said to her: woman, why are you weeping? Whom are you looking for? And she said: sir, if you have carried him away, tell me where you have laid him, and I will take him away.

{img:woman-in-garden-dawn-turning-recognizing-figure-light-flowers}
And he said to her: Mary.

And she turned, and knew him, and said: Rabbouni, which means teacher.

--

That is the story of the first Easter morning, as the Gospels tell it.

Historians cannot prove, or disprove, what happened, that morning, in the garden. But they agree that something changed the frightened, hiding, grieving friends of Jesus, so completely, that within a few weeks, they were standing up in the streets, and the Temple courts, of Jerusalem, telling everyone that he was alive. And that they went on telling it, all over the world, for the rest of their lives, whatever it cost them.

{img:sunrise-over-jerusalem-hills-golden-light-garden-birds-spring}
And every year, on Easter morning, all over the world, Christians greet each other with the old words: Christ is risen. And the answer: He is risen indeed.
