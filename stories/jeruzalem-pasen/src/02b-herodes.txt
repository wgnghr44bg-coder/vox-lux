The man who made Jerusalem so splendid was King Herod the Great, who passed away about thirty-seven years before our story.

Herod was not of a royal family. His father was an official, from Idumea, in the south, and his mother was an Arab princess, from Petra. But he was clever, and ambitious, and a friend of the Romans, of Mark Antony, and later of Augustus. And the Romans made him king of Judea, in the year 37 before Christ. He ruled for thirty-three years.

--

Herod was a cruel, suspicious, unhappy man. He was always afraid of plots, and put to death many people, including his own wife, Mariamne, whom he loved, and three of his own sons. The emperor Augustus is said to have joked, in Greek, that it was better to be Herod's pig than Herod's son, because Herod, following Jewish law, did not eat pork.

But he was also one of the greatest builders of the ancient world.

{img:masada-mesa-fortress-dead-sea-desert-cliffs-dawn-palace-terraces}
He rebuilt the Temple of Jerusalem, on an enormous scale. He built the great city of Caesarea, on the coast, with its harbour, made with Roman concrete, which set under water. He built fortresses, and palaces, all over the land: at Jericho, with gardens, and pools, in the warm winter oasis. At Masada, on top of a great flat rock, high above the Dead Sea, with a palace hanging on three terraces, down the cliff, with baths, and storerooms, and huge cisterns, for rainwater. And at the Herodium, near Bethlehem, a great palace fortress, on the top of a hill, which he made higher, and shaped like a cone, like a volcano, so that it could be seen from Jerusalem.

--

He was buried at the Herodium. And in 2007, after searching for thirty-five years, an Israeli archaeologist, Ehud Netzer, found the remains of his tomb, on the slope of the hill.

When Herod passed away, in the year 4 before Christ, his kingdom was divided among three of his sons: Archelaus, who ruled Judea, so badly that the Romans removed him, after ten years, and took Judea under direct Roman rule, with a governor. Herod Antipas, who ruled Galilee. And Philip, who ruled the lands to the north east.

{img:caesarea-harbour-herod-breakwater-ships-theatre-sea-sunset}
So in the year 33, Herod's buildings are everywhere, in Jerusalem, and in the land: the Temple, and the palace, and the towers, and the fortresses, and the cities. And the people still remember the old king, and his cruelty, and his splendour.
