On the Sunday before Passover, the Gospels tell, Jesus came to Jerusalem, from Bethany, over the Mount of Olives.

He sent two of his friends into a nearby village, to bring a young donkey, which had never been ridden. And they put their cloaks on its back, and he sat on it, and rode down the Mount of Olives, toward the city.

--

It was a deliberate sign. Five hundred years before, the prophet Zechariah had written: rejoice greatly, O daughter of Zion. Shout, O daughter of Jerusalem. Behold, your king comes to you. He is just, and having salvation, humble, and riding on a donkey, on a colt, the foal of a donkey.

Not on a war horse, like a conquering king, but on a donkey: a king of peace.

{img:road-down-mount-of-olives-view-of-temple-across-kidron-valley}
And the crowds of pilgrims, on the road, and on the hillside, many of them from Galilee, who knew him, began to spread their cloaks on the road, in front of him, and others cut branches from the palm trees, and the olive trees, and spread them on the road, and waved them, and shouted: Hosanna! Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord! Blessed is the coming kingdom of our father David! Hosanna in the highest!

--

Hosanna means: save us now. The words come from one of the psalms that the pilgrims sang, at the festivals, Psalm 118: Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord.

The road down the Mount of Olives, from the top, goes steeply down, toward the Kidron valley, and from it, there is a magnificent view of the whole city, across the valley: the walls, and the Temple, shining in the spring sunshine. The Gospel of Luke says that, when Jesus came near, and saw the city, he wept over it.

{img:olive-branches-spread-on-road-crowd-singing-joyful-spring-light}
Today, Christians all over the world remember this day, a week before Easter, as Palm Sunday, with processions, and palm branches, or branches of olive, or willow, or box, or whatever grows where they live.

And in Jerusalem, every year, on Palm Sunday, thousands of Christians, from all over the world, walk in a great procession, from Bethphage, on the top of the Mount of Olives, down the steep road, singing, and waving palm branches, across the Kidron valley, and into the old city, through Saint Stephen's Gate, the Lions' Gate, as the crowds did, nearly two thousand years ago.

--

The crowds went with Jesus into the city, the Gospels say, and the whole city was stirred, and asked: who is this? And the crowds said: this is the prophet Jesus, from Nazareth in Galilee.

And in the evening, he went back out, with his friends, over the Mount of Olives, to Bethany, to spend the night.
