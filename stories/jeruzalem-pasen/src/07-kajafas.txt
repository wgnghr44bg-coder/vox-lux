The most powerful Jewish leader in Jerusalem, in the year 33, is the High Priest, Joseph, called Caiaphas.

He has been High Priest for fifteen years, since the year 18, which is a very long time. The Romans appoint the High Priest, and remove him, when they like. That Caiaphas has lasted so long shows that he is a clever, careful politician, who knows how to work with the Romans, and keep the peace.

--

The High Priest is the head of the Temple, and of the Jewish religion, and the president of the Sanhedrin, the great council of seventy elders, priests, and scholars, which governs the Jewish people, in religious matters, and many others, under the Romans.

He belongs to a small group of rich, powerful, priestly families, the Sadducees, who control the Temple, and its enormous wealth. His father-in-law, Annas, was High Priest before him, and five of Annas's sons will be High Priest after him. They live in fine houses, in the Upper City.

{img:upper-city-priestly-mansion-courtyard-mosaic-ritual-bath-stone}
Archaeologists have found some of these houses, in the Jewish Quarter of the old city, buried under the ruins of the year 70: large, elegant houses, with mosaic floors, and painted walls, and fine glass, and pottery, and stone vessels, and their own ritual baths, deep in the rock. One of them is so large, and so rich, that it is called the Palatial Mansion.

--

And in 1990, workers building a road, south of Jerusalem, found an old family tomb, cut into the rock. Inside, there were ossuaries, little stone boxes, in which the bones of the dead were placed, a year after burial, as was the custom. One of them, beautifully carved, had a name scratched on it, in Aramaic: Joseph, son of Caiaphas. Many scholars think that it is the ossuary of the High Priest himself.

The High Priest's greatest fear is that the crowds at Passover will riot, or rebel, against the Romans, and that the Romans will come, in force, and destroy the Temple, and the nation.

{img:carved-limestone-ossuary-rosettes-aramaic-name-museum-light}
The Gospel of John says that Caiaphas said to the council: it is better for you that one man should die for the people, than that the whole nation should perish. Because, in those days, any man who drew great crowds, and was hailed as a king, or a messiah, could start a rebellion. And a rebellion would bring the Roman legions.

--

In fact, that is exactly what will happen, thirty-three years later, in the year 66, when the Jews rebel against Rome. And in the year 70, the Romans will destroy Jerusalem, and the Temple.

But in the spring of the year 33, the Temple stands, shining, above the crowded city, and the High Priest prepares for Passover.
