To the east of Jerusalem, across the Kidron valley, there is a long hill, higher than the city: the Mount of Olives.

In the time of Jesus, its slopes are covered with olive groves, silver-green, and with vineyards, and fig trees, and almond trees, and little villages, Bethphage, and Bethany, and with tombs, cut into the rock, because the Jews believe that the Messiah will come to Jerusalem by the Mount of Olives, and that the dead buried there will be the first to rise.

--

From the top of the Mount of Olives, there is the most beautiful view of Jerusalem: the whole city, spread out, across the valley, with its walls, and its houses, and its palaces, and the Temple, shining, gold and white, on its great platform, directly opposite. Pilgrims coming from the east, from Jericho, see the city first from here.

And to the east, on the other side, the view goes down, over the wilderness of Judea, to the Dead Sea, and the mountains of Moab, beyond, blue, and purple, and pink, at sunset.

{img:olive-harvest-mount-of-olives-families-ladders-nets-autumn-sun}
The olive trees of the Mount of Olives are very important to Jerusalem. Their oil is used in the Temple, for the great lampstand, the menorah, and for the offerings, and in every house of the city, for cooking, and lamps, and the skin. In the autumn, families come out, to harvest the olives, beating the branches with long sticks, and gathering the olives from the ground, and taking them to the presses, like the press at Gethsemane, where great stone wheels crush them, and the oil runs out, golden green.

--

At Passover, the Mount of Olives is covered with the tents, and shelters, and fires, of thousands of pilgrims, who cannot find room in the city. And at night, from the city walls, you can see their fires, all over the hill, like stars.

Jesus, the Gospels say, often went to the Mount of Olives, with his friends, to rest, and pray, and teach. It was on the Mount of Olives, the Gospels say, that he sat with his disciples, looking at the Temple, across the valley, and told them that one day, not one stone would be left upon another.

{img:pilgrim-fires-hillside-night-mount-of-olives-stars-city-walls}
And it was from the Mount of Olives, the Acts of the Apostles says, forty days after Easter, that he was taken up into heaven, while his disciples watched.

Today, the Mount of Olives is covered with churches, and with a great Jewish cemetery, one of the oldest and largest in the world, with tens of thousands of graves, stretching down the hill, facing the Temple Mount. And from the top, the view of Jerusalem is still the most beautiful in the world.
