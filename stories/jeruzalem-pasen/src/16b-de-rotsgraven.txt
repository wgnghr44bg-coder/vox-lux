How were the dead buried, in Jerusalem, in the time of Jesus?

The families of Jerusalem, who could afford it, had family tombs, cut into the rock, of the hillsides, around the city, outside the walls. Because, by Jewish law, the dead must be buried outside the city.

--

A tomb is like a little cave, cut into the limestone, with a small, low doorway, which can be closed with a great round stone, like a millstone, rolled in a groove, across the entrance, or with a square stone, like a plug. Inside, there is a small room, with a bench, or a pit, in the middle, so that people can stand, and around the walls, there are niches, long, narrow shelves, cut into the rock, for the bodies, or benches, under arches.

When a person died, the family washed the body, and anointed it with oils, and spices, and wrapped it in linen cloths, and laid it in the tomb, on a bench, or in a niche, on the day of death, before sunset, if possible. And they mourned, for seven days, at home, and came to visit the tomb.

{img:ossuaries-stone-boxes-niche-tomb-interior-soft-lamp-light}
And a year later, when only the bones were left, the family would come back, and gather the bones, and place them in a little stone box, an ossuary, carved of limestone, often decorated, with rosettes, and patterns, and with the name of the person scratched on it, and put it in a niche, so that the space could be used again, for the next member of the family.

--

Hundreds of these tombs have been found, around Jerusalem, and thousands of ossuaries, with names, in Hebrew, and Aramaic, and Greek: Mary, and Martha, and Joseph, and Simon, and John, and Jesus, and Judas, which were all very common names, in the time of Jesus. One of them, as we have heard, belonged to the High Priest, Caiaphas.

In the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, behind the tomb that is honoured as the tomb of Jesus, there is a little chapel, with an old rock-cut tomb, of the first century, with niches for bodies, cut into the rock: a tomb like the ones the Gospels describe, still there, inside the great church.

{img:quiet-garden-dawn-jerusalem-old-stones-birds-spring-calm}
And outside the walls of the Old City, north of the Damascus Gate, there is a beautiful, quiet garden, called the Garden Tomb, with an old rock-cut tomb, discovered in the nineteenth century, which some Protestant Christians believe may be the tomb of Jesus. Archaeologists think it is much older. But it is a peaceful place, with trees, and flowers, and birdsong, where pilgrims come, especially on Easter morning, at dawn, to sing, and pray, in the garden.
