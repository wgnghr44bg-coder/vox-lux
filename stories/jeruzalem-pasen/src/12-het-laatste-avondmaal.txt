On the Thursday evening, the Bible says, Jesus ate a last meal with his friends. It was in a large room upstairs, in a house in the city.

That morning, two friends had gone ahead to get it ready. Jesus had told them what to look for. You will meet a man carrying a jar of water. Follow him. That was a clear sign. In those days, carrying water was mostly done by women. The man led them to the house and the room upstairs. It all felt a little secret. And perhaps it was. The priests were watching, and nobody was to know where Jesus would be that night.

--

The meal was eaten in the evening, by the light of oil lamps. They lay on cushions around a low table, as people did at a feast. They leaned on their left elbow and ate with their right hand.

On the table there was bread and wine. Perhaps there were bitter herbs and the other foods of Passover. Three of the gospels say it was the Passover meal itself. The gospel of John says it was the evening before.

John tells that during the meal, Jesus stood up. He took off his cloak and tied a towel around his waist. He poured water into a bowl. And he began to wash his friends' feet, as a servant would. Peter did not want this. Lord, he said, you will never wash my feet. But Jesus said, I am your teacher, and I have washed your feet. So you too must wash each other's feet. I have shown you the way.

And he said, I give you a new command. Love one another, as I have loved you.

--

Then, the Bible says, he took bread. He gave thanks and broke it and gave it to them. He said, take and eat. This is my body, given for you. Do this to remember me.

After supper he took the cup of wine. He gave thanks and passed it to them. He said, drink from it, all of you. This is my blood, poured out for many. And they all drank from it.

{img:bread-broken-wine-cup-table-lamplight-quiet-hands-close}
Christians have remembered this meal ever since, for two thousand years. Every week, or even every day, they share bread and wine. They do it in churches and chapels and homes, in every language and in every land.

--

At that same meal, the Bible says, Jesus said something that made the room go still. One of you will betray me. His friends were very sad. One after another they asked, surely it is not me, Lord? And one of the twelve, Judas Iscariot, stood up and went out. Outside, it was night.

At the end of the meal they sang a song of praise, as people do at Passover. Then, late in the evening, they left the city. They crossed the Kidron valley toward the Mount of Olives, under the full Passover moon.

{img:full-moon-over-kidron-valley-mount-of-olives-figures-walking-night}
Today in Jerusalem, on Mount Zion, there is an old room upstairs with pointed arches. It was built by knights from Europe many centuries later. The old story says it stands on the place of the Last Supper. Pilgrims from all over the world come to see it.
