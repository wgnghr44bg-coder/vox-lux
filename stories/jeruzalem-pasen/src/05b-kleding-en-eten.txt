What do the people of Jerusalem wear, in the year 33?

Men wear a tunic, a long shirt, of wool, or linen, reaching to the knees, or the ankles, with a belt, and over it, a cloak, a mantle, a large rectangle of wool, wrapped around the body. At the four corners of the mantle, there are tassels, called tzitzit, with a thread of blue, as the Law of Moses commands, to remind them of the commandments. On their feet, they wear sandals, of leather.

--

Women wear a longer tunic, to the ankles, and a mantle, and a veil, or a scarf, over their hair, when they go out. The clothes are mostly of natural colours: white, and cream, and brown, and grey, from the wool of the sheep, and the linen of the flax. The rich wear finer cloth, dyed in colours: blue, and red, and purple, which is very expensive.

Archaeologists have found pieces of cloth, from the time of Jesus, in the dry caves of the Judean desert, near the Dead Sea, perfectly preserved: tunics, and mantles, with coloured stripes, woven in, and the remains of tassels, and sandals, and baskets, and mats.

What do they eat?

--

Bread is the most important food: wheat bread, for those who can afford it, and barley bread, for the poor. Bread is so important that the word for a meal is breaking bread.

With the bread, they eat olive oil, and olives, and cheese, from goats, and sheep, and lentils, and beans, and chickpeas, and onions, and garlic, and leeks, and cucumbers, and vegetables. And fruit: figs, and grapes, and dates, and pomegranates, and melons, and almonds. And honey. And fish, from the Sea of Galilee, dried, and salted, and pickled, brought to Jerusalem in great quantities. And eggs, and chickens, and pigeons. Meat, lamb, and goat, and beef, is eaten mostly at festivals, after a sacrifice in the Temple.

{img:olive-press-stone-wheel-donkey-oil-jars-grove-autumn}
They drink water, and milk, and wine, mixed with water. And the olive oil is used for everything: for cooking, and eating, and for the lamps, which give light at night, and for the skin, and the hair, and for medicine, and for anointing kings, and priests. The word Messiah, in Hebrew, Mashiach, and Christ, in Greek, Christos, both mean the anointed one: the one anointed with olive oil.
