Let us leave the story for a moment and jump forward, to the winter of 1946. A young shepherd boy is looking after his goats. He is in the cliffs above the Dead Sea, near a place called Qumran. One of his goats wanders off.

He looks for it. He throws a stone into a cave in the cliff. And he hears something break. When he climbs inside, he finds tall clay jars with lids. In some of them there are old rolls of leather, wrapped in linen.

--

They were the oldest Bible writings ever found. Today we call them the Dead Sea Scrolls.

Over the next ten years, people searched all the caves around Qumran. In eleven caves they found scrolls and scraps of scrolls. There were about nine hundred different writings. Some were in Hebrew, some in Aramaic and some in Greek. They were written over a few hundred years, up to the year 68. Many were written in the time of Jesus.

{img:ancient-scroll-unrolled-hebrew-columns-museum-glass-soft-light}
There are copies of almost every book of the Jewish Bible. They are a thousand years older than any copies known before. There is a scroll of the prophet Isaiah, nearly whole, seven metres long. There are songs and prayers and rules for living together. And there is a strange scroll made of copper. It lists hidden treasures. Nobody has ever found them.

--

Many scholars think the scrolls belonged to a Jewish group called the Essenes. They lived in the desert by the Dead Sea, away from the world. They shared everything. They prayed and studied and copied the holy books. And they waited for the end of the world and the coming of the promised king. You can still see the ruins of their small settlement. There are baths and a dining room, and a room where the scrolls may have been written.

In the year 68, Roman soldiers came. It seems the people of Qumran hid their scrolls in the caves, in jars. They hoped to come back for them. They never did. The scrolls waited in the dark for almost two thousand years. Until a goat wandered off.

{img:shrine-of-the-book-white-dome-museum-jerusalem-later-garden}
Today the scrolls are kept in a museum in Jerusalem. The building has a white roof shaped like the lid of one of the jars. And through the scrolls, we can hear the voices of people who lived and prayed and hoped in the time of Jesus.
