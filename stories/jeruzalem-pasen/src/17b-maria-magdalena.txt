Mary from Magdala is one of the most important people in the Easter story.

She came from Magdala, a busy fishing town on the Sea of Galilee. The town was known for its salted fish, sent all over the Roman world. Her name simply means Mary from Magdala. The Bible says Jesus had healed her of a serious illness. After that she followed him with other women. She helped pay for the journey with her own money.

--

She was there at the cross, when most of the men had run away. She was there at the burial. And all four gospels say she was the first to come to the grave on Easter morning. In the gospel of John, she is the first to see Jesus alive in the garden. And she is the first to tell the others. So the early Christians gave her a special name. The messenger to the messengers.

Later, people mixed her up with other women in the Bible. They said she had been a sinful woman. But the Bible does not say that anywhere. In 2016 the Pope gave her a great feast day, the same as the twelve apostles.

In 2009, archaeologists dug at Magdala by the lake. They found a synagogue from the time of Jesus. It is one of the oldest ever found in Galilee. It has a mosaic floor and painted walls and stone benches. There is a carved stone with a picture of the seven-branched lamp of the Temple. Jesus may have taught there. And Mary may have prayed there.

--

There is an old legend from the Greek church. After Easter, it says, Mary went all the way to Rome to see the emperor. She told him that Christ had risen. And she held up an egg, as a sign of new life. The emperor laughed. He said a man could no more rise from the dead than that egg could turn red. And at that very moment, the egg in her hand turned red.

{img:red-painted-easter-eggs-basket-candles-table-orthodox-spring}
That, the legend says, is why people in Greece and Russia and many other lands paint their Easter eggs red. On Easter morning they tap their eggs together. And they say, Christ is risen.
