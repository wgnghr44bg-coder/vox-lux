Above everything in Jerusalem rises the Temple.

It stands on a huge flat platform on top of a hill. This is the Temple Mount. The platform is almost five hundred metres long. It is the biggest holy place in the ancient world. Great walls hold it up. Some of the stones in those walls are as heavy as a hundred elephants.

--

The Temple was made bigger by King Herod the Great. He started the work about fifty years before. Herod was a cruel man, but he was a great builder. The rabbis later said this. If you have not seen Herod's Temple, you have never seen a beautiful building.

The work is still going on in the year 33. It will only be finished thirty years later. And just seven years after that, the Temple will be gone. But nobody walking here this spring can imagine that.

{img:royal-stoa-colonnade-temple-mount-tall-columns-crowds-shade}
Around the edge of the platform there are long covered walkways. They have tall marble columns and roofs of carved cedar wood. On the south side is the biggest one, a great hall with four rows of columns. Here the money changers sit at their tables. Here people sell doves and lambs for the offerings. And here the teachers of the Law sit and talk and argue.

--

The big open yard is for everyone, Jews and foreigners. In the middle, there is a low stone wall with signs on it. The signs say, in Greek and Latin, that foreigners may go no further. The warning is serious. Two of these stones have been found by archaeologists. Archaeologists are people who dig up the past.

Past the low wall, there are more yards, one inside the other. First the yard where women and men may come. Then the yard for Jewish men. Then the yard of the priests, with the great stone altar. Each yard is a little more holy than the one before.

{img:temple-sanctuary-facade-golden-vine-veil-steps-priests-white}
And at the top of twelve steps is the holy house itself. It is tall and white, about fifty metres high. The front is covered with plates of gold. A writer of that time, Josephus, says the gold shone so brightly in the morning that people had to look away. Over the great door hangs a golden vine. Its golden grapes are as big as a man.

--

Inside, there is a golden lamp with seven branches. There is a golden table for holy bread. And at the back hangs a great curtain. It is blue and purple and red, and stars are sewn into it. Behind the curtain is the most holy room of all. It is small and dark and empty. Only the High Priest may go in, and only once a year.

From far away, Josephus says, the Temple looked like a mountain covered with snow. Where it was not gold, it was pure white.
