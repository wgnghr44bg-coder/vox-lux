Above everything in Jerusalem rises the Temple.

It stands on a great flat platform, on the top of Mount Moriah, the Temple Mount, on the east side of the city, the largest sacred enclosure in the ancient world: almost five hundred metres long, and three hundred metres wide, surrounded by enormous walls, of huge, carefully cut stones, some of them weighing hundreds of tons.

--

The Temple was rebuilt, and enlarged, by King Herod the Great, who began the work about fifty years before, in the year 20 before Christ. Herod was a cruel and suspicious king, but a great builder. And he made the Temple one of the wonders of the world. The rabbis later said: whoever has not seen the Temple of Herod has never seen a beautiful building.

The work is still going on, in the year 33. It will not be finished until the year 63, only seven years before it is destroyed.

{img:royal-stoa-colonnade-temple-mount-tall-columns-crowds-shade}
Around the edges of the great platform, there are colonnades, covered walkways, with tall columns of marble, and roofs of carved cedar wood. On the south side, there is the greatest of them, the Royal Stoa, a huge hall, like a basilica, with four rows of columns, where the money changers, and the sellers of animals for the sacrifices, have their tables, and where the teachers of the Law sit, and teach, and argue.

--

The great open court, the Court of the Gentiles, is open to everyone, Jews and foreigners alike. And in the middle of it, there is a raised area, surrounded by a low stone wall, with warning notices, in Greek and Latin, telling foreigners that they may go no further. Two of these stones have been found by archaeologists.

Beyond the wall, there is the Court of the Women, where all Jews, men and women, may come, with great treasury chests, shaped like trumpets, for offerings. Then the Court of Israel, for Jewish men. Then the Court of the Priests, with the great altar, of unhewn stones, where the sacrifices are offered, and the great bronze basin, for washing.

{img:temple-sanctuary-facade-golden-vine-veil-steps-priests-white}
And then, at the top of twelve steps, there is the Sanctuary itself: a tall building, of white stone, and gold, about fifty metres high. The front is covered with plates of gold, which shine so brightly in the morning sun, the historian Josephus says, that people had to turn their eyes away, as from the rays of the sun. Over the great doorway, there is a golden vine, with bunches of golden grapes, as tall as a man.

--

Inside the Sanctuary, there is the Holy Place, with the golden lampstand, the menorah, with its seven branches, and the golden table, for the bread of the Presence, and the golden altar of incense. And behind a great curtain, a veil, of blue, and purple, and scarlet, embroidered with the stars of heaven, there is the Holy of Holies: a small, empty, dark room, which only the High Priest may enter, once a year, on the Day of Atonement.

From far away, Josephus says, the Temple looked like a snow-covered mountain, because where it was not covered with gold, it was of the purest white.
