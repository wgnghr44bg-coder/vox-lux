In the year 33, the land of the Jews is ruled by Rome.

The south of the land is a Roman province. It is ruled by a Roman governor. Most of the year he lives in Caesarea, the new city by the sea. It has a harbour, a theatre and a palace on the beach, all built by Herod.

--

Since the year 26, the governor is a man called Pontius Pilate. He is a Roman soldier and official. We do not know very much about him. A Jewish writer of the time, Philo, did not like him at all. He says Pilate was stubborn and harsh, and quick to get angry.

In 1961, Italian archaeologists were digging in the old theatre at Caesarea. They found a stone that had been used as a step. On it, in Latin, was his name. Pontius Pilate, governor of Judea. It is the only stone with his name from his own lifetime. Today it is in a museum in Jerusalem.

{img:pilate-stone-latin-inscription-limestone-museum-soft-light}
Pilate has already had many quarrels with the Jews. Once his soldiers carried pictures of the emperor into Jerusalem at night. The Jews believed this was forbidden in the holy city. So crowds went all the way to Caesarea. They lay down outside his palace for five days. They said they would rather die than let this happen. In the end, Pilate gave in. But he did not forget.

--

At the great festivals, Pilate comes up to Jerusalem with extra soldiers. The crowds are huge. And Passover is a festival about freedom from a foreign ruler. It is easy to see why the Romans get nervous. One spark, and a riot could start.

Pilate stays in Herod's old palace, with its towers and gardens. From there, and from the fortress by the Temple, his soldiers watch the crowds.

{img:roman-soldiers-antonia-fortress-roof-watching-temple-courts-crowds}
Most of his soldiers are not from Italy. They come from the non-Jewish towns nearby. Many of them do not like the Jews, and the Jews do not like them.

--

Above Pilate there is the Roman governor of Syria. And above him is the emperor, Tiberius. He is an old, gloomy man who trusts nobody. He lives on an island near Naples and rarely visits Rome. A governor who lets a riot happen could lose everything.

In the north, in Galilee, there is also a Jewish ruler, Herod Antipas. He is a son of the old king Herod. He rules Galilee for the Romans. And this week he too has come to Jerusalem for Passover.
