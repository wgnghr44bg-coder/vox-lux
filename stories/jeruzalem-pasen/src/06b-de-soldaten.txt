The Roman soldiers in Jerusalem, in the year 33, are not legionaries, from Italy, but auxiliaries: soldiers recruited from the non-Jewish peoples of the region, from the Greek-speaking cities of Caesarea, and Sebaste, in Samaria, and other places. There are perhaps a few thousand of them, in the whole province, and a cohort, about five hundred, or a thousand men, in Jerusalem.

They live in the Antonia fortress, beside the Temple, and in Herod's palace, in the Upper City.

--

They wear helmets of iron, or bronze, and tunics, and armour of mail, or scales, and carry oval shields, and swords, and spears. Their commander is a tribune. Under him, there are centurions, officers, each in charge of about eighty men, who carry a vine stick, as a sign of their rank.

Their life is mostly boring: guard duty, on the walls, and the gates, and the towers. Patrols, in the streets. Training, and drill, and marching. Cleaning their weapons, and their armour. And at night, in the barracks, playing dice, and board games, scratched on the stone pavements, which can still be seen, in some places, in Jerusalem. And waiting for their pay, and their leave.

Many of them do not like the Jews, and the Jews do not like them. They are foreign soldiers, of a foreign power, in the holy city. And there are often tensions, and quarrels, especially at the festivals, when the crowds are enormous, and excited. Josephus tells of a soldier, on the roof of the colonnade of the Temple, at Passover, a few years later, who made a rude gesture to the crowd, and caused a riot.

--

But the Gospels also tell of a Roman centurion, in Capernaum, in Galilee, who loved the Jewish people, and built them a synagogue, and whose servant Jesus healed. And Jesus said of him: I have not found such great faith, even in Israel.

And of the centurion at the cross, who said: truly, this man was the Son of God.

{img:centurion-standing-quiet-evening-light-helmet-under-arm-thoughtful}
And in the Acts of the Apostles, the first non-Jew to become a Christian is also a Roman centurion: Cornelius, of Caesarea, a good man, who prayed, and gave alms to the poor, and was baptized by Peter, with all his household.
