Today, Jerusalem is a great city, holy to three religions, Jews, Christians, and Muslims, with nearly a million people.

The Old City, inside its walls, built by the Ottoman Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent, in the sixteenth century, is divided into four quarters: Jewish, Christian, Muslim, and Armenian. It is a World Heritage Site, a maze of narrow, stone-paved streets, and markets, and churches, and mosques, and synagogues, and old houses, and courtyards, crowded with people, and pilgrims, and visitors, from all over the world.

--

On the Temple Mount, where Herod's Temple once stood, there are now two of the holiest places of Islam: the golden Dome of the Rock, built in the seventh century, and the al-Aqsa Mosque. Below them, the Western Wall, where Jews pray, every day, and every night.

And in the Christian Quarter, there is the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, a great, dark, ancient, crowded church, built and rebuilt over sixteen centuries, which contains, under one roof, both the little hill of Golgotha, and the tomb, which, since the fourth century, Christians have honoured as the places where Jesus was crucified, and buried. Six different Christian churches share it, and each has its own chapels, and its own times for worship, and the key to the great door has been kept, for centuries, by a Muslim family, who open it every morning, and close it every night.

Through the Old City, there is the Via Dolorosa, the way of sorrows, the route that, according to tradition, Jesus walked, carrying his cross, from the place where Pilate judged him, to Golgotha, with fourteen stations, marked on the walls, and the pavements, where pilgrims stop, and pray. Every Friday, the Franciscan friars lead a procession along it.

--

At Easter, Jerusalem is full of pilgrims again, as it was in the year 33.

On Palm Sunday, they walk down the Mount of Olives, with palm branches, singing. On Holy Thursday, they gather in the Upper Room, and in Gethsemane, among the old olive trees. On Good Friday, thousands of them walk the Via Dolorosa, some carrying wooden crosses. And on the Saturday, the Orthodox Christians gather, in the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, for the ceremony of the Holy Fire, when, they believe, a flame appears in the tomb, and is passed, from candle to candle, through the great church, until thousands of candles are burning, and the light is carried out, into the city, and flown, on special planes, to Orthodox churches in Greece, and Russia, and other lands.

{img:candles-held-crowd-easter-church-holy-fire-glow-faces}
And at the same time, in the Jewish Quarter, the families are celebrating Passover, with the Passover meal, the Seder, with the unleavened bread, and the bitter herbs, and the four cups of wine, and the questions of the children, and the story of the escape from Egypt, as they have done for more than three thousand years. And at the end of the meal, they say, as they have said for two thousand years, all over the world: next year, in Jerusalem.

--

So we leave Jerusalem tonight, as the spring evening falls over the hills of Judea, and the bells of the churches ring, and the old olive trees of Gethsemane stand silver in the moonlight, at the foot of the Mount of Olives.

The pilgrims singing on the roads, the families on their roofs at sunset, the women in the garden at dawn, and two friends walking home to Emmaus, with their hearts burning within them.
