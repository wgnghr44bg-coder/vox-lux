Passover, Pesach, is the greatest festival of the Jewish year.

It remembers the night, more than a thousand years before, when, the Book of Exodus tells, God brought the people of Israel out of slavery, in Egypt, under Moses. On that night, each family killed a lamb, and put its blood on the doorposts of their house, so that the angel of death would pass over it. And they ate the lamb, roasted, with unleavened bread, made in haste, without time for the dough to rise, and bitter herbs, standing, dressed for a journey, ready to leave.

--

Every year since, the Jewish people have remembered that night, with a special meal, the Passover meal, eaten together, in families, on the evening of the fourteenth day of the month of Nisan, the first full moon of spring.

And in the time of the Temple, everyone who can, comes to Jerusalem for Passover, to sacrifice the Passover lamb, in the Temple, and eat the Passover meal, inside the walls of the holy city.

{img:families-walking-road-galilee-to-jerusalem-donkey-children-spring}
So, in the weeks before Passover, the roads to Jerusalem are full of pilgrims: families, from Galilee, in the north, and from all over Judea, and from the cities of the Jewish communities abroad, walking, for days, along the roads, with their donkeys, and their children, singing the Psalms of Ascent, the special psalms, for the journey up to Jerusalem: I was glad, when they said unto me, let us go into the house of the Lord. Our feet shall stand within thy gates, O Jerusalem.

--

When they come over the top of the Mount of Olives, and see the city, and the Temple, shining gold and white, across the valley, they stop, and many of them weep, for joy.

The city cannot hold them all. So they camp outside the walls, on the hills around, on the Mount of Olives, and in the villages nearby, in tents, and shelters of branches, or stay with relatives, and friends, in the city, and the villages, like Bethany. The historian Josephus says, perhaps exaggerating, that once, more than two and a half million people came to Jerusalem for Passover. Modern historians think perhaps several hundred thousand.

{img:temple-court-crowds-passover-lambs-priests-levites-singing}
In the days before Passover, everyone must purify themselves, in the ritual baths, and the pools, and the families clean their houses, and search, by the light of a lamp, in every corner, for any crumb of leavened bread, which must be removed, and burned, before the festival.

And on the afternoon of the day before Passover, in the Temple, the Passover lambs are sacrificed, in great crowds, in turns, while the Levites, the Temple singers, sing the Hallel, the psalms of praise, with trumpets, and harps, and cymbals. And then each family carries its lamb home, to roast it, for the Passover meal, that evening.
