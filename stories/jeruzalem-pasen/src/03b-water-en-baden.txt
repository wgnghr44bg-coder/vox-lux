Water is precious in Jerusalem. The city stands on a dry hill at the edge of the desert. It has only one spring, down in the valley.

So the people catch the rain. In winter, the rain runs off their roofs into tanks cut in the rock under their houses. These tanks are called cisterns. They are covered with plaster to keep the water in. It has to last all through the long dry summer. If the winter rains are poor, the whole city worries.

--

Water is also important in the Jewish faith. It is about being clean in a holy way.

The Law says that people must be clean before they go into the Temple. This is not about dirt. It is a kind of holy cleanness. You can lose it by touching certain things, or after certain events in life. To get it back, you go fully under water. You can do this in a river or a spring. Or you can use a special bath called a mikveh.

A mikveh is a small pool cut into the rock. Steps lead down into the water. Often a low line divides the steps in two. You go down one side, not yet clean. You come up the other side, clean again. The water must be living water. That means rain or spring water that flowed in by itself.

--

Archaeologists have found hundreds of these baths in Jerusalem. They are in the rich houses and the poor houses. Many are near the big stairs that lead up to the Temple. Thousands of pilgrims washed there before going up to pray.

At Passover the city is full of pilgrims. All of them need to be clean before the festival. So the baths and the pools are crowded from morning to night.

{img:stone-water-jars-limestone-house-courtyard-purity-vessels}
People also used cups and jars made of soft white stone. The rabbis taught that stone could not become unclean, the way clay pots could. Archaeologists find these stone cups and jars everywhere in the old houses. One story in the Bible tells of six big stone water jars at a wedding in Galilee. Jesus was a guest there. And the story says he turned the water in those jars into wine.
