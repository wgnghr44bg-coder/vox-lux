Drift off to the building of the Taj Mahal in Agra in 1643, the white marble tomb that the emperor Shah Jahan raised for his beloved wife Mumtaz Mahal, told gently through the eyes of a family of craftsmen who worked on it. A calm history for sleep about love and memory, patient hands, flowers of precious stone and a great dome glowing in the moonlight.
Hear the love story of the prince and the girl at the palace bazaar, watch elephants haul white marble from Makrana, carve lace-like marble screens, set tiny flowers of lapis and carnelian into the stone, learn the calligrapher's clever trick, walk in the paradise garden, wait for the monsoon rains, light the lamps for the anniversary and for Diwali, and follow the old emperor's last look at the tomb from his marble tower.

A calm, slow-paced history documentary for sleep and relaxation. No loud
sounds, no jump scares, just a gentle voice, soft 432 Hz background music
with smooth, gentle rain sounds and softly changing images.
