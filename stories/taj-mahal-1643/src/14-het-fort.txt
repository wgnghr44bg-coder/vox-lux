Upstream from the great tomb, on the same bank of the river, there stands the Red Fort of Agra. It is a huge fortress, with high walls of red sandstone, more than two kilometres around, and a deep moat. It was built by Shah Jahan's grandfather, the great emperor Akbar, about eighty years before our story. Inside the walls, there are palaces, and halls, and courtyards, and gardens, and mosques. It is the home of the emperor, and the centre of the empire.

Shah Jahan has rebuilt many of the palaces inside the fort, in white marble, in his own style. Light, and elegant, with slender columns, and carved arches, and inlaid flowers, like those on the great tomb. There is a pavilion, high on the wall, overlooking the river, from which the emperor can see the tomb of his wife, downstream, shining white, in the distance.

--

{img:emperor-at-jharokha-window-fort-wall-crowd-below-sunrise-darshan}
Every morning, at sunrise, the emperor appears at a window, high on the wall of the fort, above the river. Crowds of people gather below, on the river bank, to see him. This is called darshan, which means seeing. The people believe that seeing the emperor brings good luck, and blessings. Some of them come every day. They will not eat their breakfast, until they have seen him. The emperor stands at the window for a little while, and looks down at them, and they bow, and cheer.

Then, every day, the emperor holds court, in the Hall of Public Audience. It is a great hall, with rows of white columns, and arches, open on three sides. The emperor sits on a raised throne, in an alcove, at the back of the hall. The nobles stand in front of him, in rows, according to their rank. Ordinary people can come, and bring their complaints, and their requests, to the emperor himself. He listens, and decides, and gives orders.

{img:hall-of-public-audience-columns-nobles-in-rows-throne-alcove-mughal-court}
In the afternoon, the emperor meets his most important ministers, and generals, in the Hall of Private Audience, a smaller, more beautiful hall, decorated with inlay, and gold. Here, they discuss the affairs of the empire. War, and peace, and taxes, and buildings, and the reports from the provinces. And here, too, the emperor discusses the great tomb, with his architects, and looks at their plans, and their drawings.

Shah Jahan is famous for his jewels. He has the greatest collection of jewels in the world. Diamonds, and rubies, and emeralds, and pearls. In 1635, a new throne was finished for him. It had taken seven years to make. It is made of gold, and covered with precious stones. On its canopy, there are two peacocks, made of jewels, with their tails spread. People call it the Peacock Throne. It is the most valuable object in the world.

--

Twice a year, on his birthdays, the emperor is weighed, in a great ceremony, in the fort. He sits on one side of a big pair of scales, and on the other side, the officials put gold, and silver, and silk, and precious things, until the scales balance. Then all of it is given away, to the poor. The people of Agra wait eagerly for these days. Karim once received a silver coin, from the emperor's weighing, when he was a boy. He still keeps it.

{img:weighing-ceremony-emperor-on-golden-scales-gold-coins-courtiers-watching}
The emperor does not live in the fort alone. There are his children, and his other wives, and the ladies of the court, and their servants, in the harem, the private part of the palace. Thousands of people. Since Mumtaz Mahal passed away, their eldest daughter, Princess Jahanara, has become the most important lady at court. She is clever, and kind, and generous, and a fine poet. She looks after her father, and runs the household, and gives great sums of money to the poor, and to build mosques, and caravanserais.

Shah Jahan is getting older. He is fifty-one, in 1643. His beard is white. He still wears white, on the anniversaries of his wife's death. He still visits her tomb, often, by boat, along the river. And in the evenings, people say, he sits in his pavilion, high on the wall of the fort, and looks down the river, at the great white dome, glowing in the sunset. And he does not say anything, for a long time.
