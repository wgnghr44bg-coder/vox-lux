The story of the Taj Mahal begins with a love story. More than thirty years before our story, in the year 1607, a young prince called Khurram went to the royal bazaar, in Agra. It was a special bazaar, held inside the palace, where the ladies of the court sold silk, and jewels, and little trinkets, and the princes and nobles came to buy them, and to joke, and to flirt.

At one of the stalls, there was a girl selling silk, and glass beads. She was fourteen years old. Her name was Arjumand Banu Begum. She was the granddaughter of an important minister, and the niece of a woman who would later become empress. The story says that the prince saw her, and fell in love with her, at first sight. And that he went to his father, the emperor, the very same day, and asked to marry her.

--

{img:mughal-wedding-procession-1612-elephants-lanterns-prince-on-horse-night}
They had to wait five years. They were married in 1612, when she was nineteen, and he was twenty. It was a great royal wedding, with feasts, and music, and fireworks, and processions of elephants. And the prince gave his bride a new name, Mumtaz Mahal. It means the chosen one of the palace. Or the jewel of the palace. From that day on, they were never apart.

Mumtaz Mahal was beautiful, and clever, and kind. She was the prince's closest friend, and his wisest adviser. She went everywhere with him. On his journeys, and on his military campaigns, even into war, riding in a covered howdah on the back of an elephant. She helped him with his letters, and his decisions. She gave him advice, about the empire, and the court. And she looked after the poor, and the widows, and the orphans, and asked him to be merciful to his enemies.

{img:shah-jahan-coronation-peacock-court-agra-fort-1628-jewels-nobles}
In 1628, the prince became emperor. He took the name Shah Jahan, which means the king of the world. He was the fifth emperor of the great Mughal dynasty, which had ruled India for a hundred years. He was rich beyond imagination. He loved beautiful things. Jewels, and fine buildings, and gardens, and music, and poetry. He would become the greatest builder of all the Mughal emperors.

And Mumtaz Mahal became his empress. He gave her the royal seal, so that she could sign orders in his name. He gave her a great palace, inside the fort at Agra. He gave her jewels, and gardens, and an enormous income. But she did not care much for riches. She cared most for him, and for their children. Over nineteen years of marriage, she gave birth to fourteen children. Seven of them lived to grow up.

--

The court poets wrote poems about their love. They said that the emperor and the empress were like two bodies with one soul. Shah Jahan had other wives, as Mughal emperors did. But they were wives only in name. He spent all his time with Mumtaz Mahal. The court historians wrote that his love for her was a thousand times greater than for any other.

{img:mughal-miniature-painting-emperor-and-empress-garden-pavilion-roses}
The Mughal painters made pictures of them, in the small, detailed, colourful style of the Mughal court. Pictures of Shah Jahan, with his dark beard, and his jewelled turban, and a halo of gold around his head. And pictures of Mumtaz Mahal, holding a rose, gentle and graceful. Some of these little paintings still exist today, in museums. They show two people who look very much in love.

For three years after he became emperor, Shah Jahan and Mumtaz Mahal were very happy. They lived in the palaces of Agra, and travelled across the empire, and planned great buildings, and gardens. And then, in the year 1631, everything changed.
