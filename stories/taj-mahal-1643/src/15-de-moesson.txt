In June, after the terrible heat of the summer, the monsoon comes. For weeks, the people of Agra have been waiting for it. The heat has been unbearable. The sun has beaten down, from a white sky. The earth is cracked, and dry. The river is low, and slow. The leaves on the trees are grey with dust. Everyone is tired, and irritable, and longing for the rain.

Then, one afternoon, the sky in the south-east grows dark. Great clouds pile up, black, and purple, and grey, towering into the sky. The wind rises, suddenly, and blows the dust around in clouds. The birds fly low, and the animals grow restless. There is a rumble of thunder, far away. And then, the first big drops of rain fall, on the dusty ground, making little craters, and a wonderful smell of wet earth rises up.

--

{img:children-dancing-in-first-monsoon-rain-rooftop-agra-joy-wet}
And then the rain comes down, in torrents. A great roaring curtain of water, pouring from the sky. In a few minutes, the streets are rivers. Everyone runs outside, to stand in the rain, and feel it on their faces. The children dance on the rooftops, and shout, and laugh. The grown-ups smile, and lift their faces to the sky. Old Yusuf sits in the courtyard, under the neem tree, and lets the rain soak him, and says that it is the best feeling in the world.

The monsoon lasts for about three months. It does not rain all the time. There are sunny days, and cloudy days, and days of heavy rain. But everything changes. The air is cooler, and fresher. The dust is washed away. The trees turn bright green again. The fields fill with water, and the farmers plant their rice, and their millet. Frogs croak, all night, in the pools. And peacocks dance, and call, in the gardens, spreading their great tails.

{img:peacock-dancing-monsoon-garden-rain-drops-green-lush-taj-background}
The river rises, too. The Yamuna, which was low and slow in the summer, becomes wide, and fast, and brown, with the mud from the hills. It rises up the bank, almost to the walls of the great tomb. The builders watch it carefully. But the foundations are strong, and the river does not reach the tomb. It flows past, wide and powerful, carrying branches, and leaves, and sometimes whole trees.

For the workers on the great tomb, the monsoon is a difficult time. Some of the work must stop. The mortar does not dry properly, in the wet. The scaffolding becomes slippery and dangerous. The roads are muddy, and the carts carrying the marble get stuck. So some of the workers rest, during the heaviest rains. Others work inside, in the workshops, under the thatched roofs, carving, and cutting, and inlaying.

--

{img:inlay-workshop-rain-outside-craftsmen-working-oil-lamps-cozy-dim}
Karim likes working in the monsoon. The workshop is cool, and quiet, and the sound of the rain on the thatched roof is soothing. He works on his flowers, by the light of oil lamps, on the dark days. The other inlayers talk, and tell stories, and sing, as they work. Sometimes, someone plays a flute. And outside, the rain falls, and falls, softly, on the great white dome.

The tomb is beautiful, in the rain. The white marble turns grey, and soft, and shining, when it is wet. The rain runs down the dome, in little streams. And when the sun comes out, after a storm, the marble glows, and steams, and sparkles, as if it were covered in diamonds. And sometimes, there is a rainbow, over the river, and the dome stands beneath it, white against the dark clouds.

In the evenings, during the monsoon, the family sits on the covered veranda of their house, and watches the rain, and listens to it. Zainab makes hot milk, with ginger, and spices, and fried snacks, which everyone loves in the rainy season. Yusuf tells stories, of monsoons long ago. And Noor draws the rain, falling on the flowers, in the courtyard.
