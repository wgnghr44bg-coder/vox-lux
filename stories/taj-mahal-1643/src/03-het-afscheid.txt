In 1631, Shah Jahan went south, with his army, to the city of Burhanpur, in the Deccan, far from Agra. There was a rebellion there, and he went to put it down. As always, Mumtaz Mahal went with him, although she was expecting a baby. They lived in the royal camp, in great tents of silk and velvet, beside the river, in the hot, dry country of the south.

In June 1631, the baby was born. A little girl, the empress's fourteenth child. But the birth was very long, and very difficult. And afterward, Mumtaz Mahal grew weaker and weaker. The doctors could do nothing. She knew that she was dying. She called her husband to her side, and they talked, quietly, for the last time.

--

The story says that she asked him for two things. That he would never marry again. And that he would build her a tomb, so beautiful that the world had never seen anything like it. Some historians are not sure if this story is true. But Shah Jahan did build the tomb. And whatever she asked of him, he never forgot her. That night, in the early hours of the morning, Mumtaz Mahal passed away, peacefully, in his arms. She was thirty-eight years old.

{img:empty-royal-tent-single-lamp-silk-cushions-grief-night-quiet}
Shah Jahan's grief was terrible. The court historians wrote that he shut himself away, in his tent, for a week, and would not eat, or drink, or see anyone. When he came out again, his beard had turned white. He wore plain white clothes, the colour of mourning, for two years. He gave up music, and jewels, and fine food, and perfume. He wept so much, they said, that he had to wear spectacles. He said that the empire had lost all its sweetness for him.

The whole empire mourned with him. The court wore white, and there were no celebrations, no music, no festivals, for two years. In the mosques and the temples, people prayed for the empress. She had been loved by many people, rich and poor, for her kindness, and her charity. The poor of Agra remembered how she had given them food, and money, and help.

{img:funeral-procession-golden-coffin-elephants-mourners-white-desert-road}
At first, Mumtaz Mahal was buried in a garden in Burhanpur, beside the river. But Shah Jahan had already decided that she must lie in Agra, in the most beautiful tomb in the world. So, six months later, in December 1631, her body was taken out of the garden, and carried north, in a golden coffin, on the long road to Agra. Her son, Prince Shah Shuja, went with her, and a great procession of nobles, and soldiers, and servants.

All along the way, the procession stopped, and gave food, and money, to the poor, in her memory. It took many weeks to reach Agra. When it arrived, in January 1632, the empress was buried again, for a while, in a small building in a garden, on the bank of the river. In the place where the great tomb would be built, around her.

--

{img:riverside-garden-agra-1632-small-temporary-tomb-river-yamuna-morning}
The land on the river bank belonged to a Hindu prince, the Raja Jai Singh of Amber. It was a beautiful garden, with a palace in it, on a bend of the river, where the water was wide, and calm. Shah Jahan asked for it. The raja gave it to him, as a gift, and the emperor gave him four great houses in Agra, in exchange. It was the perfect place. From the fort, where the emperor lived, he could look down the river, and see it.

And so, early in 1632, the work began. The emperor gathered the greatest architects, and builders, and craftsmen of his empire, and told them what he wanted. A tomb in a garden. A garden of paradise. With a building in the middle, of white marble, more beautiful than anything the world had ever seen. And he told them that money did not matter. They could have whatever they needed.

Later, people would say that the Taj Mahal was the greatest monument to love ever built. A husband's love for his wife, made into stone. And when you stand in front of it, and see it shining, white and perfect, in the sunlight, it is easy to believe. Shah Jahan could not bring Mumtaz Mahal back. But he could make sure that the world would never forget her. And it never has.
