Like every great building, the Taj Mahal has many legends. Some of them are beautiful, and some of them are strange, and some of them are sad. And most of them are not true. But people have told them, for hundreds of years, and they are part of the story of the great tomb.

The most famous legend is the story of the Black Taj. People say that Shah Jahan planned to build a second tomb, for himself, on the other side of the river, exactly like the first, but of black marble. And that the two tombs would be joined by a bridge, of silver. One white, for the empress, and one black, for the emperor, facing each other, across the river, for ever. It is a beautiful story. But there is no evidence that it was ever planned.

--

{img:moonlight-garden-mehtab-bagh-pool-reflection-taj-across-river-night}
On the other side of the river, opposite the tomb, there is a garden. It is called the Moonlight Garden, Mehtab Bagh. For a long time, people thought it might be the place where the Black Taj was to be built. Archaeologists dug there, in the 1990s. But they found no foundations for a black tomb. Instead, they found the remains of a beautiful garden, with a great octagonal pool. And when the pool was full, at night, the white tomb was reflected in it, perfectly, across the river.

So the Moonlight Garden was probably made for looking at the tomb, in the moonlight, reflected in the water. A place where the emperor could sit, at night, and see his wife's tomb, shining white, and its reflection, shining in the pool. Today, the Moonlight Garden has been restored, and visitors can go there, in the evening, and see the great tomb, across the river, as the emperor once did.

{img:craftsmen-hands-at-work-old-legend-sad-story-marble-dust-gentle}
Another legend is a sad one. People say that, when the great tomb was finished, Shah Jahan ordered that the hands of the workers should be cut off, so that they could never build anything as beautiful again. This story is told to many visitors, even today. But it is almost certainly not true. There is no record of it, anywhere. And we know that many of the workers went on to work on other great buildings, for Shah Jahan, in Delhi. Including the Red Fort, and the great mosque.

There are other legends too. That the marble changes colour, according to the mood of the empress. That on nights of the full moon, the tomb glows by itself. That there are secret rooms, beneath the platform, full of treasure. That the workers were paid in gold, by weight. That the emperor wept so much, while it was being built, that the river rose.

--

These stories are not true. But they show how much the great tomb has touched people's hearts, over the centuries. People need stories, to explain things that are too beautiful to understand. And the Taj Mahal is one of those things. It is so perfect, so calm, so lovely, that people cannot quite believe that it was made by human hands.

{img:taj-mahal-full-moon-night-dome-silvery-glow-garden-dark-cypress}
But it was. It was made by human hands. By twenty thousand pairs of hands. Hands that cut, and carried, and carved, and inlaid, and polished, for more than twenty years. The hands of Hindus, and Muslims, and Persians, and Indians. Of master craftsmen, and humble labourers. Most of their names are forgotten. But their work is not. It is still there, shining, by the river, for everyone to see.

And on nights of the full moon, even if it does not really glow by itself, the great tomb does look as if it is made of light. The moonlight falls on the white marble, and it shines, softly, silver, and blue, against the dark sky. And many people who see it say that it is the most beautiful thing they have ever seen.
