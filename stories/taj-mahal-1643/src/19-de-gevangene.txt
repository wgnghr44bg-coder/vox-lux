The great tomb was finished in about 1653, with all its gardens, and gateways, and buildings. Shah Jahan was very proud of it. He wrote a poem about it. He said that it was like the tomb of a sinner who has been forgiven, and that its sight made the sun and the moon shed tears. But his happiness did not last.

In 1657, Shah Jahan fell seriously ill. His four sons, who were grown men now, each ruling a part of the empire, began to fight each other, for the throne. It was a long and bitter struggle. In the end, the third son, Aurangzeb, won. He was a stern, serious, very religious man. He did not approve of his father's love of luxury, and beautiful buildings.

--

{img:musamman-burj-octagonal-marble-tower-agra-fort-river-view-evening}
In 1658, Aurangzeb took his father prisoner, in the fort at Agra. He did not harm him. But he kept him there, in the palace, for the rest of his life. Shah Jahan was never allowed to leave. He lived in his marble rooms, inside the fort, with his daughter, Jahanara, who chose to stay with him, and look after him, and a few servants. He was no longer the emperor. But he was still the father of the emperor.

From the windows of his rooms, in a beautiful octagonal tower, of white marble, on the wall of the fort, Shah Jahan could see the great tomb of his wife, down the river. People say that he spent his days sitting at the window, looking at it. In the morning, when it was pink, and at midday, when it was white, and in the evening, when it was golden. And at night, when it was silver, in the moonlight. He looked at it for eight years.

{img:princess-jahanara-reading-to-old-father-marble-room-lamplight-gentle}
Jahanara looked after him lovingly, all those years. She read to him, and talked to him, and comforted him. She was a poet, and a scholar, and a deeply spiritual woman. Her father loved her very much. She was the one person who stayed with him, through everything. Without her, his last years would have been very lonely.

In January 1666, Shah Jahan fell ill, and knew that he was dying. He asked to be carried to the window, so that he could see the great tomb, one last time. And there, the story says, looking at the white dome of his wife's tomb, in the distance, he passed away, peacefully, with his daughter beside him, reciting prayers. He was seventy-four years old.

--

Shah Jahan was buried in the great tomb, beside his wife. His cenotaph was placed next to hers, in the great hall, beneath the dome. It is the only thing in the whole building that is not perfectly symmetrical. Her cenotaph is in the exact centre of the hall. His is beside it, to the west, a little larger, and a little higher. The perfect balance of the building was broken, at last, so that he could lie beside her.

{img:two-cenotaphs-side-by-side-inside-taj-jali-screen-soft-lamplight}
Jahanara lived on for many more years, in Delhi. She passed away in 1681. She did not want a grand tomb, like her mother. She asked for a simple grave, open to the sky, with grass growing on it. On her gravestone, she wrote that nothing should cover her grave but the green grass, because grass is the best covering for the grave of the humble. Her grave can still be seen, in Delhi, today, with the grass still growing.

So the emperor and the empress were together again, after thirty-five years apart. Side by side, in the great hall, beneath the dome, in the soft light that comes through the marble screens. And the river flows past, outside, as it did then. And the garden blooms. And the great white dome rises above them both, as it has for almost four hundred years.
