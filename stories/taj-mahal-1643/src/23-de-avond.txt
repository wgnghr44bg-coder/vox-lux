So we leave Agra tonight, in the year 1643, as the evening falls. The sun is setting over the river, in a blaze of orange, and pink, and gold. And the great white tomb is glowing. First golden, then rose pink, then soft violet, as the light fades. The workers are putting down their tools, and leaving the building site, in long lines, walking home to Mumtazabad, tired, and dusty, and content.

In the garden, the gardeners are turning on the fountains, for the evening. The water rises, and falls, and splashes, in the marble pools. The cypress trees stand dark, and still. The scent of the roses, and the jasmine, fills the warm evening air. And the parakeets, and the mynah birds, are settling noisily into the trees, for the night.

--

{img:courtyard-neem-tree-family-evening-meal-oil-lamp-rooftop-dome-view}
In Karim's house, the family is gathering in the courtyard, under the neem tree. Zainab has cooked the evening meal. Rice, and lentils, and spiced vegetables, and fresh flat bread, hot from the oven. They sit on a mat, on the ground, and eat together, with their hands, from the shared dishes. Old Yusuf tells a story about the old emperor Akbar. Ali tells everyone about Champa, the elephant. And Noor shows her father the letters she wrote today.

After supper, they go up to the flat roof, to sit in the cool evening air. Zainab spreads mats, and cushions, and they lie down, and look at the sky. The stars are coming out, one by one, over the river. And just a few hundred steps away, above the dark rooftops, the great white dome rises up, pale, and glowing, in the starlight.

--

{img:family-on-rooftop-night-looking-at-moonlit-dome-stars-quiet-agra}
Then the moon rises, over the river. A big, round, golden moon, climbing slowly into the sky. And as it rises, it shines on the great white dome, and the dome begins to glow. Silver, and blue, and white. As if it were made of light, or of the moon itself. The whole family is silent. They have seen it many times. But it is always new.

Karim lies on his back, with his hands behind his head, and looks at the dome. He thinks about all the flowers he has made, over ten years. And all the hands that have worked beside him. And the emperor, in his fort, looking down the river at the same dome, in the same moonlight, thinking of his wife. Then Karim closes his eyes. And the children are already asleep, curled up on their cushions, under the stars.
