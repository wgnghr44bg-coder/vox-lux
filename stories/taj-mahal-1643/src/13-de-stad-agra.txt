Agra, in 1643, is one of the greatest cities in the world. It stretches for miles, along both banks of the river Yamuna. It is full of palaces, and mansions, and gardens, belonging to the nobles of the court. And mosques, and temples, and markets, and caravanserais, and narrow, crowded streets, full of people, and animals, and noise, and colour, and smells.

The bazaars of Agra are famous. There are bazaars for everything. A bazaar for cloth, where you can buy silks, and cottons, and muslins, so fine that a whole length of cloth can be pulled through a ring. A bazaar for spices, with great sacks and baskets of pepper, and cardamom, and cloves, and cinnamon, and turmeric, and saffron. A bazaar for jewels, and gold, and silver. A bazaar for horses, and camels, and elephants.

--

{img:spice-merchant-stall-heaps-colourful-spices-baskets-scales-agra-bazaar}
Zainab goes to the bazaar once a week, with Noor, to buy what the family needs, and to sell her embroidery. They walk from Mumtazabad, along the river, into the city. It takes about an hour. The streets are crowded with people, from every part of the empire, and beyond. Persians, and Afghans, and Turks, and Armenians, and Arabs. Hindus, and Muslims, and Jains, and Christians. And even, sometimes, a European, in strange clothes, with a pale face, and a beard.

In the cloth bazaar, Zainab sells her embroidery to a merchant, an old friend of her father's. She embroiders flowers, on cotton and silk, with coloured silk threads. Little shawls, and caps, and cushion covers. The merchant pays her well. He says that her flowers are almost as beautiful as the ones on the great tomb. Zainab laughs, and says that her husband would not agree.

{img:mother-and-daughter-buying-sweets-halwai-shop-jalebi-frying-agra}
Then they go shopping. They buy rice, and lentils, and flour, and ghee, and spices, and vegetables, and fruit. Mangoes, in the summer, and oranges, in the winter. And, if there is a little money left over, a treat. Sweets, from the sweet shop, where a fat, cheerful man fries spirals of sweet batter, in hot oil, and dips them in syrup. Noor loves them. She eats them on the way home, very slowly, licking the syrup from her fingers.

The city is full of sounds. The calls of the sellers, in the bazaars. The bells of the temples. The calls to prayer, from the minarets of the mosques, five times a day. The music of drums, and flutes, from the houses of the rich. The trumpeting of elephants. The creak of the carts. The bells on the necks of the camels. And always, everywhere, the murmur of thousands of voices, talking, laughing, arguing, bargaining.

--

At midday, the city slows down. It is too hot to do anything. The shops close, and the streets empty, and everyone rests, in the shade, until the heat is less. In the late afternoon, the city wakes up again. And in the evening, it is at its busiest, and most beautiful. Lamps are lit, in the shops, and the houses. People stroll along the streets, and visit their friends, and eat, and listen to music.

{img:agra-street-evening-oil-lamps-shops-people-strolling-mosque-dome}
On the way home, in the evening, Zainab and Noor walk along the river bank. The river is wide, and calm, and golden, in the evening light. Boats glide along it, with their sails, carrying goods, and passengers. Women wash clothes on the steps, and children swim, and splash. On the other side of the river, the sun is setting, red and gold, behind the trees. And ahead of them, the great white dome rises above the trees, glowing pink, in the last of the light.

Noor says that it looks like it is made of rose petals. Zainab says that it looks like it is made of light. They stand together, for a moment, and look at it. Then they walk on, home, through the dusk, as the lamps are lit, and the first stars come out, over the river.

At home, Zainab unpacks the shopping, and puts it away, in the cool storeroom. Noor shows her grandfather the drawings she made in the bazaar. A camel, and a spice seller, and a merchant from Persia, with a tall hat. Yusuf looks at them for a long time, holding them close to his eyes. Then he nods, and says that she has a good hand. From Yusuf, that is great praise.
