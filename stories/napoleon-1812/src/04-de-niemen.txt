On the morning of the twenty-fourth of June 1812, the sun rises in a clear blue sky over the river Niemen, and the great army begins to cross.

For three days, the columns march over the bridges of boats without stopping, day and night. Foot soldiers, horsemen, cannons and wagons pass in an endless stream. Their flags are flying and their bands are playing. They cheer as they pass the Emperor, who sits on a little hill by the bridges, watching them.

--

On the other side of the river lies Russia. And there is nothing there.

No Russian army. No soldiers. Nobody to fight. Only forests and fields, and an empty road leading east. The Russian army has pulled back deep into its huge country. It is waiting.

The soldiers are surprised, and a little disappointed. They expected a battle. Instead there is only the road. And nobody knows where it will end.

--

At first they march through Lithuania. The land is flat or gently rolling, with great forests of pine and birch, wide fields of rye and wheat, marshes and lakes. The villages are small and poor. The little wooden houses have thatched roofs and fences of wooden stakes. There are wooden churches with onion-shaped domes.

The roads are not paved. They are tracks of earth. In the summer heat they turn to deep, soft dust, and in the rain to thick mud.

On the second day after the crossing, a great thunderstorm breaks. Rain pours down, the air turns suddenly cold and the roads become rivers of mud. Thousands of horses, already worn out and sick from eating wet green rye, collapse and do not get up. It is a warning of what is to come. But few people want to see it that way yet.

--

At the end of June, the army reaches the city of Vilnius, the capital of Lithuania. Only a few days before, the Tsar himself was there. He was at a ball at a country house nearby when he heard that Napoleon had crossed the river.

Napoleon stays in Vilnius for more than two weeks, organising his army and his supplies. Many Poles and Lithuanians in the city welcome him. They hope he will free them from Russian rule.

{img:vilnius-1812-baroque-churches-old-town-streets-soldiers-arriving}
Vilnius is a beautiful city, with many churches painted white, pink and yellow, and a castle on a hill. The soldiers rest there, eat and drink, and write letters home.

--

Then the army marches on, east, deeper into Russia. They follow the Russian armies, which always seem to be one day ahead, always just out of reach.

The Russian generals have decided not to fight a big battle near the border against Napoleon's enormous army. Instead they retreat, and retreat again. They draw the French further and further into the vast spaces of Russia, away from their supplies and their homes.

{img:long-dusty-road-east-columns-soldiers-horizon-heat-haze}
And as they retreat, they burn the villages, the crops and the stores behind them, so the French will find nothing to eat. Every day, the smoke rises ahead of the army. And every day, the road is emptier.
