The soldiers of the great army do not sleep in tents. Napoleon believes tents slow an army down. So every night, the soldiers sleep in the open air, around their campfires. This is called a bivouac.

In summer, it is not so bad. In autumn and winter, it will be very hard indeed.

--

When the army stops for the night, the soldiers split into little groups of six or eight. They share their food, their fire and their cooking pot, and look after each other. These little groups are like families. The men in them are friends, and they stay together through everything.

Some go to fetch wood from the forest, or from the fences of the nearest village. Some fetch water. And some go looking for food.

{img:foraging-soldiers-village-searching-barns-hens-sacks-evening}
Finding food is the most important job of all. The army cannot carry enough for so many men. The wagons with bread and flour are far behind on the bad roads. So the soldiers must find food for themselves in the villages and farms near the road. Grain and potatoes, cabbages and chickens, anything at all. But the Russian farmers have often fled into the forests with their animals. More and more often, the villages are empty.

--

Back at the fire, the group's cook makes soup in the big iron pot.

A soldier's soup is made of whatever they can find. Water, and some meat if they are lucky. Flour or rice, cabbage or potatoes, and salt if they have any. It boils for a long time over the fire. Then the soldiers sit around the pot and eat it with their spoons. They carry their spoons stuck in a buttonhole, or in their hats.

When there is grain but no flour, they grind it themselves between stones, and bake flat little cakes in the ashes.

--

After supper, the soldiers sit by the fires. They smoke their pipes, talk and sing. They play cards, mend their clothes and clean their muskets.

The old soldiers tell stories of their old campaigns, of Egypt and the pyramids, and of great victories long ago. The young soldiers listen, and dream of glory.

{img:old-grenadier-telling-stories-young-conscripts-listening-fire-pipe}
Sergeant Bourgogne remembers these summer evenings. The fires stretched away for miles across the plain, like stars in the sky. He could hear thousands of voices talking and singing in many languages, and the bands playing at the generals' tents.

--

At last the soldiers lie down to sleep on the ground. They lie on straw if they can find any, wrapped in their coats, with their packs as pillows, close to the fire and close to each other.

Guards walk up and down at the edge of the camp, looking out into the dark and listening. The fires burn low. The horses stamp and shift. And the great army sleeps under the stars, in the middle of Russia.

{img:sleeping-soldiers-greatcoats-around-embers-stars-sentry-silhouette}
In summer, the nights are short and warm. Dawn comes at three or four in the morning. Then the drum beats again, and another day of marching begins. Nobody thinks much about how long the nights will be in winter.
