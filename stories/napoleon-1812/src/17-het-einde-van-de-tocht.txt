After the Berezina, in December 1812, the cold gets even worse.

The temperature falls to twenty, thirty, even thirty-five degrees below zero. The air is so cold it hurts to breathe. The dry snow squeaks under your feet. The trees crack in the frost with sounds like pistol shots. On clear days the sky is pale blue, and the low sun shines without any warmth on the white land.

--

On the fifth of December, in a little town, Napoleon leaves the army.

He has heard that in Paris, someone tried to overthrow his government while he was away. And he knows that after this disaster, his enemies all over Europe will rise against him. He must get back to Paris quickly to raise a new army.

{img:emperor-leaving-in-sleigh-officers-saluting-snowy-village-dusk}
So he hands command to his brother-in-law, and leaves at night in a sleigh, with a few companions. He travels in disguise, day and night, across Poland and Germany. Thirteen days later, at midnight, he arrives at his palace in Paris, so muddy and bearded that the guards do not recognise him.

On the journey, it is said, he spoke a famous sentence: from the sublime to the ridiculous, there is only one step.

--

The army goes on without him to Vilnius, where there are big stores of food and clothes. The soldiers arrive there frozen and starving. For a day or two they eat and warm themselves in the houses of the city.

But the Russians are close behind. So the army must go on again, west, to the river Niemen and the border.

In the middle of December, the last survivors of the great army cross the river Niemen, out of Russia. It is the same river they crossed so proudly in the summer sunshine, with their bands playing, six months before.

Of the more than six hundred thousand men who marched into Russia, only a small part came back. Many tens of thousands were taken prisoner, and some of them came home years later. Some stayed in Russia and made new lives there. But very many were lost.

--

It was one of the greatest disasters in the history of war. And it was the beginning of the end for Napoleon.

In 1813, all of Europe rose against him, as he had feared. He raised a new army of young recruits and fought brilliantly. But in October 1813 he was beaten in a great battle at Leipzig. In 1814 the allied armies, with Tsar Alexander at their head, entered Paris. Napoleon gave up his throne and was sent away to the little island of Elba.

{img:allied-sovereigns-entering-paris-1814-spring-crowds-boulevard}
A year later he escaped, came back to France and ruled again for a hundred days. But in June 1815, at Waterloo in Belgium, he was beaten for the last time. He was sent far away to the lonely island of Saint Helena, in the middle of the South Atlantic Ocean. He died there six years later.

--

Marshal Ney fought for him again at Waterloo. Afterwards, the new king of France had him put on trial for treason. Ney met his end bravely in Paris. Today there is a statue of him in Paris, with his sword raised.

And the Tsar, who had refused to make peace, became for a while the most powerful man in Europe. He died mysteriously some years later. Some people believed he did not really die, but became a hermit in Siberia, and lived there in secret as a holy man.

{img:saint-helena-island-cliffs-ocean-lonely-house-clouds-calm}
And Moscow was rebuilt, slowly, more beautiful than before, with new houses and churches of stone. Once again, it became the great heart of Russia.
