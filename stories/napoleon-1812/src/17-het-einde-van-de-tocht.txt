After the Berezina, in December 1812, the cold becomes even worse.

The temperature falls to twenty, thirty, even thirty-five degrees below zero. The air is so cold that it hurts to breathe. The snow is dry, and squeaks under the feet. The trees crack in the frost, with sounds like pistol shots. And the sky, on clear days, is pale and blue, and the sun shines, low, without warmth, on the white land.

--

On the fifth of December, at the little town of Smorgoni, Napoleon leaves the army.

He has heard that, in Paris, there has been an attempt to overthrow his government, while he was away. And he knows that, after the disaster in Russia, his enemies all over Europe will rise against him. He must get back to Paris, quickly, to raise a new army.

{img:emperor-leaving-in-sleigh-officers-saluting-snowy-village-dusk}
So he gives the command of the army to his brother-in-law, Joachim Murat, the King of Naples, and leaves, at night, in a sleigh, with a few companions, and a small escort, travelling in disguise, as the secretary of his friend Caulaincourt, and rides west, through the snow, day and night, across Poland, and Germany, to Paris. He arrives at the Tuileries palace, in Paris, thirteen days later, at midnight, on the eighteenth of December, so muddy and bearded that the guards do not recognize him.

On the journey, he talks for hours with Caulaincourt, who writes it all down. And he says, it is reported: from the sublime to the ridiculous, there is only one step.

--

The army goes on, without him, to Vilna, where there are great stores of food, and clothes, and shelter. The soldiers arrive there, in the second week of December, frozen and starving. And for a day or two, they eat, and warm themselves, in the houses of the city.

But the Russians are close behind. And the army must go on, again, west, to the river Niemen, and the border.

In the middle of December, the last survivors of the Grande Armée cross the river Niemen, at Kovno, out of Russia: the same river that they had crossed, so proudly, with their bands playing, in the summer sunshine, six months before.

Of the more than six hundred thousand men who had marched into Russia, only a small part came back. Many tens of thousands had been taken prisoner, and some of them, in the end, came home, years later. Many others had stayed behind, in Russia, and made new lives there. But very many were lost.

--

It was one of the greatest disasters in the history of war. And it was the beginning of the end for Napoleon.

In 1813, all of Europe rose against him, as he had feared: Russia, and Prussia, and Austria, and Sweden, and Britain. He raised a new army, of young conscripts, and fought brilliantly. But at Leipzig, in October 1813, he was defeated, in a great battle. And in 1814, the allied armies, with the Tsar Alexander at their head, entered Paris. And Napoleon gave up his throne, and was sent into exile, on the little island of Elba, in the Mediterranean.

{img:allied-sovereigns-entering-paris-1814-spring-crowds-boulevard}
He escaped, a year later, and came back to France, and ruled again, for a hundred days. But in June 1815, at Waterloo, in Belgium, he was defeated, for the last time. And he was sent far away, to the little island of Saint Helena, in the middle of the South Atlantic Ocean, where he passed away, in 1821, at the age of fifty-one.

--

Marshal Ney fought for him again, at Waterloo, where he led the great charges of the French cavalry. After Waterloo, the restored king of France had him tried, and condemned, for treason. Ney met his end bravely, in Paris, in December 1815. He refused a blindfold. Today, there is a statue of him, in Paris, near the place where he fell, with his sword raised.

And the Tsar, Alexander, who had refused to make peace, became, for a while, the most powerful man in Europe. He passed away, mysteriously, in 1825, in the south of Russia. Some people believed that he did not really die, but became a hermit, in Siberia, and lived there, in secret, as a holy man, for many years.

{img:saint-helena-island-cliffs-ocean-lonely-house-clouds-calm}
And Moscow was rebuilt, slowly, more beautiful than before, with new houses, and palaces, and churches, of stone, and became once again the great heart of Russia.
