Drift off to life in London during the Blitz in the autumn of 1940, told gently through the eyes of a family in the East End and their neighbours. A calm history for sleep about courage, kindness and endless cups of tea, about how ordinary people helped each other and kept going through the long autumn nights.
Draw the blackout curtains at dusk, queue with the ration books, spend the night in the little garden shelter covered with marigolds, sleep on the platforms of the Underground, pour tea for the firemen at dawn, meet the king and queen in the East End streets, listen to Churchill on the wireless, hear Myra Hess play Bach in the empty National Gallery, and read the letters from a little brother safe on a farm in Devon.

A calm, slow-paced history documentary for sleep and relaxation. No loud
sounds, no jump scares, just a gentle voice, soft 432 Hz background music
with smooth, gentle rain sounds and softly changing images.
