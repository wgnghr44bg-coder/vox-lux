Tonight we travel back more than eighty years, to the year 1940. We go to London, the capital of Britain, at the end of a long, warm summer. Britain is at war. And in a few days, something will begin that the people of London will never forget. They will call it the Blitz. But tonight, we will not look at the war very closely. We will look at the people. At how they lived, helped each other, and kept going, through the long autumn nights.

--

It is a Sunday morning, at the beginning of September. The sun is shining. The sky is blue, with a few white clouds. In a narrow street in the East End of London, near the river Thames, the houses stand in long rows, small and close together, made of dark brick, with little front steps, scrubbed white. Children are playing in the street. Women are chatting on their doorsteps. And somewhere, a radio is playing a dance tune.

In one of the houses, a family is having breakfast. Tea, toast, a little margarine, and a little jam. The father is reading the Sunday paper. The mother is pouring the tea. The grandmother is sitting by the window, knitting. And a girl of thirteen is reading a letter from her little brother, who is far away, in the country, safe from the war.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

{img:family-breakfast-1940-small-kitchen-teapot-radio-ration-books-table}
Tonight, we will spend an autumn with this family, in the East End of London, in 1940. We will see their house and their street, their neighbours and their friends. We will see how they lived with the blackout, the rationing and the sirens. How they slept in shelters in their gardens, and in the stations of the Underground. How they listened to the radio, and sang together, and made jokes, and drank endless cups of tea.

We will meet the wardens and the firemen, the volunteers who served tea and soup, and the king and queen, who came to visit the bombed streets. We will hear about the young pilots in the skies above, and the children sent away to the country, and the music that was played, at lunchtime, in an empty art gallery. And we will see the people of London go to work, every morning, after another long night, as if nothing had happened.

{img:londoners-queue-tea-van-volunteers-smiling-cups-street-morning-1940}
This is a gentle story, about a hard time. There was danger in London in 1940, and sadness, and loss. We will not look at those things closely. We will see them only from a distance, as the people of London tried to see them. Because most of all, this is a story about ordinary people, and their courage, their humour, their kindness, and their quiet determination to carry on.

--

{img:east-end-street-sunday-1940-neighbours-doorsteps-radio-music-calm}
So let us begin, on a sunny Sunday morning, in a little street in the East End of London, in September 1940. The children are playing. The radio is playing. The kettle is on. And for one more day, at least, everything is calm.
