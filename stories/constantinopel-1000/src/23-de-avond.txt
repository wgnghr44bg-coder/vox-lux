So we leave Constantinople tonight, in the year 1000, as the evening falls.

The sun is setting behind the hills to the west. The water of the Golden Horn turns gold and red, just as its name says. The great dome of Hagia Sophia glows softly in the last light. The ships in the harbour are tied up for the night. Their little lamps are being lit, one by one, and reflected in the still water. Seagulls settle on the rooftops. And the church bells ring out for evening prayer, all over the city.

--

In the street near the Mese, the bakery is closed for the day. The ovens are cooling. Old Stephanos has gone home, and Basil is sweeping the last flour from the floor. Eirene is upstairs, lighting the lamps. Nikolaos is having a short sleep before his night's work begins. In a few hours, he will be down in the bakehouse again, kneading the dough for tomorrow.

{img:byzantine-family-evening-upper-room-lamps-icons-grandmother-children}
Grandmother Anna is sitting by the window, with her spinning in her lap. She is watching the last light fade over the rooftops. Zoe is sitting beside her, holding the little white wooden panel from Father Theophanes. Tonight, at last, she has decided what she will paint on it. She will paint the dome of Hagia Sophia, with a little golden light above it. She smiles, and puts the panel safely away.

--

Leon is lying on his bed, still talking about the chariot race to anyone who will listen. Nobody is listening anymore, but he does not mind. He is telling it to the ceiling now. The Blue chariot took the turn so tightly, he says to himself. And the Green chariot was right behind. And then, at the very end... But his eyes are closing. And soon he is asleep, dreaming of horses.

{img:boy-asleep-small-bed-blue-ribbon-lamp-dim-room-quiet}
Down the street, Bjorn the Varangian is sitting on his doorstep, looking up at the first stars. He is thinking of the forests and the lakes, far away in the north. He is thinking of his mother and his sisters. Then he hears the bells of Hagia Sophia, ringing softly through the dusk. He smiles. This is home too, now, he thinks. At least for a while.

--

In the family's house, Eirene gathers everyone in front of the icons for the evening prayer. The little oil lamp flickers in front of the Virgin Mary and Saint Nicholas and Saint Anna. They say the old prayers together, softly. Then Eirene kisses the children goodnight. Grandmother Anna makes the sign of the cross on their foreheads. And one by one, the lamps in the house go out.

Outside, the stars come out over the Queen of Cities. They shine over the great dome and the palace and the Hippodrome. They shine over the walls and the harbours and the little houses on the seven hills. They shine on the dark water of the Bosphorus, between Europe and Asia. And the great city falls asleep, under the quiet stars.
