Near Hagia Sophia, stretching down the hill to the sea, there is the Great Palace of the emperors. It is not one building, but many. There are halls and churches, courtyards and gardens, baths and barracks, all joined together by covered walkways. It is like a city within the city.

The palace has been built and rebuilt by many emperors over the centuries. Its floors are covered with mosaics, showing scenes of hunting and country life. There are pictures of children playing with hoops, and of a boy feeding a donkey. There are pictures of tigers and elephants, and of a man milking a goat. Its walls are covered with marble and gold. Its gardens are full of fountains, flowers and fruit trees, looking out over the sea.

--

The most famous room in the palace is the great throne room, called the Magnaura. Foreign visitors who come to see the emperor are brought here. And they are amazed by what they see. The emperor sits on a great golden throne. Beside the throne, there is a golden tree, with golden birds on its branches. And at the foot of the throne, there are golden lions.

{img:golden-throne-room-mechanical-lions-golden-tree-birds-emperor-visitors}
When a visitor comes in and bows, something wonderful happens. The golden birds on the tree begin to sing. The golden lions open their mouths and roar, and swish their tails. And when the visitor looks up again, the emperor's throne has risen up high into the air, almost to the ceiling. The emperor is now wearing different clothes. The visitors do not know how it is done. It is all done by clever machines, with water and air and hidden ropes.

--

A visitor from Italy wrote about this, about fifty years before our story. He said that he was not frightened, because he had been told about it beforehand. But he was very surprised. He bowed three times, as he had been told. And when he lifted his head, the emperor was up near the ceiling, dressed in new robes. The emperor did not speak to him directly. An official spoke for him. It was all very grand and very strange.

{img:palace-ceremony-officials-silk-robes-procession-candles-golden-hall}
Life in the palace is full of ceremonies. There is a ceremony for every feast day and every important event. There are processions through the halls, with officials in coloured silk robes. Everyone has their place, and everyone knows exactly what to do. There is a great book that describes all these ceremonies, step by step. It was written by an emperor called Constantine the Seventh, about fifty years before our story.

--

But the palace is also a home, for the emperor and his family and servants. There are thousands of people living and working there. There are cooks and bakers, gardeners and grooms, guards and secretaries. Nikolaos's cousin works in the palace bakery. He bakes fine white bread for the emperor's table. Sometimes, he brings a little of it home for the family to taste. It is very soft and very white. But Nikolaos says his own bread is better.

{img:palace-gardens-terrace-sea-view-fountains-fruit-trees-evening-calm}
In the evenings, the palace gardens are quiet and beautiful. The fountains splash softly. The roses and the lilies smell sweet. The sea below is calm and blue, with the sun setting over the water. Peacocks walk slowly along the paths, their long tails trailing behind them. And from somewhere inside the palace, there is the sound of music, as the lamps are lit in the great halls.

Some of the floor mosaics of the palace are very funny. One shows a boy being kicked by a donkey. Another shows a man trying to milk a goat that will not stand still. Another shows a bear eating apples from a tree. The emperors and their guests must have walked over these little pictures every day. Perhaps they smiled at them, just as we do today.

The palace also has a great library, full of old books. There are books of history and law, medicine and astronomy. There are copies of the old Greek poets and thinkers. The scribes of the palace copy them out again and again, so they will not be lost. Some of the books are decorated with pictures in gold and bright colours. They are among the most precious treasures of the whole empire.
