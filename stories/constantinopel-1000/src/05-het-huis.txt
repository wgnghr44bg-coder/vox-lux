The family lives in a tall narrow house of brick and stone, three storeys high. The bakery is on the ground floor, at the front, opening onto the street. The family lives on the floors above.

On the ground floor, there is the shop, with a wooden counter at the front. Behind it, there is the bakehouse, with two big ovens of brick. They are shaped like domes, with a small opening at the front. There are long wooden tables for kneading the dough. There are sacks of flour stacked against the walls. And everything is covered with a fine white dust of flour, even the cat.

--

A narrow wooden staircase leads up to the family's rooms. On the first floor, there is the main room, where the family eats and spends the evenings. There is a wooden table and benches, and a few chests for clothes and linen. There is a small brazier, a metal bowl of burning charcoal, to keep warm in winter. And there is a little corner with icons, where the family prays.

{img:byzantine-family-icon-corner-oil-lamp-icons-mother-praying-evening}
The icon corner is the most special place in the house. On the wall, there are three small icons, painted on wood. One shows the Virgin Mary holding the baby Jesus. One shows Saint Nicholas, the patron saint of Nikolaos. And one shows Saint Anna, the patron saint of the grandmother. In front of the icons, a little oil lamp burns all the time, day and night. Its small flame flickers softly. Every morning and every evening, the family stands in front of the icons and prays.

--

On the second floor, there are the bedrooms. Nikolaos and Eirene sleep in one room, on a wooden bed with a mattress of wool. The children share another room. Grandmother Anna has a small room of her own, at the back, where it is quiet. The windows have wooden shutters. In summer, they keep them open at night, to let in the cool breeze from the sea.

{img:wooden-balcony-byzantine-house-potted-herbs-view-domes-sea-evening}
At the front of the house, on the second floor, there is a little wooden balcony, sticking out over the street. Grandmother Anna has pots of herbs and flowers there. There is basil and mint, rosemary and little red roses. From the balcony, you can see the street below, busy with people. And between the rooftops, you can see the great dome of Hagia Sophia, and a little glimpse of the blue sea beyond.

--

The house has no running water. Every day, someone must fetch water from the fountain at the end of the street. Usually it is Zoe or Leon. They carry it in big clay jars, one in each hand, and bring it back carefully. The water comes from far away, through great channels in the hills. It is clean and cold and good to drink.

{img:children-fetching-water-street-fountain-clay-jars-neighbours-morning}
In the evenings, the family eats together at the table. There is always good bread, of course. There is soup of lentils or beans, cheese and olives, and vegetables from the market. On feast days, there is fish or a little meat. Nikolaos says a blessing before the meal. Then everyone eats and talks about the day. Leon talks about the races. Zoe talks about church. Grandmother Anna talks about the saints. And Eirene talks about the customers, and makes everyone laugh.

In winter, the house can be cold. The wind blows from the north, across the Black Sea, and it brings rain and sometimes snow. The family gathers around the charcoal brazier in the evenings, with blankets over their knees. But the bakery is always warm, because of the ovens. On the coldest nights, Leon and Zoe like to sit in the bakehouse with their father, watching him work.

In the mornings, Grandmother Anna waters the plants on the balcony. She talks to them as she works, as if they were old friends. The pigeons come and sit on the railing, hoping for crumbs. She always has a few in her pocket for them. Leon says the pigeons know her better than they know anyone else in the street. She laughs and says that is because she is the only one who feeds them.
