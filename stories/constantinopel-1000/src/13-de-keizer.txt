The emperor in the year 1000 is Basil the Second. He is one of the most hard-working emperors in the history of Constantinople.

Basil became emperor when he was very young, together with his younger brother Constantine. For many years, older men ruled for them. There were difficult years, with rivals who wanted to take the throne. But Basil grew up strong and determined. When he was about thirty, he took charge of the empire himself. And he worked hard for it every day.

--

Basil is not like many other emperors. He does not care for feasts and luxury. He dresses simply, in dark clothes. He eats simple food. He spends most of his time not in the beautiful palace, but in army camps on the borders of the empire. He lives in a tent, like his soldiers, and shares their hardships. The soldiers respect him for it.

{img:emperor-riding-with-soldiers-mountain-road-plain-cloak-banners}
Basil is also very careful with money. He keeps the empire's treasury full. When he dies, many years later, the treasury will be fuller than it has ever been. There will be so much gold that new storerooms will have to be dug under the palace to hold it. Basil wants the empire to be strong and safe. And he believes that a full treasury helps to keep it that way.

--

Basil is also known for something unusual. About ten years before our story, he made a friendship with the prince of Kiev, called Vladimir. Basil needed help, because some rivals were fighting him. Vladimir sent him six thousand soldiers from the north. In return, Basil agreed that his sister Anna could marry Vladimir. And Vladimir agreed to become a Christian, and his people with him.

{img:byzantine-princess-anna-ship-leaving-for-kiev-sad-farewell-harbour}
This was a big decision for the princess Anna. In those days, the princesses of Constantinople almost never married foreign princes. Anna did not want to go to Kiev, so far away in the cold north. She cried when she said goodbye to her brothers. But she went. And in Kiev, she helped to build churches and to bring the faith and learning of Constantinople to the Rus. So she is remembered in Kiev as a good and wise princess.

--

The six thousand soldiers from the north stayed in Constantinople. They became the emperor's personal guard. They are called the Varangian Guard. They are big, strong men from the lands of the Rus and from Scandinavia. They carry great axes and wear long cloaks. They are completely loyal to the emperor. And the emperor trusts them more than anyone.

{img:varangian-guard-tall-norse-soldiers-axes-palace-gate-standing-guard}
The people of Constantinople have mixed feelings about the Varangians. Some of them find them frightening, because they are so big and so strange. Others find them funny, because they speak Greek badly and drink a lot of wine. Some people call them the emperor's wine-bags, behind their backs. But most people respect them. And when there is trouble in the city, the Varangians keep order calmly and firmly.

Eirene's neighbour Bjorn is one of these guards. He has been in Constantinople for about five years now. He says the city still amazes him, every single day.

Even though Basil is away so much, the people of Constantinople feel his presence. His name is said in the prayers in every church. His face is on every gold coin. When he comes home from his campaigns, there are great processions through the city. He rides through the Golden Gate and along the Mese, all the way to Hagia Sophia. The people line the streets and cheer. And then, a few weeks later, he is gone again, back to his army.

Basil's younger brother, Constantine, is very different from him. Constantine loves feasts and music and hunting. He does not like hard work or wars. So he stays in the palace in Constantinople, and enjoys himself. He is officially emperor too, together with Basil. But everyone knows that Basil is the one who really rules. The two brothers get along well enough, as long as each one does what he likes best.
