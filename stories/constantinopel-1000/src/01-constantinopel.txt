Tonight we travel a thousand years back in time. We go to a great city on the edge of two worlds, where Europe meets Asia. The year is 1000. And the city is called Constantinople.

--

It is early morning in late spring, and the sky is turning pale gold. The city lies on a hilly point of land, with water on three sides. To the north is a long, sheltered harbour called the Golden Horn. To the east is a narrow strait of blue water, the Bosphorus. And to the south is a wide sea, the Sea of Marmara. Ships are already moving on the water, with their sails catching the first light.

High above the rooftops rises an enormous dome, shining softly in the dawn. It is the great church of Hagia Sophia, the church of Holy Wisdom. Around it, there are palaces and churches, columns and statues, gardens and markets. There are thousands of houses with red-tiled roofs, climbing up the hills. Church bells begin to ring, one after another, all over the city. And the smell of fresh bread drifts through the narrow streets.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

In the year 1000, Constantinople is the biggest and richest city in all of Europe. Perhaps three or four hundred thousand people live here. That is many times more than any city in western Europe at that time. Travellers who come here are amazed. They have never seen so many churches, so many palaces, so much gold and marble. They call it the Queen of Cities.

Constantinople is the capital of an empire that we call the Byzantine Empire. But the people who live there do not call it that. They call themselves Romans. They believe their empire is the old Roman Empire, still going on, a thousand years after Julius Caesar. And in a way, they are right. Their emperor is the heir of the Roman emperors. Their laws are Roman laws. But they speak Greek, and they are Christians.

{img:byzantine-bakery-family-ovens-loaves-father-mother-children-morning}
Tonight we will spend some time in this great city. We will meet a family of bakers, who live in a busy neighbourhood near the main street. We will meet the father, who gets up before dawn to bake the bread. We will meet the mother, who sells it at the counter and keeps the accounts. We will meet their daughter, who loves the shining mosaics in the churches. And we will meet their son, who dreams of the chariot races.

--

We will visit the great church of Hagia Sophia and the markets full of silk and spices. We will look into the emperor's palace, with its golden throne and mechanical lions. We will meet a tall guardsman from the far north, who has come to serve the emperor. We will watch a chariot race in the great Hippodrome. And we will celebrate Easter, the most important feast of the year.

{img:golden-horn-harbour-1000ad-ships-sails-seagulls-city-hills-morning}
So let us begin on the shore of the Golden Horn, on a spring morning in the year 1000. The water is calm and silver. Fishing boats are coming in with their night's catch. Merchant ships are being unloaded at the quays. Seagulls are crying and circling above the masts. And up the hill from the harbour, in a little street off the main road, a baker is pulling the first loaves of the day out of his oven.

Along the shore, the fishermen are pulling their small boats up onto the pebbles. Their wives come down with baskets to collect the fish. A little boy helps his father fold the wet nets. An old man sits on an upturned boat, mending a hole in a net with a wooden needle. He hums an old hymn as he works. The sun climbs slowly over the hills of Asia, across the water.

The baker's name is Nikolaos. He has been working since the middle of the night. His arms are white with flour, and his face is red from the heat of the oven. He lays the first loaves on a wooden shelf to cool. Then he steps outside for a moment, to breathe the fresh morning air. He looks up at the great dome on the hill, glowing in the dawn. And he makes the sign of the cross, as he does every morning.
