The most important feast of the year in Constantinople is Easter. It is a time of great joy, after a long time of quiet preparation.

For forty days before Easter, people keep a fast called Lent. They do not eat meat, eggs, cheese or butter. They eat bread and vegetables, beans and olives, and a little fish on some days. The churches are quiet and plain. People pray more and go to church more often. They give food and money to the poor.

--

In the last week before Easter, called Holy Week, there are services in all the churches every day. On Thursday, people remember the last supper of Christ. On Friday, they remember his death. The churches are dark and sad. The icons are covered. People walk in slow processions through the streets, carrying candles. The bells do not ring. The whole city is quiet and solemn.

{img:bakery-preparing-easter-bread-sweet-dough-red-eggs-family-helping}
In the bakery, Nikolaos is very busy in Holy Week. He is preparing the special Easter bread. It is a sweet bread, made with eggs and butter and a little honey, braided into a ring. The family helps to dye eggs bright red, by boiling them with onion skins and special roots. The red colour means joy and new life. The eggs will be pressed into the Easter bread, and shared with friends.

--

On Saturday night, the whole family goes to church, in their best clothes. Zoe wears her blue silk ribbon with the golden birds. Grandmother Anna wears her best headscarf. Everyone carries an unlit candle. The church is completely dark and silent. The people stand and wait, in the darkness.

{img:midnight-easter-church-candles-lit-one-by-one-faces-glowing-joy}
Then, at midnight, the priest comes out from behind the screen of icons, holding a single lit candle. He calls out: Christ is risen! And the people answer: He is truly risen! The priest lights the candles of the people nearest to him. They light the candles of the people next to them. And so the flame passes from candle to candle, until the whole church is full of light. The bells begin to ring, loud and joyful. Everyone is smiling and embracing.

--

After the service, the family walks home through the streets, carrying their lit candles. The whole city is full of little flames, moving through the dark streets. People call out to each other: Christ is risen! And the answer comes back: He is truly risen! At home, they use the Easter flame to light the lamp in front of the icons. Then they break the fast, and eat the Easter bread and the red eggs, and a little cheese and meat.

{img:family-easter-meal-red-eggs-sweet-bread-table-lamplight-night-joy}
The children play a game with the red eggs. Each one holds an egg, and they knock them together, end to end. The one whose egg does not crack is the winner. Leon wins against Zoe, and then Zoe wins against her father. Grandmother Anna's egg beats everyone. She laughs and says it is because she has been playing this game for seventy years. Bjorn comes by with a gift of wine, and they give him bread and red eggs. He knocks eggs with Leon, and loses, and laughs. And the joyful feast goes on until the sky begins to grow light.

On Easter Monday, the whole city celebrates. There are processions with icons and banners, and music and dancing in the streets. Families visit each other, carrying red eggs and Easter bread. The emperor gives gifts to the poor and to his officials. And everyone greets each other with the joyful Easter words, again and again, all day long.

In the days after Easter, Nikolaos bakes extra Easter bread for the poor of the neighbourhood. Eirene and the children carry it to the little church at the end of the street. The priest shares it out among the poor families. Zoe gives the first loaf to an old widow who lives alone. The old woman smiles and blesses her. And Zoe walks home feeling warm inside.
