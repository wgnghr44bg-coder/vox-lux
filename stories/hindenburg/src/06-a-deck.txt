Let us follow the passengers up the stairs.

The passenger rooms are not in the gondola at all. They are inside the body of the ship itself, in the middle of the hull, on two decks, one above the other. The upper deck is called A deck, and the lower one is called B deck. It is as if a small ocean liner had been built inside a silver whale.

At the top of the staircase, there is a wide corridor with a soft carpet, and a faint scent of flowers.

--

Down the middle of A deck runs the corridor, and on each side of it are the cabins.

Each cabin is small and neat, like a cabin on a night train. There are two narrow beds, one above the other, with clean white sheets and a warm blanket. There is a little folding table, a wardrobe with a mirror on the door, and a basin that folds out of the wall, with hot and cold running water.

There are no windows in the cabins. But a little lamp glows by every pillow, and the walls are painted in soft colours, cream and pale green and grey.

--

{img:passenger-unpacking-in-cabin}
The passengers open their doors and step inside, one after another.

A woman puts her hat on the shelf and sits for a moment on the lower bed, trying the mattress. A man hangs up his overcoat and takes out a book from his bag. Somewhere along the corridor, a child laughs, and a steward answers in a low voice.

Everything on board is made of the lightest materials. The beds are aluminium, and the walls are thin, and even the furniture has been designed to weigh as little as possible. But nothing looks cheap. Everything has been chosen with care, like the fittings in a good hotel.

--

On the left side of the corridor is the dining room.

It is a long, bright room with a row of tall windows that slope outwards at the bottom, so that the passengers can look down at the world as they eat. The tables are covered with white linen, and each one has a small vase with a flower in it. The chairs are made of aluminium, covered in soft cloth, and the china on the table is edged with a thin line of gold, and marked with the ship's name.

On the walls, the designers have hung pictures of lands the airship has flown over, painted lightly in pale colours.

--

{img:passenger-lounge-evening-light}
On the right side of the corridor is the lounge.

It has soft chairs, small round tables, and curtains of pale silk. The windows are as big as those in the dining room, and in the evening, the light that comes through them is rosy and gold. In the first season, there was a little piano here, covered in pigskin, but now there is just a clear space, where people can sit and talk.

Behind the lounge is a writing room, with desks and writing paper printed with the picture of the ship, where passengers can write letters and postcards. The mail is collected before they land.

--

{img:promenade-windows-looking-down}
Along both sides of the ship, outside the dining room and the lounge, there is a long, narrow promenade.

It is a kind of corridor with a sloping row of big windows, and some of them can be opened. The passengers can walk up and down, or sit on folding chairs, and watch the land go by below. In the evening the light slides along the walls, and the air from the open windows is cool and smells of fields.

There is always someone standing here. Some people stay for an hour at a time, hardly speaking, just watching the world pass beneath them.

--

Everywhere, the stewards move quietly about.

They wear white jackets, and they carry trays with cups and glasses, and they know every passenger by name. They have been trained in some of the finest hotels in Germany, and they treat every guest as if he or she were the only one.

They never hurry, but they are always there. If a passenger rings the little bell in his cabin, a steward arrives within a minute, and asks, in a soft voice, what he may bring.

--

{img:evening-view-from-promenade-over-germany}
Now the ship has risen, and the passengers go to the windows, one by one.

Below them, Germany is spread out in the evening: the green and brown squares of the fields, the winding silver line of the river, the small white villages, and the church spires casting long shadows. A train moves slowly along a track, trailing a little thread of steam, and from this height it looks like a toy.

Most of the passengers have flown before, but there are always a few who have not, and they stand with their faces to the glass, and say nothing at all.
