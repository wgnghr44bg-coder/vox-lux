So let us go back, one last time, to the evening when the journey began.

Imagine the airfield at Frankfurt, in the soft light of a May evening. The grass is green, and the pines are dark against the sky. The passengers are walking slowly across the field, in their hats and coats, talking in low voices. The ground crew stand with the ropes in their hands. And above them, the great silver ship hangs motionless, glowing in the last of the sun.

Somewhere a church bell is ringing, far away.

--

The doors are closed. The engines begin to hum. And the ship rises, slowly and without any effort, into the pale sky, and turns towards the west.

She will cross the sea in the night. She will carry her passengers over the dark water, under the stars, past the sleeping coast of Ireland and the cold edge of Newfoundland, to the green hills of America. And for a while, in the long quiet hours, everything on board will be peaceful.

That is where we will leave her tonight, floating above the clouds in the evening light, with the engines humming softly.
