At about seven years old, the son of an Athenian citizen goes to school.

There are no free schools paid for by the city. Parents pay small fees to private teachers, who teach in rooms or in the open air. Rich families send their sons for many years. Poor families send them for a few years, or not at all. Most girls do not go to school. They learn at home.

--

Every morning, the boy walks to school with an old slave who looks after him. The slave carries his things, makes sure he behaves, and even sits with him during lessons. Our word for teaching, pedagogy, comes from the name of this slave.

The boy has three teachers.

{img:paidagogos-old-slave-walking-boy-to-school-street-morning}
The first teaches him to read, write and count. The boy writes on a wooden board covered with wax, using a pointed stick. He can rub out his mistakes with the flat end. He learns his letters, then words, then long pieces of poetry, especially Homer. He learns hundreds of lines by heart. Every educated Athenian knows much of Homer by heart.

--

The second teacher teaches him music. He learns to play the lyre, a small harp made with a tortoise shell, and to sing. The Greeks believe that music shapes a person's character and makes the soul gentle.

The third teacher teaches him sport at the wrestling school. Wrestling, running, jumping, throwing the discus and the javelin.

The boys exercise naked, as Greek athletes always do. They rub their bodies with olive oil, and afterwards scrape it off with a curved metal tool. The Greeks believe a strong, healthy body is just as important as a good mind.

--

Older boys and men go to the gymnasium.

There are three great gymnasiums in Athens, outside the city walls, in pleasant places with trees and streams. They have running tracks and wrestling grounds, baths, and shady paths under plane trees and olive trees.

{img:academy-gymnasium-plane-trees-olive-grove-running-track-shade}
Men go there in the afternoon to exercise, wash and talk. Thinkers go there too, to talk with the young men and teach them. One of these places is called the Academy. Later, Plato will start his famous school there, and all the academies in the world will be named after it.

--

At eighteen, a young Athenian begins two years of training as a soldier. He guards the borders of Attica, in little forts in the mountains. After that he is a full citizen. He can vote in the assembly and serve in the army as a foot soldier, with a bronze helmet, a big round shield and a spear. If he is poor, he serves as a rower in the navy.

{img:young-men-oath-temple-ephebes-shields-spears-morning-solemn}
The young men swear an oath in a temple on the slope of the Acropolis. They promise never to disgrace their weapons, and never to leave a friend alone in battle. They promise to leave their homeland not smaller, but greater and better than they found it. In 432, the young men say these words with special seriousness. They know that soon, they may have to keep them.
