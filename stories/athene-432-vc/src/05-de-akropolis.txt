Let us climb up to the Acropolis on a summer morning in the year 432 before Christ.

The Acropolis is a great flat-topped rock in the middle of the city, about a hundred and fifty metres high. It has cliffs on every side except the west. People have lived and prayed here for thousands of years. Long ago it was the fortress of the kings of Athens. Now it is the home of the gods.

--

The only way up is from the west, by a long zigzag ramp. The great processions use it on festival days.

At the top of the ramp stands the new gateway to the Acropolis. In 432 it is still being built, of white marble. It looks like the front of a temple, with tall columns and five doorways. The middle doorway is wide enough for the processions to pass through, with the animals for the sacrifice.

{img:propylaea-marble-columns-steps-workers-scaffolding-sunlight}
To the right of the gateway, a few years later, a small and lovely temple will be built to Athena of Victory.

--

Then you pass through the gateway, out onto the top of the rock. And there, in front of you, on the highest point, shining white and gold and coloured in the sunshine, stands the Parthenon. It is the temple of Athena the Maiden.

But before we go there, let us look around.

Just past the gate stands a huge bronze statue of Athena, nine metres tall, with a spear, a shield and a helmet. Sailors coming toward Athens can see the gleam of her spear tip from far out at sea. For them, it is the first sign that they are nearly home.

--

To the left stands an old temple, the holiest one on the rock. The Persians burned it, and it has been partly repaired. Inside is a very old wooden statue of Athena. The Athenians believe it fell from heaven. Every four years, the women of Athens weave a new robe for it.

Near it grows a sacred olive tree. The Athenians tell a story about it. Long ago, Athena and Poseidon, the god of the sea, both wanted to be the god of this city. Poseidon struck the rock with his trident, and a spring of salt water came out. Athena gave the people an olive tree. The people chose the olive tree, and Athena became their goddess.

{img:sacred-olive-tree-acropolis-old-temple-marble-sunlight}
When the Persians burned the Acropolis, the story goes, the sacred olive tree burned too. But the very next day, a new green shoot had grown from the black stump. Then the Athenians knew their city would live. They still tell that story whenever times look dark.

--

The top of the Acropolis is crowded. There are altars and shrines everywhere, and hundreds of statues given to the gods over the years. There are statues of gods and heroes, of athletes and horsemen and young women. There are stones carved with the laws of the city.

It is not quiet and empty like the ruins we see today. It is busy and colourful, full of priests and visitors and workmen, with smoke rising from the altars.

{img:acropolis-crowded-statues-altars-smoke-priests-visitors-morning}
From the edge of the rock, you can see the whole of Athens spread out below. The red roofs and the market, the theatre and the hills. The olive groves and the long walls running down to the shining sea. On a clear day, everything looks safe and calm from up here. You would never guess how soon that will change.
