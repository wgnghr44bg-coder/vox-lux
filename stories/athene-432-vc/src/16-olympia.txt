Every four years, in the middle of summer, Greeks from every city travel to a quiet valley in the west of Greece, beside a river, among pine trees and olive groves. The place is called Olympia.

Olympia is not a city. It is a holy place of Zeus, the king of the gods. And every four years, the Olympic Games are held there in his honour.

--

The Games are very old. The Greeks believe the first ones were held more than three hundred years before our story.

Before the Games, messengers travel all over Greece to announce a sacred truce. All wars between Greek cities must stop, so that athletes and visitors can travel safely to Olympia and home again.

{img:herald-announcing-sacred-truce-town-square-crowd-listening}
And the next Games are this very summer, in 432. Thousands of people come. Athletes and trainers, families and merchants, poets and ambassadors. They camp in tents by the river in the summer heat. Athenians and Spartans walk past each other among the tents. For now, the truce holds. But everyone knows that when the Games are over, the truce will end too.

--

In the middle of Olympia is a sacred grove with temples, altars and hundreds of statues of winning athletes.

There is the great temple of Zeus. And inside it, there will soon be one of the Seven Wonders of the World. It is a huge statue of Zeus on his throne, more than twelve metres high, made of gold and ivory. The sculptor is Phidias, the same man who made the Athena in the Parthenon. He is working on it in these years, in a workshop at Olympia. Archaeologists have found that workshop. They even found a little cup with his name scratched on the bottom: I belong to Phidias.

The statue is so big, the Greeks said, that if Zeus stood up, he would go through the roof.

--

The Games last five days. There are foot races in the stadium, a long running track with grassy banks where the crowd sits. The most famous race is one length of the track. The winner of that race gives his name to the whole four years until the next Games.

There are longer races, and even a race in armour. There is wrestling and boxing. There is the pentathlon, five events in one. And there are horse races and chariot races, with four horses thundering around the track.

{img:olympia-stadium-runners-starting-line-crowd-banks-sun}
Only free Greek men may compete. Married women may not even watch. But unmarried girls have their own games at Olympia, with races in honour of Hera, queen of the gods.

--

The prize for an Olympic victory is a simple crown of olive leaves, cut from the sacred olive tree behind the temple of Zeus.

But the glory is enormous. The winner's name is announced to all of Greece. His home city gives him a hero's welcome. Poets write songs about him. In Athens, an Olympic winner gets free meals from the city for the rest of his life.

{img:olive-wreath-crowning-victor-temple-steps-crowd-cheering}
Today, the ruins of Olympia still lie in their quiet valley among the pines. And before each modern Olympic Games, the Olympic flame is lit there from the rays of the sun, with a curved mirror. Then runners carry it all over the world.
