What do the Athenians wear in 432 before Christ?

Their clothes are very simple. Almost every garment is just a big rectangle of cloth, woven at home on the family loom. It is draped around the body and held with pins and a belt. There is very little sewing, and no buttons at all.

--

Men wear a tunic made of two pieces of cloth, pinned at the shoulders and belted at the waist. Workmen and soldiers wear a short one, to the knees, so they can move freely. Older and richer men wear a long one, to the ankles.

Over it goes a big woollen cloak, wrapped around the body and over the left shoulder. Wearing it gracefully, so it hangs in nice folds, is the sign of a gentleman. Young horsemen wear a short cloak pinned at one shoulder.

Women wear a long, light dress of linen, falling to the feet, or a heavier one of wool, folded over at the top and pinned at the shoulders. When they go out, they pull a cloak over their heads like a veil.

--

The clothes are often brightly coloured. Saffron yellow, red, green and blue, with patterned borders. The finest purple dye is made from sea snails. It is so costly that only the rich can wear it.

At home, and often in the street, people go barefoot. Otherwise they wear leather sandals, or boots for travelling. In the sun they wear a wide hat of felt or straw.

{img:sandal-maker-workshop-leather-sandals-hanging-street-front}
In 432, men wear their hair short and their beards neatly trimmed. Women wear their hair long and tied up in elegant styles, with ribbons and scarves. They use make-up too. White powder to make their skin pale, and red from plants for their cheeks and lips.

--

Rich women wear fine jewellery of gold and silver. Earrings, necklaces and bracelets, with tiny flowers and animals. They carry fans, and little bottles of perfume.

Everyone smells of olive oil and perfume. After bathing, and before parties, Athenians rub scented oil into their skin and hair. There are perfume shops in the Agora where people meet to gossip.

{img:perfume-shop-agora-small-bottles-oils-customers-chatting}
For festivals and parties, people wear wreaths of leaves and flowers, of myrtle, ivy, violets and roses. A famous poet once called Athens violet-crowned. Perhaps he meant the purple hills at sunset. Or perhaps he meant the wreaths of violets its people wore, on happy days, when nobody yet thought of war.
