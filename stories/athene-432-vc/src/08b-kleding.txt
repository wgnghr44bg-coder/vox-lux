What do the Athenians wear, in 432 before Christ?

Their clothes are very simple. Almost every garment is just a large rectangle of cloth, woven at home, on the family loom, of wool, or linen, and draped around the body, and held with pins, or brooches, and a belt. There is very little sewing, and no buttons.

--

Men wear a chiton, a tunic, made of two rectangles of cloth, sewn at the sides, and pinned at the shoulders, and belted at the waist. Workmen, and slaves, and soldiers, wear a short chiton, to the knees, sometimes leaving one shoulder bare, to be free to move. Older men, and rich men, wear a long chiton, to the ankles.

Over it, a himation, a large cloak, of wool, wrapped around the body, over the left shoulder, and under the right arm. Wearing a himation gracefully, so that it hangs in elegant folds, is a sign of a gentleman. Young horsemen wear a short cloak, called a chlamys, pinned at the right shoulder.

Women wear a long chiton, of linen, fine and light, pleated, falling to the feet, or a peplos, of wool, heavier, folded over at the top, and pinned at the shoulders. And over it, a himation, which they can pull over their heads, as a veil, when they go out.

--

The clothes are often brightly coloured: saffron yellow, and red, and purple, and green, and blue, with patterned borders, woven in. The finest purple dye, made from sea snails, is very expensive, and worn only by the rich.

People go barefoot at home, and often in the street. Or they wear sandals, of leather, or boots, for travelling, or riding. When they travel, in the sun, they wear a wide-brimmed hat of felt, or straw, called a petasos.

{img:sandal-maker-workshop-leather-sandals-hanging-street-front}
Men wear their hair short, in 432, and have beards, neatly trimmed. Young men, before their beards grow, are thought the most beautiful. Women wear their hair long, tied up, in elegant styles, with ribbons, and bands, and nets, and scarves. They use make-up: white lead, to make their skin pale, and red, made from plants, for their cheeks and lips.

--

Rich women wear jewellery: earrings, and necklaces, and bracelets, and rings, of gold and silver, beautifully made, with tiny figures, and flowers, and animals. And they carry fans, and parasols, and little bottles of perfume, made of glass, or pottery.

Everyone smells of olive oil, and perfume. The Athenians rub perfumed oil on their skin, and hair, after bathing, and before parties. There are perfume shops in the Agora, where people meet to gossip.

{img:perfume-shop-agora-small-bottles-oils-customers-chatting}
And for festivals, and parties, people wear wreaths of flowers, and leaves: myrtle, and ivy, and violets, and roses. Athens is called violet-crowned Athens, by the poet Pindar, perhaps because of the purple colour of the hills around it at sunset, or because of the violet wreaths its people wore.
