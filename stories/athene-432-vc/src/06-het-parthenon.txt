In 432 before Christ, the Parthenon is finished at last.

It has taken fifteen years. The building itself was ready six years ago. But the sculptors have kept working on the great statues for the triangles at each end of the roof. Now, in 432, the last of them are in place.

--

The Parthenon is made almost entirely of white marble. The marble comes from a mountain about sixteen kilometres away. It was dragged down on wooden sledges, carried to the city on ox carts, and hauled up the ramp. More than twenty thousand tons of it.

The temple is about seventy metres long and thirty metres wide. All around it stand forty-six tall columns. Each column is made of round blocks of marble stacked on top of each other, with shallow grooves running up and down.

{img:column-drums-marble-masons-fluting-chisels-workshop-acropolis}
The builders did something very clever. In the Parthenon there are almost no straight lines. The floor curves up a tiny bit in the middle. The columns lean a tiny bit inward, and swell a little in the middle. You cannot see these curves. But they make the building look perfectly straight and balanced and alive, instead of stiff and heavy.

--

And the marble is not plain white, as it looks today.

In 432, the statues and parts of the building are painted in bright colours. Red and blue, green and gold. The figures have coloured clothes, painted eyes and golden weapons. The whole temple glows in the bright Greek sun.

At the east end, over the main door, the statues show the birth of Athena. The story says she sprang fully grown, in armour, from the head of her father Zeus. At the west end, they show the contest between Athena and Poseidon.

Around the top of the outside walls there are ninety-two square carved panels. They show great battles from old stories. Gods fight giants, and Greeks fight centaurs, which are half man and half horse.

--

Higher up, inside the row of columns, a long band of carving runs all the way around the temple walls. It is a hundred and sixty metres long. It shows a great procession, the festival of Athena. There are horsemen and chariots, young men and old men, young women and musicians, all moving toward the gods, who sit waiting at the end.

It shows the people of Athens themselves, honouring their goddess. Perhaps for the first time ever, ordinary people are shown on a temple, beside the gods.

{img:frieze-horsemen-procession-marble-relief-young-riders-flowing}
The horses and young riders, with their cloaks blowing in the wind, are among the most beautiful carvings ever made.

--

And inside the temple, in the great dim hall, stands the statue of Athena. It was made by the sculptor Phidias, a friend of Pericles.

It is twelve metres tall, almost touching the roof. It is made of a wooden frame, covered with ivory for the skin and gold for the clothes. More than a ton of gold. She wears a helmet, with a shield beside her. In her right hand she holds a little winged figure of Victory, as tall as a man.

{img:chryselephantine-athena-statue-dim-temple-hall-gold-ivory-pool}
In front of her lies a shallow pool of water. It reflects the light from the door onto the gold and ivory, and it keeps the air damp, so the ivory does not crack.

The Athenians come to look at her, and bow, and pray. Pericles has told the assembly something important. The gold of her robe can be taken off and melted down to pay for a war, if the city ever needs it. Everyone hopes that day will never come. But Pericles does not say things without a reason.
