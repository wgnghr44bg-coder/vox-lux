In 432 before Christ, Socrates is about thirty-seven years old.

His father was a stonemason and his mother a midwife. As a young man, he learned to work stone himself. But he has given up that work. Now he spends every day talking.

--

By Greek standards, he is very ugly. The Greeks love beauty, and Socrates has a snub nose, thick lips, bulging eyes and a big belly. His friends say he walks like a pelican, looking from side to side. He goes barefoot summer and winter, and always wears the same old cloak. He is poor, and he does not care.

But something about him draws people in. His friends say he is like the little ugly statues sold in the market. You open them up, and inside there are small golden figures of the gods.

Socrates goes everywhere and talks with everyone. Politicians and poets, craftsmen and generals, young and old, rich and poor. And he asks them questions.

--

He asks about things they think they know. What is courage? What is justice? What is a good life? What is friendship? Can goodness be taught?

When they answer, he asks more questions, gently and politely. Step by step, he shows them that they do not really know what they thought they knew. Their answers are confused, or contradict each other. Then he says: I do not know either. Let us look for the answer together.

{img:philosopher-walking-with-young-men-path-plane-trees-river}
This way of teaching by asking questions is still used by teachers all over the world today.

--

Socrates says he knows only one thing: that he knows nothing. And perhaps that makes him a little wiser than people who think they know things they do not.

He says the most important thing in life is not money or fame or power. It is to look after your soul, and try to become a good person. And he says: a life without asking questions is not worth living.

{img:socrates-standing-still-thinking-dawn-soldiers-camp-watching}
Socrates is also a brave soldier. And very soon, he will march off to war in the north of Greece. His friends will later tell how he walked barefoot over the ice in a bitter winter. Once, they say, he stood still, thinking about a problem, from one dawn to the next, while the other soldiers stared at him. And in a battle, he saved the life of a young friend.

--

Socrates never writes anything down. He believes wisdom is found in talking, between living people, not in books. Everything we know about him comes from his friends and students, above all Plato.

He has a wife called Xanthippe. The stories say she has a sharp temper, and scolds him because he earns no money and is always out talking. Socrates jokes that if he can live with her, he can get along with anyone.

Many young men love him and follow him around. Their fathers are sometimes less pleased. Because Socrates teaches the young to question everything, even the laws and the gods of their city.

--

And in the end, that will cost him his life. Many years later, when he is seventy, the Athenians will put him on trial for it. The jury will condemn him.

He could escape, but he will refuse, out of respect for the laws of Athens. He will spend his last day talking calmly with his friends about the soul, and then die peacefully among them.

{img:quiet-evening-prison-room-friends-gathered-calm-light}
But in 432, all that is far away. He is a man in his prime, walking barefoot through the Agora in the morning sun, looking for someone to talk to.
