In the summer of 432, the peace of Greece is coming to an end.

For fifteen years, there has been a peace treaty between Athens and Sparta, the two great powers of Greece. But Sparta is afraid of Athens. It fears its growing power, its navy and its empire. And Athens has quarrelled with Corinth, a friend of Sparta, over two other cities.

--

Sparta is very different from Athens. It is a city of soldiers in the south of Greece, in a green valley below high mountains. Sparta has no walls. The Spartans say their soldiers are their walls. Every Spartan man is trained as a soldier from the age of seven, to be tough, brave and obedient. The Spartans do not build great temples or write plays. Their land is farmed for them by a whole people kept in a kind of slavery.

Spartan women are freer than Athenian women. They exercise and run, they own land, and they speak their minds.

In the summer of 432, Sparta's friends meet at Sparta. They accuse Athens of breaking the peace. And the Spartans vote that the peace is broken. The historian Thucydides says the real cause of the war was simple. Athens was growing stronger, and Sparta was afraid.

--

In Athens, Pericles persuades the assembly not to give in to Sparta.

He has a plan. Sparta has the best army on land. Athens has the best navy. So, Pericles says, if the Spartans march into Attica, the Athenians should not fight them on land. Everyone from the countryside should come inside the walls of Athens. Let the Spartans burn the farms. The city will be fed by its ships. The navy will raid the coasts of Sparta's lands. And in the end, the Spartans will grow tired, and make peace.

{img:country-people-moving-into-city-carts-belongings-long-walls}
In the spring of 431, the war begins. The Spartans march into Attica. And the country people sadly leave their farms and homes and little temples. They bring their families and their furniture into the city, and even the wooden doors of their houses. They send their sheep and cattle across the water to an island. They camp wherever they can, in temples, between the long walls and in the towers.

--

At the end of the first year of the war, Pericles gives a famous speech at the funeral of the Athenians who have fallen. Thucydides wrote it down, as well as he could remember it. It is one of the most famous speeches in history.

In it, Pericles talks about Athens and what it means. Power belongs not to a few, but to the many. Everyone is equal before the law. People are chosen for public work because of their ability, not their wealth or their family. Athenians live freely and do not mind if their neighbours enjoy themselves in their own way. They love beauty and wisdom. Athens, he says again, is the school of Greece.

{img:funeral-oration-pericles-speaking-crowd-cypress-coffins-winter}
People who believe in freedom and democracy have quoted these words ever since.

--

Then, in the second summer of the war, a terrible sickness comes to Athens. Perhaps it arrived on a ship. It spreads quickly through the crowded city, full of people from the countryside.

We will not describe it. It is enough to say that very many people were lost, perhaps a quarter or a third of the people of Athens. Thucydides himself fell ill and recovered. He wrote down exactly what he saw, so that people would know it if it ever came again.

In the autumn of 429, Pericles himself fell ill and died. His two older sons had died before him. His son with Aspasia survived.

--

The war between Athens and Sparta lasted, with a few short pauses, for twenty-seven years. And in the end, Athens lost. Its fleet was destroyed, its walls were pulled down and its empire was gone.

But Athens survived. A year later, its democracy came back. And its great age of art and thought went on for many years. Plato and Aristotle were still to come.

{img:athens-rebuilding-walls-citizens-working-hopeful-morning}
And the Parthenon still stood on the Acropolis, white and gold above the city, as it does today.
