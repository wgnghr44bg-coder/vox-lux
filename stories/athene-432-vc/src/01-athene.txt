Tonight we are going nearly two thousand five hundred years back, to the city of Athens in ancient Greece, in the year 432 before Christ.

--

On a great rock above the city, a new temple has just been finished. It is made of shining white marble, and it is the most beautiful building the Greeks have ever made. Below it, in the streets and the market, in the theatre and the assembly, the people of Athens are talking and arguing and voting. They are writing plays and asking questions that people will still be asking two and a half thousand years later.

It is the golden age of Athens. Tonight we will spend a little time there, in what will turn out to be its last peaceful summer.

If you enjoy these slow evenings in the distant past, you are welcome to subscribe to the channel, and come back for another one.

--

{img:greek-coast-blue-sea-islands-olive-hills-summer-haze}
Let us picture Greece as it is in 432 before Christ.

It is a land of mountains and sea. Steep, rocky mountains rise everywhere, covered with pine trees and wild herbs like thyme and sage. In the summer heat, the air smells of them. Between the mountains lie little valleys with fields, olive groves and vineyards. And the sea is never far away, deep blue and clear, scattered with islands.

--

The Greeks do not live in one country with one king. They live in hundreds of small cities, each with its own government, its own laws and its own army. Each city, with the land around it, is called a polis. Some are big, like Athens and Sparta. Some are no bigger than a village on a small island.

They often quarrel and fight each other. But they all speak Greek and worship the same gods. They all know the same old poems about the war at Troy. And every four years, they all come together at Olympia for the Olympic Games.

Greek cities are scattered all around the Mediterranean, wherever Greek sailors have settled. A wise man called Plato will later say that the Greeks live around the sea like frogs around a pond.

--

And of all the Greek cities in 432, the richest, the strongest and the most brilliant is Athens.

Athens leads a great alliance of cities around the Aegean Sea. Every year, they pay it silver in return for protection. Athens has the greatest navy in the world, three hundred warships. It has silver mines in the south of its land. And it has a great port full of ships and goods from all over the Mediterranean.

{img:piraeus-harbour-triremes-merchant-ships-warehouses-sunlight}
And Athens has something new, something no other people has tried in quite this way before. It is called democracy. That means: rule by the people.

--

In Athens, ordinary citizens meet on a hill every few days. Farmers, shoemakers, potters and sailors sit side by side. Together they vote on all the big questions. War or peace, laws and taxes, new buildings, and who will lead them.

It is not a perfect democracy. Women cannot vote. Slaves cannot vote. Foreigners cannot vote. Perhaps only one person in five or six has a say. But for its time, it is something remarkable.

{img:pnyx-hill-assembly-citizens-seated-speaker-platform-morning}
And under this democracy, Athens has become the most extraordinary city in the world. A city of builders and sculptors, poets and playwrights, thinkers and doctors. Some of their names are still known everywhere today. But across the mountains, in the city of Sparta, people are watching Athens grow. And they do not like what they see.
