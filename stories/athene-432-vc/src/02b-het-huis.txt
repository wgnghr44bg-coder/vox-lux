Let us step inside an ordinary Athenian house in 432 before Christ.

From the street there is not much to see. A plain white wall with a few small, high windows, and a wooden door. Beside the door stands a little stone pillar for the god Hermes, with a few flowers in front of it. The house turns its back on the street, and looks inward.

--

Through the door, a short passage leads to the heart of the house. It is a courtyard, open to the sky. In the middle there is an altar to Zeus, who protects the household, and perhaps a well that collects rain from the roofs. A vine may grow over a frame for shade, or there may be a fig tree and a few pots of herbs.

All the rooms open onto the courtyard. There is a kitchen with a hearth and a clay oven. There are storerooms with big jars of oil, wine and grain. There is a room for weaving, with a tall wooden loom. There are bedrooms. And near the front door there is a special dining room, where the husband entertains his friends.

{img:greek-kitchen-hearth-clay-pots-oven-woman-cooking-light}
Many houses have a second floor, reached by a wooden ladder or stairs. The women and children often live and sleep up there, away from visitors.

--

The rooms are small and simple. There are wooden couches, which are beds at night and seats by day. There are stools, low tables and wooden chests for clothes. There are baskets of wool, and pottery of every kind, for water, oil and wine.

The walls are plastered, sometimes painted red or white. The floors are beaten earth, or in better houses, small pebbles set in patterns. There is no glass in the windows, only wooden shutters. In winter the house is cold. It is heated only by little pans of glowing charcoal, carried from room to room.

{img:greek-bedroom-wooden-couch-blankets-chest-lamp-shutters}
At night the house is lit by little clay lamps filled with olive oil. They give a small, soft, flickering light. People go to bed early, and get up with the sun.

--

It is a simple house. But it is home. The family and their slaves live here together, perhaps with a dog, a few hens, and even a cat, which the Greeks are just beginning to keep as pets. They all live around the courtyard, under the bright Greek sky. They have no idea yet how crowded the city will soon become, when people from the countryside come flooding in behind the walls.
