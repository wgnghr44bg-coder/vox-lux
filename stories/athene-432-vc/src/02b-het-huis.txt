Let us step inside an ordinary Athenian house, in 432 before Christ.

From the street, there is not much to see: a plain wall, of mud brick, whitewashed, with a few small, high windows, and a wooden door. Beside the door, there is the herm, the little stone pillar of the god Hermes, with a few flowers laid in front of it. The house turns its back on the street, and looks inward.

--

Through the door, there is a short passage, and then the heart of the house: a courtyard, open to the sky, paved with stones, or beaten earth. In the middle, there is an altar, to Zeus, the protector of the household, and perhaps a well, or a cistern, which collects the rain from the roofs. There may be a vine, growing over a trellis, for shade, or a fig tree, or a few pots of herbs and flowers.

All around the courtyard, there are the rooms of the house, opening onto it: the kitchen, with its hearth, and its clay oven. The storerooms, with great jars of oil, and wine, and grain. A workroom, where the weaving is done, on the great upright loom. The bedrooms. And the andron, the men's dining room, near the street door, where the husband entertains his friends.

{img:greek-kitchen-hearth-clay-pots-oven-woman-cooking-light}
Upstairs, in many houses, there is a second floor, reached by a wooden ladder, or stairs, with more rooms, where the women and children often live and sleep, away from visitors.

--

The rooms are small, and simply furnished. There are wooden couches, which are beds at night, and seats in the day. Wooden chairs and stools. Low tables. Wooden chests, for clothes and blankets. Baskets, for wool. Pottery of every kind: jars for water, and oil, and wine, and cups, and bowls, and plates, and lamps.

The walls are plastered, and sometimes painted, in plain colours, red, or white. The floors are of beaten earth, or in better houses, of pebbles, set in mortar, in patterns. There is no glass in the windows, only wooden shutters. In winter, the house is cold, and heated only by little charcoal braziers, carried from room to room.

{img:greek-bedroom-wooden-couch-blankets-chest-lamp-shutters}
At night, the house is lit by little clay lamps, filled with olive oil, with a wick, which give a soft, small, flickering light. People go to bed early, and get up with the sun.

--

It is a simple house. But it is home: the family, and its slaves, and its animals, a dog, perhaps, and a few hens, and a cat, which the Greeks are just beginning to keep as pets, living together, around the courtyard, under the bright Greek sky.
