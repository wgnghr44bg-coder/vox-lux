Athens is not only a city. It is a whole little country, called Attica: a triangle of land, about the size of a small English county, or a Dutch province, jutting out into the sea, in the south east of mainland Greece.

Attica has mountains: Mount Parnes, in the north, and Mount Pentelikon, in the north east, where the white marble comes from, and Mount Hymettus, to the east of the city, famous for its honey, and the purple colour it turns at sunset. And between the mountains, there are plains, with olive groves, and fields of barley and wheat, and vineyards, and little villages.

--

Perhaps two hundred and fifty thousand, or three hundred thousand people live in Attica, in 432. About half of them live in the city of Athens, and its port, the Piraeus. The rest live in the country, in about a hundred and forty villages, called demes.

The city itself lies in the middle of the plain, about seven kilometres from the sea. It is surrounded by a great wall, of stone and mud brick. And from the city to the port of Piraeus, there are two long walls, running side by side, all the way to the sea, with a road between them, so that even if an enemy army comes into Attica, the city can always reach the sea, and its ships, and get food.

{img:long-walls-athens-to-piraeus-road-between-plain-sea}
In the middle of the city, there is a great rock, rising steeply out of the plain, with cliffs on every side, and a flat top: the Acropolis, the high city. On its top, there are the temples of the gods, and above all, the temple of Athena, the goddess of the city.

--

Around the Acropolis, the city spreads out, on every side: a great jumble of houses, and streets, and temples, and markets, and workshops, and gardens, and fountains, and graveyards, outside the walls.

The houses of ordinary people are small, and plain, built of mud bricks, on stone foundations, with roofs of red clay tiles, and whitewashed walls. The streets are narrow, and winding, and dusty in summer, and muddy in winter, and often smelly, because there are no drains, and people throw their rubbish into the street.

{img:narrow-athens-street-whitewashed-walls-tiles-donkey-shade}
Even the rich live in quite simple houses. The Athenians believe that private houses should be modest, and that the money of the city should be spent on beautiful public buildings, for everyone: temples, and theatres, and colonnades, and fountains.

--

The climate is hot and dry, in summer, with clear blue skies, day after day, and hardly any rain from May to September. The light is very bright, and very clear, and the shadows are sharp. In the afternoon, the cicadas sing in the olive trees, loud and endless.

In winter, it is cooler, with rain, and wind, and sometimes, for a few days, snow on the mountains, and even in the city.

{img:olive-grove-cicadas-midday-heat-light-shade-stone-wall}
The Athenians love their city, and their land. The great playwright Sophocles, who is about sixty-five in 432, will write a famous song, in one of his plays, about the beauty of Attica: about the white town of Colonus, near Athens, where he was born, where the nightingale sings in the green glades, among the ivy, and the narcissus and the crocus bloom, and the river never runs dry, and the olive trees grow, grey-green, which no enemy has ever been able to destroy.
