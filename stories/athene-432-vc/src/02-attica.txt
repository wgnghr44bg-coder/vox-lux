Athens is not just a city. It is a whole little country, called Attica. It is a triangle of land jutting out into the sea, about the size of a small Dutch province.

Attica has mountains. From one of them comes white marble. Another is famous for its honey, and for the purple colour it turns at sunset. Between the mountains lie plains with olive groves, fields of barley and little villages.

--

Perhaps two or three hundred thousand people live in Attica. About half of them live in the city of Athens and its port. The rest live in the countryside, in about a hundred and forty villages.

The city lies in the middle of the plain, about seven kilometres from the sea. A great wall surrounds it. And from the city to the port, two long walls run side by side, all the way to the sea, with a road between them. The idea is simple. Even if an enemy army comes into Attica, the city can always reach its ships, and get food. The Athenians hope they will never need those walls. Soon, they will find out whether they work.

{img:long-walls-athens-to-piraeus-road-between-plain-sea}
In the middle of the city rises a great rock with cliffs on every side and a flat top. It is called the Acropolis, which means the high city. On top stand the temples of the gods, and above all, the temple of Athena, the goddess of the city.

--

Around the Acropolis, the city spreads out in every direction. It is a great jumble of houses, streets, temples, markets, workshops and fountains.

Ordinary houses are small and plain. They are made of mud bricks on stone foundations, with red clay tiles on the roofs and whitewashed walls. The streets are narrow and winding. They are dusty in summer and muddy in winter. And they often smell, because there are no drains, and people throw their rubbish into the street.

{img:narrow-athens-street-whitewashed-walls-tiles-donkey-shade}
Even the rich live in quite simple houses. The Athenians believe that private homes should be modest. The city's money, they say, should go to beautiful buildings for everyone, like temples, theatres and fountains.

--

In summer, the weather is hot and dry, with clear blue skies, day after day. From May to September it hardly rains. The light is very bright and the shadows are sharp. In the afternoon, the cicadas sing in the olive trees, loud and endless.

In winter it is cooler, with rain and wind, and sometimes snow on the mountains.

{img:olive-grove-cicadas-midday-heat-light-shade-stone-wall}
The Athenians love their land. The great playwright Sophocles will one day write a famous song about it. He sings of the village where he was born, where the nightingale sings among the ivy, and the river never runs dry, and the grey-green olive trees grow, which no enemy has ever been able to destroy. When he writes those words, the Athenians will know just how precious that promise is.
