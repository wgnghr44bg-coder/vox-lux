The greatest festival of Athens is the Panathenaia. It is the festival of all the Athenians, held every summer on Athena's birthday.

Every year there is a small festival. And every four years there is the Great Panathenaia, which lasts about a week, with games, music, sacrifices and a great procession.

--

The games are like the Olympic Games, but with even more events. There are foot races, horse races and chariot races. There is wrestling and boxing. There are music contests, singing to the lyre and playing the flute. And there are contests for reciting Homer. Professional reciters compete to tell the old stories of Troy most beautifully.

There are boat races in the harbour. And there is a torch race at night. It is a relay, from an altar outside the city to the altar of Athena on the Acropolis. The runners pass burning torches from hand to hand. If the flame goes out, the team loses. So everyone watches the little flames bobbing through the dark streets, holding their breath.

{img:torch-relay-race-night-runners-flames-crowds-cheering}
There is even a dance in full armour, and a contest of strength and good looks between the tribes.

--

The prizes for the athletes are wonderful. They are big jars filled with olive oil from Athena's sacred olive trees. Each jar holds about forty litres. A winner of the chariot race might win a hundred and forty jars. That is a fortune.

The jars are painted. On one side Athena strides forward with her spear and shield. On the other side is a picture of the event, like runners or wrestlers. And the words: one of the prizes from Athens. Many of these jars are still in museums today.

On the last great day, Athena's birthday, comes the great procession.

--

At dawn, everyone gathers at the big double gate in the northwest of the city.

Then the procession moves off along a broad road that crosses the market and climbs up to the Acropolis. Everyone is there. Priests and priestesses. Young women from noble families, carrying baskets on their heads. Old men with olive branches. Young men leading cattle and sheep for the sacrifice. Musicians, horsemen and chariots. Even the foreigners who live in Athens walk along with offerings.

{img:new-peplos-robe-carried-like-sail-ship-on-wheels-procession}
In the middle is the most important thing of all. It is a new robe for Athena, woven by the women and girls of Athens over the past nine months. It is saffron yellow, with pictures woven into it. At the Great Panathenaia, the robe is so big that it hangs like a sail from the mast of a ship on wheels, pulled along the road.

--

The procession climbs up through the great gateway. The robe is given to the priestess of Athena, to dress the ancient wooden statue in the old temple.

Then comes the great sacrifice, a hundred cattle or more, at the altar of Athena. Afterwards the meat is roasted and shared among all the people of Athens in a great feast. For many poor families, it is the best meal of the whole year.

{img:great-feast-athenians-sharing-roasted-meat-bread-evening-joy}
Perhaps this is the procession that is carved around the Parthenon. The horsemen, the young women, the old men and the cattle, and the gods waiting to receive them. The people of Athens, honouring their goddess, in marble, forever. As they eat and laugh together this summer, they hope there will be many more festivals like it.
