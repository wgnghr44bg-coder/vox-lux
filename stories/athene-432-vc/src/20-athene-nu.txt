Today, Athens is a big modern city, the capital of Greece, with more than three million people. It spreads across the whole plain of Attica, from the mountains to the sea.

And in the middle of it all, the Acropolis still rises, as it did in 432. On top stands the Parthenon, its columns shining golden white in the sunshine.

--

The Parthenon is a ruin now. Over two and a half thousand years it has been a temple, a church and a mosque. In 1687, during a war, it was being used to store gunpowder. A cannonball struck it, and the explosion blew out the middle of the temple. Many of its sculptures were later taken to London, and they are now in the British Museum. The Greeks would very much like them back.

But the building still stands, with its columns and its gentle curves. Every year, millions of people from all over the world climb the old path through the gateway to see it.

{img:acropolis-museum-glass-floor-frieze-gallery-light}
At the foot of the Acropolis there is a new museum with great glass walls and glass floors. Through the floors you can see the ruins of the old city below. On the top floor, the great carved procession of the Parthenon is laid out in its old order. You can walk all around it, with the real Parthenon just outside the windows.

--

The Agora is still there below the Acropolis. It is a peaceful park of ruins, trees and grass, where tortoises wander among the stones. The temple of Hephaestus still stands on its hill, almost complete. And one of the long covered buildings has been rebuilt, and is now the museum of the Agora.

In it you can see things found there. The pottery pieces with famous names scratched on them. The stone machine for choosing jurors. A water clock for timing speeches. Children's toys, cooking pots, lamps and coins from everyday life.

The theatre of Dionysus is still there on the slope of the Acropolis, with its stone seats. Nearby is another old theatre, where on summer evenings, ancient Greek plays are still performed under the stars, with the Acropolis lit up above.

--

On the hill where the assembly met, you can still see the stone speaker's platform cut into the rock. It is a quiet place with pine trees and a view of the Acropolis. You can stand where Pericles stood, and imagine six thousand citizens on the slope, raising their hands to vote.

And from the Piraeus, ferries still sail out every day to the islands, past the old harbours where the warships once lay.

{img:pnyx-hill-pine-trees-acropolis-view-sunset-quiet}
In the evening, when the sun goes down, the marble of the Parthenon turns golden, then pink, then soft grey. The lights of the city come on across the plain. And the Acropolis glows above the city, as it has for two and a half thousand years.

--

So we leave Athens tonight, in its last peaceful summer, with the new temple shining on its rock, and the people talking in the Agora as the evening comes.

The potter at his wheel, the farmer in his olive grove, the barefoot thinker with his questions, and a city that tried, for the first time, to let its people rule themselves.
