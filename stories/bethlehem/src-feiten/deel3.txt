TOPIC: From Nazareth to Bethlehem
===
IMG: nazareth-small-village-galilee-hills | a small village of simple stone houses on a green hillside in Galilee about two thousand years ago, olive trees, terraced fields and a spring with women drawing water, soft morning light
HEAD: Mary and Joseph lived in Nazareth, a small village in the hills of Galilee, far to the north of Bethlehem.
BODY: According to the Gospel of Luke, Mary and Joseph lived in Nazareth, in Galilee, the northern region of the land. Historians note that Nazareth was a very small village, perhaps home to a few hundred people. It is not mentioned in the Jewish scriptures or in any other writing from before the time of Jesus. Archaeologists have found remains of simple houses, cisterns, storage pits and a few tombs there. Even so, Nazareth was not completely isolated. Only about six kilometres away stood the city of Sepphoris, which was being rebuilt as a grand Roman-style city by Herod's son Antipas. Some historians have suggested that craftsmen from Nazareth may have found work there. The people of Nazareth were farmers and craftsmen, who lived much like the people of Bethlehem. They grew grain, olives and grapes, kept animals and followed the Jewish law. The hills of Galilee were greener and more fertile than those of Judea.
===
IMG: joseph-carpenter-workshop-wood-tools | a craftsman with a short beard in a simple tunic shaping a wooden beam with an adze in a small open workshop beside a stone house, tools, planks and wood shavings around him, ancient Galilee, warm daylight
HEAD: Joseph was a craftsman, called a tekton, who worked with wood and perhaps with stone.
BODY: The Gospels describe Joseph as a tekton, a Greek word usually translated as carpenter. Historians note that the word could mean a craftsman or builder who worked with wood, stone or both. In a village like Nazareth, a tekton might make ploughs, yokes, doors, beams, furniture and tools, and help to build houses. Wood was scarce in the region, so building in stone was also common. Even so, little else is known about Joseph. He appears in the stories of Jesus's birth and childhood, but not in the accounts of his adult life, and many historians think he may have died before Jesus began his work. The Gospels say that he was a descendant of King David. Historians note that later Christian tradition describes him as a kind and protective man. His trade would have been passed on to his son, as was the custom, and Jesus himself is called a tekton in the Gospel of Mark.
===
IMG: young-woman-mary-village-doorway | a young woman in a simple blue-grey robe and head covering standing quietly in the doorway of a small stone house in an ancient Galilean village, a clay jar beside her, soft morning light
HEAD: Mary was a young woman from Nazareth, engaged to be married to Joseph.
BODY: According to the Gospels, Mary was a young woman of Nazareth, engaged to Joseph. Historians note that girls in Jewish villages of the time were often engaged in their early or mid teens, and marriages were arranged by families. An engagement was a binding agreement, almost as serious as marriage. After a period of time, often a year, the bride moved to her husband's family home. Even so, the Gospel of Luke tells that before the marriage was completed, the angel Gabriel appeared to Mary and told her that she would bear a son. Mary is described as answering with simple faith. Historians note that later Christian tradition gave Mary great honour, and she became one of the most important figures in Christian art and devotion. Yet in her own time, she lived the ordinary life of a village woman, fetching water, grinding grain, baking bread and spinning wool, like the women of Bethlehem.
===
IMG: donkey-journey-road-hills-couple | a man walking beside a donkey carrying a young woman in a robe and head covering along a dusty road through the hills of Samaria about two thousand years ago, a few other travellers ahead, soft late afternoon light
HEAD: The journey from Nazareth to Bethlehem was about one hundred and fifty kilometres and took several days.
BODY: The road from Nazareth to Bethlehem was about one hundred and fifty kilometres long. Historians note that the journey would have taken about four to seven days on foot. Travellers usually walked, and those who could afford it used a donkey to carry their goods or a person who could not walk far. The Gospels do not mention a donkey, but later tradition added one, and it has appeared in nativity scenes ever since. Even so, the journey was not easy. The roads were rough and dusty, and there were hills to climb. Travellers had to find water and places to rest each night. They usually travelled in groups for safety, because robbers could attack lonely travellers. Historians note that there were two main routes. One went through Samaria, the region between Galilee and Judea. The other went down the Jordan Valley, avoiding Samaria, since Jews and Samaritans were often unfriendly towards each other.
===
IMG: travellers-camp-night-fire-road | a small group of travellers resting for the night beside a road in the hills, a small fire burning, donkeys tethered nearby, people wrapped in cloaks under a starry sky, about two thousand years ago
HEAD: At night, travellers slept in villages along the way, with relatives or in the open beside the road.
BODY: When people travelled in ancient Judea, they needed places to stop each night. Historians note that there were few inns, especially on country roads. Travellers often stayed with relatives or friends, or asked for hospitality in villages along the way. Hospitality was a sacred duty in Jewish culture, and families were expected to welcome strangers. Even so, sometimes travellers had to sleep in the open, beside the road. They wrapped themselves in their cloaks and slept by a small fire, while one person kept watch. In winter, the nights could be cold and wet. Historians note that the Jordan Valley route was warmer, because it lay far below sea level. Travellers going that way would climb up from Jericho to Jerusalem through the desert, a steep and lonely road known for its robbers. From Jerusalem, it was only a short walk south to Bethlehem. The final part of the journey led over the hills.
===
IMG: first-sight-bethlehem-ridge-travellers | two weary travellers with a donkey pausing on a hilltop road and looking towards a small village of stone houses on a distant ridge, terraced hills and olive trees around, soft golden evening light
HEAD: After days on the road, the travellers finally saw the little village of Bethlehem on its ridge.
BODY: Coming from Jerusalem, the road to Bethlehem passed through gentle hills, olive groves and fields. Historians imagine the travellers' first view of the village: a cluster of stone houses on a ridge, with smoke rising from the cooking fires and terraced fields around it. To the east, the land fell away towards the desert, and the Herodium rose in the distance. Even so, the Gospels say little about the journey itself. Luke simply says that Joseph went up from Galilee to Bethlehem with Mary, who was expecting a child. Historians note that Joseph may have had relatives in Bethlehem, since his family came from there. In Jewish culture, families were close, and relatives would usually offer a place to stay. Arriving in a village where one's ancestors had lived, perhaps for generations, would have been an important moment. Yet the house would also have been crowded, especially if other relatives had come too.
===
IMG: mary-resting-courtyard-evening-tired | a tired young woman in a robe and head covering resting against the stone wall of a small courtyard in the evening, a kind older woman offering her a cup of water, a clay oil lamp glowing nearby, ancient Judea
HEAD: Mary arrived in Bethlehem late in her pregnancy, tired from the long journey.
BODY: According to Luke, Mary was expecting her child when she and Joseph travelled to Bethlehem. Historians note that a long journey late in pregnancy would have been tiring and uncomfortable. In ancient times, giving birth was dangerous for both mother and child. Women usually gave birth at home, helped by other women of the family and by midwives, who had great experience. Even so, Mary was far from her own home and her own mother. Historians note that in a village like Bethlehem, the women of the household where they stayed would have helped her. Childbirth was a shared event, in which the women of the community supported each other. Newborn babies were washed, rubbed with salt and wrapped tightly in strips of cloth, called swaddling bands. People believed that this would help the child's limbs to grow straight and strong. Luke says that Mary wrapped her baby in swaddling clothes, as every mother in the village would have done.

TOPIC: No room in the guest room
===
IMG: crowded-village-house-relatives-gathering | a crowded small stone house in an ancient Judean village in the evening with many relatives sitting together on mats, children sleeping, oil lamps glowing, an indoor scene, enclosed by walls, a plain ceiling, warm lamplight
HEAD: Luke writes that there was no room for them in the kataluma, a word that can mean an inn or a guest room.
BODY: In the Gospel of Luke, the baby is laid in a manger because there was no room for them in the kataluma. Historians note that this Greek word has often been translated as inn, which led to the familiar picture of an unkind innkeeper turning Mary and Joseph away. Yet the same word is used elsewhere in Luke to mean a guest room, the upper room where Jesus later ate his last supper with his disciples. Many scholars now believe that Luke meant a guest room in a family house. Even so, this changes the picture of the story. Mary and Joseph may not have been rejected by strangers. They may have stayed with relatives whose guest room was already full, perhaps with other family members. So they stayed in the main part of the house, where the family lived and where the animals were brought in at night. Historians note that this was a normal arrangement in a crowded village home.
===
IMG: inn-caravanserai-courtyard-animals-night | a simple roadside inn with a stone-walled courtyard where travellers rest beside their donkeys and camels, small rooms around the edges, a fire in the centre, at night about two thousand years ago
HEAD: Roadside inns did exist, but they were usually rough places with rooms around a courtyard for animals.
BODY: In ancient Judea, there were some roadside inns, especially on main routes. Historians note that they were usually simple buildings with rooms arranged around an open courtyard, where travellers' animals were kept. Travellers brought their own food and bedding. Such inns were often noisy, dirty and had a bad reputation. In the parable of the Good Samaritan, Luke uses a different Greek word, pandocheion, for an inn on the road from Jerusalem to Jericho. Even so, a small village like Bethlehem probably did not have a proper inn. Visitors usually stayed with relatives or were taken in by local families. Historians note that this is why many scholars prefer to translate kataluma as guest room in the Christmas story. Yet the image of the inn with no room has become part of the Christmas tradition. It has been retold in countless nativity plays, carols and paintings, and remains a beloved part of the story.
===
IMG: stone-manger-feeding-trough-hay | a manger carved from a single block of limestone filled with hay inside the lower part of a simple stone house, a donkey and an ox standing nearby, soft lamplight, an indoor scene, enclosed by walls, a plain ceiling
HEAD: Mangers in ancient Judea were feeding troughs for animals, often carved from stone.
BODY: A manger is a feeding trough for animals. Historians note that in ancient Judea, mangers were often carved from blocks of limestone, or built into the floor of the house at the edge of the family's raised platform. Archaeologists have found stone mangers in houses and stables from that period. They were usually about a metre long, big enough to hold hay or grain for animals. Even so, a manger would also have made a suitable resting place for a newborn baby. It was sturdy, safe and raised above the floor. Filled with clean straw and lined with cloth, it would have kept the baby warm and secure. Historians note that the word manger in English comes from the French word for to eat. In Latin, it was called praesepe, which became the name for nativity scenes in Italian, presepe. The image of the baby in the manger became one of the most important symbols of Christmas.
===
IMG: ox-donkey-quiet-stable-lamplight | an ox and a donkey standing peacefully side by side in the lamplit lower part of a simple stone house, straw on the floor and a stone manger before them, an indoor scene, enclosed by walls, a plain ceiling, soft warm light
HEAD: The ox and the donkey of the nativity scene come from an old prophecy, not from the Gospels.
BODY: In nativity scenes, the baby Jesus is often shown with an ox and a donkey looking on. Historians note that these animals are not mentioned in the Gospels. They come from a verse in the Book of Isaiah, which says that the ox knows its owner, and the donkey its master's manger. Early Christian writers connected this verse with the birth of Jesus, and the animals began to appear in Christian art from the fourth century onwards. Even so, it is quite likely that animals were present. In a village house, a family's ox and donkey would normally be kept in the lower part of the house at night, near the manger. Historians note that the first living nativity scene, with real people and animals, was organised by Saint Francis of Assisi in 1223, in the Italian village of Greccio. He wanted people to see how humbly Jesus was born. The custom of nativity scenes spread throughout the Christian world.
===
IMG: midwife-women-helping-birth-lamplight | two older village women in robes and head coverings preparing cloths and a basin of warm water by lamplight in a simple stone house at night, a quiet and caring scene, an indoor scene, enclosed by walls, a plain ceiling, warm lamplight
HEAD: In the village, experienced women and midwives helped mothers through childbirth.
BODY: In the ancient world, childbirth was an important and sometimes dangerous event. Historians note that it took place at home, with the help of women of the family and experienced midwives. Midwives were respected members of the community. They knew how to help a mother through labour, how to care for the newborn baby and what to do if something went wrong. They used simple tools, warm water, oil, salt and cloths. Even so, many mothers and babies died in childbirth. Families prayed for a safe birth and welcomed every healthy child with joy. Historians note that the Gospels do not mention a midwife at the birth of Jesus. Later Christian writings, such as the Gospel of James from the second century, added stories about midwives. Yet in a village like Bethlehem, it would have been natural for the women of the household to help. A birth was an event that brought the women of the community together.
===
IMG: oil-lamp-night-house-silence | a single small clay oil lamp glowing on a stone ledge inside a quiet village house at night, soft shadows on the rough stone walls, a manger with straw in the dim background, an indoor scene, enclosed by walls, a plain ceiling
HEAD: The Gospels describe the birth itself very simply, in just a few words.
BODY: The Gospel of Luke describes the birth of Jesus in a single sentence. Historians note that it says simply that while they were in Bethlehem, the time came for Mary to give birth, and she gave birth to her firstborn son, wrapped him in swaddling clothes and laid him in a manger, because there was no room for them in the kataluma. Even so, these few words have inspired centuries of art, music and devotion. Painters, poets and composers have imagined the scene in countless ways. Historians note that Luke's account is calm and quiet. There is no description of the birth itself, no crowds and no drama. The scene is humble and ordinary, set in a simple village house among animals and straw. For the early Christians, this humility was important. It showed that the one they believed to be the Messiah was born not in a palace, but among ordinary people, in the most modest of places.

TOPIC: The shepherds
===
IMG: shepherds-night-sudden-light-sky | shepherds in cloaks on a dark hillside at night looking up in wonder at a soft golden light breaking through the clouds above them, their sheep around them, the distant village on the ridge
HEAD: According to Luke, an angel appeared to shepherds in the fields near Bethlehem that night.
BODY: According to the Gospel of Luke, there were shepherds living in the fields near Bethlehem, keeping watch over their flock by night. Suddenly, an angel of the Lord appeared to them, and the glory of the Lord shone around them, and they were terrified. Historians note that the angel told them not to be afraid. He said that he brought good news of great joy for all the people. A saviour had been born in the city of David, and they would find the child wrapped in swaddling clothes and lying in a manger. Even so, historians approach this story differently from the accounts of daily life. It is a story of faith, told by the early Christians to express their belief about who Jesus was. Historians cannot test whether angels appeared. Yet they can note the meaning the story had for its first readers. The good news was given first to humble shepherds, not to kings or priests.
===
IMG: host-of-angels-sky-stars-hills | a vast night sky over the dark hills of Judea filled with soft golden light and countless stars, small figures of shepherds on a hillside below gazing upward, a sense of wonder and peace
HEAD: Luke says that a great company of angels then appeared, praising God and singing of peace on earth.
BODY: In Luke's account, after the angel had spoken, a multitude of the heavenly host suddenly appeared with him, praising God. They said, Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace among those with whom he is pleased. Historians note that these words became one of the most famous phrases in Christian worship. In Latin, they begin Gloria in excelsis Deo. They are sung in church services around the world and appear in many Christmas carols. Even so, the words had a special meaning in their own time. The Roman emperor Augustus was praised as the bringer of peace, and his birthday was celebrated as good news for the world. Historians note that Luke may have been deliberately contrasting the peace brought by Augustus, imposed by armies, with the peace announced at the birth of Jesus. For the shepherds on the hillside, the experience was overwhelming, a moment of light and music in the darkness of the night.
===
IMG: shepherds-hurrying-path-village-night | shepherds with staffs hurrying along a stony path towards a small village of stone houses at night, a few lamps glowing in the windows, stars shining above, ancient Judea
HEAD: The shepherds hurried to Bethlehem and found Mary, Joseph and the baby lying in the manger.
BODY: After the angels had gone, the shepherds said to one another, let us go to Bethlehem and see this thing that has happened. Historians note that Luke says they went with haste and found Mary and Joseph, and the baby lying in the manger. Even so, it would not have been hard for them to find the child. Bethlehem was a small village, and the shepherds probably knew every house. News of a birth would have spread quickly. A baby lying in a manger would have been unusual enough to be noticed. Historians note that the shepherds then told everyone what they had heard and seen, and all who heard it were amazed. Mary, Luke says, treasured all these words and pondered them in her heart. The shepherds returned to their flocks, glorifying and praising God. For the early Christians, these simple shepherds were the first witnesses of the Christmas story, the first to share the news with others.
===
IMG: shepherds-kneeling-manger-lamplight | humble shepherds in rough cloaks kneeling quietly beside a stone manger where a newborn baby sleeps wrapped in cloth, a young mother and a man watching, soft lamplight in a simple stone house, an indoor scene, enclosed by walls, a plain ceiling
HEAD: The visit of the shepherds has been a favourite subject for artists for more than a thousand years.
BODY: The scene of the shepherds visiting the baby in the manger has inspired artists for centuries. Historians note that it appears in early Christian art and became especially popular in the Middle Ages and the Renaissance. Painters showed the shepherds kneeling in wonder, sometimes bringing gifts such as a lamb, milk or cheese. They showed the poor shepherds alongside the holy family in a humble setting. Even so, the artists usually painted the scene in the style of their own time and place. Italian painters showed Italian landscapes, Dutch painters showed Dutch farms, and snow often appeared, although snow is rare in Bethlehem. Historians note that these paintings tell us as much about the artists' world as about ancient Judea. Yet they share a common message. The shepherds represent ordinary people, welcomed into the story. In carols such as While Shepherds Watched Their Flocks by Night, written in 1700, they are remembered every Christmas.
===
IMG: swaddled-baby-mother-quiet-night | a young mother in a simple robe holding a newborn baby wrapped tightly in white cloth strips, sitting quietly on a mat in a dimly lit stone house at night, a small oil lamp beside her, an indoor scene, enclosed by walls, a plain ceiling, warm soft light
HEAD: Mary is described as quietly treasuring all these things and pondering them in her heart.
BODY: In the Gospel of Luke, Mary is a quiet figure. Historians note that after the shepherds' visit, Luke says that Mary treasured all these things, pondering them in her heart. This gentle line has been loved by readers for centuries. It suggests a young mother, tired after the birth, holding her baby and thinking about the strange events of the night. Even so, Luke may have had another purpose. Some scholars suggest that he was hinting that Mary was one of the sources for his account, remembering events that she later shared with others. Historians cannot confirm this. Yet the image of Mary quietly reflecting has been very important in Christian art and devotion. Many paintings show her gazing at her child in silent wonder. For mothers in Bethlehem, as everywhere, the first nights with a newborn were a time of tiredness, love and quiet watchfulness, by the light of a small lamp.

IMG: circumcision-naming-family-house | a small family gathering in a simple stone house in ancient Judea, an older man in a prayer shawl blessing a newborn baby held by its father, the mother and relatives watching, an indoor scene, enclosed by walls, a plain ceiling, soft light
HEAD: On the eighth day, the baby was circumcised and given the name Jesus.
BODY: According to Jewish law, a baby boy was circumcised on the eighth day after his birth. Historians note that this was a sign of the covenant, the special relationship between God and the people of Israel, going back to Abraham. At the same time, the child was given his name. Luke says that the baby was named Jesus, the name the angel had given before he was conceived. Even so, Jesus was a common name at the time. In Hebrew or Aramaic, it was Yeshua, a shorter form of Joshua, meaning God saves. Many Jewish boys were given this name, in honour of Joshua, who had led the Israelites into the promised land. Historians note that the circumcision and naming were family occasions, often celebrated with relatives and neighbours. Prayers and blessings were said, and the family shared a meal. For Mary and Joseph, far from Nazareth, these ceremonies would have connected their child to his people and their traditions.

IMG: presentation-temple-elderly-simeon-anna | an elderly man with a white beard holding a baby in his arms in a great stone temple courtyard, an old woman in a head covering beside him, a young mother and father watching with a pair of doves in a cage, about two thousand years ago, soft light
HEAD: Forty days after the birth, Mary and Joseph took the baby to the Temple in Jerusalem.
BODY: According to Jewish law, forty days after the birth of a son, the mother went to the Temple to offer a sacrifice for her purification. The firstborn son was also presented to God. Historians note that Luke says Mary and Joseph took Jesus to the Temple in Jerusalem and offered a pair of turtledoves or two young pigeons. This was the offering that the law allowed for poor families who could not afford a lamb. Even so, Luke says that something unexpected happened there. An old man named Simeon, who had been waiting for the Messiah, took the child in his arms and praised God. His words became a famous prayer, known in Latin as the Nunc dimittis, which is still sung in evening services. An elderly widow named Anna, who spent her days in the Temple, also gave thanks for the child. Historians note that Bethlehem was close enough to Jerusalem for such a visit to be easy.

TOPIC: The star and the wise men
===
IMG: magi-astrologers-roof-stars-babylon | learned men in long robes and tall caps studying the night sky from the flat roof of a building in an ancient eastern city, clay tablets and instruments beside them, a bright star among many in the sky
HEAD: The Gospel of Matthew tells of wise men from the east who followed a star to find a newborn king.
BODY: According to the Gospel of Matthew, wise men from the east came to Jerusalem, asking where the newborn king of the Jews was, for they had seen his star at its rising. Historians note that Matthew calls them magoi, a Greek word for the magi. The magi were originally priests of the Persian religion, but the word came to mean learned men, especially those who studied the stars. Even so, the Gospel does not say exactly where they came from, or how many they were. They may have come from Persia, from Babylon, where there was a long tradition of studying the stars, or from Arabia. Historians note that in ancient times, many people believed that unusual events in the sky announced the birth or death of great rulers. Astronomers in Babylon kept careful records of the movements of the planets and stars. To such men, a new light in the sky could have seemed like a royal sign.
===
IMG: night-sky-planets-conjunction-bright | two bright planets shining close together low in a deep blue night sky above the dark silhouette of hills and a small village, many stars around them, peaceful and clear
HEAD: Astronomers have long wondered whether the star of Bethlehem could have been a real event in the sky.
BODY: For centuries, people have tried to explain the star of Bethlehem. Historians and astronomers have suggested several possibilities. In 1604, the astronomer Johannes Kepler suggested that it might have been a conjunction, a close meeting in the sky, of the planets Jupiter and Saturn. Such a conjunction happened three times in 7 BC, in the constellation of Pisces. Jupiter was often linked with kings, and some have connected Saturn and Pisces with the Jewish people. Even so, other explanations have been offered too. Chinese astronomers recorded a new star or comet in 5 BC, which some have linked to the star of Bethlehem. Others have suggested a supernova, an exploding star. Historians note that none of these explanations is certain. Matthew describes the star moving ahead of the wise men and stopping over the place where the child was, which is not how real stars or planets behave. Many scholars see the star as a symbol of faith rather than an astronomical event.
===
IMG: camel-caravan-desert-road-dusk | a caravan of richly dressed travellers riding camels across a rocky desert road at dusk, servants walking beside them, a bright star rising in the violet sky ahead, about two thousand years ago
HEAD: The wise men would have travelled for weeks or months across deserts and mountains.
BODY: If the wise men came from Persia or Babylon, they would have faced a long journey. Historians note that the distance from Babylon to Jerusalem was more than a thousand kilometres, and the journey could have taken weeks or even months. Travellers in that region usually went in caravans, groups of people and animals travelling together for safety. They followed trade routes along the Euphrates river and across the Syrian desert, stopping at oases and towns. Even so, Matthew does not mention camels. They come from later Christian tradition, inspired by a verse in Isaiah about camels bringing gold and frankincense. Historians note that caravans on these routes did often use camels, which could carry heavy loads and survive long periods without water. The wise men may have travelled with servants and guards. Their journey has been imagined in countless paintings and poems, including T. S. Eliot's Journey of the Magi, which describes a cold, hard journey.
===
IMG: magi-herod-palace-audience-scrolls | richly robed foreign travellers standing before an elderly king on a throne in a grand palace hall with columns, priests and scribes with scrolls nearby, about two thousand years ago, soft warm light
HEAD: When the wise men arrived in Jerusalem, King Herod called them to his palace in secret.
BODY: According to Matthew, when the wise men arrived in Jerusalem asking about a newborn king, King Herod was troubled, and all Jerusalem with him. Historians note that this fits what is known about Herod, who was extremely jealous of his power. He called together the chief priests and scribes and asked them where the Messiah was to be born. They answered that it was Bethlehem in Judea, quoting the prophet Micah. Even so, Herod hid his true intentions. He called the wise men secretly and asked them exactly when the star had appeared. Then he sent them to Bethlehem, telling them to search carefully for the child and to bring him word, so that he too could come and worship him. Historians note that Matthew presents Herod as a dangerous and deceitful king, in contrast to the wise men, who came in good faith, and to the child, who was born as a humble king.
===
IMG: gold-frankincense-myrrh-gifts-cloth | three precious gifts laid on a woven cloth in a simple stone house, a small chest of gold coins, a bowl of pale frankincense resin and a small jar of dark myrrh, soft lamplight, an indoor scene, enclosed by walls, a plain ceiling
HEAD: The wise men brought gifts of gold, frankincense and myrrh, which were precious treasures of the ancient world.
BODY: Matthew says that the wise men followed the star to Bethlehem, to the house where the child was. They knelt down and paid him homage, and they opened their treasure chests and offered gifts of gold, frankincense and myrrh. Historians note that these were among the most valuable goods of the ancient world. Gold was a gift fit for a king. Frankincense was a fragrant resin from trees in southern Arabia and Somalia, burned as incense in temples. Myrrh was another precious resin, used in perfumes, medicines and for anointing the dead. Even so, Christians later gave the gifts symbolic meanings. Gold represented kingship, frankincense represented divinity, and myrrh foreshadowed death. Historians note that because there were three gifts, tradition later decided that there were three wise men. By the sixth century, they had been given names, Caspar, Melchior and Balthasar. Matthew himself never says how many there were, only that they came from the east.
===
IMG: magi-departing-another-road-dawn | a small group of richly dressed travellers leading their camels away along a different road through the hills at dawn, a small village behind them, soft pink and gold light
HEAD: Warned in a dream, the wise men returned home by another road, avoiding Herod.
BODY: After visiting the child, the wise men were warned in a dream not to return to Herod. Historians note that Matthew says they left for their own country by another road. Joseph, too, was warned in a dream and took Mary and the child to Egypt, where they remained until Herod died. Even so, the wise men then disappear from the Gospel. Nothing more is said about them. Yet later Christian tradition developed many stories about them. They were described as kings, an idea drawn from the Psalms, and as representatives of the different peoples of the world. In medieval art, one of them was often shown as African, to show that the message was for all nations. Historians note that in the twelfth century, relics said to be theirs were brought to Cologne, in Germany, where they are kept in a golden shrine in the cathedral. The feast of the Epiphany, on the sixth of January, celebrates their visit.
===
IMG: epiphany-celebration-church-candles | a candlelit old stone church decorated for the feast of the Epiphany with a nativity scene showing three richly robed kings kneeling before a mother and child, worshippers gathered quietly, an indoor scene, enclosed by walls, a vaulted ceiling, warm light
HEAD: Christians remember the wise men on the feast of the Epiphany, twelve days after Christmas.
BODY: The feast of the Epiphany is celebrated on the sixth of January, twelve days after Christmas. Historians note that the word Epiphany comes from Greek and means appearance or revelation. For many Christians, the feast remembers the visit of the wise men, showing that Jesus was revealed to people of all nations. Even so, the feast has been celebrated in different ways in different places. In the Eastern churches, Epiphany remembers mainly the baptism of Jesus in the river Jordan. In some countries, Epiphany is more important than Christmas for giving presents. In Spain and Latin America, children receive gifts from the Three Kings on the night of the fifth of January. In parts of Europe, children dressed as the three kings go from house to house singing and writing a blessing over the door in chalk. Historians note that the twelve days between Christmas and Epiphany became the twelve days of Christmas, celebrated in the famous English song.

TOPIC: Bethlehem through the centuries
===
IMG: church-nativity-ancient-stone-facade | the ancient stone facade of a large old church with a very small low doorway in the middle, a quiet stone square in front with a few people, soft morning light
HEAD: In the fourth century, the emperor Constantine built a great church over the cave in Bethlehem.
BODY: In the early fourth century, the Roman emperor Constantine became a Christian. Historians note that his mother, Helena, travelled to the Holy Land around 326 and helped to found churches at the most important sites of Christian tradition. In Bethlehem, a great church was built over the cave believed to be the place of the birth of Jesus. It was dedicated in 339. Even so, the church that stands today is not quite the same building. Constantine's church was badly damaged, probably in a revolt in the sixth century, and it was rebuilt on a larger scale by the emperor Justinian. Historians note that this sixth century church, the Church of the Nativity, is one of the oldest churches in the world still in daily use. Its main entrance is a very small doorway, called the Door of Humility, which forces visitors to bow as they enter. It was made smaller centuries ago, probably to stop people riding in on horseback.

IMG: grotto-silver-star-floor-lamps | an underground grotto with rough stone walls hung with many small hanging oil lamps, a silver star set in the marble floor at the end, soft golden lamplight, an indoor scene, enclosed by walls, a low rock ceiling
HEAD: Beneath the church lies the Grotto of the Nativity, where a silver star marks the traditional place of the birth.
BODY: Beneath the main altar of the Church of the Nativity lies a cave called the Grotto of the Nativity. Historians note that visitors descend narrow stone steps to reach it. The grotto is small and dimly lit, its walls hung with tapestries and dozens of small oil lamps. In the floor is a fourteen-pointed silver star, set in marble, marking the place where tradition says Jesus was born. Around it is a Latin inscription meaning, here Jesus Christ was born of the Virgin Mary. Even so, the star has its own history. It was placed there in 1717. In 1847, it was stolen, and the dispute over its replacement was one of the quarrels between European powers that led up to the Crimean War. A new star was installed in 1853. Historians note that nearby is a small chapel with a stone manger. The church is shared today by several Christian communities, who care for it according to ancient agreements.

IMG: jerome-scholar-cave-writing-lamp | an elderly bearded scholar in a simple robe writing on parchment by the light of an oil lamp in a small rock-cut cave room, scrolls and books around him, an indoor scene, enclosed by walls, a low rock ceiling, warm lamplight
HEAD: The scholar Jerome lived in Bethlehem for many years and translated the Bible into Latin there.
BODY: In 386, a learned priest named Jerome came to live in Bethlehem. Historians note that he was one of the greatest scholars of the early church. He had studied in Rome and in the East, and he knew Latin, Greek and Hebrew. In Bethlehem, he founded a monastery, and his friend Paula, a wealthy Roman widow, founded a convent nearby. Jerome lived and worked in caves near the Church of the Nativity. Even so, his most important work was a translation of the Bible into Latin. It took him many years, and he worked directly from Hebrew and Greek texts. His translation, known as the Vulgate, became the standard Bible of the Western church for more than a thousand years. Historians note that Jerome died in Bethlehem in 420. The caves where he is believed to have worked can still be visited, beneath the Church of Saint Catherine, next to the Church of the Nativity.

IMG: medieval-pilgrims-arriving-bethlehem | medieval pilgrims in long cloaks with staffs and wide hats arriving at a small hilltop town with an old stone church and a monastery, about the twelfth century, olive trees along the road, soft golden light
HEAD: For centuries, pilgrims travelled from all over Europe and the East to visit Bethlehem.
BODY: From the fourth century onwards, Bethlehem became a place of pilgrimage. Historians note that Christians from all over the Roman world travelled there to pray at the place of the birth of Jesus. One of the earliest pilgrims to leave a written account was a woman named Egeria, who visited the Holy Land in the 380s. Even so, the journey was long and difficult. Pilgrims travelled by sea and over land, facing storms, bandits and illness. Many did not return home. Over the centuries, Bethlehem was ruled by Byzantines, Arab caliphs, Crusaders, Mamluks and Ottoman Turks. Historians note that in 1100, the Crusader Baldwin was crowned the first king of Jerusalem in the Church of the Nativity on Christmas Day. Pilgrims continued to come in every age. They brought back stories, souvenirs and small objects made of mother-of-pearl and olive wood, crafts for which Bethlehem has been famous for centuries.

IMG: olive-wood-carvers-workshop-bethlehem | craftsmen carving small nativity figures from olive wood at wooden workbenches in a simple workshop, shavings on the floor and finished figures on shelves, an indoor scene, enclosed by walls, a plain ceiling, warm daylight
HEAD: For centuries, the craftsmen of Bethlehem have carved nativity figures from olive wood.
BODY: The olive trees of Bethlehem have provided more than oil. Historians note that for centuries, the craftsmen of the town have carved olive wood into crosses, rosaries, boxes and nativity figures. They also make objects decorated with mother-of-pearl, the shiny inner layer of shells. These crafts may have been introduced or encouraged by Franciscan friars, who came to the Holy Land in the fourteenth century to care for its holy sites. Even so, the crafts became a central part of Bethlehem's life. Families passed the skills down from generation to generation. Pilgrims bought the carvings and took them home to every corner of the world. Historians note that olive wood is hard, with a beautiful grain, and it is cut from pruned branches or old trees, so that living trees are not destroyed. Many families in Bethlehem still earn their living from these traditional crafts, which carry the memory of the town into homes far away.

IMG: manger-square-christmas-eve-crowd-lights | a large stone square in front of an ancient church on Christmas Eve at night, crowds of people, a tall decorated tree with lights and a star, the old stone buildings of the town around, festive warm light
HEAD: Today, Christmas in Bethlehem is celebrated three times, by different Christian traditions.
BODY: Today, Bethlehem is a town of about thirty thousand people, in the West Bank. Historians note that its population includes both Muslims and Christians. Christmas is the most important time of year in the town. It is celebrated three times. Catholic and Protestant Christians celebrate on the twenty-fifth of December. Greek Orthodox and some other Eastern churches celebrate on the seventh of January, following the old Julian calendar. Armenian Christians celebrate on the eighteenth or nineteenth of January. Even so, the heart of the celebration is the same. On Christmas Eve, crowds gather in Manger Square, in front of the Church of the Nativity. A great tree is lit, processions arrive, scouts' bands play music, and a midnight mass is held. Historians note that the town's celebrations have been affected at times by conflict and hardship. Yet every year, the story of the first Christmas is told again in the place where, according to tradition, it began.

IMG: december-25-date-roman-calendar-scroll | an old Roman calendar carved on a stone tablet with rows of dates and symbols, displayed in a quiet museum room with soft warm light, an indoor scene, enclosed by walls, a plain ceiling
HEAD: The date of the twenty-fifth of December was chosen by Christians in Rome in the fourth century.
BODY: The Gospels do not say on what day Jesus was born. Historians note that the earliest record of Christmas being celebrated on the twenty-fifth of December comes from a Roman calendar of the year 354, which records it for the year 336. Before that, some Christians had suggested other dates, and some did not celebrate the birth at all. Even so, the reason for choosing the twenty-fifth of December is not certain. Historians offer several explanations. Some believe the date was chosen to replace pagan festivals held near the winter solstice, such as Saturnalia or the birthday of the unconquered sun. Others believe early Christians calculated it, counting nine months from the date they believed Jesus was conceived, the twenty-fifth of March. Historians note that the question is still debated. Yet whatever the reason, the date spread across the Christian world. The quiet birth in a small village became a festival celebrated by billions of people every year.

IMG: bethlehem-hills-dusk-star-peaceful | the hills around a small town of pale stone houses at dusk, olive groves on the terraces, a single bright star in the deep violet sky above, quiet and peaceful
HEAD: More than two thousand years later, the hills around Bethlehem still turn gold and violet at dusk.
BODY: The village of Bethlehem has changed greatly over two thousand years. Historians note that it has grown into a town, with streets, shops, churches, mosques, schools and homes. Yet the hills around it would still be familiar to the shepherds of long ago. Olive trees still grow on the terraces, and in places sheep and goats still graze on the slopes. The winter rains still turn the hills green, and the summer sun still bakes them gold. Even so, the most important change is not in the landscape, but in the name. Bethlehem, once a small village known mainly as the birthplace of King David, became known around the world because of a child born in a humble house, laid in a manger. Historians note that every December, its name is sung in carols in hundreds of languages. And at dusk, when the first star appears over the hills, it is easy to imagine the quiet village as it once was.
