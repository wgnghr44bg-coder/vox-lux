In the same region, the Gospel of Luke says, there were shepherds out in the fields, keeping watch over their flock by night.

They were the shepherds of Bethlehem, ordinary men and boys, sitting around their little fire beside the sheepfold, on a cold winter night, wrapped in their rough cloaks, talking quietly, or dozing, or looking up at the stars, as they did every night.

--

The sheep were asleep in the fold, lying close together for warmth, their fleeces pale in the darkness. The dogs lay by the fire. Somewhere, far away, a jackal called. The fire crackled, and a few sparks rose up into the black sky.

It was a night like any other.

--

And then, the story says, an angel of the Lord stood before them, and the glory of the Lord shone around them, and they were terrified.

But the angel said to them: Do not be afraid. For see, I am bringing you good news of great joy, for all the people. To you is born this day, in the city of David, a Saviour, who is the Messiah, the Lord. This will be a sign for you: you will find a child, wrapped in swaddling cloths, and lying in a manger.

{img:soft-golden-light-over-hills-shepherds-looking-up}
And suddenly, Luke says, there was with the angel a multitude of the heavenly host, praising God, and saying: Glory to God in the highest heaven, and on earth peace, among those whom he favours.

--

Then, as suddenly as it had come, the light faded, and the angels were gone, and the night was dark and quiet again, with only the stars, and the little fire, and the sleeping sheep.

The shepherds looked at each other.

Then they said: Let us go now to Bethlehem, and see this thing that has taken place, which the Lord has made known to us.

--

So they left their flock, perhaps with one or two of the boys to watch it, and went, in a hurry, through the darkness, along the rocky paths, up the hill to the village.

It was not hard for them to find what they were looking for. Bethlehem was a small place, and they knew every house in it. And a sign like that, a newborn baby lying in a manger, in a stable, would be easy to find. Perhaps they saw the light of a lamp still burning in the doorway of the stable, late in the night.

They found Mary and Joseph, and the child lying in the manger, just as the angel had said.

--

We can imagine them, standing in the doorway of the stable, rough men in their sheepskin cloaks, with their staffs in their hands, smelling of sheep and smoke, shy and awkward, with their caps in their hands.

They came in quietly, and looked at the baby, sleeping in the hay, in his swaddling cloths. And they told Mary and Joseph what they had seen and heard, out in the fields: the light, and the angel, and the singing.

{img:rough-shepherds-kneeling-by-manger-lamplight-awe}
Mary listened, and said nothing. But Luke says that she treasured all these words, and pondered them in her heart.

--

Then the shepherds went back to their flocks, in the fields, in the darkness, glorifying and praising God for all they had heard and seen.

And as they went, Luke says, they told everyone what had been told to them about this child. And all who heard it were amazed.

By the time they got back to the sheepfold, the sky over the eastern hills was beginning to grow pale, and the morning star was shining, low and bright, over the desert. The sheep were waking up, and bleating, and the boys who had stayed behind were waiting, wanting to know everything.

--

It is a strange and beautiful part of the story, that the first people to hear the news were shepherds.

Not kings, or priests, or rich men, or the great people of Jerusalem. But poor men, working through the cold night in the fields, ordinary, humble, overlooked people, keeping watch over their sheep, as shepherds had done on those same hills for a thousand years, since the time of David.

{img:shepherds-field-dawn-beit-sahour-olive-trees}
And ever since, at Christmas, all over the world, people have remembered those shepherds, and sung about them, and made little figures of them, with their sheep on their shoulders, to put beside the manger in the nativity scene.

While shepherds watched their flocks by night, all seated on the ground, the angel of the Lord came down, and glory shone around.
