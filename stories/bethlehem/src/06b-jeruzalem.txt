Six miles to the north of Bethlehem, along the ridge road, lies the great city of Jerusalem.

For the people of Bethlehem, Jerusalem is the centre of the world. It is the holy city, the city of David, the city of the Temple. They go there to buy and sell at the markets, to work on the building sites, to visit relatives, and above all, three times a year, for the great festivals.

--

The walk from Bethlehem to Jerusalem takes about two hours.

The road follows the ridge, past olive groves and vineyards and little villages, and the tombs of the ancestors cut into the rocks beside the road. Near Bethlehem, beside the road, there is the tomb of Rachel, the beloved wife of Jacob, who, the old story says, died there long ago, giving birth to her son Benjamin. Travellers stop there to pray.

Then, as the road climbs over the last hill, the city appears, spread out across its hills, behind its great walls of golden stone, with its towers and its palaces, and above all, the Temple.

--

Jerusalem is a big, busy, crowded city, of perhaps forty or fifty thousand people, or even more.

Its streets are narrow and steep and paved with stone, with steps going up and down the hills, and arches over them, and houses built close together, two and three storeys high. There are markets for everything: for grain and fruit and vegetables, for sheep and cattle and doves, for cloth and pottery and spices, for perfume and jewellery.

{img:jerusalem-narrow-market-street-stalls-crowds-ancient}
There are workshops of craftsmen: potters and weavers, dyers and tanners, glassblowers and goldsmiths, stonemasons and carpenters. There are money changers, who change foreign coins into the coins that can be used at the Temple. There are inns, and bath houses, and the grand houses of the rich and the priests, with mosaic floors and painted walls.

--

And everywhere, there is the sound of building.

King Herod is rebuilding the Temple, and thousands of workers are labouring on it: stonemasons cutting the great blocks of white limestone, some of them as big as a house, and hauling them into place with cranes and ropes and rollers, and carpenters and gilders and sculptors, decorating the porticoes and the gates.

The Temple stands on an enormous platform, built up on top of the hill, surrounded by great walls. Some of those walls still stand today. One of them, the Western Wall, is still visited and prayed at, every day, by people from all over the world.

--

On the great festivals, Jerusalem is crowded with pilgrims.

At Passover, in the spring, people come from all over the land, and from every country where Jews live: from Egypt and Syria and Babylon, from Rome and Greece and Asia. The city is full. The pilgrims camp on the hillsides around the walls, in tents and shelters, and the whole valley glows with their fires at night.

{img:passover-pilgrims-tents-hillsides-jerusalem-fires-night}
The families of Bethlehem walk up to Jerusalem for the festivals, with their children and their grandparents, singing the old songs of pilgrimage as they go, the Psalms of Ascent: I was glad when they said unto me, let us go into the house of the Lord. Our feet are standing within your gates, O Jerusalem.

--

At the Temple, the pilgrims climb up the great stairs, and pass through the gates, into the vast outer courtyard, paved with stone, surrounded by tall colonnades.

The courtyard is crowded with people from every nation, speaking every language. There are teachers of the law, sitting in the shade of the colonnades, with their students around them. There are sellers of doves and lambs for the sacrifices. There are Levites, singing psalms, with harps and lyres and trumpets.

And in the centre, behind more walls and gates, there is the sanctuary itself, the holiest place, gleaming white and gold, with smoke rising from the great altar in front of it, and the priests in their white linen robes moving about in the courtyard.

--

At the end of the festival, the families walk home to Bethlehem, tired and happy, singing.

{img:family-walking-home-bethlehem-evening-ridge-road}
And the children talk, all the way, about everything they have seen: the crowds, the Temple, the gold, the music, the priests, the markets, the soldiers, the great walls. And in the evening, at home, the grandparents tell them, once again, that one day, when the Messiah comes, Jerusalem will be the most beautiful city in the whole world, and all the nations will come to it, in peace.
