While Armstrong and Aldrin walk on the moon, Michael Collins is alone in Columbia, circling high above.

Every two hours, Columbia goes all the way around the moon. On the near side, Collins can talk to Houston on the radio. He listens to the news of his friends on the surface. He tries to see Eagle on the ground through his telescope, but it is too small to find. He keeps busy, checking the systems of the spacecraft and preparing for the return of his friends.

--

Then, each time around, Columbia goes behind the moon, to the far side. And there, the radio goes silent. For about forty-eight minutes, Collins cannot talk to anyone. He cannot hear anyone. He is more alone than any person has ever been. On the other side of the moon, there are three billion people, and two friends. On this side, there is only Collins, and the stars.

{img:far-side-of-moon-dark-stars-command-module-window-solitude-peace}
But Collins does not feel lonely. He later wrote that he felt a kind of awareness and joy. He liked the quiet. He looked out of the window at the stars, which were brighter than anything he had ever seen on Earth. He made himself a cup of hot coffee. He listened to the hum of the spacecraft. And he felt quite at peace.

--

On the far side, the moon is dark, because the sun is behind it. Collins cannot see its surface at all. He can only see where it is, because it blocks out the stars. There is a great black shape in front of him, with no stars in it. Then, slowly, the edge of the moon begins to glow. The sun comes up over the moon's horizon, bright and dazzling. And Columbia comes round to the near side again, into the light, and the radio crackles back to life.

{img:sunrise-over-lunar-horizon-bright-light-command-module-silhouette}
Collins was not jealous of his friends on the moon. He knew that his job was very important too. Without him, they could not get home. He had to be ready to help them, if anything went wrong with Eagle. He had practised many ways to rescue them. He hoped very much that he would not need to use any of them.

--

In Houston, Bill thinks about Collins too. He thinks it must be a strange feeling, to be so far away, all alone. He mentions it to Carol when he comes home for a few hours of sleep. Carol says she thinks Collins must be a very calm man. Bill says that all the astronauts are calm. That is why they were chosen.

{img:engineer-kitchen-early-morning-1969-coffee-wife-talking-tired-happy}
Later, Michael Collins wrote a book about his journey. It was one of the best books ever written by an astronaut. He wrote about the beauty of the Earth, seen from far away. He wrote that the Earth looked small and fragile and precious. He said that if world leaders could see the Earth from space, they might learn to get along better. And he wrote that he would not have changed places with anyone.

While he is alone, Collins also talks to himself a little, and hums songs. He writes notes in his checklist. Later, people found that he wrote little jokes and comments in the margins. He also wrote, on the wall of the spacecraft, a message about the journey. It was later found, carefully preserved, when the spacecraft was put in a museum.

On each orbit, when Columbia comes round from the far side, the Earth rises over the moon's horizon. Collins watches it every time. He never gets tired of the sight. The Earth looks so small and so far away, a little blue and white marble in the blackness. He thinks of his wife and children down there. And he thinks that everything he has ever loved is on that little ball.
