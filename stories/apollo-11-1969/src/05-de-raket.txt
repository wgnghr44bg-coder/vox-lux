The rocket that will carry Apollo 11 to the moon is called the Saturn V. It is one of the most amazing machines ever built.

The Saturn V is a hundred and ten metres tall. That is taller than the Statue of Liberty, taller than a thirty-storey building. It is white, with black markings and the letters USA down its side. When it is full of fuel, it weighs almost three million kilograms. That is as heavy as about four hundred elephants. Or about thirty blue whales.

--

The rocket is made of three parts, called stages, one on top of the other. The first stage is at the bottom. It is the biggest, with five huge engines. It burns for only two and a half minutes. In that time, it uses up an enormous amount of fuel, and lifts the rocket high into the sky. Then it falls away into the ocean. The second stage takes over, with five smaller engines, and carries the rocket almost into space. Then it falls away too. The third stage carries the astronauts into orbit around the Earth, and later sends them toward the moon.

{img:saturn-v-stages-diagram-style-cutaway-engines-fuel-tanks-illustration}
On top of the third stage, there is the spacecraft itself. It is quite small compared with the rocket. It has three parts too. There is the command module, a little cone where the three astronauts will live. There is the service module, a cylinder with an engine, oxygen and power, joined to the command module. And there is the lunar module, the little spacecraft with legs that will land on the moon.

--

The command module of Apollo 11 is called Columbia. It is about three metres high and four metres wide at the bottom. Inside, there is about as much space as inside a large car. The three astronauts will live in it for eight days. They will sleep, eat, work and look out of its little windows. It has hundreds of switches and dials and little lights. It is the only part that will come back to Earth.

{img:command-module-columbia-interior-three-couches-switches-small-windows}
The lunar module is called Eagle. It looks a little strange, like a big golden spider. It has four long legs, a body covered with shiny gold foil, and little windows like eyes. It is very light and thin, because it only has to fly in space and on the moon, where there is no air. In some places, its walls are as thin as a few sheets of paper. On Earth, it could not even stand up under its own weight. But on the moon, where everything weighs much less, it will work perfectly.

{img:lunar-module-eagle-gold-foil-four-legs-spider-shape-hangar-workers}
Thousands of people worked to build the Saturn V and the spacecraft. In factories in California and Alabama, New York and Louisiana, men and women built every part by hand, very carefully. Some women sewed the spacesuits and the parachutes, stitch by stitch. Others wove the computer memories, threading tiny wires through little rings by hand. Everything had to be perfect. Nobody wanted to make a mistake. Because three men's lives depended on it.

The rocket is assembled in a huge building at Cape Kennedy, called the Vehicle Assembly Building. It is one of the biggest buildings in the world. It is so big that, on humid days, little clouds can form near the ceiling inside. When the rocket is ready, it is carried slowly out to the launch pad on a giant crawler. The crawler moves very slowly, slower than a person walking. It takes many hours to travel just a few kilometres.

The fuel for the rocket is very cold. Some of it is liquid oxygen and liquid hydrogen, which must be kept at hundreds of degrees below zero. When the rocket is full of fuel, ice forms on its outside. Little pieces of ice fall off it as it stands on the pad. White clouds of vapour drift around it in the warm Florida air. It looks as if the rocket is breathing.

The computer that will help to fly the spacecraft is very small for its time, about the size of a suitcase. But it is very slow and simple compared to computers today. It has less memory than a modern calculator. Its programs were written by a team of clever programmers. One of the leaders of the team was a young woman called Margaret Hamilton. Her careful work would help to save the landing on the moon.
