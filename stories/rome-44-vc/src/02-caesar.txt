Who is Julius Caesar?

In the winter of 44 before Christ, he is fifty-five years old. He is tall, and slim, and pale, with dark, piercing eyes, and a high forehead. He is going bald, and is rather sensitive about it, and likes to wear a wreath of laurel leaves on his head, which the Senate has given him the right to wear at all times. He dresses carefully, and rather unusually, with a loose belt, and long sleeves, fringed at the wrists.

--

He was born in the year 100 before Christ, in Rome, into an old and noble family, the Julii, who claim to be descended from the goddess Venus herself, through her grandson Iulus, the son of the hero Aeneas, who escaped from the burning city of Troy.

But his family is not rich, or very powerful, when he is young. He grows up in an ordinary part of the city, the Subura, a crowded, noisy neighbourhood of shops and apartment houses, among ordinary people. Perhaps this is why he always understands the common people of Rome, and why they love him.

His father dies when he is fifteen. His mother, Aurelia, a wise and strong woman, brings him up, and is one of the most important people in his life, until she passes away, ten years before the events of tonight.

--

As a young man, Caesar is clever, and charming, and very ambitious.

He is a brilliant speaker, one of the best in Rome. He writes well too, in a clear, plain, simple Latin, which is still read in schools today. He is generous, and spends huge sums of money, much of it borrowed, on games and shows and feasts for the people, to win their favour. At one time, he owes so much money that he cannot leave Rome until a rich friend promises to pay his debts.

{img:roman-orator-speaking-crowd-forum-rostra-sunlit}
There is a famous story about him. When he is about twenty-five, he is captured by pirates, on his way to Greece, and held for ransom. The pirates ask for twenty talents of silver. Caesar laughs, and says that he is worth at least fifty. While he waits for the ransom to arrive, he joins in the pirates' games, and reads them his poems and speeches, and tells them, laughing, that one day he will come back and punish them all. They think it is a joke. It is not. When the ransom is paid, and he is free, he gathers some ships, and comes back, and captures them, just as he said he would.

--

Step by step, Caesar rises to the top of Roman politics.

He is elected to one office after another. He becomes Pontifex Maximus, the chief priest of Rome, which is a great honour, and gives him a fine house in the Forum. He becomes governor of Spain. And in 59 before Christ, he is elected consul, the highest office in the Republic, for the first time.

Then he becomes governor of Gaul. And for nine years, he fights in Gaul, at the head of his legions, and conquers the whole country, and becomes enormously rich, and enormously famous, and his soldiers love him.

--

But his enemies in the Senate are afraid of him. They think he is becoming too powerful, and that he wants to make himself king.

The Romans hate the idea of a king. More than four hundred and fifty years ago, they drove out their last king, Tarquin the Proud, and set up the Republic, in which the people elect their own leaders, every year, and no one man can hold power for long. For the Romans, the word king, rex, is the most hateful word in the language.

{img:river-rubicon-small-stream-winter-soldiers-crossing}
In January of the year 49 before Christ, the Senate orders Caesar to give up his army, and come back to Rome as an ordinary citizen. Caesar knows that, if he does, his enemies will put him on trial, and his career will be over. So instead, he leads his army across a little river, the Rubicon, which is the border between his province and Italy. It is an act of war. He is supposed to have said, as he crossed: the die is cast.

--

The civil war lasts for four years, and is fought all around the Mediterranean: in Italy, and Spain, and Greece, and Egypt, and Africa.

Caesar's great rival, Pompey the Great, is defeated at the battle of Pharsalus, in Greece, and flees to Egypt, where he is killed, on the beach, by the advisers of the young king of Egypt, who hope to please Caesar. When Caesar arrives in Egypt, and is shown the head of his old friend and rival, it is said that he turns away, and weeps.

{img:caesar-tent-maps-lamp-night-campaign-thoughtful}
In Egypt, Caesar meets the young queen, Cleopatra, and becomes her friend and her lover, and helps her to win the throne of Egypt. Then he goes on to fight in Asia Minor, where he wins a battle so quickly that he sends a famous message home to Rome, just three words: veni, vidi, vici. I came, I saw, I conquered.

--

At last, in 45 before Christ, after the last battle, in Spain, the civil war is over, and Caesar comes home to Rome, as master of the Roman world.

He celebrates great triumphs, magnificent processions through the streets of Rome, with his soldiers and his captives and the treasures he has won, and gives feasts for the whole city, at thousands of tables, and games and shows, and money to every citizen.

And the Senate, full of his friends, gives him one honour after another. He is made dictator, an old office that was meant to be held for only six months, in times of emergency. First for ten years. And then, in February of the year 44 before Christ, dictator for life. Dictator perpetuo.

--

Caesar is not a cruel man, for a Roman of his time.

After the civil war, he forgives many of his enemies, and lets them come back to Rome, and keep their property, and even gives them important positions. He is proud of his mercy, his clementia, as the Romans call it. He does not want to be like the dictators of the past, who killed their enemies by the thousand.

{img:caesar-receiving-former-enemies-courtyard-gesture}
But some of the men he has forgiven do not want to be forgiven. They do not want to owe their lives to Caesar. And some of them begin to think that, for the sake of the Republic, Caesar must be stopped.
