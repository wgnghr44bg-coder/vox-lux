The plotters had hoped that, with Caesar gone, the old Republic would come back, and Rome would be free and at peace again.

It does not happen. Instead, there are more wars.

--

For more than a year, Antony and Octavian and the Senate and the plotters struggle against each other for power, in a confusing series of quarrels and alliances and battles.

At first, Octavian joins the Senate against Antony. Then, in the autumn of 43, he changes sides, and makes an alliance with Antony, and with Lepidus. The three men meet on a little island in a river near Bononia, the modern Bologna, and agree to rule Rome together, as a commission of three, a triumvirate.

They draw up a list of their enemies, who are to be put to death. It is a cruel and terrible time. Many good men lose their lives, and many more flee from Italy.

--

Cicero is on the list.

Antony hates him. Because in the year after Caesar's death, Cicero gave a series of fourteen great speeches against Antony, in the Senate and in the Forum, which he called the Philippics, after the speeches that the great Greek orator Demosthenes once gave against King Philip of Macedon. They are the last and some of the finest speeches of his life.

{img:coastal-villa-formiae-sea-evening-calm-cypress}
In December of 43, Cicero is at his house by the sea, at Formiae. His servants try to carry him to the coast in a litter, to escape by ship. But the soldiers find him on the road, among the trees. Cicero, the story says, leans out of his litter, calmly, and looks at them, and does not try to escape. He is sixty-three years old.

--

Years later, the story goes, Octavian, now the emperor Augustus, finds one of his grandsons reading a book by Cicero, and the boy, frightened, tries to hide it under his cloak.

Augustus takes the book, and stands there, reading a long part of it. Then he gives it back to the boy, and says: he was a learned man, my child, a learned man, and a lover of his country.

Cicero's books, and his letters, saved by his faithful secretary Tiro, and by his friend Atticus, will be read for two thousand years. In the Middle Ages, and in the Renaissance, he will be the great model of how to write Latin. And the founders of the United States will read him, and think about his ideas of the Republic, and of liberty, and of the duties of a citizen.

--

Brutus and Cassius, meanwhile, have gone to the East, to Greece and Asia Minor, and have gathered great armies.

In the autumn of 42, at Philippi, in northern Greece, there are two great battles, between the armies of Brutus and Cassius, on one side, and the armies of Antony and Octavian, on the other.

{img:plains-philippi-greece-hills-autumn-quiet-distance}
After the first battle, Cassius, thinking wrongly that all is lost, takes his own life. And after the second battle, three weeks later, Brutus, defeated, does the same.

Plutarch tells a story, that one night before the battles, Brutus, sitting alone in his tent, by the light of a lamp, reading, as he always did late into the night, saw a strange figure standing beside him, who said: I am your evil spirit, Brutus. You will see me at Philippi.

--

Almost all of the plotters lose their lives within three years of the Ides of March, in battle, or by their own hand, or in other ways. None of them, it is said, outlives Caesar by more than a few years.

After Philippi, Antony goes to the East, and Octavian stays in Rome. And in the East, Antony meets Cleopatra, the queen of Egypt, again, at Tarsus, where she comes to meet him, sailing up the river in a golden barge, with purple sails, and silver oars, dressed as the goddess Venus.

{img:golden-barge-purple-sails-river-tarsus-sunset}
Antony falls in love with her, and goes with her to Alexandria, and stays there. And they have three children together.

--

For years, Antony and Octavian share the Roman world: Antony in the East, Octavian in the West. But at last, they quarrel.

In the year 31 before Christ, at Actium, off the coast of Greece, Octavian's fleet, commanded by his old friend Agrippa, defeats the fleet of Antony and Cleopatra. The next year, in Alexandria, Antony and Cleopatra both take their own lives, rather than be taken prisoner and led through the streets of Rome.

Cleopatra's son by Caesar, Caesarion, who is seventeen, is also lost, on Octavian's orders. There can be only one son of Caesar.

--

And Octavian, at the age of thirty-three, is now the sole master of the whole Roman world.

He is clever enough not to call himself king, or even dictator. He remembers what happened to Caesar. He says that he has restored the Republic. He keeps the Senate, and the consuls, and all the old offices, and the old forms. But in fact, he holds all the power.

{img:augustus-mature-statue-toga-calm-forum-dignified}
In the year 27 before Christ, the Senate gives him the name Augustus, which means the revered one. He rules for forty-one years, until he passes away, an old man, in the year 14 after Christ. He brings peace to the Roman world, after a century of civil wars. The Romans will call it the Pax Augusta, or the Pax Romana, the Roman peace.

--

He is the first of the Roman emperors. The Republic, which Brutus and Cassius wanted to save, is gone forever.

And the month after July, which was called Sextilis, the sixth month, is renamed in his honour: Augustus. August.
