The family's house is built around a courtyard.

From the street, you see only a wall and a gate. The gate has two wooden doors painted black. Above it is a little roof of grey tiles. Inside the gate there is a short wall. It stands right in front of you, so you have to walk around it. People say bad spirits can only move in straight lines. So the little wall keeps them out.

--

Then you step into the courtyard. It is a square of beaten earth with a well in one corner. A small tree grows in the middle. In summer it gives shade. Now its branches are bare. Rooms stand on all four sides. The best room faces south, toward the sun. That is where the grandparents live. The father and mother have rooms on the east side. The children sleep on the west side. The kitchen and the store rooms and the servants' rooms are near the gate.

The rooms have wooden floors raised a little above the ground. People sit on mats on the floor. They kneel, sitting back on their heels. There are no chairs yet in China. Low tables stand in front of them. When guests come, the best mats are rolled out. The most honoured guest sits facing east.

{img:han-room-interior-low-table-mats-folding-screen-lamp-winter}
In winter the house is cold. The walls are thin and the windows are covered with paper or cloth. People wear many layers of padded clothes. They warm their hands over small bronze braziers full of glowing charcoal. In the north, some houses have a raised brick bed with a fire underneath. It is called a kang. The whole family gathers on it on the coldest nights.

--

At night the rooms are lit by little lamps. Some are made of bronze. One famous Han lamp is shaped like a kneeling girl holding a lantern. The smoke goes up through her arm and into her body. That way the room stays clean. Most families have simpler lamps of clay. They burn oil and give a soft yellow light.

{img:han-bronze-lamp-kneeling-figure-glowing-soft-light-dark-room}
Every corner of the house has its own small spirit. The spirit of the gate. The spirit of the well. The spirit of the stove. In the New Year weeks, all of them must be honoured. If one is forgotten, people believe, the new year could bring trouble. So the grandmother keeps a list in her head. And she checks it every day.
