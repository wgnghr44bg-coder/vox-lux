Let us meet a family in Luoyang.

The father is a small official. He works in one of the government offices near the palace. He keeps records and writes letters. He is not rich. But he is respected. He studied the old books when he was young. He can read and write well. And he hopes his son will one day do even better.

--

The mother runs the house. She manages the money and the servants and the food. She weaves silk on a loom in a room off the courtyard. She is clever and careful. In the New Year weeks, she is the busiest person in the family. Everything must be ready in time. And everything depends on her.

There are three children. The eldest is a boy of twelve. He studies the old books every morning with a teacher. He finds them hard. There is a girl of nine. She helps her mother and learns to weave and sew. And there is a little boy of four. He mostly runs around and gets in everyone's way.

{img:han-grandmother-sitting-mat-children-listening-brazier-warm-room}
And there are the grandparents. The father's mother and father live with them. In China, that is the right way. The oldest son takes care of his parents. The grandfather is seventy. He has a long white beard and walks with a stick. The grandmother is sixty-five. She knows every old custom and every old story. And she makes sure nobody forgets them.

--

There are also two servants. A cook and a young girl who helps with the cleaning. And there is a dog that sleeps by the kitchen fire. And a cat that sleeps wherever it likes.

{img:han-mother-checking-store-room-jars-grain-new-year-list}
This year the New Year worries the mother a little. The harvest was not good in the countryside. Prices in the market have gone up. Rice and wine and meat cost more than last year. She counts the coins in the family box. She looks at the store room. Will there be enough for a proper New Year? Enough food for the family and the guests? Enough for the gifts to the ancestors? She is not sure. But she does not say anything yet. There are still a few weeks to go.

In the evenings, when the work is done, the whole family sits together in the main room. The grandfather reads quietly from an old book, moving his lips as he reads. The grandmother sews by the light of the lamp. The girl practises her stitches on a small piece of cloth. The eldest son copies characters onto his wooden board. The little boy plays with the cat, who lets him for a while and then walks away. And the mother sits with her hands still at last, watching them all, and thinking about the New Year.

The grandfather has a favourite place in the courtyard, a stone bench under an old plum tree. Even in winter, he sits there for a while every morning, wrapped in his thick padded coat. He watches the sparrows hopping in the snow and listens to the sounds of the house. The little boy often comes to sit beside him. They do not say very much. But the grandfather lets the boy hold his walking stick, and that is a great honour.
