Luoyang is the capital of the Han empire. It is one of the biggest cities in the world.

It lies in a wide valley between hills, beside the river Luo. That is how it got its name. Luo-yang means the sunny side of the Luo. The city is shaped like a long box. Thick walls of pounded earth surround it. They are about ten metres high. You could walk around the whole wall in half a day. Twelve gates lead in and out. Each gate has a name and a tower on top.

--

Inside, there are two great palaces, one in the north and one in the south. A covered walkway joins them. The emperor can walk between his palaces without ever stepping into the street. Around the palaces are the offices of the government, the houses of nobles, the barracks of the guards, and the homes of ordinary people.

The streets are straight and wide. They cross each other like the lines on a game board. Rows of trees line the main streets. At night the gates of each neighbourhood are closed. Watchmen walk the streets with drums. Nobody should be out after dark without a good reason.

{img:han-luoyang-street-wooden-houses-tiled-roofs-carts-people-snow}
The houses are built of wood and pounded earth. Their roofs are covered with grey clay tiles. The tiles curve down at the edges. At the end of each row of tiles is a round disk with a pattern or a lucky word. Some say long life. Some say ten thousand years of joy.

Perhaps half a million people live in and around Luoyang. Officials and soldiers. Traders and craftsmen. Scholars and servants. And people from far away. Travellers from the western deserts with camels. Monks from India. Envoys from the north and the south. Luoyang is the centre of the world, as far as the Han are concerned.

--

{img:luoyang-markets-han-dynasty-stalls-silk-pottery-crowds-cold}
The city has three great markets. The biggest is in the south-west. It is a large square surrounded by walls. Inside are rows of shops, each row for one kind of goods. A tall drum tower stands in the middle. When the drum sounds in the morning, the market opens. When it sounds in the evening, the market closes. Officials walk around and check the prices and the scales. Anyone caught cheating will be in trouble.

In the last month of the year, the markets are busier than ever. Everyone is buying for the New Year. And the drum tower can hardly keep up.

--

The father of our family walks through this market every day on his way to work. Today he stops at a stall that sells writing brushes. He looks at a fine brush with a handle of red lacquer. It would make a good New Year gift for his son. He asks the price. It is too much. He puts it back and walks on. But he keeps thinking about it all day.
