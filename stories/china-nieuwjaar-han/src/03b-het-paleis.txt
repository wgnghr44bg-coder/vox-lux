In the north of Luoyang stands the emperor's palace.

It is a whole world inside walls. There are great halls with red pillars and roofs of shining tiles. There are courtyards paved with stone and gardens with ponds and bridges. There are store rooms and kitchens and stables. And there are thousands of people. Officials and guards. Cooks and musicians. Doctors and writers. And the ladies of the palace with their servants.

--

The grandest building is the hall where the emperor meets his officials. It stands on a high platform of earth. Wide steps lead up to it. Its roof is huge and heavy, held up by rows of thick wooden pillars painted red. Inside it is dim and cold in winter. Braziers of glowing charcoal stand along the walls. They give a little warmth.

The emperor sits at the far end, on a raised seat. He wears a robe of black and yellow silk. On his head is a flat board with strings of jade beads hanging down in front and behind. The beads hide his eyes a little. Nobody looks straight at the emperor. People bow low to the ground before him.

{img:han-palace-officials-bowing-hall-emperor-distant-dais-incense}
The emperor is called the Son of Heaven. People believe heaven gives him the right to rule. But only if he rules well. If he is cruel or careless, heaven may send warnings. Floods. Droughts. Earthquakes. Strange lights in the sky. Then people whisper that heaven is unhappy. So the court watches the sky and the seasons very closely. And the New Year matters a great deal. If the year starts well, it is a good sign for everyone.

--

In the last month of the year, the palace is very busy. The halls must be swept and polished. New robes must be sewn for the ceremonies. Food and wine must be gathered for great feasts. Gifts arrive from every corner of the empire. Silk and lacquer and jade. Horses and fruit and rare birds.

{img:han-palace-kitchen-cooks-steaming-baskets-new-year-feast-preparing}
The palace kitchens work day and night. Cooks steam bread and boil meat. They make sauces from beans and salt fish. They stack food in tall lacquered boxes, red and black. There are hundreds of mouths to feed. And on New Year's Day, every guest at court will expect a feast.

Deep inside the palace, there are quiet gardens with ponds and little bridges. In winter, the ponds are covered with thin ice. Old gardeners sweep the snow from the paths every morning with long brooms. They wrap the young trees in straw, to keep them warm until spring. Sometimes a lady of the court walks slowly along the paths, with her hands tucked into her sleeves. She stops on a bridge to watch the goldfish moving slowly under the ice. Then she walks on, and the garden is silent again.

Outside the palace walls, the people of Luoyang sometimes stop to look at the great gates. Guards in red stand on either side, holding long spears. Officials in tall hats come and go in carts pulled by horses. Children stare at the gilded roofs rising above the walls. They wonder what it is like inside, where the emperor lives. Their parents tell them it is like a whole city of its own. And they walk on, still looking back over their shoulders.
