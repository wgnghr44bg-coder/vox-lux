Before the new year comes, the front gate of the house must be protected.

For this, people use peach wood. The peach tree is special in China. Its fruit is a sign of long life. And its wood, people believe, has power over bad spirits. They are afraid of it.

--

So at New Year, families hang two boards of peach wood on their gate. One on each door. On the boards, they paint or carve two figures. They are two brothers, the door gods. Their names are Shentu and Yulü.

An old Han story explains why. Far out in the eastern sea, there is a mountain. On the mountain stands a huge peach tree. Its branches spread for many kilometres. Between its branches, in the north-east, is a gate. Through that gate, all the spirits come and go. And at the gate stand two brothers, Shentu and Yulü. They watch every spirit that passes through. If a spirit has done harm to people, they tie it up with a rope of reeds. And they feed it to a tiger.

{img:giant-mythical-peach-tree-mountain-sea-gate-two-guardians-misty}
So people paint the two brothers on their gates. They hang a rope of reeds there too. And sometimes they paint a tiger on the wall nearby. That way no harmful spirit dares to come in.

--

It is a fierce story. But for the people of Luoyang, it is a comfort. It means the house is safe. The two brothers stand guard all year. They look stern. But they are on the family's side.

Later in Chinese history, other door gods will take their place. Famous generals painted in bright colours. And much later, people will hang red paper with lucky words on each side of the door. That custom grows out of the old peach wood boards. Even today, at Chinese New Year, millions of front doors have red paper and painted guardians. And the idea started here, with two brothers and a peach tree.

{img:han-father-hanging-peach-boards-gate-son-holding-ladder-evening}
In our family, the father and the eldest son hang up the new peach boards on New Year's Eve. The old ones from last year come down. The boy has painted the faces himself this year. He took a lot of care. But one brother looks a bit cross-eyed. The father looks at it for a long time. Will he make him do it again? Then he smiles. It will scare the spirits even more, he says. And they hang it up.
