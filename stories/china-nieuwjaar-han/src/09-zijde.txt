In the weeks before New Year, there is one sound you hear in many houses of Luoyang. It is the soft clack and thump of the loom.

China is the land of silk. For thousands of years, Chinese families have kept silkworms. They are small white caterpillars that eat only the leaves of the mulberry tree. When they are fully grown, each one spins a little case around itself, made of one very long, very fine thread. This case is called a cocoon. One cocoon can hold a thread almost a kilometre long.

--

To get the silk, the cocoons are dropped into hot water. That loosens the thread. Then a woman finds the end of the thread and gently unwinds it. Several threads are twisted together to make one stronger thread. It is slow and careful work. One thread breaks, and you must start that part again.

Then the thread is dyed and woven on a loom. Han silk can be plain and light. Or it can be thick and heavy, with patterns of clouds and animals and words woven into it. Some silk is so fine that a whole robe weighs less than fifty grams. That is less than a small apple.

{img:mulberry-trees-silkworms-trays-leaves-han-farm-spring-women}
Silk is very precious. It is worn by the rich. It is given as gifts and paid as taxes. It is even used as money. Rolls of silk of a fixed length can buy grain or horses or land. And it travels far. Along the roads to the west, through the deserts, all the way to Rome. The Romans love Chinese silk. They do not know how it is made. Some of them think it grows on trees.

--

The Chinese keep the secret carefully. Taking silkworms out of China is strictly forbidden. For hundreds of years, nobody outside China knows how silk is made. It is one of the best-kept secrets in history.

In our family, the mother weaves silk in the evenings and in the winter months. The loom stands in a room by the courtyard. Her daughter sits beside her and watches. Sometimes she is allowed to throw the shuttle, the small wooden piece that carries the thread across the loom. Her hands are getting quicker. Next year, her mother says, you will weave your first piece on your own. The daughter is excited and a little nervous. What if she makes a mistake? Then you start again, says her mother. That is how everyone learns.
