On the last day of the year, there is a great event at the palace in Luoyang. It is called the Great Nuo. It is a parade to chase out the bad spirits of the old year.

People believe that sickness and bad luck gather over the year like dust. Before the new year can begin, all of it must be swept away. So at the palace, a huge ceremony is held. And the whole city waits for it.

--

It begins in the evening. One hundred and twenty boys gather in the palace. They are between ten and twelve years old. Each wears a red cap and a black tunic. Each carries a big drum shaped like a hand drum with little beads that rattle. They are the helpers.

Then the leader appears. He is called the exorcist. He wears a bearskin over his shoulders. His face is covered with a golden mask with four eyes. He wears a black coat and a red skirt. In one hand he holds a spear and in the other a shield. He looks strange and fierce. But he is on the people's side. His job is to frighten away whatever should not stay.

{img:han-boys-red-caps-drums-parade-palace-courtyard-torches-night}
Behind him come twelve dancers dressed as animals. They wear masks and fur and feathers. Each one stands for a spirit that chases away a different kind of trouble. One eats bad dreams. One eats sickness. One eats bad luck. And so on.

--

The parade moves through every hall of the palace. The boys beat their drums and shout. The exorcist stamps and waves his spear into every corner. They sing a song together. It tells the bad spirits to leave right now, or the twelve animals will catch them. The song is loud and a little silly. Some of the officials watching try not to smile.

Then the whole parade goes out of the palace gates with flaming torches. Riders take the torches and gallop out of the city. They throw them into the river Luo. The flames hiss out in the dark water. And that is the sign. The bad spirits have been carried far away. The city is clean.

{img:horsemen-carrying-torches-galloping-toward-river-night-sparks}
In the streets of Luoyang, people crowd to watch the torches race past. Children sit on their fathers' shoulders. Some of the smaller ones hide their faces when the masked dancers come by. But the grown-ups laugh and cheer.

--

In the villages too, people hold their own small Nuo. Young men put on masks and go from house to house, beating drums. They chase the old year out of every courtyard. And families give them food and a few coins.

{img:village-nuo-masked-young-men-drums-house-to-house-winter-dusk}
In some parts of China, these masked dances are still performed today, almost two thousand years later.
