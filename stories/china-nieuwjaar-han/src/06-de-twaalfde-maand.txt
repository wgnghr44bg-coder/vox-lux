The last month of the year is called the La month. La is the name of a great winter offering.

Long ago, at the end of the year, people went hunting. They offered the animals they caught to their ancestors and to the gods of the fields. They thanked them for the harvest. And they asked for a good year to come. In the Han time, the La offering is still one of the biggest days of the year. It falls a few days after midwinter.

--

On La day, families honour their ancestors at home. They lay out meat and grain and wine on a table. They light incense. They bow and say the names of the dead. In the villages, people gather to thank the spirits of the soil and the grain. There is music and drinking and dancing. Farmers who have worked hard all year can rest a little.

A special porridge is cooked in this month in many places. It is made of grains and beans and nuts and dried fruit, all boiled together. Later it will be called La-month porridge. It is thick and sweet and warm. It fills you up on a cold day. Families share it with neighbours and give some to the poor.

{img:han-family-cooking-grain-porridge-big-pot-kitchen-steam-winter}
In the La month, people also try to settle what is left over from the old year. Debts must be paid. Borrowed things must be given back. Quarrels must be made up. Nobody wants to carry old troubles into the new year. So people knock on doors with money in their hands. They return a borrowed pot or a borrowed book. They send small gifts to neighbours they have argued with.

--

In our family, the father has a small debt. In the summer, he borrowed money from a colleague to fix the roof after a storm. Now he must pay it back before the year ends. He counts the coins carefully. It will leave the family with less for the New Year. But he does not want to start the year owing money. That would be bad luck. And bad manners. So one cold evening he walks to his colleague's house and pays it all. His colleague smiles and pours him a cup of warm wine. And the father walks home lighter, even though his purse is lighter too.

{img:han-man-walking-home-snowy-street-evening-lantern-relieved}
At home, the mother has been thinking too. She has decided to sell a roll of the silk she wove this year. It is fine silk, the best she has made. She was saving it for her daughter. But a good New Year matters more right now. Tomorrow she will take it to the market. She hopes she will get a fair price.

That evening, the mother sits by the loom and runs her fingers over the roll of silk. It is soft and smooth, with a faint pattern of little clouds. She remembers the many evenings she spent weaving it, while the children slept. Her daughter comes and sits beside her. It is beautiful, the girl says softly. Yes, says her mother. And I will weave another one, even more beautiful, just for you. The girl leans against her shoulder, and they sit quietly together for a while.
