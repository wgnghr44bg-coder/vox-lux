In Luoyang there is a great school. It is called the Imperial Academy.

It was founded long ago by a Han emperor. He wanted the best young men of the empire to study the old books together. And then to serve the government. In the time of our story, the Academy is huge. Perhaps thirty thousand students live and study there. That makes it one of the biggest schools in the ancient world.

--

The students study the five classics. These are very old books of poems and history and rituals and sayings. They are written in an old style that is hard to understand. The students learn them by heart. They study what the old teachers said about them. And they argue about what every line means.

The best students take examinations. If they pass, they can become officials. That is the dream of many families. A son who becomes an official brings honour to his parents and his ancestors. And a steady income too.

{img:stone-classics-tablets-row-scholars-copying-texts-han-courtyard}
In the years after our story, there will be a famous project at the Academy. The classics will be carved in stone, on great flat tablets. They will be set up in a long row in front of the Academy. That way everyone can read the true words, without mistakes from copying. Scholars will come from all over China to copy them. So many people will come that the streets will be blocked with carts.

--

At New Year, many students go home to their families. But some live too far away. They stay in Luoyang. They celebrate together, far from home. They share what little food they have. They write poems and letters to their families. And they wonder how things are back in their villages.

The eldest son of our family dreams of going to the Academy one day. But the studying is hard. Some days he wonders if he is clever enough. His father went there for two years when he was young. But he never passed the highest examination. He became a small official instead. He does not want his son to feel the same pressure he felt.

{img:father-and-son-walking-past-academy-gate-evening-snow-talking}
On the fourth day of the new year, the father takes his son for a walk past the Academy. They stand at the gate and look in at the courtyards and the halls. Do you want to study here one day, the father asks. The boy thinks for a long time. I do not know, he says honestly. I want to. But I am afraid I will fail. The father puts his hand on the boy's shoulder. Then we will find out together, he says. And they walk home through the snow.
