In the time of our story, Luoyang is home to some of the cleverest people in the world. One of them is a man called Zhang Heng.

He has passed away not long before our story. But everyone in Luoyang still knows his name. He was a poet and a painter. He was a mathematician and an astronomer. He was the head of the court astronomers. And he was an inventor.

--

Zhang Heng studied the stars. He made a map of the sky with more than two thousand five hundred stars. He built a great bronze globe with rings around it. It showed the movements of the stars and the sun. Water turned it slowly, so it kept pace with the real sky. He understood that the moon does not make its own light. It shines because the sun lights it up.

And he built a famous machine to detect earthquakes. Earthquakes happen often in China. They can be terrible. The court wanted to know about them quickly, even when they happened far away. So Zhang Heng built a great bronze jar about two metres wide. Around the outside were eight dragons, facing eight directions. Each dragon held a small bronze ball in its mouth. Below each dragon sat a bronze toad with its mouth open.

{img:han-seismoscope-bronze-jar-eight-dragons-toads-balls-palace-room}
When the earth shook, a weight inside the jar moved. It pushed a little lever. And one dragon dropped its ball. Clang! The ball fell into the toad's mouth below. The direction of that dragon showed where the earthquake had come from.

--

At first, nobody believed it worked. One day, a ball dropped. Clang. But nobody in Luoyang had felt anything. The officials laughed. Zhang Heng's machine is broken, they said. Then, a few days later, a messenger arrived on a tired horse. He came from a province far to the west. There had been an earthquake there. In exactly the direction the dragon had pointed. After that, nobody laughed.

The machine is lost now. But people have tried to rebuild it from the old descriptions. And it is still one of the most famous inventions of ancient China.

{img:han-astronomers-night-sky-luoyang-tower-new-year-stars-cold}
On New Year's night, the court astronomers are on their tower as always. They watch the sky. They look for anything unusual. A new star, a comet, a strange light. Anything that might be a message from heaven about the year ahead. This year the sky is clear and calm. The stars are in their places. The astronomers write it down carefully. A good sign, they report to the emperor. And everyone in the palace is glad.

Zhang Heng was not only a scientist but also a poet and a painter. In his poems, he wrote about the city of Luoyang and the beauty of the seasons. He wrote about spring flowers and autumn moons, and the quiet joy of a simple life. In the evenings, after a long day of work, he liked to sit in his garden. He looked up at the stars he knew so well, and he felt at peace. And perhaps, on New Year's night, he did the same.
