On the fifteenth day of the first month, there is the first full moon of the new year. And on that night, the city glows with lanterns.

This is the last night of the New Year celebrations. After this, everyone goes back to work. So people want to make it special.

--

In later centuries, the Lantern Festival will be one of the great festivals of China. People will hang lanterns of every shape and colour all over the cities. Lanterns shaped like fish and rabbits and dragons. There will be riddles written on the lanterns. People will walk the streets all night.

In the Han time, the festival is just beginning. An old story says it started with a Han emperor. He had heard about the Buddha from far away. On the night of the first full moon, he ordered that lamps be lit in the palace and in the temples, to honour the Buddha. And the people of the city lit lamps too.

{img:white-horse-temple-luoyang-han-lamps-monks-night-full-moon}
Whether that story is true, nobody knows. But the Buddha's teaching really did arrive in Luoyang in the Han time. The story says that a Han emperor once dreamed of a golden figure flying over his palace. His advisers told him it must be the Buddha, a holy man from the west. So the emperor sent messengers to find out more. They came back with two monks from India. And the monks came with a white horse carrying holy books.

--

The emperor built them a temple outside Luoyang. It was called the White Horse Temple, after the horse. It is said to be the first Buddhist temple in China. It still stands near Luoyang today. And every year, thousands of visitors walk through its gates.

On the night of the first full moon, the people of Luoyang go out. They walk through the streets in the cold air. Lamps glow in doorways and windows. The full moon rises huge and white over the city walls. Children carry little lamps of their own.

{img:han-children-carrying-small-lamps-street-full-moon-snow-parents}
In our family, the children each have a little clay lamp. The father lights them from the kitchen fire. The family walks together through the neighbourhood. The little boy holds his lamp very carefully with both hands. He walks slowly so it will not go out. A gust of wind comes down the street. His flame flickers. He stops. He holds his breath. The flame bends and shrinks. And then it stands up straight again. He looks up at his mother with a huge smile.

--

They walk until their feet are cold. Then they go home. The moon follows them all the way. The New Year is over now. Tomorrow, life will go back to normal. But tonight, the whole city is shining.

The children each carry a small lantern, made of paper on a frame of thin bamboo. The girl's lantern is shaped like a rabbit, with little painted ears. The little boy's lantern is round and red, like a small sun. He holds it up very carefully, so the candle inside will not go out. Every few steps he stops to look at it, and his face glows in the soft red light. The eldest son carries his little brother on his shoulders for the last part of the walk home.
