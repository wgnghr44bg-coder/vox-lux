The Han dynasty is one of the great ages of China. A dynasty is a family of rulers, one after another. The Han ruled China for about four hundred years. They started a little more than two hundred years before Christ.

Before them, China had been split into many warring kingdoms. Then a fierce king joined them all together. He made himself the first emperor of China. He built roads and walls. But he was harsh, and people suffered. After he passed away, his empire fell apart quickly. There were years of fighting. And then a man of humble birth won. He was a village official who had become a rebel leader. He became the first emperor of the Han.

--

The Han learned from what had gone wrong. They kept the big empire together. But they tried to rule more gently. They chose officials who had studied the old books. They lowered taxes in good years. And they followed the teachings of a wise man who had lived long before. His name was Confucius.

Confucius taught that a good life is built on kindness and respect. Children should honour their parents. The young should honour the old. Rulers should be fair. And everyone should behave well toward everyone else. In the Han time, his teachings become the heart of Chinese life. And they shape the New Year too. Because the New Year is above all about family.

{img:confucian-scholar-han-dynasty-robes-reading-bamboo-strips-study}
The Han empire is huge. It reaches from the cold grasslands in the north to the warm green hills of the south. It reaches from the sea in the east to the deserts in the far west. Perhaps fifty or sixty million people live under the Han. That is about as many as live in the whole Roman Empire at the same time. Far away to the west, Rome is at its height too. The two great empires know a little about each other. But they will never meet.

--

Our story takes place in the second half of the Han time. The capital has moved east, to Luoyang. Things are mostly peaceful. Farms are busy. Trade is growing. But at court, there are rivals and whispers. Powerful families and palace servants compete for the emperor's ear. Not everyone is sure the peace will last.

{img:han-chinese-farmers-rice-millet-fields-water-buffalo-village}
Most people, though, do not live at court. Nine out of ten people in Han China are farmers. They grow millet and wheat in the north, and rice in the south. They keep pigs and chickens and oxen. They pay taxes in grain and cloth. And every year, when the harvest is in and the cold comes, they turn their thoughts to the New Year.

In a farming village, a family sits by the fire on a winter evening. The father is mending a basket, and the mother is spinning hemp thread. The children are roasting millet cakes on the hot stones. Outside, the fields are bare and frozen, and the ox is asleep in its shed. They talk about the harvest and about the New Year. Perhaps this year, the father says, we can buy a little pork for the feast.
