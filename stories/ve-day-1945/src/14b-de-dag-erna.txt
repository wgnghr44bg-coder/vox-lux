The next day, the ninth of May, is a holiday, too, in Britain. VE Day plus one.

In the morning, London is quiet. The streets are covered with paper, and streamers, and flags, and confetti, and broken bottles, and people are sleeping, in the parks, and on the benches, and on the steps of the buildings, with their flags, wrapped around them.

--

The street sweepers come out, early, with their brooms, and their carts, and begin to clear up. The buses, and the trains, start running again. And slowly, the people wake up, and stretch, and go home, for breakfast.

But by the afternoon, the crowds are back, in the West End, and outside the palace, and in Trafalgar Square, and the celebrations begin again, a little quieter, a little more tired, but still happy.

{img:king-queen-car-driving-east-end-crowds-cheering-1945}
On this day, the King, and the Queen, drive through the East End of London, in an open car, through the streets that were bombed, so badly, during the war, and the people of the East End come out, in their thousands, to cheer them, and wave their flags, from the windows, and the doorways, and the bomb sites.

--

In the streets, the parties go on: the children who had a party yesterday, have another one today, with the food that was left over, and the grown-ups sit outside, in the sunshine, on their chairs, on the pavement, and talk, and drink tea, and rest.

And in many homes, the people sit quietly, and think about everything that has happened, in the six years of the war, and the people they have lost, and the people who are still far away, and they write letters, and read the newspapers, with their great headlines: VICTORY, and IT'S OVER, and the photographs of the crowds, the day before.

{img:family-garden-afternoon-tea-1945-deckchairs-newspaper-headlines}
The newspapers are kept, and folded, and put away, in drawers, and boxes, and attics, as a memory of these days, and many of them are still there, today, yellowed, and fragile, found by grandchildren, and great-grandchildren, clearing out old houses.

--

And on the tenth of May, the people go back to work. The factories start again, and the offices, and the shops, and the schools, and the trains. Life goes on. There is still rationing, and there are still queues, and there are still soldiers far away, and there is still much to do.

But it is different now. The fear has gone. And in the evenings, as the sun sets, the lights come on, in the windows, and the street lamps, and nobody draws the blackout curtains, any more.
