On the evening of VE Day, as the sun goes down, something happens in London that many children have never seen in their lives.

The lights come on.

--

For nearly six years, London has been dark at night. Now, at dusk, the great buildings are lit up for the first time since 1939. Saint Paul's Cathedral, its great dome glowing white and gold against the dark sky. Buckingham Palace. The Houses of Parliament and Big Ben. Nelson's Column and the Tower of London.

And in the sky above the city, the searchlights move. For years they swept the sky at night, looking for enemy planes. Now they make patterns of light. They cross and wave and form great V signs, high over the rooftops, like a great silent firework show.

The ships on the river turn on their lights and fire rockets. In the parks, people light bonfires and dance around them. In the streets, people take down their black curtains. They leave their windows bare and their lights on. Golden warm light pours out onto the streets.

--

A little girl born during the war stands at the window with her mother. She looks out at the street. All the windows are lit. The street lamps are glowing. What is it, she asks. She has never seen a street lit up at night.

All over the country there are bonfires. On the hills and the village greens and the bombed sites. People pile up wood and old furniture and the hated black curtains. They burn them joyfully and dance around the flames.

{img:village-green-bonfire-night-ve-day-people-dancing-around-fire}
People let off the fireworks they have been saving. Rockets go up and burst into showers of coloured stars over the rooftops. And the children cheer.

--

In London the crowds go on all night. People dance in Piccadilly Circus. The famous statue there was taken away during the war, to keep it safe. Only its boarded-up base is left. People climb on it anyway. In Trafalgar Square the singing goes on until dawn.

At last, in the early hours, people walk home through the lit streets. They are tired and happy and hoarse from singing. Some sleep in the parks under the trees, wrapped in their coats and flags. The lights still shine on Saint Paul's. The searchlights slowly fade. And the sky in the east grows pale.

{img:dawn-after-ve-day-london-park-people-sleeping-grass-flags-quiet}
And for the first time in six years, nobody anywhere in Britain is afraid of the night sky.
