On VE Day, all over Britain, the church bells ring.

For most of the war, the church bells were silent. Early in the war, people feared that an enemy army might land in Britain. So the government said the bells must not ring. They would only ring as a warning, if the enemy came. For years, the bells hung silent in their towers. On Sundays the churches were quiet.

--

Later in the war, when the danger had passed, the bells were allowed to ring again on Sundays. But on VE Day they ring as they have never rung before.

In every church tower in the country, the bell ringers climb the narrow winding stairs. At the top is a little room. Ropes hang down from the bells above. Each rope has a soft striped woollen grip, red and white and blue.

{img:bell-ringers-chamber-circle-ropes-sallies-pulling-tower-window}
The ringers stand in a circle, each holding a rope. They pull. The great bells above them swing right round and ring out, one after another. Then the bells change places in the order, again and again. It is like a kind of maths, played on bells.

--

Many of the ringers have been away at war. Some of the ringers are new and young. Some are very old and have rung since they were boys. And they ring and ring all day long. Their arms ache. They do not care.

The sound of the bells spreads over the rooftops and the fields and the woods. One church answers another, from village to village, across the whole country. Wherever you are in Britain on VE Day, you can hear the bells.

{img:village-church-ve-day-congregation-leaving-sunshine-bells-flags}
In London there are great church services to give thanks. People queue in long lines to go in. Saint Paul's Cathedral is full all day. All through the bombing of London, it stood. Fires burned all around it. But its great dome rose out of the smoke. It became a sign of hope for the whole country in the darkest days.

--

In the evening, as the sun sets, the bells ring again. The people of the villages walk to church in the long golden light of a May evening. They sing the old hymns. Then they come out again into the dusk. The bells are ringing. And on the hills, the bonfires begin to glow.
