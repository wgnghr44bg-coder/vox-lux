On VE Day, all over Britain, the church bells ring.

For most of the war, the church bells of Britain were silent. In June 1940, when it was feared that Britain might be invaded, the government ordered that the bells must not be rung, except as a warning, if the enemy came. So, for years, the bells hung silent, in their towers, in every town, and village, and on Sundays, the churches were quiet.

--

In 1942, after a great victory, in the desert, in Egypt, at El Alamein, the bells were rung, once, for a Sunday, to celebrate. And in 1943, when the danger of invasion had passed, the bells were allowed to ring again, on Sundays. But on VE Day, they ring as they have never rung before.

In every church tower, in the country, the bell ringers climb up the narrow, winding stairs, to the ringing chamber, a little room, high up, in the tower, where the ropes hang down, from the bells, above, with their striped woollen grips, called sallies, red, and white, and blue.

{img:bell-ringers-chamber-circle-ropes-sallies-pulling-tower-window}
They stand in a circle, each one holding a rope, and they pull, and the great bells, above them, swing round, full circle, and ring out, one after another, in a falling peal, and then in changes, the patterns of English bell ringing, which are like a kind of mathematics, played on bells, the bells changing places, in the order, every time they ring.

--

Many of the bell ringers have been away, at the war, and some of the ringers are new, and young, and some are very old, and have been ringing since they were boys. And they ring, and ring, all day long, on VE Day, and their arms ache, and they do not care.

And the sound of the bells spreads, out over the rooftops, and the streets, and the fields, and the woods, and the rivers, one church answering another, from village to village, across the whole country, so that, wherever you are, on VE Day, in Britain, you can hear the bells.

{img:village-church-ve-day-congregation-leaving-sunshine-bells-flags}
At Westminster Abbey, and Saint Paul's Cathedral, in London, there are great services of thanksgiving, and the people queue, in long lines, to go in, and give thanks. Saint Paul's Cathedral stood, through all the bombing of London, with the fires, all around it, and its great dome, rising out of the smoke, became a symbol of hope, for the whole country, in the darkest days. On VE Day, it is full, all day, with services, one after another.

--

And in the evening, as the sun sets, the bells ring again, for evensong, and the people of the villages walk to church, in the long, golden light of a May evening, and sing the old hymns, Now Thank We All Our God, and O God, Our Help in Ages Past, and come out, again, into the dusk, and the sound of the bells, and the bonfires, beginning to glow, on the hills.
