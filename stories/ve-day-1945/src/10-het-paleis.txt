In front of Buckingham Palace, on the afternoon of the eighth of May, the crowd is enormous.

It fills the whole space in front of the palace. It stretches back all along the wide road under the trees. Tens of thousands of people wave flags and call out. We want the King! We want the King!

--

The doors to the balcony open. The royal family come out. King George in his navy uniform. Queen Elizabeth in a pale blue dress and hat. And their two daughters. Princess Elizabeth is nineteen. She wears her army uniform. She has been training as a driver and a mechanic. Princess Margaret is fourteen.

The crowd roars. They wave, and the royal family waves back. They stand there for a few minutes, looking out over the great sea of people and flags. Then they go back in.

But the crowd will not go away. They keep calling. So the royal family comes out again and again. Eight times during the afternoon and evening. Once, Mr Churchill comes out with them. He stands beside the King. And the crowd cheers louder than ever.

--

The King and Queen stayed in London all through the war. Even when Buckingham Palace was bombed. They visited the bombed streets and the hospitals and the factories. And the people loved them for it.

And now, this evening, the two princesses ask their parents a question. May they go out into the crowds themselves, just this once? The King and Queen think about it. And they say yes.

{img:princess-elizabeth-margaret-incognito-crowd-mall-evening-officers}
At about ten o'clock in the evening, the two princesses slip out of the palace by a side door. A small group of young officers and friends go with them, to look after them. They go out into the crowds. Nobody knows who they are.

--

Princess Elizabeth pulls her cap down low over her eyes so no one will recognise her. Will anyone notice? Nobody does. They walk through the streets with everyone else, arm in arm, in the dark and the lights. They join in the singing and the dancing among crowds of strangers.

Many years later, when she was Queen, she remembered that night. She said she remembered lines of strangers linking arms and walking down the street. All of us, she said, just swept along on a tide of happiness and relief.

{img:crowds-linking-arms-whitehall-night-singing-floodlights-1945}
At the end of the evening, the princesses stand in the crowd outside Buckingham Palace. They call out with all the other people. We want the King! We want the Queen! And then they watch from the crowd as their own parents come out onto the balcony and wave.

--

It was one of the most special nights of her life, Princess Elizabeth said later. At last, after midnight, the two sisters slip back in through the side door and go to bed. And outside, the crowds go on singing under the bright lights, until the early hours of the morning.
