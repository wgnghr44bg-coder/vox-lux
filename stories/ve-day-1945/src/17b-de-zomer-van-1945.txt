And then it is the summer of 1945.

For six years, the beaches of the south and east of England have been closed. They were covered with barbed wire and concrete blocks. Gaps were cut in the piers so an enemy could not use them. Nobody could go to the seaside.

--

Now, slowly, soldiers and engineers clear the beaches. They take away the wire and the concrete. They check every metre of sand. They make the beaches safe again. And in the summer of 1945, the people of Britain go to the seaside for the first time in years.

They go by train from the cities in their thousands. The carriages are packed. They bring buckets and spades and sandwiches and flasks of tea. They go to all the seaside towns along the coast.

{img:children-building-sandcastles-beach-1945-buckets-spades-sunshine}
On the beaches, many children see the sea for the first time. They run down to the water's edge. They paddle and squeal at the cold water. They build sandcastles and dig holes. They look for crabs in the rock pools. And they ride donkeys along the sand.

--

The grown-ups sit in deckchairs with their trousers rolled up. Some tie knotted handkerchiefs on their heads against the sun. They sleep and read the newspaper. They eat ice cream when they can find it. And they eat fish and chips wrapped in paper. Fish and chips were never rationed, all through the war.

The piers are mended. The funfairs open again, and the puppet shows. Brass bands play on the seafront. And the seaside landladies put up their signs again. Rooms free. Sea views.

{img:seaside-promenade-evening-1945-lights-bandstand-couples-walking}
In the evenings, families walk along the seafront in the warm summer air. They watch the sun set over the sea. Then the lights come on along the front and on the pier. Strings of coloured bulbs shine in the water. Only a few months ago, all of this had to be dark.

--

The soldiers come home on leave, and for good. They go to the seaside too, with their wives and children. They sit on the beach in the sunshine. They listen to the waves and the gulls and the children laughing. And for the first time in years, they feel they can rest.

Many people later remembered it as a golden summer. The first summer of peace. With long light evenings, beaches and gardens full of roses, and families together again.
