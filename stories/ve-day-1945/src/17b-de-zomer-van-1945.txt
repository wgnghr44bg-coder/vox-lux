And then, it is the summer of 1945.

For six years, the beaches of the south, and east, of England have been closed. They were covered with barbed wire, and concrete blocks, and mines, and gun emplacements, and the piers had gaps cut in them, so that they could not be used by an enemy landing. Nobody could go to the seaside.

--

Now, slowly, the soldiers, and the engineers, clear the beaches, and take away the barbed wire, and the concrete, and make them safe again. And in the summer of 1945, the people of Britain go to the seaside, again, for the first time in years.

They go by train, from the cities, in their thousands, packed into the carriages, with their buckets, and spades, and their sandwiches, and their flasks of tea, to Brighton, and Margate, and Southend, and Blackpool, and Scarborough, and Weymouth, and all the other seaside towns.

{img:children-building-sandcastles-beach-1945-buckets-spades-sunshine}
And on the beaches, the children, many of whom have never seen the sea, run down, to the water's edge, and paddle, and squeal, at the cold water, and build sandcastles, and dig holes, and look for crabs, in the rock pools, and ride on the donkeys, along the sand.

--

The grown-ups sit in deckchairs, with their trousers rolled up, and handkerchiefs, knotted, on their heads, against the sun, and sleep, and read the newspapers, and eat ice cream, when they can find it, and fish and chips, wrapped in newspaper, which were never rationed, all through the war.

The piers are mended, and the funfairs open again, and the Punch and Judy shows, and the bandstands, with the brass bands, playing, on the promenade, and the seaside landladies, in their boarding houses, put up their signs again: Vacancies. Sea views.

{img:seaside-promenade-evening-1945-lights-bandstand-couples-walking}
In the evenings, the families walk along the promenade, in the warm summer air, and watch the sun set, over the sea, and the lights come on, along the seafront, and on the pier, strings of coloured bulbs, reflected in the water, which, only a few months before, had to be dark.

--

And the soldiers, coming home, on leave, and for good, go to the seaside, too, with their wives, and their children, and sit on the beach, in the sunshine, and listen to the waves, and the gulls, and the children laughing, and feel, for the first time in years, that they can rest.

It is, many people remember later, a golden summer, the summer of 1945: the first summer of peace, with the long, light evenings, and the beaches, and the gardens, full of roses, and the families, together again.
