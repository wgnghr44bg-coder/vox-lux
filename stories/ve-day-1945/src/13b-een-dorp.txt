Let us go to a little village in the English countryside on VE Day.

It has old stone cottages with straw roofs and gardens full of flowers. They stand around a village green. There is a church and a pub and a pond with ducks. And there is a stone memorial from the first great war. On it are the names of the young men of the village who did not come home.

--

The village has been fairly quiet during the war, far from the bombs. Many of the young men are away in the army. Land girls have come to work on the farms. And some children from London have lived here, sent away to be safe. They went to the village school. They learned to milk cows and find eggs and climb trees. Some have stayed all through the war.

On the morning of VE Day the church bells ring. The whole village comes out onto the green. Flags fly from the church tower and the pub and the cottages. Bunting hangs across the lane and between the trees.

In the afternoon there is a tea for the children in the village hall. There are sandwiches and jelly and a big cake with VICTORY written on it in icing. Then there are games on the green. Races for children and grown-ups. A tug of war between the men and the women. The rope goes this way, then that way. And the women win.

--

The old men sit on the bench outside the pub in the sunshine. They watch. And they remember the end of the other war, when they were young and came home from France.

The vicar holds a service of thanks in the church. It is fuller than it has been for years. People sing and pray. They read the names of the young men of the village who died in this war. Later, those names will be added to the old memorial on the green.

{img:village-pub-evening-ve-day-singing-piano-lanterns-garden}
In the evening the pub is crowded. The doors are open and the singing spills out onto the green. Someone plays the piano and someone plays the accordion. People dance on the grass in the long evening light.

--

When it gets dark, there is a bonfire on the hill above the village. The whole village climbs up to watch. From the top they can see other bonfires burning on other hills, far away. One after another, across the dark countryside. Like a chain of lights from village to village.

The children and the land girls and the old men and the mothers stand around the fire in the dark. Their faces glow. They sing and watch the sparks fly up into the sky and the stars.

{img:hilltop-bonfire-night-villagers-silhouette-distant-fires-hills}
Then they walk home slowly down the hill along the lanes in the dark. The hedges smell of white blossom. The owls are calling. The singing still comes faintly from the pub. And they go to bed tired and happy in the quiet village, at the end of the war.
