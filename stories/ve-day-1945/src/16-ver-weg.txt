On VE Day, many soldiers of Britain and the Commonwealth are not at home. They are far away.

In Italy and Germany and Holland and Norway, they celebrate where they are. In their camps and in the towns they have reached. They fire flares into the sky and sing. And they write letters home. It's over, they write. I'll be home soon.

--

But for many of them it is not over. Far away in Asia, the war still goes on.

In Burma, in the jungle and the mountains, a British army is still fighting. It has soldiers from Britain and India and Nepal and Africa. They call themselves the Forgotten Army. Nobody at home thinks about us, they say. Nobody even knows where we are. Just a few days ago, they reached a great city there, just before the heavy rains began.

On VE Day they hear the news on their radios, in the heat and the rain. They are glad, of course. But for them the war is not finished yet. They will have to wait until the middle of August. Only then will the war in the east come to its end too.

--

In the Pacific Ocean, too, soldiers and sailors from America and Australia and New Zealand and Britain are still fighting, far from home.

So the soldiers far away do not celebrate the way people do at home. They think of home. They picture their families celebrating in the streets. And they wonder when they will see them again.

{img:soldier-writing-letter-home-tent-lamp-night-1945-photo-family}
And they write letters. For the soldiers and their families, letters are everything.

--

In the war, millions of letters are written every day. They travel thousands of miles by ship and plane. To save space, some letters are photographed onto tiny bits of film. The film is flown home. There the letters are printed out again, small and grey, but you can still read them.

The soldiers write about the weather and the food and their friends. But they may not write where they are or what they are doing. That is secret. Before a letter is sent, someone reads it. Sometimes words are cut out or blacked out.

{img:mother-reading-letter-kitchen-window-1945-smiling-children}
At home, the families write back. About the children and the garden and the neighbours. About the dog and the weather. They send photos of the children, who are growing up so fast, without their fathers.

--

On VE Day and in the days after, the letters go back and forth, full of joy and hope. Not long now. I'll be home soon. Keep the light on for me.
