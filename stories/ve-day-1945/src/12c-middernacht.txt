At one minute past midnight, on the night of the eighth to the ninth of May, the war in Europe officially ends.

All day long people have been celebrating. But it is only now, in the middle of the night, that the guns everywhere in Europe must go silent.

--

In London the crowds are still in the streets, under the bright lights. As midnight comes, Big Ben strikes twelve. Slowly, deep and clear, over the crowds and the river. And the whole of London listens.

And then, on the Thames and in every harbour around the coasts of Britain, the ships sound their horns and whistles all together. Tugs and barges and steamers and warships. Many of them play the letter V in Morse code. Three short blasts and one long one. Dot, dot, dot, dash. V for victory.

{img:harbour-night-ships-lights-sirens-searchlights-celebration-1945}
It is the same rhythm as the first notes of a famous piece by Beethoven. All through the war, the BBC played those notes at the start of its broadcasts to the occupied countries. It was a secret sign of hope. Da, da, da, dum.

--

On the warships, sailors fire rockets and turn on their lights. The harbours glow and flash red and green and white. And the water reflects it all.

Then slowly the sound of the horns dies away. And the night grows quieter.

--

All over Europe, on all the battlefields, soldiers hear that it is over. Some of them cheer. But many of them simply sit quietly in the dark. They look at the stars. They think about home and about their friends. And they listen to the silence.

For the first time in nearly six years, there is no war in Europe.

{img:soldier-sitting-quiet-night-stars-camp-fire-after-war-1945}
And in the little houses and flats and farms all over Europe, mothers and grandmothers and children are asleep. Or they lie awake, listening to the singing and the horns far away. And then to the silence.

--

It is a strange and wonderful silence. The silence of a night with nothing to fear. All over Europe, people turn over in their beds. They pull up their blankets. And at last they sleep, deeply and peacefully, until the morning.
