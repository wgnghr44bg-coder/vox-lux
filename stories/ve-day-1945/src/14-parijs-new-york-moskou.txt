On the eighth of May, it is not only London that celebrates.

In Paris, the bells of all the churches ring. Crowds pour into the streets, onto the great avenue and around the famous arch. Flags of France and Britain and America and the Soviet Union wave everywhere. General de Gaulle speaks on the radio. People sing the French anthem and dance in the streets and squares all night long. They climb onto the jeeps of the American soldiers.

--

Paris was freed the summer before. So the people of Paris have already had their great day. But now the whole war in Europe is over. And they celebrate again. The lights of Paris shine. The great monuments are lit up for the first time in years.

In New York, it is morning when the news comes. President Truman speaks on the radio at nine o'clock. It is his birthday. He tells the people of America that the war in Europe is over. But the war in the Pacific still goes on, he says. There is still work to do.

But in New York, the crowds gather anyway in Times Square, hundreds of thousands of them. Office workers in the tall buildings throw paper out of the windows. Torn paper and long paper strips fall like snow on the streets below. People cheer and dance and kiss. And the ships in the harbour sound their whistles.

--

In Moscow the news comes in the early hours of the next morning. The radio announces it at about two o'clock at night. People wake up and get dressed. They pour into the streets and into Red Square, in the dark and the dawn. They sing and cheer and cry.

All day long the streets of Moscow are full of people. They pick up soldiers walking past and toss them up into the air, again and again, and catch them, laughing. In the evening there is a huge salute of guns and fireworks and searchlights over the Kremlin and the river.

{img:moscow-red-square-victory-fireworks-night-may-9-1945-crowds-kremlin}
The Soviet Union has suffered more in the war than any other country. Millions of its people have died. Its cities and villages have been destroyed. So the joy in Moscow is mixed with deep sorrow too, for all that has been lost.

--

And all over the world there are bells and flags and singing and prayers of thanks. In Canada and Australia and New Zealand. In South Africa and India. In the countries of Europe, freed one by one. In every place where people have waited for this day.

{img:small-town-church-thanksgiving-service-may-1945-congregation-flowers}
In churches and chapels and synagogues all over the world, there are services of thanks. People come in their thousands. They give thanks. And they pray quietly for those they have lost, and for peace.
