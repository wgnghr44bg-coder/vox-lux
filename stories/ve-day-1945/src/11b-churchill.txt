On VE Day, after the King, the man the crowds most want to see is Winston Churchill.

He is seventy years old. He is short and round, with a round pink face and a big cigar. He wears a spotted bow tie and a black hat. He became Prime Minister exactly five years ago. The war was going very badly then. And he has led the country through all of it.

--

In the summer of 1940, Britain stood alone. Many feared an enemy army would land any day. Churchill spoke to the people on the radio and in Parliament. His words were big and rolling and stirring. They gave people courage and hope. We shall fight on the beaches, he said. We shall never surrender.

All through the war he worked day and night. Sometimes he worked from secret rooms under the ground in London. He even slept there sometimes. He travelled all over the world to meet the other leaders. He visited the bombed streets and the soldiers. And people cheered him wherever he went.

{img:cabinet-war-rooms-underground-map-room-telephones-lamps}
He worked very hard. He took naps in the afternoon. Then he worked late into the night, often in his dressing gown, walking up and down with his cigar. He loved painting and building brick walls in his garden. He loved his cats and his poodle. And he loved his wife Clementine. He wrote her loving letters all his life.

--

On VE Day he has lunch with the King at the palace. Then he drives back through the crowds. They are so thick that his car can hardly move. The people push it the last part of the way. He sits up high on the back of the open car. He waves and makes his V sign. And the crowds cheer and sing.

At three o'clock he speaks to the nation. Then he goes to Parliament and gives the news there too. The members cheer and wave their papers. Then he leads them all across the road to a church to give thanks.

{img:whitehall-ministry-of-health-balcony-evening-floodlit-crowd-singing}
In the evening he comes out again onto the balcony, in the bright lights. The crowd sings a great old song, and he waves his arms like a conductor. Then he speaks to them again. In all our long history, he says, we have never seen a greater day than this.

--

Two months later there will be an election. The people will vote for a new government. And Churchill will no longer be Prime Minister. He is hurt and surprised. But many who voted against him still love him and are grateful. They say he was the right man for the war. But for the peace they want something new.

He will become Prime Minister again a few years later. He will live to be ninety. And when he passes away, the whole country will mourn him. As his coffin passes on a boat along the Thames, the tall cranes along the river will slowly bow down.
