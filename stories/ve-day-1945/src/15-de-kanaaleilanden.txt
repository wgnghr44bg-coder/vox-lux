On VE Day, one small part of Britain is still waiting. The Channel Islands.

These are little islands in the sea between England and France. They lie much closer to France. They are called Jersey and Guernsey and Alderney and Sark. They belong to the British Crown. And they are the only British land that the German army took during the war. For nearly five years, the Germans have been in charge there.

--

For five years, the islanders have been cut off from Britain and from the world. In the last winter it was even worse. Food ran very short. People were hungry and cold, with no fuel and almost nothing to eat. Then, just before Christmas, a Red Cross ship came with food parcels. It came back several times. And it saved many lives.

On the afternoon of VE Day, the islanders listen to Mr Churchill on hidden radios. Radios are still forbidden on the islands. They hold their breath. And they hear him say it. Our dear Channel Islands are also to be freed today.

{img:jersey-islanders-waving-flags-harbour-wall-royal-navy-destroyer}
The next morning, two British warships arrive off the islands. The German commanders come aboard and sign the surrender. And British soldiers come ashore at the island harbours.

--

The whole population comes down to the harbours to meet them. For five years they have kept their British flags hidden. Now they hang them from every window. They wave them on the harbour walls. They cheer and sing. The church bells ring. People climb onto the lorries and hug the soldiers and give them flowers.

The soldiers give the children sweets and chocolate and oranges. Many of the children have never seen an orange. They look at it. They do not know what it is, or how to eat it.

{img:island-children-receiving-oranges-soldiers-1945-harbour-sunshine}
On Sark, the smallest island, there is a lady who rules the island for the Crown. She stayed all through the war. She stood up to the German officers with great calm and courage. Now she greets the British soldiers at the harbour.

--

In 1940, just before the Germans came, many island children were sent away to England. For five years they have grown up there, far from their families. Now, slowly, they come home on the ships. They are taller and older. They speak with English accents. And their parents, who have waited for them every day, are standing on the quay.

Every year on the ninth of May, the people of Jersey and Guernsey still celebrate Liberation Day. There are parades and music and flags, in the harbours where the ships came in.
