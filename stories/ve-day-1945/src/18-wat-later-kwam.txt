After the celebrations, the world slowly begins to rebuild.

In June 1945, people from fifty countries meet in San Francisco, in America. They sign the plan for a new organisation, the United Nations. Its job is to keep the peace between the nations of the world. So that there will never be a war like this again.

--

In Britain there is an election in July, the first for ten years. To the surprise of many, Mr Churchill loses. A new government comes in, led by a quiet modest man called Clement Attlee. It promises to build a better Britain. With new houses and work and help for those in need. And free health care for everyone. A few years later, that health service begins.

In the middle of August, the war in Asia comes to its end too. There are celebrations in the streets again. And at last the Second World War is over everywhere.

But life after the war is not easy. In Britain, rationing goes on for years. In some ways it gets even stricter. The last rationing, of meat, will not end until nine years after VE Day.

--

There are not enough houses, because so many were destroyed by bombs. So the government builds little houses in factories, in pieces. They are put together in a day or two on the bombed sites. Each one has a kitchen and a bathroom and a small garden. They were meant to last ten years. Some are still lived in today. Their families love them.

In Europe the damage is much greater. Whole cities lie in ruins. Millions of people have lost their homes. They are far from where they were born. They must find their way home, or find a new home somewhere else. America sends money and food and machines to help Europe rebuild.

{img:european-city-rebuilding-1950s-new-buildings-old-church-tower}
And slowly, over many years, the cities are rebuilt. The rubble is cleared away. The bridges are repaired. Churches and town halls and old streets are rebuilt, sometimes stone by stone, just as they were. And new buildings rise up in the gaps.

--

And slowly the countries of Europe begin to work together. They fought each other in two great wars in thirty years. Now they start to trade with each other. They want to make sure there will never be another war between them. A few years after the war, six countries agree to share their coal and steel. From that small beginning, over many years, the European Union grows.

{img:children-1950s-street-playing-new-housing-peaceful-afternoon}
And the children of the war grow up in a Europe at peace. They are the same children who stood at their windows on VE Day and saw the lights come on. They go to school and work and marry. They have children and grandchildren of their own. And sometimes they tell them about the day the war in Europe ended. About the street parties and the jellies and the bonfires. And the lights.
