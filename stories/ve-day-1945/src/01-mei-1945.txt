Tonight we go more than eighty years back. We go to the spring of 1945, to the last days of the Second World War in Europe.

--

It is the beginning of May. In the villages of England, the apple trees are in blossom. Bluebells cover the woods. The swallows have come back from Africa. They fly low over the fields and the village greens. In the towns and cities the trees are green again. A tall pink flower grows in the ruins where houses used to stand.

And everyone is waiting.

If you like calm stories from the past, like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

For nearly six years, Europe has been at war. Millions of men and women have worn uniforms, far from home. Cities have been bombed. Families have been split up. Children have been sent away to the countryside to be safe. Food and clothes and almost everything else have been rationed. That means each person may only buy a small amount. The nights have been dark, with no lights allowed. And the days have been full of worry, waiting for news and letters.

{img:family-gathered-around-wireless-radio-1945-living-room-listening}
But now, in the spring of 1945, everyone knows the end is coming. Every evening, families sit around the radio to hear the news. And every day the news is better. The armies are moving forward from the west and from the east. Town after town, country after country, is being set free.

--

The question is no longer if the war in Europe will end. The question is when. Next week? Tomorrow? Today? People buy flags and put them away, ready. They save a little of their food for a party. And they wait.

Tonight we will follow those last days, gently and from far away. We will visit the hungry cities of Holland, where food falls from the sky. We will go to a river in Germany, where two armies meet. We will see candles lit in the windows of Denmark. We will visit a little red school in France in the middle of the night. And we will be in London and Paris and New York and Moscow on the days of joy.

{img:london-street-1945-bunting-flags-terraced-houses-spring-sunshine}
We will not stay with the battles or the hard things. We will stay with the people. The ordinary people who lived through it all. In May 1945, after six long years, they came out into the streets and the sunshine. They danced and sang and cried. They hugged strangers. Because the war in Europe was over.

--

So let us go back to the beginning of May 1945. The world is tired and hopeful and waiting, with blossom on the trees.
