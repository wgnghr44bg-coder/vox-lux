In the spring of 1945, the war in Europe is coming to its end.

In the west, the Allied armies have crossed the great river Rhine. These are the Americans and the British, the Canadians and the French and many others. They are moving east across Germany, from town to town. They are moving faster than anyone expected.

--

In the east, the great armies of the Soviet Union are moving west toward Berlin. In the south, in Italy, other Allied armies are moving north toward the mountains.

Everywhere, the war is ending, place by place. In town after town, people come up out of their cellars. They hang white sheets from the windows. And the soldiers move on. Prisoners of war are set free from the camps. They are thin and tired and overjoyed. And they begin the long journey home.

The world is also learning of terrible things that happened in some camps during the war. We remember them still, with great sorrow. And we will leave them here, gently, in silence.

--

In April, in America, President Roosevelt passes away suddenly. He has led his country for twelve years, through nearly the whole war. People all over the world are sad. The new President is Harry Truman, a modest man from Missouri.

At the end of April, the war is ending in Berlin too. On the first of May, German radio says that Hitler is dead. And the next day, Berlin gives up.

{img:village-people-waving-white-sheets-windows-soldiers-passing-1945}
Day after day, the German armies give up in different places. In Italy. In the north of Germany and in Holland and Denmark. And a few days later, in the south.

--

All over Europe, people are waiting for the very last surrender. The end of the war.

In Britain and America, people are waiting for the big announcement. The day will be called VE Day. Victory in Europe Day. It has been talked about for weeks. The newspapers are full of plans. The shops are full of flags. The churches are ready to ring their bells. But nobody knows the date yet.

{img:english-shop-window-1945-union-jacks-bunting-for-sale-queue}
And in the meantime, life goes on. People go to work. They go to the shops with their ration books and queue for food. They go home and listen to the news. They go to bed and wonder. Will it be tomorrow?
