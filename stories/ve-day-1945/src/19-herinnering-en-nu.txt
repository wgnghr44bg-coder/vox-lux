Today, more than eighty years later, VE Day is still remembered.

Every year on the eighth of May, there are ceremonies and moments of silence in Britain and France and many other countries. In France it is a holiday. In Russia and many other countries, the ninth of May is Victory Day. In the Netherlands there is the evening of remembrance on the fourth of May, and Liberation Day on the fifth. And in Denmark, candles are lit in the windows.

--

On the big anniversaries, the celebrations are larger. Fifty years after VE Day, the Queen stood again on the balcony of Buckingham Palace. She was the same Elizabeth who had danced in the crowds as a young princess. Her mother and her sister stood beside her. They looked out over the crowds, just as they had in 1945. And Vera Lynn sang again for the crowds.

Seventy-five years after VE Day, people had to stay at home because of an illness going round the world. But they had little parties anyway, on their doorsteps and in their front gardens. There was bunting and cups of tea. And people sang We'll Meet Again from their windows. Eighty years after, there were parades and street parties once again. Church bells rang. And fires were lit on hills across the country.

{img:ve-day-anniversary-street-party-today-bunting-families-tea}
The people who remember VE Day are very old now. Most of them have gone. But their memories have been written down and recorded and filmed. Their photos are kept in albums and boxes. So we can still see them and hear their voices. The children in their paper hats. The sailors in the fountains. The princesses in the crowds. The families around the radio.

--

In many places, quiet reminders of those days remain. The memorials in every town and village, with their lists of names, and the red poppies laid in front of them every year. The little red school in Reims. The river bank at Torgau. The tulips in Canada. The candles in the windows of Denmark.

So we leave the spring of 1945 tonight, as the evening falls on the eighth of May.

{img:english-street-dusk-1945-lights-in-windows-bunting-quiet-after-party}
The street parties are ending. The tables and chairs are carried back into the houses. The children have gone to bed, tired and full of cake. Some still have their paper hats on. They fall asleep to the singing that drifts in through the open windows. The bunting stirs gently in the evening breeze. And all along the street, for the first time in six years, the windows are glowing. Warm golden light, with no curtains drawn. The street lamps shine softly on the pavements. And in the sky above, the stars come out one by one, over a quiet and peaceful sky.
