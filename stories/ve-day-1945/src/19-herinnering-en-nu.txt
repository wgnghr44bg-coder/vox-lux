Today, more than eighty years later, VE Day is still remembered.

Every year, on the eighth of May, in Britain, and France, and many other countries, there are services, and ceremonies, and moments of silence, and the flags fly. In France, the eighth of May is a public holiday, Victoire 1945. In Russia, and many other countries, the ninth of May is Victory Day. In the Netherlands, there is the fourth of May, the evening of remembrance, and the fifth of May, Liberation Day. In Denmark, the candles are lit, in the windows.

--

On the great anniversaries, the celebrations are larger. In 1995, fifty years after VE Day, the Queen, Elizabeth, who had danced in the crowds as a young princess, stood again, on the balcony of Buckingham Palace, with her mother, and her sister, Princess Margaret, and looked out, over the crowds, in the Mall, as they had done in 1945, and Vera Lynn sang, again, for the crowds.

In 2020, on the seventy-fifth anniversary, people stayed at home, because of the pandemic, but they had little street parties, anyway, on their doorsteps, and in their front gardens, with bunting, and cups of tea, and sang We'll Meet Again, from their windows. And in 2025, on the eightieth anniversary, there were parades, and flypasts, and street parties, once again, and church bells, and beacons, lit across the country.

{img:ve-day-anniversary-street-party-today-bunting-families-tea}
The people who remember VE Day are now very old. Most of them have gone. But their memories have been written down, and recorded, and filmed, and their photographs kept, in albums, and boxes, and archives, so that we can still see them, and hear their voices: the children in their paper hats, the sailors in the fountains, the princesses in the crowds, the families around the wireless.

--

And in many places, quiet reminders of those days remain: the memorials, in every town, and village, with their lists of names, and the poppies, and the wreaths, laid in front of them, every year. The little red schoolhouse, in Reims. The riverbank, at Torgau. The tulips, in Ottawa. The candles, in the windows of Denmark.

So we leave the spring of 1945, tonight, as the evening falls, on the eighth of May.

{img:english-street-dusk-1945-lights-in-windows-bunting-quiet-after-party}
The street parties are ending. The tables are being carried back into the houses, and the chairs, and the children have gone to bed, tired, and full of cake, with their paper hats still on, and are falling asleep, to the sound of the singing, still drifting in, through the open windows. The bunting is stirring, gently, in the evening breeze. And all along the street, for the first time in six years, the windows are glowing, with light, warm, and golden, without any curtains drawn, and the street lamps are shining, softly, on the pavements. And in the sky above, the stars are coming out, one by one, over a quiet, peaceful sky.
