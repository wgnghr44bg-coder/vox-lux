During the war, the women of Britain have done almost everything.

When the men went away to fight, someone had to do their work. In the factories and on the farms. On the railways and the buses. In offices and hospitals. And so the women did it.

--

In the factories they built aeroplanes and ships and tanks. They made parachutes and uniforms. They worked long shifts, day and night, in overalls. They tied scarves on their heads to keep their hair out of the machines. They drove buses and lorries and ambulances. They worked as engineers and welders and crane drivers.

On the farms there was the Women's Land Army. Tens of thousands of young women went to work on farms. Many came from the cities and had never seen a cow before. Now they ploughed and milked and brought in the harvest. They fed the country. They wore green jumpers and brown trousers and felt hats. People called them the land girls.

{img:women-factory-workers-headscarves-aircraft-assembly-line-1940s}
There were women in the army and navy and air force too. Princess Elizabeth served as well. Women worked as drivers and mechanics and radio operators. Some stood around big map tables. They moved little markers to show where the planes were. Others worked the big searchlights at night.

--

And at home there were the mothers and grandmothers. They kept the families going through the bombing and the rationing and the worry. They queued for food and mended clothes and cared for the children and the old. Many worked part time too. Some served tea and soup and comfort to people who had lost their homes in the bombing.

On VE Day, the women of Britain celebrate with everyone else in the streets. Some wear their uniforms. Some wear their overalls. Some wear their best dresses.

{img:wrens-waaf-women-uniform-celebrating-ve-day-arms-linked-street}
After the war, things are not quite the same for them. Many give up their jobs when the men come home. But they have learned something. They can do things that people thought only men could do. They do not forget it. And their daughters and granddaughters do not forget it either.

--

Many years later, a memorial was set up in London for the women of the war. It is a tall bronze statue. All around it hang uniforms and overalls and work clothes, on pegs. It looks as if the women have just taken them off at the end of the war, and gone home.
