In London, on Monday the seventh of May, everybody knows something has happened. But nobody has told them for sure.

The news from Reims is being kept secret for a day. It is meant to be announced at the same time in London and Washington and Moscow. But a news agency in America has already told the story. By the morning, rumours are all over London.

--

All day, people wait. Crowds gather near the government buildings and outside Buckingham Palace and in the big squares. Office workers lean out of their windows. People buy every new newspaper, looking for the news. Some start to celebrate anyway. They throw paper out of office windows like snow. The flags come out on the buildings. The church bells are ready.

But the hours pass, and nothing is announced. People are confused and a little impatient. Is it today? Is it not?

In the shops, people buy flags. The government says people may buy red, white and blue cloth without using their clothing tickets. The shops sell out fast. Women sit at their sewing machines. They make flags and decorations out of anything they can find. Old dresses and curtains and scraps of cloth.

--

At last, at about twenty to eight in the evening, a short message comes on the radio. Tomorrow will be Victory in Europe Day. It will be a holiday. The Prime Minister will speak at three o'clock in the afternoon. The King will speak at nine in the evening. And the day after will be a holiday too.

So it is true. It is over. And tomorrow is the day.

{img:family-kitchen-1945-evening-sewing-flags-children-excited-wireless}
That evening, in houses all over Britain, people get ready. Mothers take out the flags they have saved. The children help hang them from the windows and across the street, from house to house. Neighbours talk over the garden walls about tomorrow's party. Everyone gives a little of their food. A little sugar, some flour, some dried egg. They will make cakes and sandwiches and jellies for the children.

--

And in the night, a great thunderstorm comes over London.

Thunder rolls over the rooftops. Lightning flashes over the city. Heavy warm rain pours down. It washes the streets and the dust of the bombed buildings. And it soaks the flags hanging ready in the streets.

{img:thunderstorm-over-london-night-lightning-rooftops-st-pauls-rain}
For six years, the sky over London has been full of danger at night. But tonight the flashes and rumbles are only lightning and thunder. People lie in their beds and listen to the rain. Some of them laugh. They say it is the sky's own fireworks, for the end of the war.

--

In the morning the storm has passed. The sky is washed and clear. The sun shines warm on the wet streets. And the flags hanging from every window dry and lift in the breeze.

It is the eighth of May, 1945. It is VE Day.
