In Denmark too, on the evening of the fourth of May, people are listening to the radio.

At twenty-five minutes to nine in the evening, a voice from London stops the programme. There is a special message. The German forces in Denmark have given up. Denmark is free.

--

All over the country, people jump up from their chairs. They hug each other and cry and laugh. And they run out into the streets.

And then they do something beautiful. They tear down the black paper and the dark curtains from their windows. For five years they have had to keep them closed every evening. And they put candles in the windows. Lit candles. To show that the darkness is over and the light has come back.

{img:copenhagen-crowds-street-cheering-danish-flags-evening-1945}
That evening, all over Denmark, every window glows with the soft light of candles in the spring twilight. People walk through the streets singing. They look at the lights in the windows, all the way along. Thousands and thousands of little flames, flickering.

--

The next day, the red and white Danish flag flies everywhere. From every flagpole and every window. People celebrate in the streets and parks and squares. British soldiers arrive and are greeted with flowers.

And every year since then, on the evening of the fourth of May, the Danes put candles in their windows again. They remember the evening the news came and the darkness ended.

{img:quiet-danish-street-today-candles-windows-evening-may-blue-dusk}
If you walk through a Danish town on that evening, even today, you will see them. Little candles glowing in the windows of houses and flats and shops. One after another, all along the street, in the blue spring dusk. A quiet thank you, and a memory, of the day the lights came back.

--

In Norway too, the war ends a few days later, peacefully. On Norway's national day in May, people celebrate as they have not been able to for five years. Children march in parades with flags and music. And in June, the king comes home from England by ship. He sails into the harbour of Oslo on the same day he left, five years before. And the whole city comes out to welcome him.
