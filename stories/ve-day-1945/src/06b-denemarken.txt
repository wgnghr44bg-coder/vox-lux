In Denmark, too, on the evening of the fourth of May, 1945, the people are listening to the wireless.

At twenty-five minutes to nine, in the evening, a voice, on the Danish service of the BBC, from London, interrupts the programme, with a special announcement. The German forces in Denmark have surrendered. Denmark is free.

--

And all over the country, in Copenhagen, and Aarhus, and Odense, and every little town, and village, and farmhouse, people jump up, from their chairs, and hug each other, and cry, and laugh, and run out into the streets.

And then, they do something beautiful. They tear down the black paper, and the blackout curtains, from their windows, which they have had to keep closed, every evening, for five years. And they put candles, lit candles, in the windows, to show that the darkness is over, and the light has come back.

{img:copenhagen-crowds-street-cheering-danish-flags-evening-1945}
All over Denmark, that evening, in every street, every window glows, with the soft light of candles, in the spring twilight. And the people walk, through the streets, in the evening, singing, and looking at the lights, in the windows, all the way along, flickering, in thousands, and thousands, of windows.

--

The next day, the fifth of May, the Danish flag, the Dannebrog, red, with a white cross, flies everywhere, from every flagpole, and window, and the people celebrate, in the streets, and the parks, and the squares, and the British soldiers arrive, and are greeted with flowers.

And every year since then, on the evening of the fourth of May, the Danes put candles in their windows, again, to remember the evening when the news came, and the darkness ended.

{img:quiet-danish-street-today-candles-windows-evening-may-blue-dusk}
If you walk through a Danish town, on the evening of the fourth of May, even today, you will see them: little candles, glowing, in the windows of the houses, and the flats, and the shops, one after another, all along the street, in the blue spring dusk, as a quiet thank you, and a memory, of the day the lights came back.

--

In Norway, too, the war ends, a few days later, on the eighth of May, peacefully. And on the seventeenth of May, Norway's national day, the people celebrate, as they have not been able to for five years, with the children's parades, and flags, and music, through the streets. And in June, the King, Haakon, comes home, from exile, in England, by ship, into the harbour at Oslo, on the same day that he had left, five years before, and the whole city comes out to welcome him.
