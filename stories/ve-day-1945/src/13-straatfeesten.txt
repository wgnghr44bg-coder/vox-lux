In the streets of Britain, VE Day is the day of the street parties.

In thousands of streets all over the country, neighbours bring out their kitchen tables and chairs. They put them in a long row down the middle of the street. They cover them with white tablecloths and sheets.

--

Across the street, from house to house, they hang flags and paper chains, red and white and blue. They put pictures of the King and Queen and Mr Churchill in the windows. And on the walls they hang hand-painted signs. Welcome Home. Victory. Well Done, Boys.

Then the food comes out. For weeks, everyone has saved a little from their rations for the party. There are sandwiches of fish paste and jam and egg. There are sausage rolls and little cakes and jam tarts. There are jellies in every colour, wobbling on their plates. And there is a big cake with icing, made with sugar the whole street has saved.

The children sit at the long tables in their best clothes and paper hats. They eat and eat, more than they have eaten for years. The mothers and grandmothers pour the tea from big brown teapots and pass round the plates.

--

Then there are games and races for the children down the street. Running races and sack races and egg and spoon races. Who will keep the egg on the spoon all the way to the end? The whole street cheers. There are prizes. A sixpence, or a little toy, or a bag of sweets.

Someone wheels a piano out of one of the houses into the street. They play all afternoon and evening. Everyone sings the old songs and dances in the street. Old people and young ones. Mothers and children and soldiers home on leave.

{img:piano-in-street-neighbours-singing-dancing-evening-terraced-houses}
There is a dressing up parade for the children. They wear costumes made of crepe paper and old clothes. Some dress as soldiers and sailors and nurses. Some dress as the King. And one boy dresses as Mr Churchill, with a cigar made of rolled-up paper.

--

In the evening there is a bonfire at the end of the street, on the bomb site. The old black curtains are thrown on it and burned. And everyone cheers.

People kept the photographs of that day all their lives. In them we see the long tables down the middle of the little streets. The children in their paper hats with their mouths full of cake. The mothers in their aprons. The flags over their heads. And everyone smiling.

{img:old-photograph-style-street-party-group-smiling-1945-black-white-feel}
And yet, at many of the tables, there are empty chairs. Fathers and sons and brothers are still far away. They will not come home for many months. And some will not come home at all. Many of the people at the parties think of them quietly, in the middle of the singing and the laughter.

--

So VE Day is a day of joy and relief. And of sadness too, all mixed together. And people remember it all their lives as one of the most important days they ever lived through.
