In the autumn of 1845, as the ice begins to close in, Erebus and Terror find a place to spend their first winter: a sheltered bay, beside a small island, called Beechey Island, at the southwest corner of Devon Island, at the western end of Lancaster Sound.

It is a bleak and lonely place. There are high cliffs of grey and brown rock, and a long, low beach of gravel, curving around the bay. Behind it, there is nothing but stony ground, rising to a bare plateau, without a tree, or a bush, or a blade of grass, as far as the eye can see.

--

But before they reach it, they have done something remarkable.

They have sailed north, up a channel called Wellington Channel, between the islands, much further north than anyone has ever sailed in this part of the Arctic, and then come back down, around Cornwallis Island, to Beechey Island. Now the first snows are falling. The sea is freezing. And the ships are frozen in, for the winter, in the bay.

The men prepare for the long winter. They take down the topmasts, and cover the decks with a great roof of canvas, like a tent, to keep out the snow and the wind. They bank snow up against the sides of the ships, to keep in the warmth. They build storehouses on the beach, and a forge, and a carpenter's workshop, and an observatory, for the scientific instruments.

--

And then the darkness comes.

At the beginning of November, the sun sinks below the horizon, and does not rise again for three months. There is a little twilight at midday, a grey, blue light on the southern horizon. And then darkness again. The moon and the stars shine brightly, on the white snow. And the temperature falls lower and lower, to forty degrees below zero.

{img:polar-night-moon-stars-ships-snow-lantern-light}
Life on board in the Arctic winter follows a strict routine, to keep the men busy, and healthy, and cheerful. The officers have learned this from the earlier expeditions of Parry and Ross.

--

Every morning, the men clean the ship, and air their bedding. Every day, they must take exercise, walking up and down on the ice, or around a track marked out with poles, from the ships to the shore. When the weather is too bad to go out, they walk around the deck, under the canvas roof.

There is school for the men, in the evenings, taught by the officers: reading and writing and arithmetic and navigation. On earlier expeditions, men who could not read when they left England came home able to read the Bible, and write letters to their families.

{img:lower-deck-school-sailors-slates-officer-teaching-lamp}
There are plays, and concerts, and fancy dress balls, organized by the officers. On the expeditions of Parry, twenty years earlier, the officers acted plays on the deck, with scenery painted by the men, and the sailors played the women's parts, in dresses made of sailcloth. There is no record of the plays of Franklin's men, but they almost certainly did the same.

--

At Christmas, there would have been a great dinner, with the best food from the stores: tinned meats, and plum puddings, and extra rum, and decorations of flags, and songs, and toasts to absent friends, and to the Queen.

And on New Year's Eve, they would have rung in the new year, 1846, with the ship's bells, and cheers, out in the great white silence of the Arctic night.

{img:christmas-dinner-ship-lower-deck-flags-puddings-cheer}
But that first winter at Beechey Island also brings sadness.

--

Three of the men fall ill, and pass away, during the winter.

The first is John Torrington, the young leading stoker of Terror, who passes away on the first of January, 1846, at the age of twenty. Then John Hartnell, an able seaman of Erebus, on the fourth of January, at the age of twenty-five. And in April, William Braine, a Royal Marine of Erebus, at the age of thirty-two.

Their shipmates bury them on the gravel beach of Beechey Island, in graves cut into the frozen ground, with great difficulty, with picks. And they put up wooden headboards, painted, with their names, and their ages, and their ships, and a verse from the Bible. On Hartnell's board, they write: Thus saith the Lord of Hosts, consider your ways. On Braine's: Choose you this day whom ye will serve.

--

In the spring, the sun returns, and the light comes back.

The men go out on sledging trips, across the ice, to explore and hunt. They build a great cairn of stones, on the top of the cliff, made of hundreds of empty tins, filled with gravel, which they have eaten in the winter.

{img:spring-sunlight-returning-ice-cairn-cliff-men-sledge}
But, strangely, they leave no message in the cairn, to say where they have gone, or what they plan to do. Perhaps they think they will not need to. Perhaps they leave it somewhere that has never been found. It is one of the many mysteries of the expedition.

And in the summer of 1846, when the ice breaks up, Erebus and Terror sail away from Beechey Island, and disappear into the Arctic.
