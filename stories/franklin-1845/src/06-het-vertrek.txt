On the nineteenth of May, 1845, Erebus and Terror leave Greenhithe, and sail down the Thames, toward the sea.

They are towed by steam tugs, at first, and then by a steamship of the Navy, the Rattler, to help them on their way north, along the east coast of England and Scotland, heavily loaded, low in the water.

--

Some days later, they arrive at Stromness, in the Orkney Islands, off the north of Scotland, where they take on fresh water, and the men are allowed a last walk on shore.

And then, at the beginning of June, they sail out into the Atlantic Ocean, westward, toward Greenland. With them is a supply ship, the Barretto Junior, carrying more provisions, and live oxen, to be killed for fresh meat.

The voyage across the Atlantic takes about a month. The weather is often rough, and the heavily loaded ships roll and pitch in the waves. But the men are cheerful. Fitzjames writes that they all like Sir John very much. He writes that Franklin is full of fun, and has a great talent for making everyone happy.

--

At the beginning of July, they arrive at the coast of Greenland, at a place called Disko Bay, among the Whalefish Islands, where there is a little Danish settlement.

It is a beautiful place. There are mountains, and great glaciers, and huge icebergs, floating in the bay, glowing white and blue in the sunshine, which shines all night long. The men see their first icebergs, and their first Inuit, the Greenlanders, who paddle out to the ships in their kayaks, to trade.

{img:disko-bay-greenland-huge-icebergs-midnight-sun-kayaks}
Here, the stores from the supply ship are moved onto Erebus and Terror, until the ships are packed to the very top. The oxen are killed, and the meat is hung in the rigging, to keep fresh in the cold air. The men write their last letters home, to be carried back to England by the supply ship.

--

Franklin writes to Lady Franklin, and to his daughter. He writes that the officers and the men are all in good spirits, and that he is confident.

Fitzjames writes to his friends that they hope to reach the Pacific within a year. He writes: I don't think we shall be out more than two winters.

{img:officer-writing-letter-cabin-greenland-window-ice}
Five men are sent home on the supply ship: men who are ill, or not strong enough. They are the lucky ones, although they do not know it.

And on the twelfth of July, 1845, the supply ship sails for home. And Erebus and Terror sail on, alone, toward the northwest, across Baffin Bay.

--

At the end of July, two whaling ships, the Prince of Wales and the Enterprise, see Erebus and Terror in Baffin Bay.

The two Navy ships are moored to a great iceberg, waiting for the ice to open, so that they can cross to Lancaster Sound, the entrance to the Arctic channels. The captain of the Prince of Wales, Robert Martin, and some of the officers visit each other's ships, and talk.

Franklin invites the whaling captains to dinner. He tells Captain Martin that he has enough food for three years, and could make it last for five, by hunting birds and seals. The officers are all cheerful and well. The men are busy shooting seabirds, for fresh meat.

--

A few days later, the wind changes, and the ice opens.

And the whalers watch Erebus and Terror sail away, westward, toward Lancaster Sound, until they disappear among the ice.

{img:two-ships-sailing-away-into-ice-mist-whalers-watching}
That is the last time that any European ever sees them.
