In fact, Jeanne had already tried once. In May 1428, before the siege of Orléans, she had gone to Vaucouleurs, a small fortified town, about twenty kilometres north of Domrémy, along the river. It was the nearest town that was still loyal to the Dauphin, in that part of France. Its captain was a knight called Robert de Baudricourt. A tough, practical soldier, who held the town for the Dauphin, surrounded by enemies.

Jeanne went to Vaucouleurs with her uncle, Durand Laxart, who was married to her mother's cousin. She told her father that she was going to help her cousin, who was expecting a baby. Which was true. But when she got to Vaucouleurs, she went to the castle, and asked to see the captain. And she told him that God had sent her, to go to the Dauphin, and help him.

--

{img:gruff-knight-captain-castle-hall-laughing-at-peasant-girl-red-dress-uncle}
Robert de Baudricourt laughed at her. A peasant girl, of sixteen, in a red dress, saying that God had sent her to save France? He told her uncle to take her home to her father, and to box her ears. So Jeanne went home, to Domrémy, disappointed. But she was not discouraged. She knew that she would go back. Her voices had told her that it would take three tries.

A few weeks later, in July 1428, trouble came to Domrémy. A band of Burgundian soldiers was riding through the region, burning, and stealing. The people of Domrémy were warned that they were coming. So the whole village packed up their things, and gathered their animals, and fled, together, to the walled town of Neufchâteau, a few kilometres away, where they would be safe, behind the walls.

{img:villagers-fleeing-with-cattle-carts-road-to-walled-town-summer-dust}
It was a strange time for Jeanne. The whole family stayed in Neufchâteau, for about two weeks, in an inn, kept by a kind woman, called La Rousse. Jeanne helped at the inn, with the work, and looked after the family's animals, in the fields outside the town. She went to church, every day. And she prayed for her village, and her home.

When it was safe, the villagers went home. And they found that the soldiers had been there. The church had been set on fire, and damaged. Some of the houses had been burned. The fields had been trampled. Some of the animals that had been left behind had been taken. It was a sad homecoming. But the people set to work, at once, to repair the damage, and to bring in the harvest. They had been through this kind of thing before.

--

For Jeanne, the burned church was a terrible sight. She had loved that church all her life. She had been baptised there. She had prayed there, every day. Now its roof was blackened, and its walls were scorched. She knelt in the ruins, and prayed. And she felt, more strongly than ever, that the war must end. That France must have peace. And that she must do something to help.

{img:village-church-repaired-roof-villagers-working-together-autumn-carpenters}
The villagers repaired the church, as best they could, through the late summer, and the autumn. The men cut new timbers, for the roof. The women cleaned the inside, and scrubbed away the soot. The priest blessed it again. And by the autumn, it was being used again, for mass, on Sundays. It was not as beautiful as before. But it was standing.

Jeanne's father, at about this time, had a bad dream. He dreamed that his daughter went away, with soldiers. He was very upset. He told his sons, very sternly, that they must never let such a thing happen. It showed how much he feared for her. And Jeanne knew that she could not tell him what she planned to do.
