Jeanne first heard her voices when she was about thirteen years old. It was a summer day, at noon, in her father's garden, beside the church. She heard a voice, coming from the right, from the direction of the church. And she saw a great light, at the same time. She was very frightened. She did not know what it was. But the voice was kind, and gentle. It told her to be good, and to go to church often.

She heard the voice again, many times, after that. At first, she did not know who it was. Then she came to believe that it was the voice of Saint Michael, the archangel, the great warrior angel, who leads the armies of heaven. Later, she heard two other voices too. The voices of Saint Catherine, and Saint Margaret. Two saints, who were young women, long ago, and who were famous for their courage, and their faith.

--

{img:painted-church-statues-saint-michael-saint-catherine-saint-margaret-candles}
These saints were very well known, in Jeanne's time. There were statues of them, and paintings, in many churches. Saint Michael with his sword, and his wings, and his armour. Saint Catherine with her wheel, and her crown. Saint Margaret with a little dragon, at her feet. There was a statue of Saint Margaret in the church at Domrémy. Jeanne had looked at it, every Sunday, since she was a child.

Jeanne said that she heard the voices two or three times a week. Sometimes more. Often, when the church bells were ringing. Or when she was alone, in the fields, or the woods, or the garden. She said that she saw them, sometimes, too, and that they were beautiful, and that they smelled sweet. She said that when they left her, she cried, and wished that they had taken her with them.

{img:young-woman-praying-alone-small-chapel-bermont-candle-woods-window}
Nobody knows exactly what Jeanne's voices were. People have argued about it, for six hundred years. Some people believe that they were truly the voices of saints, sent by God. Others think that they came from Jeanne's own mind, from her deep faith, and her imagination, and her longing to help her country. We cannot know. But we do know that Jeanne believed in them, completely, with all her heart. And that they gave her extraordinary courage.

For a long time, Jeanne told nobody about her voices. Not her mother, or her father, or her brothers, or Hauviette, or the priest. She kept them secret, in her heart. She was afraid that people would not believe her. Or that her father would stop her. She made a promise, to her voices, that she would stay pure, and good, and serve God, all her life, as long as it pleased Him.

--

As she grew older, the voices began to tell her about France. About the great sadness of the kingdom, and the war, and the English. And they told her that she must go to France. In those days, the people of Domrémy said they were going to France when they went west, toward the lands of the king, because their own little region, on the border, felt a little separate. The voices told her that she must go to the Dauphin, and help him, and lead him to Reims, to be crowned.

{img:girl-looking-at-distant-road-from-hill-dusk-thoughtful-courage-wind}
Jeanne was very frightened. She said to the voices that she was only a poor girl, who did not know how to ride a horse, or how to fight in a war. But the voices said that God would help her. And that she must go. Again, and again, they told her. Go to France. Go to the Dauphin. Go to Vaucouleurs, to the captain there, Robert de Baudricourt, and he will give you men, to take you to the Dauphin.

In the autumn of 1428, when the news came that Orléans was under siege, the voices became more urgent. They told her that she must go, soon. That she must help to save Orléans. And Jeanne, who was sixteen, and had never been further than the next town, began to make her plans. She decided that she would go to Vaucouleurs again, as she had once before. And this time, she would find a way, somehow, to reach the Dauphin.
