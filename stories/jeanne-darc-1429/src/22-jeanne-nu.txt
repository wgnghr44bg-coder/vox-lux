Today, Domrémy is still a small village, in the east of France. It is called Domrémy-la-Pucelle now. Domrémy of the Maid. About a hundred and fifty people live there. And the house where Jeanne was born is still there, beside the church. It is a simple stone house, with a steep roof, and thick walls, and small windows. Visitors can go inside, and see the rooms where she lived, as a child. The big room, with the great fireplace. And the little room where, people say, she slept.

Above the door, there is a carved coat of arms, and a little statue of Jeanne, kneeling in prayer. Next to the house is the church, where she was baptised, and where she prayed, every day. Some parts of it are still the same as in her time. There is the old stone font, where she was baptised. And there is an old statue of Saint Margaret, which may be the very statue that Jeanne looked at, every Sunday.

--

{img:today-basilica-bois-chenu-hillside-above-domremy-autumn-trees-view}
On the hillside above the village, near where the Ladies' Tree once stood, there is now a great church, built about a hundred years ago, in Jeanne's honour. It is called the Basilica of the Bois Chenu, the old oak wood. Pilgrims come there, from all over the world. From the terrace, there is a beautiful view, over the valley of the Meuse, and the village, and the meadows, where Jeanne once watched her father's cows.

The old Ladies' Tree is gone now. It died, long ago. But the spring is still there, in the woods, near the basilica. And people still come to it, to drink the water, and to remember the girl who danced there, as a child. In the autumn, the woods are golden, and the leaves fall, and the beechnuts, and the acorns, just as they did in 1428.

{img:today-orleans-fetes-johanniques-procession-girl-on-horse-banners-crowd}
In Orléans, every year, in the spring, there is still the great festival of Jeanne, around the eighth of May. A young girl from the city is chosen to play Jeanne, and she rides through the streets, on a horse, in armour, with a banner, in a great procession, with music, and soldiers, and churchmen, and thousands of people. At night, the cathedral is lit up with beautiful pictures, projected onto its walls, telling the story of the siege.

In Reims, the great cathedral still stands, where the king was crowned, with Jeanne beside him. Outside, in the square, there is a statue of Jeanne, on horseback, in armour, holding her sword high. And inside, there is a beautiful modern statue of her, in a chapel, where people light candles. Many people come, every year, to see the place where her greatest moment happened.

--

In Paris, there is a famous golden statue of Jeanne, on horseback, with her banner, in a little square, near the Louvre. It shines in the sun. And all over France, in towns, and villages, there are statues of her, and streets named after her, and churches, and schools. She is one of the most famous people in French history. And in many other countries too. There are statues of her in America, and Canada, and Australia, and many other places.

There have been hundreds of books, and plays, and films, and operas, and songs, about her. Great writers, like Shakespeare, and Schiller, and Mark Twain, and George Bernard Shaw, have written about her. Mark Twain said that she was the most extraordinary person the human race has ever produced. He spent twelve years writing a book about her, and said it was the best thing he ever wrote.

{img:today-meuse-river-meadows-domremy-cows-grazing-autumn-evening-mist}
But perhaps the best place to remember her is not in a great cathedral, or a famous square. It is by the river Meuse, at Domrémy, on an autumn evening. When the mist is rising from the water, and the cows are grazing in the meadows, and the church bell is ringing, for the end of the day. Just as it was, when a girl of sixteen sat there, with her spindle, and listened, and prayed, and dreamed of saving France.
