After Orléans, Jeanne wanted to go straight to Reims, to crown the Dauphin. But first, the English must be driven out of the other towns along the Loire. So, in June 1429, the French army, with Jeanne, and the Duke of Alençon, marched along the river, and took the towns, one by one. Jargeau, and Meung, and Beaugency. Each time, the people of the towns welcomed the French, and the maiden, with joy.

On the eighteenth of June, near a village called Patay, the French army met the English army, in open country. The English were surprised, before they were ready. The French cavalry charged, and the English were defeated. It was a great victory. The English commander, Lord Talbot, a famous and brave old soldier, was taken prisoner. Jeanne was not in the front of the fighting, that day. She came afterward, and was sad, to see the wounded, and comforted them.

--

{img:girl-in-armour-comforting-wounded-soldier-field-evening-gentle-sad}
Jeanne was always sad, after a battle. She did not like fighting, or seeing people hurt, even the English. She wept for the dead, on both sides. She once held the head of a wounded English soldier, in her lap, and comforted him, and asked a priest to come and hear his confession. She believed that the English should go home. But she did not hate them. She prayed for them.

After Patay, the road to Reims was open. But Reims was far away, about three hundred kilometres to the north-east, through country still held by the English, and the Burgundians. The Dauphin's advisers were worried. They said it was too dangerous. That the army should go somewhere else, and attack Normandy, or Paris. But Jeanne insisted. She said that the Dauphin must be crowned, at Reims, as soon as possible. Once he was crowned, everyone would know that he was the true king.

{img:royal-army-procession-countryside-dauphin-and-maid-riding-banners-summer}
At last, the Dauphin agreed. At the end of June, he set out, with Jeanne, and a large army, for Reims. It was a strange journey. They marched through enemy country, but they hardly had to fight at all. Town after town opened its gates to them, without a battle. Auxerre gave them food, and supplies. Troyes, the town where the terrible treaty had been signed, surrendered after a few days. Châlons opened its gates. The people came out to cheer.

In many of the towns, people came to see Jeanne. They crowded around her horse, and touched her, and asked her to bless them, and their children. Some of them brought her rosaries, and medals, to touch, so that they would be holy. Jeanne laughed, kindly, and said, touch them yourselves. They will be just as good, from your touch, as from mine. She did not want people to treat her like a saint.

--

At one village, on the road, Jeanne met some people from Domrémy. They had walked a long way, to see her. Among them was her godfather, and some of her old neighbours. She was overjoyed to see them. She asked about her family, and the village, and everyone she knew. She gave them gifts, and asked them to take her love to her parents. And for a little while, she was just a girl from Domrémy again, talking with her friends about home.

The army marched on, in the summer heat, through the fields of Champagne. The wheat was golden, and ripe, in the fields. The peasants stopped their harvesting, to watch the army pass, and wave. In the evenings, the army camped in the fields, and the soldiers sat around their fires, and sang. And Jeanne prayed, in her tent, and thanked God for every day, and asked Him to bring them safely to Reims.

{img:distant-view-reims-cathedral-towers-across-golden-wheat-fields-july}
On the sixteenth of July, 1429, the army came over a hill, and saw, in the distance, across the golden fields, the great towers of the cathedral of Reims, rising above the city. The English garrison had already left. The people of Reims opened the gates, and welcomed the Dauphin, and the maiden, with cheers, and bells, and flowers. And that night, the whole city prepared, for the great ceremony of the next day.
