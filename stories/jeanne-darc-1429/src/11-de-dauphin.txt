In the evening, Jeanne climbed up the steep path, from the town, to the castle of Chinon. It was dark, and the castle was lit with torches. She was led through the great gate, and across the courtyard, and up the stairs, into the great hall of the castle. It was a long, high room, with a huge fireplace, at one end, and tall windows. It was lit by fifty torches, people said. And it was full of people.

Three hundred lords, and knights, and ladies, and officials, of the Dauphin's court, had come to see the strange girl from Lorraine. They were dressed in their finest clothes, in silk, and velvet, and fur, and jewels. They talked, and laughed, and whispered, as Jeanne came in. She was dressed in her simple boy's clothes, of black and grey, with her short dark hair. She looked very small, and very plain, among all the fine people.

--

{img:peasant-girl-kneeling-before-plain-dressed-dauphin-among-courtiers-surprise}
The story says that the Dauphin had decided to test her. He did not sit on his throne. He dressed in ordinary clothes, and hid among the courtiers, in the crowd, while another man sat on the throne, pretending to be him. If Jeanne was really sent by God, he thought, she would know which one was the real Dauphin. And if she did not, she was a fraud.

Jeanne had never seen the Dauphin before. There were no photographs, in those days, and very few pictures of him. But she walked straight past the man on the throne, without stopping. She looked around the crowd, for a moment. Then she walked up to the Dauphin, among the courtiers, and knelt in front of him, and said, God give you a good life, gentle king. The Dauphin said, I am not the king. And he pointed to the man on the throne. But Jeanne said, in God's name, gentle prince, it is you, and nobody else.

{img:dauphin-and-girl-speaking-privately-window-alcove-candle-serious}
Then Jeanne told him why she had come. She said that she was sent by God, to lift the siege of Orléans, and to lead him to Reims, to be crowned and anointed, as the true king of France. The Dauphin took her aside, to a window, away from the crowd, and they talked privately, for a long time. Nobody knows exactly what they said. But when they came back, the Dauphin looked very different. People said that he looked radiant, as if he had heard something wonderful.

Later, people said that Jeanne had told him a secret. Something that only he knew. Perhaps a prayer, that he had said, alone, in his chapel, asking God to show him if he was really the true heir to the throne. And Jeanne had told him that he was. Nobody knows if this is true. But something she said convinced him. From that moment, the Dauphin began to believe in her.

--

The Dauphin's advisers were not so sure. Some of them thought she was mad. Others thought she might be a witch, or a spy, sent by the English. Others thought she might be useful, whatever she was, because the people believed in her, and the soldiers might follow her. They argued about it, for days. In the meantime, Jeanne stayed in a tower, in the castle, with a lady to look after her.

{img:young-duke-of-alencon-meeting-joan-tilting-yard-horse-lance-spring-sun}
While she was at Chinon, Jeanne met a young nobleman, the Duke of Alençon. He was a cousin of the Dauphin, about twenty-two years old, handsome, and cheerful. He had been a prisoner of the English, for several years, and had just been released. He came to Chinon to meet the maiden. He liked her at once. And she liked him. She called him, my fair duke. He would become one of her closest friends, and fight beside her, in the months to come.

One day, the duke watched Jeanne ride, with a lance, in the meadow, below the castle. She rode so well, and handled the lance so skilfully, that he was amazed. He gave her a fine horse, as a gift. The girl from Domrémy, who had looked after cows, by the river, was learning to be a knight. And she was learning very fast.
