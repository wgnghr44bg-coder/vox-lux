Tonight, we travel back almost six hundred years, to the autumn of the year 1428. We go to the east of France, to a small village on the banks of a quiet river, called the Meuse. The village is called Domrémy. It is a tiny place, of perhaps thirty or forty families. Stone houses, with steep roofs, along a single street. A little church, with a square tower. Gardens, and orchards, and meadows, running down to the river. And all around, the gentle green hills, and the dark oak woods.

It is a peaceful place, in many ways. The people are farmers, and shepherds. They grow wheat, and oats, and hemp. They keep cows, and sheep, and pigs, and chickens. They work hard, from dawn until dusk, through the seasons, as their parents, and grandparents, did before them. They go to church on Sundays, and on the many holy days. And they know everyone in the village, and everyone in the next village, and the one after that.

--

But France, in 1428, is not a peaceful country. It has been at war for almost a hundred years. A long, slow, terrible war, against the English, who claim to be the rightful kings of France. English armies, and their allies, the Burgundians, have taken half of the country. Paris, the capital, is in their hands. And now, this very autumn, an English army has surrounded the great city of Orléans, on the river Loire. If Orléans falls, the rest of France may fall too.

{img:village-girl-1428-red-dress-spinning-wool-doorway-cottage-autumn-light}
In Domrémy, there is a girl of sixteen. Her name is Jeanne. Her father is a farmer, and one of the leading men of the village. She is strong, and healthy, and good at spinning, and sewing, and looking after the animals. She is kind, and cheerful, and very religious. She goes to church more often than anyone else in the village. The other young people tease her about it, a little. But they like her.

If you enjoy calm journeys into the past like this one, you are warmly welcome to subscribe to the channel, so you can find the next one easily. And now, let us begin.

--

{img:young-peasant-girl-kneeling-in-garden-light-through-trees-church-bells}
Jeanne has a secret. For about three years, since she was thirteen, she has been hearing voices. She believes they are the voices of saints, sent by God. At first, they told her to be good, and to go to church. But now, they are telling her something much bigger. Something impossible. They are telling her that she must leave her village, and go to the king, and help him to save France. And she has begun to believe that she must obey.

In the next few months, this girl from a tiny village will do something that nobody has ever done before. She will travel across France, through enemy country, to meet the uncrowned king. She will ride at the head of his army, in armour, with a white banner. She will help to lift the siege of Orléans. And she will stand beside the king, in the great cathedral of Reims, when he is crowned. Today, we know her as Joan of Arc.

{img:white-banner-fleur-de-lis-golden-light-cathedral-distance-hopeful}
This will be a gentle telling of her story. We will spend much of our time in her village, in the autumn, at harvest time, and in the quiet places she knew. And we will follow her journey, from a distance, without dwelling on the battles, or the sad things. We will see the France of her time. The villages, and the roads, and the castles, and the cities. And the ordinary people, who prayed, and hoped, and waited, for peace.

So let us go to Domrémy, in the autumn of 1428. Let us walk along the village street, in the early morning, as the mist rises from the river, and the church bell rings. And let us meet Jeanne, and her family, as they begin another day, in the fields.
