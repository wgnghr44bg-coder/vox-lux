After the coronation, Jeanne wanted to go on, and free Paris, and the rest of France, from the English. But the new king, Charles, was cautious. His advisers wanted to make peace, by talking, with the Duke of Burgundy. So the army waited, and wandered, and lost time. In September 1429, they finally attacked Paris. But the attack failed. Jeanne was wounded, again, by an arrow, in the leg. And the king ordered the army to retreat.

It was the first time Jeanne had failed. She was very sad. She hung up her armour, in the great abbey church of Saint Denis, near Paris, as an offering to God. And the army went back to the Loire. That winter, Jeanne stayed with the court, in various castles, and towns. The king gave her family a great honour. He made them nobles. From then on, they had a coat of arms, with a sword, holding up a crown, and two golden lilies.

--

But Jeanne was not happy at court. She did not like the politics, and the plotting, and the waiting. She wanted to be doing something. In the spring of 1430, she left the court, with a small group of soldiers, and went north, to help the towns that were still fighting against the Burgundians. Her voices told her, around this time, that she would be captured, before the summer. She was afraid. But she went on.

In May 1430, she went to help the town of Compiègne, which was being besieged by the Burgundians. On the evening of the twenty-third of May, she rode out of the town, with some soldiers, to attack the enemy camp. But the attack went wrong. The enemy came from all sides. Jeanne and her soldiers had to retreat, back toward the town. She stayed at the back, to protect the others, as they retreated.

{img:compiegne-bridge-evening-1430-town-gate-closing-distant-riders-sunset}
When she reached the town, the gate had been closed, and the bridge raised, to keep the enemy out. Jeanne was left outside. A Burgundian archer pulled her from her horse, by her cloak. And she was taken prisoner. She was eighteen years old. The Burgundians were delighted. They had captured the famous Maid of Orléans. They took her away, to one of their castles.

For several months, Jeanne was kept as a prisoner, in castles belonging to the Burgundians. She was treated fairly well, at first. In one castle, at Beaurevoir, there were three kind ladies, who looked after her, and gave her women's clothes, and were very fond of her. She was allowed to walk in the garden, and to hear mass. But she was a prisoner. And she longed for freedom.

--

{img:castle-tower-beaurevoir-window-girl-looking-out-over-fields-autumn}
The king of France, Charles, did nothing to help her. He did not pay a ransom for her, or try to rescue her, as kings sometimes did for their important prisoners. Nobody really knows why. Perhaps his advisers told him not to. Perhaps he was busy with other things. Perhaps he no longer believed in her. It is one of the sad parts of the story. The king she had crowned did not come to save her.

The English wanted her very much. They believed that she was a witch, who had used evil magic to defeat them. They wanted to put her on trial, and to show the world that she was not sent by God. If they could prove that, they could prove that Charles was not a true king, because he had been crowned with her help. So they paid the Burgundians a great sum of money, ten thousand gold coins, and the Burgundians sold Jeanne to the English.

{img:prisoner-transport-cart-guards-autumn-road-normandy-grey-sky-solemn}
In the winter of 1430, Jeanne was taken to the city of Rouen, in Normandy, the capital of the English part of France. She was kept in a castle, in a tower, guarded by English soldiers, day and night. It was cold, and dark, and lonely. She was far from her family, and her friends, and her village. But she still prayed, every day. And her voices, she said, still came to comfort her.
